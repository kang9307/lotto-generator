When a Real Estate Agent Is at Fault - Scope of Liability and Claim Procedure in Korea

The boundary of the duty comes first. What falls outside it is handled a different way

Aug 1, 2026 Law & Legal

This article is for information only. It is not legal advice and does not substitute for an assessment of any specific matter. The outcome of a real case turns on the contract, the confirmation and explanation statement, the property register, and what was actually said between the parties, so anyone in an actual dispute should consult a lawyer. All examples below are invented for illustration and correspond to no real case, office, or individual. This article describes the law of the Republic of Korea only and does not apply to property transactions in other jurisdictions. The provisions cited were verified on 1 August 2026; legislation can be amended, so the original text should be checked again before any decision.

Introduction: Saying the agent was wrong is not the same as saying a claim exists

Problems often surface months after the contract is signed. An encumbrance on the register turns out to have been there all along. The scale of the deposits paid by other tenants in the same building becomes clear too late. A defect that was invisible at viewing appears after move-in. In every version of this, one thought arrives first: why did the agent not tell me about this.

It is a fair reaction. Legally, though, that single thought splits into two different questions.

  • 1. Is it true that the agent did not say it. This is a question of fact. It is settled with the confirmation statement and the record of what was exchanged
  • 2. Was that item inside the scope of the duty the statute places on the agent. This is a question of law. It is settled by reading the Act and its Enforcement Decree, which enumerate the items

If only the first holds and the second does not, a claim does not move easily. If the item is inside the enumerated scope and the first is established as well, there is a starting point for discussion. The duty of a Korean licensed agent is not open-ended; the legislation lists the matters and thereby draws a boundary.

This article maps that boundary using provisions that were actually verified. Following the map leads to one conclusion. Getting the confirmation statement right at the moment of signing is far more effective than arguing about it afterwards. An after-the-fact dispute is an attempt to reconstruct what was said in a room, and there is almost nothing to reconstruct it with except that document.

Note that this article covers liability arising in the brokerage process only. Whether a right is extinguished or assumed at auction is covered in the guide to auction rights analysis; the procedure for recovering a lease deposit itself is in the deposit recovery guide; and a tenant position when renewal is refused is in the renewal refusal guide.

1. What the agent duty covers - where the statutory boundary runs

The starting point is Article 25 of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act. As verified, paragraph 1 requires a licensed agent who has been asked to broker a property to verify, before the brokerage is completed, the condition, location and rights relating to the property, restrictions on transaction or use imposed by law, and similar matters, to explain them to the client faithfully and accurately, and to present supporting documents such as a certified copy of the land register, a comprehensive real estate certificate, or a certified copy of the property register (verified on 1 August 2026 through the National Law Information Center and the Ministry of Government Legislation plain-language service).

Three distinct acts are packed into that sentence, and the distinction matters.

  • Verification. The agent must investigate. Repeating what the other side said is not enough
  • Explanation. What was verified must be conveyed so the client can understand it. The provision uses the words faithfully and accurately
  • Presentation of supporting documents. The provision goes beyond speaking and reaches showing the paperwork. This is the step most often skipped in practice, and also the one a client has the clearest basis to demand

So what has to be verified and explained? The list sits not in the Act but in the Presidential Decree. It is Article 21(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act, and the items verified through the Ministry of Government Legislation plain-language service are as follows.

Basic particularsType, location, lot number, land category, area, use, structure, year of constructionLand register, building ledger, comprehensive real estate certificate

The last row carries a caveat. According to the same source, certain items apply only where the brokerage concerns a residential tenancy. The list of duties therefore differs between a sale and a lease. Arguing a tenancy case on the standard applicable to sales points the discussion in the wrong direction.

1.1 Where the duty stops

Read the table in reverse and the outside edge appears. The items below are either absent from the list or beyond any means of verification an agent has. A large share of disputes turns on this line.

  • Whether the price is fair. The provision requires the expected transaction price to be recorded; it does not require an opinion on whether that figure matches the market
  • Investment value or future prices. Prediction is not a matter to be verified and explained
  • Whether the counterparty will perform later. Whether a landlord will still be able to return a deposit years afterwards is not something verifiable at signing
  • Facts that appear in no register, ledger or site inspection. It is difficult to read the duty as requiring discovery of circumstances for which no means of verification exists
  • Decisions that belong to the client. Loan eligibility, funding plans, how the family intends to use the property

Where the argument most often goes wrong. The proposition that the agent must answer for a deposit that was not returned does not stand on its own. Non-return of a deposit is the landlord breach of contract; what can be put to the agent is the part of the loss traceable to something that should have been verified and explained at signing and was not. The two may overlap or may not overlap at all. Section 4 examines the split in detail.

1.2 Explaining the guarantee is also a duty

One provision is less widely known. As verified, Article 30(5) of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act requires that, when the brokerage is completed, the agent explain the matters concerning the guarantee of liability for damages to the parties and deliver a copy of the relevant certificate or provide it as an electronic document.

In plain terms, the client should have been told at signing how much cover this office carries and where a claim would be directed. Keeping that copy of the certificate saves considerable time later in identifying where to file. It is one of the documents a client can ask for at the table with a clear statutory basis.

2. The confirmation statement is the evidence - signature, seal, delivery, preservation

This is the most practical section of the article. Brokerage disputes are usually decided not by interpretation of the law but by whether anyone can show what was explained at the time, and the document that shows it is the confirmation and explanation statement for the brokered property.

The verified provisions read as follows.

  • Article 25(3) of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act. When the brokerage is completed and the transaction contract is drawn up, the matters verified and explained must be recorded in writing in the manner prescribed by Presidential Decree, delivered to the parties, and the original, a copy or an electronic document preserved for the period prescribed by Presidential Decree
  • Article 21 of the Enforcement Decree. That prescribed period is three years
  • Article 25(4) of the Act. The licensed agent must sign and seal the statement, and where an employed licensed agent carried out the brokerage in question, that person must sign and seal it together

Translated into working language: the confirmation statement is not a helpful handout but a document the law requires to be delivered, and the signature on it identifies who did the explaining. That is precisely why the employed agent must co-sign. The person who actually dealt with the client is named on the paper.

2.2 When it is missing or thin

When the document is absent or full of blanks, people commonly conclude that the missing record puts them at a disadvantage. That is not accurate. Recording and delivery are duties placed on the agent side, so the absence of the document carries meaning for both parties.

  • If it was never delivered, record that fact itself. When it was requested and what answer came back is useful material
  • A blank is itself information. If verification was difficult, the reason should appear; a field with nothing at all leaves room to revisit how the verification was carried out
  • A blank does not translate straight into compensation. The three requirements in section 4 are still needed. A blank is a starting point, not a conclusion
  • The three-year preservation period cuts both ways. In an older matter the other side may no longer hold the original either, so keeping your own copy well beyond that is the safer course

3. Common dispute types and the issue in each

Each type argues over something different. Below is a map of what the live issue is, read against the verified list of matters to be explained. It is a map of arguments, not a set of outcomes.

3.1 Why the fourth row is structurally hard

Senior deposits in a multi-household house are awkward for a structural reason: the information does not come from the register. Deposits paid by other households do not appear on a certified copy of the property register. The route to the information therefore runs through what the landlord presents.

  • The verified list includes an item connected to landlord information disclosure under Article 3-7 of the Housing Lease Protection Act. It applies to tenancy brokerage only
  • The issue therefore shifts away from whether the agent should have discovered other deposits and towards whether the landlord presented information, and whether it was explained and recorded
  • The most practical thing a tenant can do at the table is ask for that information to be written into the confirmation statement. A number heard aloud leaves no trace
  • How priority is actually determined and what is extinguished or assumed belongs to the auction stage, and is covered in the auction rights analysis guide

4. Anatomy of a damages claim - fault, loss, causation

As verified, Article 30(1) of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act provides that a licensed agent who, in the course of brokerage, causes property damage to a party to the transaction through intent or negligence, is liable to compensate that damage. Paragraph 2 of the same Article provides that a licensed agent is also liable where property damage is caused to a party because the agent made the brokerage office available as a venue for another person brokerage activity.

Broken into elements, paragraph 1 reads as follows.

ElementWhat is examinedDocuments typically usedWhere it typically stalls
Brokerage characterWas the conduct part of a brokerage activityContract, confirmation statement, record of brokerage fee receivedRoom to treat it as an introduction unrelated to brokerage
Intent or negligenceWas an item inside the statutory list left unaddressedEntries in the confirmation statement, register recordsThe item in question falls outside the list
Property damageHow much actual financial loss occurredBank records, repair quotations, distribution resultsThe loss is not yet fixed
CausationWould the explanation have avoided the lossThe full history of how the contract came aboutRoom to say the loss would have occurred regardless

The fourth row is where real disputes most often catch. If the explanation was indeed missing but the client would have signed anyway, the link between the omission and the loss needs to be shown. Conversely, if there is material supporting the view that the client would not have signed had the information been known, the link becomes visible.

4.1 Where agent liability parts from counterparty liability

Miss this distinction and the whole discussion drifts. The two arise from different sources and do not substitute for each other.

  • Landlord and seller liability comes from the contract. A deposit not returned, ownership not transferred. The counterparty is the other side of the contract
  • Agent liability comes from a statutory duty. It is confined to loss caused by failure to perform the verification and explanation duty. The counterparty is the licensed agent
  • Establishing one does not extinguish the other. The structure is not one of double recovery for the same loss, so a judgement is needed on which route to pursue first
  • What the client could have checked personally may also be weighed. Whether the register could have been obtained, whether the site was visited
  • For that reason trying to compute an expected award in advance is of little value. Assembling the documents and moving to consultation is the better use of the time

If an unlicensed assistant handled everything. Sometimes the viewing and the explanation are done by a staff member without a licence, and the response is that the office bears no responsibility for that person. It is a verified fact that Article 30(2) extends liability to a case where the office was made available as a venue for another person brokerage activity. How far that reaches in a given case varies, so consultation is required.

5. The mutual-aid guarantee - where the money to pay comes from

Even where liability is established, recovery is difficult if the other side cannot pay. Korean law therefore requires the funds to be secured before business begins. As verified, Article 30(3) of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act requires a licensed agent, before commencing business, to take measures guaranteeing liability for damages by way of guarantee insurance, joining the mutual-aid scheme under Article 42 of the same Act, or making a deposit.

The minimum amounts sit in the Presidential Decree. The following was verified in Article 24 of the Enforcement Decree of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act (version in force from 1 January 2026, Presidential Decree No. 35824).

CategoryMinimum guarantee to be establishedNote
Licensed agent that is a corporation400 million KRW or moreAn additional 200 million KRW or more for each branch office established
Licensed agent that is not a corporation200 million KRW or moreIndividual office
Persons entitled to broker under other statutes20 million KRW or moreDistinct from licensed agents under this Act

The mutual-aid guidance published by the national association of licensed real estate agents lists actual cover bands of 200, 250 and 300 million KRW for individuals and 400, 450 and 500 million KRW for corporations, with 200 million KRW or more for each branch office. In other words the office chooses a level above the statutory floor. Obtaining a copy of the certificate before signing lets a client see that figure at the time of the contract.

5.1 The order in which a claim proceeds

The same guidance sets out six steps.

  • 1. The brokerage accident occurs. The basis is Article 30 of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act
  • 2. Notification. The accident is reported through the local branch of the association
  • 3. The compensation is fixed. This is the stage requiring a notarised settlement agreement, a conciliation protocol, or a final court judgment
  • 4. The claim is filed. The application goes to the central office
  • 5. Review. The compensation review committee examines the claim
  • 6. Payment. Payment is made within the fixed scope

The documents listed include the claim form, a copy of the mutual-aid certificate, a certificate of seal impression, and a copy of the settlement agreement, conciliation protocol or final judgment. The structure reduces to one sentence: step three comes first. Unless the scope of compensation is fixed by some route, the process does not reach step four. The idea that cover exists so a claim can simply be filed skips a step.

The time limit is short. The same guidance states that the right to claim mutual-aid payment lapses if not exercised within two years from the date the covered accident occurred (verified on 1 August 2026). In a field where losses are frequently discovered late, two years is not long. The safest first step after becoming aware of an accident is to work out the time position. How the start date is fixed can itself be contested, so it needs individual checking.

  • The cover belongs to the office, not to an individual transaction. How it is apportioned where one office has several accidents therefore needs separate checking
  • Where an office chose guarantee insurance or a deposit instead, the place and the procedure for claiming differ from the association scheme. The certificate copy states which method applies
  • The cover figure does not set the amount of compensation. It functions as a ceiling within which a fixed award is paid
  • This article does not recommend one method over another. The choice belongs to the office; the client task is to check the position at signing

6. Gathering evidence - what to keep before and after signing

Revisit the elements in section 4 and the necessary material follows automatically. It is whatever can show that an explanation was or was not given, how large the loss is, and how the two connect.

6.1 Secure these at signing

  • A copy of the confirmation and explanation statement. Take it in signed and sealed condition. This is the single most important page
  • A copy of the transaction contract. If special terms were added by hand, keep that part exactly as it is
  • A copy of the damages guarantee certificate. This is a document required to be delivered under Article 30(5)
  • A certified copy of the property register obtained on the day. The issue timestamp shows the position as at signing
  • The building ledger and land use plan confirmation document. If any were presented as supporting documents, keep them together
  • The brokerage fee receipt and transfer record. These connect to the fact that a brokerage activity took place at all

6.2 Records of what was said

  • Do not delete messages. Export them before changing devices. A format that preserves dates is preferable
  • Re-send important points as a message. A line such as noting that there are no senior deposits as explained converts a spoken statement into a record
  • Recording may be considered only where you are a party to the conversation. Recording a conversation you are not part of is an entirely different matter and needs separate legal review. What can actually be done with a recording also varies by case, so consult a lawyer
  • Photograph the site in a way that preserves dates. Keeping both the pre-contract and post-move-in condition makes any discussion of when a defect arose far easier
  • Note the name and role of whoever dealt with you. It can be checked against the signature on the confirmation statement

6.3 Material showing the loss

  • Proof of expenditure. Transfer records, receipts, repair quotations and tax invoices
  • Proof of what was not recovered. Distribution schedules, records of amounts actually returned
  • Proof of sequence. A timeline of when you learned what connects directly to the time calculation in section 5
  • Write unfixed losses down as unfixed. Inflated figures do not help

7. Routes to resolution - negotiation, standards, reporting, civil process

There is more than one route and they serve different purposes. The most common misunderstanding is that reporting to an administrative authority produces money back. Reporting deals with sanctions; compensation runs on a separate track.

RouteWhat it addressesNature of the outcomeVerified basis or standard
Negotiation between the partiesScope and amount of compensationA settlement. Notarised, it becomes step three materialDocument list in the association guidance
Use of the consumer dispute standardsA reference point for settlement and recommendationA non-binding standard. A reference for negotiationConsumer dispute resolution standards under Article 16 of the Framework Act on Consumers

Report to the registering authorityAdministrative sanction against the agentA decision on sanction. Separate from compensationPenalty and disposition provisions of the Act
Civil processExistence and extent of liabilityJudgment or settlement. Becomes step three materialArticle 30(1) of the Act

Read the table vertically and the structure appears. Negotiation, standards and civil process are procedures that fix the scope of compensation; the mutual-aid claim is the procedure that pays out what has been fixed. That sets the order. The scope must be fixed by some route before the next stage opens.

7.1 What reporting does and does not do

  • A brokerage office is registered with a local authority, and where the law is breached that authority considers sanctions. Within what was verified, Article 49 of the Act provides for imprisonment of up to one year or a fine of up to 10 million KRW for matters such as disclosure of confidential information or taking brokerage fees in excess of the permitted amount
  • Reporting does not return money. The sanction track and the compensation track are distinct
  • There can nonetheless be an effect in that facts get organised during any inquiry, which may serve as a reference in later negotiation
  • This article does not recommend any particular route. Cost, duration and burden differ, so the choice belongs in a consultation once the documents are assembled

7.2 Time limits: what is verified and what is not

  • Verified. The right to claim mutual-aid payment is stated as two years from the date the covered accident occurred
  • Verified. The confirmation statement preservation period is three years under Article 21 of the Enforcement Decree. That is a preservation period, not a claim period
  • Needs checking. The general limitation period for damages claims under the Korean Civil Act was not verified from the original text for this article. In an aged matter this must be checked separately
  • Needs checking. The jurisdiction and timeframes of any conciliation body handling brokerage disputes were not verified. Section 9 records how to check

8. Pre-contract prevention checklist

Compressed into a form usable at the table, everything above comes to the following. This list is effectively the conclusion of the article.

8.1 Documents to check yourself before signing

  • Certified copy of the property register. Not one handed to you, but one you obtain on the day. Check the owner name and address and any mortgages, attachments or chonsegwon entries
  • Building ledger. Check actual use, area, and whether the building is flagged as unlawful
  • Land use plan confirmation document. Check for public law restrictions on use
  • For a residential tenancy, the information the landlord presents. The verified list includes an item connected to Article 3-7 of the Housing Lease Protection Act
  • The damages guarantee certificate. Explanation and delivery of a copy are required under Article 30(5)

8.2 Questions to ask at the table

  • May I see the supporting documents for this explanation. Article 25(1) requires presentation of supporting documents, so it is a grounded request
  • Are there any rights not shown on the register. If you get an answer, ask for it to be written into the confirmation statement
  • Are there other tenants in this building, and is the scale of their deposits confirmed. In a tenancy this goes directly to priority
  • What is included in the maintenance charges. Both the amount and the basis of calculation are recordable items
  • Will the person who dealt with me today sign the confirmation statement. This connects to who must sign and seal under Article 25(4)
  • May I have a copy of the guarantee certificate now. It is a document required to be delivered under Article 30(5)

8.3 What to look at on the confirmation statement

  • Does the rights section reproduce what the register says. Type and priority must both appear for a comparison to be possible
  • Is the field for circumstances not on the register left empty. If it is, ask why
  • Is the condition of fixtures marked item by item. A document ticked uniformly leaves room to revisit how the inspection was done
  • Are the expected price and the basis for the brokerage fee recorded. Most fee disputes turn on this field
  • Are the signatures and seals present. The licensed agent, and the employed agent who dealt with you if there was one
  • Have you kept the copy you were given. The other side has a three-year duty; your own retention has no deadline

Conclusion: most of the outcome is decided at signing

In summary: Korean legislation enumerates the items and thereby fixes the scope of the agent duty. Article 25 of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act commands verification, explanation and the presentation of supporting documents; Article 21 of the Enforcement Decree fills in the list; Article 30 sets liability for breach and the source of funds behind it. For a claim to move, the item must sit inside that scope, a loss must exist, and the two must connect.

Put differently, the proposition that fault by an agent means recovery is only half right. Whatever happened outside the boundary is difficult to address by this route however unfair it feels. And even inside the boundary, discussion stalls without material showing what was explained.

The practical conclusion therefore lies not in the response but in the hour spent signing. Obtaining the register yourself, asking for blanks to be filled on the spot, confirming the signatures and seals, and keeping a copy of the guarantee certificate. These four cost almost nothing and completely change where any later discussion begins.

And if an accident has already happened, start with the time position. On the verified material alone, the right to claim mutual-aid payment runs two years from the date the covered accident occurred. Losing that window while assembling documents is the most avoidable loss of all.

## 2. Pre-contract checklist (use in this order at the table)

### 2-1. Documents to obtain and check yourself

- [ ] **Certified copy of the property register** - not one handed to you, but obtained by you ON THE DAY
  - [ ] Owner name and address match the person you are contracting with
  - [ ] Mortgages / provisional attachments / chonsegwon / trust registrations, and their priority
  - [ ] The issue timestamp is the same day as the contract

| Where to look | Normal | Ask on the spot if it looks like this |
|---|---|---|
| Rights section | Registered rights with type and priority | Blank or marked none while the register shows entries |
| Actual rights section | Circumstances not on the register written in | Every field empty |
| Signature and seal | Licensed agent + the employed agent who dealt with you | No signature / a name other than the person you met |
| Delivery | Copy received at the table | Promised for later and never received |
| Fixtures and condition | Marked item by item | Everything ticked satisfactory in one sweep |
| Maintenance charges | Amount + basis of calculation | Amount only |
| Guarantee certificate | Explained and copy delivered | Never seen the certificate |

## 3. Evidence checklist

### 3-1. Secure at signing

- [ ] **Copy of the confirmation and explanation statement**, signed and sealed - THE most important page
- [ ] **Copy of the transaction contract** (including handwritten special terms)
- [ ] **Copy of the damages guarantee certificate**
- [ ] **Certified copy of the property register obtained on the day** (check the issue timestamp)
- [ ] **Building ledger / land use plan confirmation document** presented as supporting material
- [ ] **Brokerage fee receipt** and transfer record

### 3-2. Records of what was said

- [ ] Do NOT delete messages. **Export** before changing devices (a format that keeps dates)
- [ ] Re-send important spoken points **as a message** - this converts speech into a record
      e.g. noting that there are no senior deposits, as explained
- [ ] Recording may be considered **only where you are a party to the conversation.** Recording a
      conversation you are not part of is an entirely different matter needing separate legal review.
      What can be done with a recording also varies by case - **consult a lawyer**
- [ ] Site photographs - **before contract** and **after move-in** separately, in a way that keeps dates
- [ ] Note the **name and role of whoever dealt with you** - to check against the signature

### 3-3. Material showing the loss

- [ ] Proof of expenditure - transfers, receipts, repair quotations, tax invoices
- [ ] Amounts not recovered - distribution schedules, records of what was returned
- [ ] **Timeline** of when you learned what - directly feeds the limitation calculation
- [ ] Record unfixed losses **as unfixed.** Do not inflate

## 4. The 3+1 elements of a damages claim

| Element | What is examined | Documents used | Where it stalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brokerage character | Was it part of a brokerage activity | Contract, statement, fee record | Room to treat it as an unrelated introduction |
| Intent or negligence | Was an item from section 1 left unaddressed | Statement entries, register records | The item falls **outside** scope |
| Property damage | How much financial loss actually occurred | Bank records, quotations, distributions | The loss is not yet fixed |
| Causation | Would the explanation have avoided the loss | Full history of the contract | Room to say it would have occurred anyway |

**Basis** - Act Art. 30(1): liability where intent or negligence in the course of brokerage causes property damage to a party.
Act Art. 30(2): liability also where the office was made available as a venue for another person brokerage activity.

**Splitting the responsibility**
- Landlord / seller liability arises from the **contract** (deposit not returned, ownership not transferred)
- Agent liability arises from a **statutory duty** (confined to loss from breach of the verify-and-explain duty)
- Establishing one does not extinguish the other, but it is not a structure of double recovery
- What the client could reasonably have checked personally may also be weighed

## 5. Routes and their procedures

| Route | What it addresses | Nature of outcome | Verified basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negotiation between parties | Scope and amount | Settlement. **Notarised, it becomes step-3 material** | Document list in the association guidance |
| Consumer dispute standards | Basis for settlement and recommendation | Non-binding. A reference for negotiation | Framework Act on Consumers Art. 16 |
| Mutual-aid claim | Payment of fixed compensation | Mutual-aid payment | Act Art. 30(3), association guidance |
| Report to registering authority | **Administrative sanction** on the agent | Sanction decision. **Separate from compensation** | Penalty and disposition provisions |
| Civil process | Existence and extent of liability | Judgment or settlement. **Step-3 material** | Act Art. 30(1) |

**STRUCTURE** - negotiation, standards and civil process **fix the scope**; the mutual-aid claim **pays out what is fixed.**
The scope must be fixed by some route before the next stage opens.

### 5-1. Mutual-aid claim, six steps (per the association guidance)

1. **Brokerage accident occurs** (basis: Act Art. 30)
2. **Notification** - through the local branch of the association
3. **Compensation is fixed** - THIS COMES FIRST
   - Notarised settlement agreement / conciliation protocol / final court judgment
4. **Claim filed** - with the central office
5. **Review** - by the compensation review committee
6. **Payment**

**Documents** - claim form / copy of the mutual-aid certificate / certificate of seal impression / copy of the settlement agreement, conciliation protocol or final judgment

### 5-2. Minimum guarantee (Enforcement Decree Art. 24, in force 2026-01-01, Presidential Decree No. 35824)

| Category | Minimum | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed agent that is a corporation | 400 million KRW or more | Plus 200 million KRW or more per branch office |
| Licensed agent that is not a corporation | 200 million KRW or more | Individual office |
| Persons entitled to broker under other statutes | 20 million KRW or more | Distinct from agents under this Act |

Actual association cover bands - individual 200 / 250 / 300 million KRW, corporation 400 / 450 / 500 million KRW, branch office 200 million KRW or more

## 8. Do not

- Jump from a deposit not returned straight to agent liability - run the **section 1 scope test** first
- **Sign with blanks left** on the confirmation statement - ask for them to be filled on the spot
- Rely on a spoken explanation alone - **re-send it as a message**
- Assume reporting brings money back - the **sanction track and the compensation track are separate**
- **Let the limitation period run out** while gathering documents - mutual-aid is two years from the accident
- Build expectations from a pre-computed award - it varies by case and is not something to predict
- Record a conversation you are not a party to - a separate legal problem entirely
