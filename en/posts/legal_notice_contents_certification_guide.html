Introduction: What Is Naeyong-Jeungmyeong (Certified Mail)?

Naeyong-Jeungmyeong (Korean certified mail) is a postal service in which the Korean post office certifies what document a sender mailed to a recipient and when. The Enforcement Rules of the Postal Act define it as a special handling service, premised on registered handling, in which the post office certifies that the sender mailed a document with certain content to the recipient at a certain time, whether through a post office counter or an information and communications network (Article 25(1)4(a)). The detailed handling rules are in Articles 46 to 55 of the same Rules. When a legal dispute arises, it is the most basic way to leave objective proof that "I clearly notified the other party of this."

Many people see it as a mere "warning letter," but what matters more is that it records that a declaration of intent reached the other party. A declaration of intent addressed to another person takes effect when it reaches that person (Civil Act, Article 111(1)), so for notices that change a legal relationship, such as contract termination, a demand for performance, refusal to renew a lease, or a demand to repair defects, the key is proving that the notice "arrived," not merely that it was "sent." The post office certifies the mailing and the content, and adding delivery certification also proves the delivery date and the person who received it. That is why certified mail is used for dispute prevention, managing limitation periods, and securing evidence.

This guide covers the legal effect of certified mail, how to draft it, the post office procedure and fees, and the steps that follow. Fees and rules were re-checked on September 29, 2026 against the Korea Post website and the current statutes on the National Law Information Center. The template in this guide is only an example; if your matter is complex or the amount is large, getting professional help is the safer course.

1. Legal Effect and Use Cases of Naeyong-Jeungmyeong

1.1 Evidentiary Value Recognized by Courts

Certified mail is not, by itself, an instrument that allows compulsory execution the way a judgment does. In litigation, however, it is strong evidence of notification. The Supreme Court held that "if certified mail containing a demand was sent and was not returned, it should be regarded as having been delivered around that time, absent special circumstances" (Supreme Court Decision 96Da38322, February 25, 1997). Because unreturned certified mail can be regarded as delivered around that time, the recipient's bare claim of "I never received it" does not easily overturn it. Conversely, if the mail was returned, for example because the recipient was absent, you cannot rely on this presumption, so always check whether it came back.

The main situations in which certified mail has legal significance are as follows.

  • A demand that can interrupt the limitation period: Certified mail demanding performance can qualify as a "demand" (choego) under the Civil Act. However, Article 174 of the Civil Act provides that a demand has no effect of interrupting prescription unless, within six months, a judicial claim, participation in bankruptcy proceedings, a summons for settlement, voluntary appearance, seizure, provisional attachment, or a provisional disposition follows. Certified mail alone does not finally interrupt the period; follow-up steps such as a lawsuit, a payment order, or a provisional attachment must come within six months. When the six months start can be disputed depending on the facts, so leave yourself a margin.
  • The starting point of delay: For an obligation with no fixed due date, the debtor is liable for delay from the time a demand for performance is received (Civil Act, Article 387(2)). For such obligations, the date the certified mail arrives can become the reference point for delay damages. For an obligation with a fixed due date, liability for delay begins when that date arrives (Article 387(1)).
  • Proof of rescission or termination: Rescission or termination is made by a declaration of intent to the other party (Civil Act, Article 543(1)), and rescinding for delay generally requires first demanding performance within a reasonable period (Civil Act, Article 544). Certified mail records when the demand and the rescission notice arrived.
  • A document with a fixed date: Article 3(4) of the Addenda to the Civil Act provides that a date entered by a public office certifying a matter on a private document is a fixed date (hwakjeong-ilja), and the post office writes the mailing date and a statement that the item is sent as certified mail on the document (Enforcement Rules of the Postal Act, Article 52(1)). This is why certified mail is used where a document with a fixed date is needed to assert a right against third parties, such as notice of an assignment of claims (Civil Act, Article 450(2)). Confirm with a professional whether the requirements are met in your own case.

1.2 Five Real-World Use Cases

Common situations in which certified mail is used in practice include the following.

  • Debt collection and repayment of loans: When repayment in a personal loan is late, the creditor formally demands performance by stating the principal, interest, and deadline. The letter later shows the history of the demand in a payment order application or civil lawsuit, and it can also serve as the demand described above.
  • Return of a jeonse (lump-sum lease) deposit: When a lease has ended but the deposit has not been returned, the tenant clearly records the demand for its return. It is not a step the law requires before applying for a lease registration order (imchagwon deunggi myeongryeong) or filing a deposit-return lawsuit, but it is often used as evidence that the lease ended and that the deposit was demanded. The tenant-side procedures as a whole, including the lease registration order and deposit-return guarantees, are covered in Tenant Rights and Lease Protection.
  • Dismissal and unpaid wages: When a worker receives a dismissal notice or wages or severance pay are unpaid, certified mail is used to demand reinstatement or payment. It can be submitted with a relief application to the Labor Relations Commission or a petition to the Ministry of Employment and Labor.
  • Contract termination and defect claims: Used in sales, construction, or lease contracts to rescind or terminate for the other party's non-performance, or to demand repair, a price reduction, or damages for defects.
  • Requests concerning online posts: It is sometimes used to request deletion, correction, or compensation when you believe a post on social media or an online community has harmed you. Even then, avoid language that condemns the other person conclusively or repeats the content of the post, and include only the location and time of the post and what you are requesting.

2. How to Draft Naeyong-Jeungmyeong — Templates and Required Elements

There is no fixed statutory form for certified mail. The Enforcement Rules of the Postal Act do, however, set rules on paper size, how to correct characters, and how names and addresses are written, and provide that the post office will not accept a document whose content is contrary to public order or good morals (Article 46(1)). Because the letter may become evidence, include every essential element and choose the wording carefully.

2.1 Required Items to Include

To serve well as evidence, the letter should contain the following.

  • Sender's information: Full name (or corporate name), address, and contact details. It is safer not to include personal data the notice does not need, such as a resident registration number.
  • Recipient's information: Full name (or corporate name) and address. The sender's and recipient's names and addresses on the original, the copies, and the envelope must be identical (Enforcement Rules, Article 51(1)). If a wrong address causes the mail to be returned, delivery becomes hard to prove, so re-check the address against the contract, the property register, or similar records.
  • Title: State the purpose in one line, such as "Demand for Return of Jeonse Deposit," "Demand for Repayment of Loan," or "Notice of Contract Termination."
  • Main body: Organize it as the facts (when, who, what, how, why), the demand, the deadline, and the procedure planned if there is no performance. Write specifically and on the basis of objective facts.
  • Date: State the year, month, and day. The official reference is the mailing date the post office writes on the document, so do not let the drafting date and the actual mailing date drift far apart.
  • Signature or seal: Write the sender's name on the original and sign or stamp it. Corporations use the corporate seal or an authorized seal. Because the copies must be photocopies of the original (Article 49(1)), make the copies after the original has been signed or sealed.

2.2 Tips for Effective Drafting

Meeting the formal requirements alone does not make a good letter. The following principles help you state your demand clearly while avoiding unnecessary disputes.

  • Avoid emotional language: Emotional or insulting expressions can work against the sender and, depending on the wording, can turn into an insult or defamation issue. Keep an objective, businesslike tone.
  • State exact amounts and deadlines: Instead of "repay within a reasonable time," be specific, for example: "Please transfer KRW 5,000,000 to the account below by [date]." If the demand is a prerequisite for rescission, give a reasonable period for performance (Civil Act, Article 544).
  • Cite legal grounds: Citing the relevant statutes (the Civil Act, the Housing Lease Protection Act, etc.) and provisions accurately makes the basis of the demand clear. Stating the facts precisely is better than forcing in provisions you are unsure of.
  • Describe next steps calmly: State the planned procedure as a fact, for example: "If performance is not made by the deadline, I plan to proceed with legal procedures such as a payment order or civil litigation." Avoid exaggerated threats and wording that trades a demand against criminal proceedings, such as "pay or I will file a criminal complaint," because it can create misunderstandings and escalate the dispute.
  • Focus on one matter: Do not mix several matters in one letter; write a separate letter for each so the issues stay clear.

3. Post Office Mailing Procedure and Cost

Certified mail must be sent as registered mail through a post office counter or the Internet Post Office; an ordinary letter or plain registered mail does not certify the content of the document. Knowing the procedure and fees in advance saves you from having to redo documents at the counter.

3.1 Preparing One Original and Two Copies

When sending at the counter, you submit the original document and two copies of it (Enforcement Rules of the Postal Act, Article 48(1)). Each is used as follows.

  • Original — sent to the recipient: The post office compares the original with the copies and writes on each the mailing date, a statement that it is sent as certified mail, and the post office name (Article 52(1)); the sender then puts the original into the envelope and seals it where the clerk can see (Article 52(4)).
  • One copy — returned to the sender: You receive back a copy bearing the certification and keep it. Store it separately, since it is the document you will submit as evidence in a lawsuit.
  • One copy — kept by the post office: The post office keeps it for three years from the day after mailing (Article 48(3)). If the sender does not need a copy, only one copy may be submitted (proviso to the same paragraph).

The copies must be photocopies of the original, and both are prepared on 210 mm × 297 mm paper (Article 49(1)). If you correct characters, you must write "corrected," "inserted," or "deleted" and the number of characters in the margin and stamp, fingerprint, or sign there (Article 50(1)); once the mail is accepted, you cannot ask to change names or addresses or to correct characters (Article 50(3)). To send the same content to several people, you can use joint certified mail (Article 47).

Until three years from the day after mailing, the sender or recipient can ask the post office to inspect the copy (Article 55) or to re-certify (Article 54), and can also request delivery certification for certified mail after mailing within the same three years (proviso to Article 59). Because the post office keeps a copy, you still have a way to obtain certification again within that period even if you lose your own copy.

The cost of certified mail is the postage plus the registered mail fee plus the certified mail fee. The current amounts shown on the Korea Post website are as follows.

Delivery certification is a service in which the delivering post office certifies the delivery date and the recipient and notifies the sender (Enforcement Rules, Article 25(1)4(c)); the certificate is sent by mail, or electronically if the sender wishes (Article 58). Even with the presumption from the Supreme Court decision above, adding it helps prove delivery when the delivery date could be disputed.

3.3 Sending Certified Mail Through the Internet Post Office

You can also apply for certified mail online through the Internet Post Office (epost.go.kr) without visiting a post office. The Rules define certified mail as sent "through a post office counter or an information and communications network," and an amendment of August 18, 2025 added rules on submitting and keeping electronic certified mail. The process is roughly as follows.

  • Step 1 — Log in: Log in to the Internet Post Office as a member. Follow the on-screen instructions for identity verification.
  • Step 2 — Open the application menu: Enter the sender's and recipient's details in the certified mail application menu. Menu names and locations may change when the website is redesigned.
  • Step 3 — Submit the document: Write the document on screen or upload it as a file. For certified mail sent electronically, the document is submitted as an electronic file as prescribed by the Korea Post commissioner's public notice (proviso to Article 48(1)).
  • Step 4 — Pay and send: Once you pay the fee, the post office prints and mails the document. According to the Internet Post Office, mailing takes about one to three days from the application date.
  • Step 5 — Look up and re-certify: You can look up the certified mail you sent free of charge on the Internet Post Office for three years from the day after acceptance; if you need a re-certified copy, apply for it separately.

From April 1, 2026, the post office also keeps documents submitted as electronic files for three years from the day after mailing, and the file and the document sent to the recipient are deemed identical (Article 48(4)). Detailed charges for online applications, such as a production fee, could not be confirmed this time, so check the total on the payment screen.

4. Legal Steps After Sending Naeyong-Jeungmyeong

Certified mail does not end a dispute on its own; it is closer to a step before formal legal proceedings. Knowing in advance what follows if the other party does not respond makes deadlines easier to manage.

4.1 Filing for a Payment Order When There Is No Response

If the other party does not respond or perform after the certified mail arrives, the first procedure to consider is a payment order (jigeup myeongryeong, the dunning procedure). A payment order is issued by the court at the creditor's request for a claim seeking payment of money or a certain quantity of other fungible things or securities (Civil Procedure Act, Article 462). Compared with an ordinary lawsuit, it has these features.

  • Cost: A payment order application bears stamps equal to one-tenth of the stamp amount for a complaint (Act on Stamps Attached in Civil Litigation, Article 7(2)). For a claim of KRW 10 million, the complaint stamp is KRW 10 million × 45/10,000 + KRW 5,000 = KRW 50,000 (Article 2(1)2 of the same Act), so the payment order stamp is KRW 5,000. Service costs are separate.
  • Written procedure: A payment order is issued without examining the debtor (Civil Procedure Act, Article 467). How long it takes depends on the court and on service of documents.
  • An enforceable title once final: If there is no objection, the objection is withdrawn, or a dismissal of it becomes final, the payment order has the same effect as a final judgment (Article 474).
  • Conversion to a lawsuit upon objection: If the debtor objects within two weeks from the date the order was served, the order loses effect to that extent (Article 470(1)), and a lawsuit is deemed filed as of the date the payment order was applied for (Article 472(2)). The creditor must then pay the difference in stamp duty up to the complaint amount (Article 473(1)).
  • You need the debtor's address: A payment order is available only where it can be served in Korea by a method other than service by public notice (proviso to Article 462). If you do not know the other party's address, this procedure may not fit.

A payment order can also be filed through the Korean courts' electronic litigation system (ecfs.scourt.go.kr), and the certified mail is attached as evidence of the history of the demand. The path from lawsuit to compulsory execution in deposit cases is covered in more detail in Jeonse Deposit Refund Lawsuit A to Z.

4.2 Strategic Use as a Pre-Litigation Step

Besides being a step before legal proceedings, certified mail has these uses.

  • An opening for negotiation: Some recipients get in touch or agree to negotiate after receiving certified mail. A settlement at this stage can save the time and cost of litigation.
  • Recording the other side's position: The recipient's reply (a certified mail in return, or an email or text message) documents their position.
  • Material for provisional attachment or injunction: When applying for a provisional attachment or injunction to preserve the other party's assets before the main lawsuit, certified mail can be one of the materials showing that the claim exists and how it was demanded.
  • Relation to criminal proceedings: Where fraud or embezzlement is suspected, the certified mail and the recipient's reaction are sometimes submitted as reference material. Silence or evasion alone does not establish a crime, however; investigative agencies and courts make that judgment.
  • Documentary evidence at trial: Once a civil lawsuit begins, the certified mail is submitted as documentary evidence to prove the cause of action and the history of the demand.

Conclusion: Five Principles for Using Naeyong-Jeungmyeong Effectively

Certified mail is an accessible legal tool that ordinary people can draft and send themselves, and it is official evidence of notification in a dispute. The points covered above come down to five principles.

First, keep to objective, fact-based statements; emotional expressions and exaggerated claims can work against the sender. Second, state specific amounts, deadlines, and grounds so the other party knows exactly what to do and by when. Third, confirm the recipient's exact address; if the mail is returned, the presumption of delivery is hard to rely on, and the names and addresses on the original, copies, and envelope must match.

Fourth, if the delivery date could be disputed, add delivery certification. The fee is KRW 1,600 per item, and you can still request it within three years after mailing. Fifth, plan the follow-up steps and deadlines in advance. A demand keeps its interrupting effect only if a judicial claim, seizure, or provisional attachment follows within six months, so decide the order and timing of a payment order, provisional attachment, and main lawsuit so that the letter does not end as empty words.

This article is for general information only and is not legal advice. Statutes and postal fees can change, and the legal effect of the same certified mail can differ depending on the facts of each case. If your matter is complex or the amount is large, consult a lawyer, a certified judicial scrivener, the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, or another professional or public body.

## 2. Before Writing

- [ ] I re-checked the recipient's name and address against the contract, property register, or similar records
- [ ] Sender and recipient names and addresses match on the original, copies, and envelope (Enforcement Rules Art. 51)
- [ ] The title states the purpose in one line (e.g., Demand for Return of Jeonse Deposit)
- [ ] The letter states the facts, demand, amount, deadline, and planned procedure if there is no performance, in that order
- [ ] Personal data not needed for the notice, such as a resident registration number, is left out
- [ ] There are no emotional or insulting expressions, exaggerated threats, or wording that trades the demand against a criminal complaint
- [ ] If the demand precedes rescission, it gives a reasonable period for performance (Civil Act Art. 544)
- [ ] The letter covers only one matter

## 3. At the Counter

- [ ] The letter is on A4 (210 mm × 297 mm) paper (Enforcement Rules Art. 49)
- [ ] I have one signed or sealed original and two copies photocopied from it (Arts. 48, 49)
- [ ] Any corrected characters are noted in the margin as "corrected," "inserted," or "deleted" with the character count, and stamped, fingerprinted, or signed (Art. 50)
- [ ] I checked the final version, knowing names, addresses, and characters cannot be changed after acceptance (Art. 50(3))
- [ ] If the delivery date could be disputed, I added delivery certification
- [ ] I stored the returned certified copy separately

## 4. After Sending

- [ ] I checked whether the letter was returned (if returned, the presumption of delivery is hard to rely on; Supreme Court 96Da38322)
- [ ] I marked on my calendar the day six months after the demand (Civil Act Art. 174)
- [ ] If there is no response, I decided which of a payment order, provisional attachment, or lawsuit to use and when
- [ ] I know the other party's address (if only service by public notice is possible, a payment order does not fit; proviso to Civil Procedure Act Art. 462)
- [ ] If I lose my copy, I can request inspection or re-certification within three years from the day after mailing
