Introduction: Why Travel Insurance Matters

When an accident or sudden illness sends you to a hospital abroad, the bill is often the second shock. Where your home country's public health insurance does not apply, even an ambulance ride or a single emergency room visit can produce a large invoice, and in high-cost countries such as the United States the burden is heavier still. Flights get cancelled, bags get stolen, and it is always possible to accidentally injure someone or damage their property while traveling.

Travel insurance is a financial tool designed for exactly these risks. Yet choosing a product, understanding how it interacts with the health insurance you already hold, and knowing when a claim will actually be paid are more complicated than they look. This guide sets out the core information for first-time buyers and existing policyholders alike, so you can choose and use a policy well.

This guide is general information based on guidance from South Korea's Financial Supervisory Service (FSS), the Ministry of Foreign Affairs travel safety portal, and the General Insurance Association of Korea (KNIA). It does not recommend a product or guarantee any coverage. It describes travel insurance sold in South Korea; details differ from one policy to the next.

Types of Travel Insurance: Short-Trip, Long-Stay, Domestic

The FSS describes travel insurance as a comprehensive policy covering bodily loss during domestic or overseas travel (death, permanent disability, and treatment costs for injury or illness) and property loss (personal belongings, liability, flight delay and so on), according to its press release of July 12, 2026. Products fall into a few types depending on trip length, destination, and length of stay, and working out which type fits your situation is the starting point.

Short-Trip International Insurance

The most common form, suited to short trips abroad for leisure or business lasting a few days to a few weeks. You set the departure and return dates and combine core benefits such as injury, illness medical costs, belongings, flight delay, and liability. The premium depends on the destination, trip length, and chosen benefits.

Long-Stay Insurance (Study Abroad, Working Holiday, Overseas Assignment)

For stays of three months or longer, standard short-trip policies run into limits on duration and scope. Working holiday participants, language students, degree students, and employees posted abroad should look at dedicated overseas study insurance or long-stay insurance products. Maximum coverage periods, renewal terms, and benefits differ from short-trip products, so check them separately.

Domestic Travel Insurance

Covers injury and illness during travel within your own country. Because national health insurance applies at home and medical costs are lower than abroad, whether you need it depends on your circumstances. It tends to be most useful for camping, outdoor activities, or group trips.

Core Coverage Components Explained

Accidental Death and Permanent Disability

A core benefit that pays out if the insured dies or suffers a permanent disability as a result of an accident during the trip. The amount depends on the sum insured you choose, and disability benefits are paid as a percentage of that sum according to the disability grade. Treatment of ordinary injuries requires a separate medical expense benefit.

Overseas Injury and Illness Medical Expenses (The Key Benefit)

The most important benefit in any travel policy. If you are treated, hospitalized, or operated on at an overseas medical facility because of an injury or illness during the trip, actual medical costs are reimbursed up to the limit. The FSS states that travel insurance pays only treatment and medication costs incurred at medical facilities during the trip (press release of April 29, 2025). There is no official minimum limit, so if you are heading somewhere with expensive healthcare such as the United States, Canada, or Western Europe, it is common practice to set a generous limit based on the destination and trip length.

Note: In South Korea, the domestic medical expense benefit in a travel policy and an existing real-loss (Silson) health insurance policy do not pay twice for the same costs (FSS press release of April 29, 2025, on consumer precautions drawn from major dispute cases). If you already hold real-loss insurance, it makes sense to reconsider whether you need the domestic medical benefit.

Personal Belongings

Covers items you carry, such as phones, cameras, and laptops, when they are stolen or damaged during the trip. The FSS states that travel insurance covers belongings only for damage, theft, and robbery, and does not pay for loss caused by the insured's own carelessness (April 29, 2025). The Ministry of Foreign Affairs likewise lists this benefit as "personal belongings damage (excluding loss)." Cash, dentures, prosthetic limbs, contact lenses, and glasses may not be covered (FSS, July 12, 2026). Per-item limits, depreciation for age, and overall limits also differ by policy. If something is stolen, be sure to obtain a written report from the local police.

Flight Delay and Cancellation

If a flight is delayed or cancelled beyond the time set in the policy, unavoidable costs of waiting such as meals and accommodation are reimbursed. The FSS states that indirect losses from changing or cancelling planned itineraries because of the delay are not covered, and in the same release explained that purchases of everyday goods unrelated to the trip are not covered either. You must obtain a delay confirmation issued by the airline, and meals or lodging the airline provided directly may not be paid twice. The qualifying delay time and limits vary by product.

Personal Liability

Covers your legal liability when you accidentally injure a third party or damage their property during the trip, for example breaking something in a hotel or colliding with a pedestrian on a rented bicycle. Accidents caused deliberately or while intoxicated are generally excluded. Limits vary by product, so check the amount before you buy.

Trip Cancellation and Interruption

Trip cancellation cover reimburses the non-refundable part of prepaid travel costs if you cancel before departure for a reason listed in the policy, such as sudden hospitalization or surgery of the insured or a family member, a natural disaster, or a travel advisory for the destination. Change of mind, visa refusal, and schedule changes are usually excluded. Trip interruption cover pays the unused travel costs and the extra cost of a return flight if you must come home early for similar reasons. The FSS also states that additional costs incurred when a trip is cut short and the traveler returns home because of a natural disaster are covered. Covered reasons and limits vary widely by product, so check the policy terms in advance.

Other Riders

  • Emergency evacuation and repatriation costs: covers transport home when treatment is not possible locally. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs recommends buying travel insurance before departure to prepare for situations requiring local treatment, hospitalization, or transport back to Korea
  • Passport reissue costs after loss: one of the coverage items the Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists for overseas travel insurance. It is separate from the belongings benefit, so check whether it is included
  • Sports and leisure rider: injury or death caused by high-risk sports may not be covered (FSS, July 12, 2026). If you plan scuba diving, skiing, bungee jumping, or similar, check the exclusion list and any separate rider
  • Pregnancy and childbirth: excluded from most standard travel policies. If you are pregnant and traveling abroad, check separately

Duplicate Coverage Caution: Domestic Health Insurance and Travel Insurance

One of the first things to check before buying is how the policy relates to real-loss health insurance you already hold. On April 29, 2025, the FSS published travel insurance dispute cases and consumer precautions, stating the following (translated from Korean):

Travel insurance covers medical expenses caused by accidents during travel; for domestic medical expenses, it does not pay duplicate compensation if you have already taken out another real-loss medical insurance policy.

In other words, when you are treated in Korea you cannot collect the full amount from both the travel policy and the real-loss policy, and the combined payout does not exceed the medical costs actually incurred. If you already have real-loss insurance, the domestic medical benefit in a travel policy may be of limited value, and after checking the terms you may be able to drop it and lower the premium.

By contrast, overseas medical costs are in most cases outside the scope of domestic real-loss insurance, so buying the overseas medical benefit of a travel policy separately is generally recommended for trips abroad. Check the policy terms for the exact scope.

Key Checkpoints Before You Buy

Comparing products against the checklist below reduces the chance of mistakes.

Coverage Checks

  • Overseas medical limit: set it according to medical costs at the destination and the trip length (there is no official minimum)
  • Illness vs. injury: check whether illness and injury medical costs are separate benefits or combined
  • Belongings scope: whether only damage, theft, and robbery are covered, which items are excluded (cash, glasses, contact lenses, etc.), and per-item limits and depreciation
  • Flight delay threshold: how many hours of delay trigger a payout
  • Sports and leisure: whether your planned activities are included in the base policy

Exclusion Checks

  • Pre-existing and chronic conditions: illnesses you had before departure are mostly excluded
  • Alcohol and drugs: accidents while intoxicated are generally excluded
  • War and travel advisory areas: injury or death caused by war may not be covered, and some benefits may be excluded for travel to areas under Korea's Level 3 (Departure Advised) or Level 4 (Travel Ban) advisories
  • Intentional acts: accidents caused deliberately by the insured are not covered
  • Pregnancy and childbirth: excluded from most base policies

Documents and Claim Checks

  • Keep originals or copies of local hospital receipts, medical certificates, and prescriptions
  • For flight delays, obtain a delay confirmation issued by the airline
  • For theft of belongings, obtain a local police report
  • A claim right lapses if you do not claim within three years of the date the insured event occurred (FSS financial consumer portal). The clock starts on the date of the incident, not the date you return home
  • Save the insurer's 24-hour emergency number and the Korean Ministry of Foreign Affairs consular call center (+82-2-3210-0404 from abroad) before you leave

Where to Compare Prices

Premiums for the same benefits can differ by channel and insurer. Boheom Damoa (e-insmarket.or.kr), run jointly by the Korea Life Insurance Association and the General Insurance Association of Korea, lets you compare overseas travel insurance premiums across insurers, and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs also points travelers to it for comparison and purchase. Product disclosures are also available from the General Insurance Association of Korea (knia.or.kr).

Short Trip vs. Long Stay: Choosing the Right Product

Short Trips (1 Day to Under 3 Months)

This covers most trips abroad, including sightseeing, business, and short language courses. Choose benefits according to trip length, destination (medical cost level), and activities (leisure or not). For destinations with expensive healthcare such as the United States, Canada, or Western Europe, a higher overseas medical limit is generally recommended.

Long Stays (3 Months or More)

This includes working holidays, university study, overseas assignments, and long-term training. Short-trip policies often cap the maximum period, so check long-stay or overseas study insurance products with each insurer. Also check, as part of the destination's visa conditions, whether local health insurance is mandatory. Some destinations require insurance as a visa condition. For example, the European Commission requires Schengen short-stay visa applicants to submit medical insurance covering emergency medical care, hospitalisation, and repatriation, including in the event of death. Check the destination's official guidance before departure for details such as minimum coverage amounts and whether the requirement applies to your nationality and purpose of stay.

Travel Advisory Areas

Korea's Ministry of Foreign Affairs issues travel advisories at four levels: Level 1 Travel Caution, Level 2 Travel Restraint, Level 3 Departure Advised, and Level 4 Travel Ban. At Level 3, people planning a trip are asked to cancel or postpone it; at Level 4, they must comply with the travel ban. Some insurers restrict cover or refuse to sell policies for such areas. Checking the destination's advisory level on the Ministry's travel safety portal (0404.go.kr) before departure is essential.

Buying a Policy and Filing a Claim

When and Where to Buy

According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, overseas travel insurance can be bought through insurers' websites, agencies, or insurer counters at the airport, and the Ministry recommends buying before departure. Many products restrict purchase once you have left the country, so it is safer to complete it before you go. Check the policy terms for when and on what conditions you can buy.

How to File a Claim

  1. Contact the insurer's 24-hour emergency service as soon as the incident happens
  2. If you use a local medical facility, keep original receipts, medical certificates, and prescriptions
  3. For theft, obtain a local police report
  4. For flight delays, get a delay confirmation from the airline
  5. After returning, submit documents and file the claim through the insurer's app, website, or customer center. According to the FSS, claims are in principle paid within three business days of receipt (some product lines differ), or within ten business days if investigation or verification is needed

Summary: Five Rules for Smart Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is a useful way to limit the financial damage of the unexpected. But if you do not understand the policy terms, you may not receive the payout you expected at the moment you need it. Keep these five points in mind and the choice becomes clear.

  1. Set an adequate overseas medical limit: match it to the medical cost level at the destination
  2. Check for duplicate coverage: the domestic medical benefit and existing real-loss insurance do not pay twice
  3. Read the exclusions: pre-existing conditions, alcohol, high-risk leisure, loss through carelessness, and similar
  4. Collect documents on the spot: without receipts, police reports, or delay confirmations, claims are difficult. Claim within three years of the incident
  5. Compare, then buy before departure: compare products on Boheom Damoa and the KNIA disclosures

- [ ] Will the trip last three months or longer? → If so, look at long-stay or overseas study insurance rather than a short-trip product
