Korea's Earned Income Tax Credit: Who Gets It and How Much

Three gates decide eligibility, and five separate mechanisms decide what actually arrives

Aug 10, 2026 Finance & Economy

Tax disclaimer

This article covers a South Korean government programme and is general information, not tax advice. The requirements and amounts here reflect National Tax Service guidance for 2025 income, claimed in 2026, and the thresholds change from year to year through legislative amendment. Everything below is an illustrative guide; actual eligibility and the amount paid are determined by the National Tax Service through its own review. Before filing, confirm your own situation on Hometax or with the tax credit call centre on 1566-3636 (weekdays 09:00-18:00) or the general tax helpline on 126.

Introduction: cleared the tests, smaller than expected

Two questions come up around Korea's earned income tax credit, and only two. Before filing: am I eligible. After the determination notice arrives: I qualified, so why is it this amount. The first question is usually answered by a table of thresholds. The second is not, because a set of separate mechanisms cuts the amount after eligibility has been settled.

The structure in one line: household type sets the income ceiling, income sets the calculated amount, and then assets, filing timing and unpaid tax adjust that calculated amount again. Cross 170 million won in household assets and the calculated amount is halved. File after the regular window closes and 5 percent disappears. Owe national tax and part of the credit is applied to the arrears first. Take the money semi-annually and part of it may have to go back at settlement. None of this appears on an eligibility chart, yet it is where most of the difference between the headline maximum and the deposit actually comes from.

What follows covers the eligibility side first, then the amount, then what happens after payment. This is the South Korean programme specifically; the section near the end places it next to credits readers elsewhere may already know. The band-by-band calculation formula and the case-by-case detail of household type classification live in the companion pieces on the credit calculator and self-assessment and the complete guide to the earned income and child care credits, so overlapping ground is left to those.

1. Three gates: household type, income, assets

Review works as three gates taken in order, and the order matters. Because the income ceiling differs by household type, you cannot evaluate the income test until the household type is fixed. That is why the question "my income is 30 million won, do I qualify" has no single answer. As a single household, no. As a single-earner household, yes.

  • Gate 1, household type: the presence of a spouse, dependent children and lineal ascendants aged 70 or over sorts the household into single, single-earner or dual-earner.
  • Gate 2, income: combined spousal income must fall below the total income threshold for that household type.
  • Gate 3, assets: the total assets of all household members must be under 240 million won.

The three gates do not even count the same unit, which is a frequent source of confusion. The income test looks at the couple combined. The asset test looks at all household members combined. And assets are valued not at the filing date but as of 1 June of the income year. For the 2025 income year that reference date is 1 June 2025.

On top of that, some people cannot file at all regardless of the three gates. The National Tax Service lists the following as excluded.

  • People who do not hold South Korean nationality
  • People registered as another resident's dependent child
  • People running a licensed professional practice, whether the applicant or the spouse
  • Regular employees with monthly average employment income of 5 million won or more

The professional practice and regular employee items apply to the spouse as well as the applicant, and that catches more people than expected. Households assume that a low income on the applicant's side is enough, file on that basis, and are blocked on the spouse's side.

2. Income thresholds and maximum payments by household type

The figures for 2025 income, exactly as published in the National Tax Service guidance, are below.

Household typeTotal income threshold (couple combined)Maximum payment
Single householdUnder 22 million won1.65 million won
Single-earner householdUnder 32 million won2.85 million won
Dual-earner householdUnder 44 million won3.3 million won

2.1 The dividing line is 3 million won

The three definitions split like this.

  • Single household: no spouse, no dependent child under 18, and no lineal ascendant aged 70 or over
  • Single-earner household: a household where the spouse's gross pay and equivalent income is under 3 million won, or a household with no spouse but with a dependent child or a lineal ascendant aged 70 or over
  • Dual-earner household: a household where the resident and the spouse each have gross pay and equivalent income of 3 million won or more

The practically important line here is the 3 million won of spousal gross pay and equivalent income. A spouse who earned 2.9 million won from part-time work puts the household in the single-earner category; 3.1 million won puts it in the dual-earner category. Dual-earner households have both a higher income ceiling and a higher maximum. So crossing that line is neither always bad nor always good. With combined income between 32 and 44 million won, dual-earner classification is what keeps the household eligible at all; in lower income bands, the classification changes the calculated amount instead. The income conditions attached to spouses and to lineal ascendants aged 70 or over, and the timing rules for a dependent child's age and income, are set out in the household type section of the calculator article.

2.2 The maximum is a ceiling, not a payout

1.65, 2.85 and 3.3 million won are the largest values obtainable in each category, not the amount received on qualifying. The credit is built to rise as earnings rise, hold flat across a middle band, and then fall again as earnings continue upward. A household with very low earnings and a household near the threshold both end up below the ceiling.

It is also worth noting that the test and the calculation look at different income. The total income threshold is assessed on combined spousal total income, while the credit itself is calculated, in the tax office's own wording, on employment income, business income or religious worker income. Income such as interest and dividends that counts toward total income but not toward the calculation therefore works in one direction only: it pushes a household toward the ceiling without increasing the amount.

3. The asset test: two lines at 240 and 170 million won

The asset test runs entirely independently of income. However low the income, crossing the asset line means no payment; and clearing the income test comfortably still leaves the amount exposed to a halving by asset band.

Household assets (as of 1 June of the income year)Payment
Under 170 million wonFull calculated amount
170 million to under 240 million won50 percent of the calculated amount
240 million won or moreNo payment

3.1 What counts as an asset

Under National Tax Service guidance, the asset total includes the following.

  • Housing, land and buildings, at standard assessed value
  • Passenger cars, at standard assessed value, excluding commercial vehicles
  • Jeonse deposits, the lump-sum rental deposits used in Korean leases
  • Financial assets and securities
  • Memberships
  • Rights to acquire real estate

And here is where most households are caught: debt is not deducted from the asset value. A home with a standard assessed value of 300 million won carrying a 300 million won mortgage leaves net worth near zero, but the asset total still reads 300 million won, well past the 240 million line. That is the answer to "I owe more than I own, so why was I rejected". The asset test measures gross holdings, not net worth.

3.2 How a rented home is valued

Rental deposits have their own valuation rule. National Tax Service guidance values them at the lower of the deemed jeonse deposit, 55 percent of the published price, and the actual deposit paid. There is one exception: a home rented from a lineal ascendant or descendant of the applicant or the spouse is not compared against the actual deposit at all, and is valued using a deemed deposit of 100 percent of the housing value. Living in a parent's property on a nominal deposit can therefore register a far larger asset figure than the amount actually handed over.

4. Five paths that reduce the payment

Separate mechanisms alter the deposited amount after the calculated figure exists. They differ in nature, so they are easier to read side by side.

TriggerEffectNature
Household assets of 170 million to under 240 million won50 percent of the calculated amountReduction
Filing after the regular window closes95 percent of the calculated amountReduction
Unpaid national taxApplied to arrears, capped at 30 percent of the refundSet-off
Semi-annual first-half claim35 percent of the calculated amount paid up frontInstalment, not a reduction
Overpayment found at semi-annual settlementDifference reclaimedClawback
Fraud or other improper conductReclaimed with penalty tax, plus a payment banSanction

Two of these are widely misread. First, the 35 percent in semi-annual filing is not a cut. It is part of the annual amount paid early, with the remainder attached at settlement. Second, a tax set-off does not swallow the whole credit. Guidance describes arrears as being covered up to a cap of 30 percent of the refund, with the balance paid out. Skipping the claim because of arrears is therefore not a rational choice.

One more protection is worth knowing. The credit is partly shielded from seizure: National Tax Service guidance states that amounts up to 1.85 million won of the refunded credit cannot be attached. Households whose accounts are constrained by debt enforcement keep the credit within that range.

5. Regular filing against semi-annual filing

There are three routes: regular filing, late filing, and semi-annual filing, which only employment income earners may choose. The income period covered and the payment date differ in each.

Late filing2 June - 1 December 2026Full year 2025Within 4 months of filing, at 95 percent
Semi-annual, first half1 - 15 September 2026January - June 202630 December 2026, at 35 percent
Semi-annual, second half1 - 15 March 2027July - December 202630 June 2027, settled

5.1 How the semi-annual settlement works

Semi-annual filing means receiving first and reconciling later. The first-half claim estimates the annual amount from first-half employment income and pays 35 percent of it on 30 December. When the second-half claim is filed the following March, the tax office rechecks full-year income together with the household and asset tests, computes the final figure, and settles on 30 June.

Settlement runs both ways. If second-half earnings raised the final calculated amount, the difference is added. If second-half earnings pushed the household toward the income ceiling, or if assets as of 1 June increased, the 35 percent already received becomes an overpayment and may have to be returned. Note that the asset reference date is not the settlement date: buying a car or moving to a more expensive lease after receiving the first-half payment is reflected against the following income year's 1 June measurement.

5.2 Which route to take

Only employment income earners have a choice at all. Business income earners and religious workers have the regular route only. For employment income earners the test is simple.

  • Stable earnings and stable assets favour semi-annual filing. The same money arrives earlier.
  • A year with a job change, a resignation or a new side business raises settlement clawback risk. Filing regularly, once annual income is fixed, is the calmer path.
  • The child care credit is not paid in the first-half instalment. Households entitled to it receive that portion at the second-half settlement.

6. How the child care credit relates to this one

The two credits share a name pattern and a filing procedure, which makes them look like one programme, but they are separate credits with different tests and calculations. Filing happens in the same window through the same process, and neither excludes the other. The evidence is in the tax office's own review and payment guidance, which states that the first-half semi-annual payment covers only the earned income credit and that the child care credit, where applicable, is paid together at the second-half settlement.

  • Eligible households: single-earner or dual-earner households. A single household has no dependent child by definition and so does not qualify.
  • Dependent child: a dependent child under 18 is required.
  • Income test: combined spousal total income under 70 million won, a far wider door than the earned income credit.
  • Asset test: identical. The 240 million won line and the 170 million won halving band apply unchanged.
  • Payment: between 500,000 and 1 million won per dependent child.

Because the income ceiling is 70 million won, a substantial number of households are rejected for the earned income credit but eligible for the child care credit. A single-earner household with 40 million won of combined income is above the earned income threshold of 32 million but still well below the child care threshold of 70 million. Abandoning the claim entirely on the assumption that the earned income credit is out of reach is the classic way to lose money here.

7. How to file: Hometax, Sontax, ARS, call centre, auto-filing

Several channels exist and the outcome is the same through any of them. The routes published by the National Tax Service are as follows.

  • Hometax on desktop: file through the tax credit menu at hometax.go.kr. Review status is checked in the same menu.
  • Sontax, the mobile version of Hometax: the same functions in the mobile app.
  • ARS telephone filing: 1544-9944. With the individual authentication number printed on the notice, a phone call completes the claim.
  • QR code on the notice: scanning the code on the paper or mobile notice opens the filing screen directly.
  • Assisted filing: the tax credit call centre on 1566-3636, weekdays 09:00-18:00, will file on the applicant's behalf.
  • Automatic filing: consent given in advance results in the claim being filed automatically in later years when the conditions are met.

Electronic filing is published as available from 06:00 to 24:00. Traffic concentrates just before midnight on the closing day, so aiming at the last day of the window is a poor plan.

The notice itself is widely misunderstood. Receiving a notice does not confirm eligibility, and not receiving one does not block a claim. The notice is closer to an estimate drawn from data the tax office already holds, with the final judgment made in review after filing. A household that believes it meets the conditions can file directly through Hometax with no notice at all.

8. Clawbacks and payment bans

The credit is not settled money once deposited. The tax office may recheck income and asset records after payment and revise the determination accordingly. The legal basis sits in the earned income tax credit provisions of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

  • Article 100-3 Eligibility to apply for the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-5 Calculation of the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-6 Filing applications for the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-7 Determination of the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-8 Refund of the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-9 Restriction on refund of the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-10 Correction of the earned income tax credit
  • Article 100-11 Verification and inspection of applicants
  • Article 100-12 Inquiries into financial transaction information
  • Article 100-28 Eligibility to apply for the child care credit

Clawbacks arise in three broad situations. The first is the semi-annual overpayment already described, which is ordinary settlement working as designed. The second is a correction when income or asset records that were not reflected at filing time surface later. The third is improper claiming.

The consequences of improper claiming are heavy. Guidance describes the amount being reclaimed with penalty tax added, and a payment ban on top of that: two years where the conduct was intentional or grossly negligent, and five years in cases of fraud or other improper conduct. One year of improper gain can cost up to five years of entitlement.

Which leads to a plain conclusion. Reporting income or assets differently from reality in order to fit the tests does not work in any form. The tax office holds verification, inspection and financial transaction inquiry powers by statute, and credit records are matched against income and property data. The only genuinely useful thing to do at filing time is to check whether any income record is missing and to complete the form accurately. This matters particularly for freelance and platform work, where income records are submitted late; filing before those records are reflected simply converts into a correction and a clawback later.

9. How Korea's credit compares with ones you may know

Korea's programme is what the National Tax Service calls a work-linked income support scheme. No work means no payment, and the amount rises alongside earnings before tapering. That direction is the opposite of a conventional welfare benefit, which pays most when income is lowest, and it is deliberate: paying more as earnings rise avoids the band where earning more leaves a household worse off.

Readers in the United States will recognise the shape. The federal Earned Income Tax Credit is likewise a refundable credit claimed through the annual tax return, and it likewise phases in, plateaus and phases out. The United Kingdom has run in-work support under the Working Tax Credit name, with a transition toward Universal Credit under way. The specific thresholds, amounts and current status of those programmes change frequently and are not reproduced here; the IRS and HMRC are the places to check them, and nothing in this article should be read as describing them numerically.

Two structural differences are worth flagging for anyone mapping Korea onto a familiar system. First, Korea applies a hard household asset ceiling measured on gross holdings with no deduction for debt, which is unusual among refundable work credits and is the single most common reason a Korean claim fails. Second, this is administered as tax law rather than social welfare law: the statute is the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, the channel is the tax office, and appeals follow the tax dispute framework. That explains rules that look strange from a welfare standpoint, such as gross rather than net assets, since the tax authority builds tests from values it can verify in its own records.

10. Common reasons claims fail

Rejections cluster in a fairly predictable set of places.

  • Household assets of 240 million won or more - the most common of all, usually filed by households unaware that debt is not deducted.
  • Wrong asset reference date - the measurement is 1 June of the income year, not the May filing date. Selling assets afterwards changes nothing.
  • Misjudged household type - the 3 million won line on spousal gross pay and equivalent income put the household in the wrong category.
  • Licensed professional practice - exclusion applies to the spouse as well as the applicant.
  • Regular employee with monthly average employment income of 5 million won or more - excluded even where annual total income sits below the threshold.
  • Registered as another resident's dependent child - filing in one's own name while still listed as a parent's dependent.
  • Total income marginally over the ceiling - interest and dividend income that does not feed the calculation still pushes the total income figure up.
  • No South Korean nationality - the nationality condition applies unless a separate exception is met.
  • Late filing - not a rejection, but it costs 5 percent of the calculated amount. Purely a matter of meeting the window.
  • First-half semi-annual claim filed, second half missed - settlement never happens, so the remaining balance is never received.

11. What could not be confirmed

Some items are deliberately left without figures here, because no settled value could be taken from published guidance.

  • Thresholds for 2026 income, claimed in 2027 - whether the income thresholds, maxima and asset lines carry over is not settled. Every number in this article is for 2025 income.
  • Exact coefficients in the band-by-band formula - the calculation applied within each band of gross pay and equivalent income sits in a schedule to the enforcement decree, and those values are not reproduced here. For an actual figure, the Hometax simulation is the accurate route.
  • The formula between 500,000 and 1 million won for the child care credit - how the per-child amount is determined across income levels could not be confirmed.
  • The full list of income types counted toward the total income threshold - guidance specifies that the calculation rests on employment, business and religious worker income, but a complete list of what feeds the threshold test could not be confirmed.
  • Who may consent to automatic filing - the conditions governing eligibility for the automatic filing consent could not be confirmed.

These belong in the Hometax tax credit menu and with the tax credit call centre on 1566-3636, checked against your own circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Who is described as eligible?

Residents with employment, business or religious worker income whose combined household income is below the threshold for their household type and whose household assets total under 240 million won. For 2025 income the thresholds are 22 million won for single households, 32 million for single-earner and 44 million for dual-earner. Non-nationals, people registered as another resident's dependent child, licensed professionals and regular employees earning a monthly average of 5 million won or more are excluded. Meeting the conditions is not a determination; the tax office decides.

Q. What is the maximum payment?

For 2025 income, 1.65 million won for single households, 2.85 million for single-earner and 3.3 million for dual-earner. These are ceilings, not fixed payouts. The calculated amount varies with the income band, and the asset band, filing route and any unpaid national tax adjust it further.

Q. Does 200 million won in assets disqualify a household?

No. Assets of 240 million won or more mean no payment, but the 170 million to under 240 million band receives 50 percent. Assets are measured as of 1 June of the income year across housing, land and buildings, passenger cars, jeonse deposits, financial assets and securities, memberships and rights to acquire real estate, with no deduction for debt.

Q. How does semi-annual filing differ from regular filing?

Regular filing covers the previous full year, runs 1 May to 1 June and pays by end of September. Semi-annual filing is open only to employment income earners: the first-half claim runs 1 to 15 September and pays 35 percent on 30 December, and the second-half claim runs 1 to 15 March of the following year and settles on 30 June against full-year figures. Earlier money is traded against the possibility of repaying part of it.

Q. Can a household receive both credits?

They are not mutually exclusive. Tax office review and payment guidance states that the first-half instalment covers only the earned income credit and that the child care credit is paid together at the second-half settlement. The child care credit applies to single-earner and dual-earner households with a dependent child under 18, uses a 70 million won income threshold and pays 500,000 to 1 million won per child.

Conclusion: what to check first

Working in a fixed order saves time. Start with the asset total. Of the three gates it is the one with the least room to adjust after the fact, and it is the axis that produces both outright rejection and the 50 percent cut. Three points cover most of it: debt is not deducted, the reference date is 1 June of the income year, and living in a parent's property can register far more than the deposit actually paid.

Household type comes next, because the 3 million won line on spousal gross pay and equivalent income is what fixes the income ceiling. Even where that ceiling rules out the earned income credit, a household with a child under 18 should separately check the 70 million won threshold on the child care side. The claim form is the same, so checking costs almost nothing.

Timing is last. Employment income earners should decide in advance whether the regular or semi-annual route suits the volatility of their earnings; in a year with a job change or a new side business, reducing exposure to a settlement clawback is worth more than receiving money early. Whichever route is chosen, missing the window costs 5 percent of the calculated amount, and that is a calendar problem rather than an eligibility one.

Every figure here is for 2025 income and can change through annual amendment. Reopen the Hometax guidance for the current year immediately before filing, and where a judgment call is genuinely unclear, the fastest answer comes from putting your own facts to the tax credit call centre on 1566-3636.
