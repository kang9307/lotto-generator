Divorce in Korea: Alimony and Property Division - Deadlines, Scope and Evidence

Two years for property division, three for alimony - what is divisible and what must be proved, as of July 2026

2026-07-29 Life & Law

Introduction: The First Thing to Check Is a Calendar, Not a Number

This article describes the law of the Republic of Korea. It is written for readers dealing with a Korean divorce, for people married to a Korean national, and for anyone whose assets or pension entitlements sit inside Korea. Family law differs enormously between jurisdictions, and the deadlines and categories set out below have no application outside Korea. If you live elsewhere, the rules of your country of residence will govern your case, and cross-border divorces raise separate questions about which court has jurisdiction and which law applies. Those questions are outside the scope of this piece.

The first thing most people search for before a divorce is a number: how much alimony. Yet when you look at how people actually lose money in Korean divorce practice, the painful cases are rarely about misjudging an amount. They are about missing a deadline and losing the right to claim at all. An amount can be argued down or up. A claim that has expired has no stage left on which to argue.

And there is not one deadline. Money questions arising from divorce split into alimony and property division. The two have different natures, rest on different statutory provisions, and carry different periods within which they must be exercised. Property division runs for two years from the date of divorce; alimony for three. The one-year gap is where an entire property division claim can disappear. More importantly, the two periods are of different legal kinds: the three years for alimony is a limitation period, while the two years for property division is an exclusion period, which has no interruption.

This article does not answer the question of how much you will receive. There is no honest answer to that beyond that it depends on the case. Instead it answers three questions with statutes and official sources: what falls within the divisible pool, by when a claim must be made, and what must be documented. Article numbers and the sources consulted are given in the text so that a later reader can check whether they are still current. Child custody, visitation and inheritance are separate axes and are handled in other articles; here they appear only where they touch this subject, with a link.

1. Alimony and Property Division Are Not Two Names for One Thing

People routinely bundle the two together as a divorce settlement. It is convenient and it is risky. Korean government legal information describes alimony as compensation paid by the spouse responsible for the breakdown for the mental suffering caused, and property division as the liquidation of assets built through the couple's cooperation during the marriage, reflecting each side's contribution. It states expressly that the two are separate institutions and may be claimed individually.

The statutory bases differ too. Alimony starts from Article 806 of the Civil Act. Paragraph 1 allows a claim for damages against the party at fault, and paragraph 2 provides that liability extends beyond pecuniary loss to mental suffering. The provision was written for broken engagements, but Article 843 applies it to judicial divorce. Article 843 incorporates Article 806 (damages), Article 837 (responsibility for a child's upbringing), Article 837-2 (visitation), Article 839-2 (the right to claim property division) and Article 839-3 (revocation of a fraudulent act to preserve that right).

Property division rests on Article 839-2. Paragraph 1 lets a person who has divorced by agreement claim division from the other party, and paragraph 2 provides that where no agreement is reached the family court determines the amount and method, taking into account the value of the property built through the cooperation of both parties and other circumstances. Through Article 843 the same structure applies to judicial divorce.

The distinction matters in practice for three reasons. First, the deadlines differ. Second, the tax treatment differs when real estate changes hands. Third, alimony requires proof of the other side's fault, whereas property division is about proving contribution regardless of fault. A spouse who was at fault can still claim property division.

  • Alimony - compensation for mental suffering. Fault on the other side is an element, and where both sides bear comparable responsibility the claim may be dismissed through comparative fault.
  • Property division - liquidation of joint assets and post-divorce support. The question is not who was to blame but who contributed how much.
  • Drafting a settlement - state alimony and property division as separate line items, each with its payment timing, method and consequences of default.
  • Personal nature - a claim for alimony is in principle neither assignable nor inheritable, but that changes once a contract on the compensation has been formed or an action has been filed (Article 806 paragraph 3).

2. The Deadlines Differ - Two Years for Property Division, Three for Alimony

If you take one thing from this article, take this table. The two claims differ not only in the length of the period but in what kind of period it is. The three years for alimony is a limitation period; the two years for property division is an exclusion period.

ItemProperty division claimAlimony claim
Period2 years3 years
Nature of the periodExclusion period (a filing window)Limitation period
Statutory basisCivil Act art. 839-2 para. 3
(applied to judicial divorce via art. 843)		Civil Act art. 766 para. 1
(substantive basis in arts. 806 and 843)
Start - consensual divorceDate the divorce report is filedDate the divorce report is filed
Start - judicial divorce or annulmentDate the judgment becomes finalDate the judgment becomes final
Interruption or suspensionNone. Negotiation or a certified letter does not extend itFollows general limitation doctrine
What must happen inside the periodFile a petition for property division with the family courtExercise the alimony claim

Article 839-2 paragraph 3 provides that the right to claim property division is extinguished once two years have passed from the date of divorce. Korean government legal information explains this as an exclusion period. An exclusion period, unlike a limitation period, has no interruption. Sending a certified letter, or meeting repeatedly to negotiate, does not stop the clock. That makes property division the one item where waiting because talks are ongoing is the most dangerous choice available.

The three-year period for alimony comes from Article 766 paragraph 1, which runs from the day the injured party became aware of the damage and of the wrongdoer. Korean government legal information treats that day as the date of divorce in this context. In a judicial divorce, alimony is usually claimed together with the divorce itself, so there is little risk of running out of time. The exposure is in consensual divorce: where the paperwork is completed without raising money at all, the three-year clock is already running while the couple is still talking.

Take a hypothetical illustration using only dates. Two fictional people, A and B, complete a consensual divorce report on 10 March 2026. The property division claim becomes problematic around 10 March 2028 and the alimony claim around 10 March 2029. If in May 2028 they are still merely discussing the assets, alimony may survive while property division has already become unclaimable. No amount was ever in dispute; the calendar decided the outcome.

  • Once the divorce is registered, write the report date or judgment date in your calendar and mark both the two-year and three-year points.
  • If negotiations drag, consider filing a petition in parallel with the talks. A petition can be withdrawn if agreement is reached.
  • Where the starting date is ambiguous - annulment, dissolution of a de facto marriage - do not decide it yourself; confirm it with a lawyer.
  • The expiry of an exclusion period is a matter a court may examine of its own motion, so it can bite even if the other side never raises it.

3. What Gets Divided - Substance, Not Whose Name Is on the Title

The most common misconception in property division is nothing is in my name, so there is nothing for me. Korean government legal information says the opposite. Property registered in one spouse's name, or in the name of a third party, can still fall inside the divisible pool if it was in substance acquired through the couple's cooperation, and childcare and household work count as cooperation.

Type of assetGeneral ruleException or the point in dispute
Real estate, deposits, shares acquired during the marriage (any name)DivisibleWhere premarital funds are mixed in, the proportion is contested
Property owned before the marriage (separate property)In principle not divisibleIf the other spouse contributed to maintaining or increasing it, the increase may be divisible
Property inherited or gifted during the marriageIn principle not divisibleSame as above. Management, repairs and loan repayment are argued with records
Assets held in a third party's name (a parent, for example)Not excluded merely by the registrationThe source of the purchase funds and who really managed it must be proved
Severance pay already receivedDivisibleHow to apportion the part corresponding to the marriage is contested
Retirement benefits not yet receivedCan be divisiblePer the reasoning of Supreme Court en banc decision 2013Meu2250. Valuation is the issue
Debts (negative assets)Debts incurred to build joint assets or for ordinary household needs are taken into accountWhether the spending was personal, and for what purpose the debt arose
Professional qualifications (doctor, lawyer and so on)Not divisible in themselvesIf obtained with the other spouse's help, they are taken into account in fixing the amount

The row argued over most often is the increase in separate property. Korean government legal information states that property owned before the marriage or received by inheritance or gift during it is in principle outside the pool, but that where the other spouse contributed to the increase, that increase can be brought in. Contribution here is not a feeling but a fact demonstrated with records: whose income serviced the loan, who paid for the repairs or extension, who managed the tenancy.

Debt is the item most often left out. Property division is not an exercise in sharing only positive assets. Debts incurred to build joint marital property, and debts arising from ordinary household life, are taken into account according to the same source. If the other spouse carries a large loan in their own name, the outcome can differ from expectations, so a list of assets should always be accompanied by a list of liabilities as of the same date.

4. Severance Pay, Pensions and the Divided Pension - Assets Not in a Bank Account

Counting only real estate and deposits misses pensions, which for a long marriage are often the single largest asset in play. There are three distinct strands here, each with its own route of claim.

First, retirement benefits. On 16 July 2014 the Supreme Court of Korea handed down two en banc decisions on the same day and changed its previous position. Decision 2012Meu2888 held that a public official retirement pension entitlement already being received at the time of divorce falls within the scope of property division, and that division may take the form of periodic payments of a fixed proportion of each monthly pension. Decision 2013Meu2250, issued the same day, took the position that future retirement benefits not yet received may also be treated as divisible, rejecting the earlier practice of merely noting the prospect of a future pension as one circumstance among others. Korean government legal information likewise explains that retirement benefits latent at the close of argument at the fact-finding stage fall within the pool even while the spouse is still employed.

Second, the divided National Pension. This is a separate mechanism from family court property division and is claimed directly from the National Pension Service. Its requirements and deadlines, per the Service's guidance, are as follows.

ItemDivided National Pension (per NPS guidance)
Marriage requirementThe marriage lasted five years or more within the former spouse's contribution period. Stretches with no substantive marital relationship are excluded from the count
Status requirementThe parties must be divorced
Requirement as to the former spouseThe former spouse must hold an old-age pension entitlement
Requirement as to the claimantThe claimant must have reached the payment commencement age for their birth year (61 to 65)
Claim deadlineWithin five years of the entitlement arising
Advance claimCan be filed within three years of the date the divorce takes effect, before all requirements are met (payment follows once they are)
ShareIn principle one half of the former spouse's old-age pension. For entitlements arising on or after 30 December 2016, a different share may be set by agreement or by court decision

The practical point is that yet another set of clocks starts. On top of two years for property division and three for alimony there run a three-year advance claim window and a five-year claim window for the divided pension. If the former spouse is still young and holds no old-age pension entitlement, nothing is payable now, but the advance claim mechanism preserves the position for later. The key constraint is that the advance claim must be made within three years of the divorce taking effect.

Third, private and occupational pension accounts. Personal pension or retirement pension reserves resemble financial assets and are handled within the ordinary property division framework, but tax rules and withdrawal conditions are entangled with them, so the timing of conversion to cash and the tax consequences have to be considered together. The Korean National Pension scheme as a whole is covered separately in the complete guide to the National Pension.

  • Establish the other spouse's employment start date and length of service. The overlap with the marriage is the starting point of the discussion.
  • Check your own National Pension record first. Contribution histories can be viewed at a Service branch or online.
  • The divided pension is a separate procedure from the family court judgment; finishing property division does not process it automatically.
  • Other public pension schemes such as the government employees' and private school teachers' schemes have their own division provisions under their own statutes, so check with the relevant institution.

5. Alimony - Fault, Third Parties, and an Honest Answer About Amounts

Alimony is paid by the spouse responsible for the divorce. Korean government legal information states that where both spouses bear comparable responsibility the claim is dismissed. The substance of an alimony dispute is therefore proving whose responsibility weighs more.

The grounds for judicial divorce are set out in Article 840 as six items: an act of unchastity by the spouse; malicious desertion by the spouse; extremely unfair treatment by the spouse or their lineal ascendant; extremely unfair treatment of one's own lineal ascendant by the spouse; the spouse's whereabouts being unknown for three years or more; and any other serious ground making it difficult to continue the marriage. Courts read the sixth item strictly, asking whether the marital community has broken down beyond recovery.

Whether a spouse at fault may themselves petition for divorce is a frequent question. The Supreme Court's en banc decision of 15 September 2015, 2013Meu568, maintained that under the fault-based framework of the current law a petition by the spouse at fault is in principle not allowed, while recognising exceptions: where the other spouse also has no intention of continuing the marriage; where protection and consideration for the other spouse and the children have offset the fault; and where the passage of time has attenuated both the fault and the suffering. This concerns whether the petition is admissible, not the size of any award.

Alimony is not claimed only against the spouse. Korean government legal information explains that it may also be claimed against a third party responsible for the breakdown, such as parents-in-law who improperly interfered in the marriage or a person involved in an act of unchastity. The same source notes that where the marital community had already broken down beyond recovery before the conduct in question, the third party's liability is not recognised.

Now the question everyone asks: how much. This article gives no figure or range. Korean government legal information explains that courts weigh how and to what degree the divorce came about, the cause of the breakdown and the degree of responsibility, the parties' financial position and standard of living, and their age and occupation, and it states that the specific amount differs from case to case. Averages circulating online are frequently untraceable to any source and distort expectations by ignoring the conditions of each case. No amount standard published by a court or an authoritative institution was located in researching this article, so no figure is stated here. Only a lawyer who has seen the full facts can indicate a plausible range for your case.

  • Factors the court weighs - how and to what degree the divorce came about, the cause of the breakdown and degree of responsibility, financial position and standard of living, age and occupation.
  • Where both sides bear comparable responsibility, the claim may be dismissed through comparative fault.
  • A claim against a third party requires separate proof of the link between that party's conduct and the breakdown.
  • The alimony claim is in principle personal, but assignment and succession can become possible once a contract on the compensation is formed or an action is filed.

6. Procedure - Where Consensual and Judicial Divorce Part Ways

There are two routes. Which one is taken changes both the start of the deadlines and how the money questions get handled.

StageConsensual divorceJudicial divorce
PreconditionAgreement on the intention to divorceOne of the six grounds in Civil Act art. 840
StartApply to the family court for confirmation of intentionFile suit with the family court (mediation comes first)
Guidance and reflection periodFrom the day guidance is received: three months where there are children to raise, one month otherwiseNot applicable
Exception to the reflection periodMay be shortened or waived where there is urgency, such as domestic violenceNot applicable
Where there are childrenSubmit an agreement or a court decision on upbringing and parental authorityThe court decides
Child supportThe court prepares a child support payment record with the force of an enforceable titleRecorded in the judgment or settlement protocol
Alimony and property divisionA matter for agreement. Without one, a separate claim is requiredUsually joined to the divorce petition
Start of the deadlinesDate the divorce report is filedDate the judgment becomes final

The reflection period rests on Article 836-2. A couple seeking a consensual divorce must receive the guidance the family court provides, and confirmation of the intention to divorce can be obtained only after three months where there are children to be raised, or one month in other cases, has passed from the day that guidance was received. The court may shorten or waive the period where there is urgency, such as domestic violence expected to cause unbearable suffering to one party.

There is a trap here. A consensual divorce confirms only the divorce itself. Alimony and property division are not items the court settles in that confirmation procedure. So filing the report first with a view to sorting the money out later means that from that moment the two-year and three-year clocks both start running. A judicial divorce, where alimony and property division are typically claimed alongside the petition, carries comparatively less exposure to a missed deadline.

7. When Assets Start Moving - Provisional Orders, Attachment and Revocation

Once divorce is on the table, assets sometimes move. Real estate is suddenly transferred to a relative, deposits are withdrawn, a mortgage is registered. Three devices exist for this situation.

A provisional disposition by the family court rests on the Family Litigation Act. Where a suit, petition or application for mediation in a family case has been made, the family court may, on its own initiative or on application, make a disposition if it considers one particularly necessary to resolve the case. The catch is that it can only be sought once the main proceedings have begun. Where assets must be frozen urgently before suit, provisional attachment or provisional injunction under the Civil Execution Act is the route to consider.

Where property has already been transferred away, Article 839-3 applies. Where one spouse performs a juristic act concerning property rights knowing that it will prejudice the other spouse's right to claim property division, the other spouse may apply to the family court for its revocation and for restitution. The provision applies Article 406 paragraph 1, and the filing period follows Article 406 paragraph 2, which requires the action to be brought within one year of learning of the cause for revocation and within five years of the juristic act.

  • For real estate, check the certified copy of the register periodically. A transfer of ownership or a new mortgage calls for immediate advice.
  • For financial assets, obtain balance certificates as at the point discussions began. They become the basis for asking later where a reduction went.
  • Remember the order: a provisional disposition requires main proceedings to exist first.
  • Revocation carries a short one year from discovery window, so act as soon as a transfer comes to light.

8. Evidence - Contribution Is Records, Not Assertion

The sentence heard most often in a property division consultation is I did everything. Regrettably, that sentence is not evidence. What a court looks at is material showing how the assets were built and who did what. And by the time divorce is being discussed, access to much of that material has often already closed. The list below is worth assembling in advance.

  • Real estate - certified copies of the register, purchase contracts, account statements showing the flow of funds at acquisition, loan agreements and repayment histories.
  • Financial assets - balance certificates and transaction histories for deposit, savings and securities accounts, and surrender value quotations for insurance policies.
  • Income - withholding tax receipts for employment income, business income filings, records of salary transfers.
  • Retirement benefits - certificates of employment, documents evidencing the start date, the type of retirement pension scheme and the accumulated reserve.
  • Pensions - National Pension contribution records and histories in other public schemes such as the government employees' scheme.
  • Debts - loan balance certificates, card statements, material showing what the borrowing was used for.
  • Traces of contribution - receipts for interior work and repairs, tenancy management records, documents relating to any business you helped run.
  • Household work and childcare - nursery and school paperwork, records of hospital visits and similar material showing who actually carried the caring load.
  • Grounds of fault (for alimony) - records that establish the facts. Note that an unlawful method of collection backfires, so always agree the scope with a lawyer first.

That last item deserves particular care. Accessing a spouse's phone or accounts without authorisation, or recording or filming without consent, can create separate legal problems. This article does not describe such collection methods, and the scope and manner of gathering material should be settled under a lawyer's direction. Legitimate routes exist. Once litigation is under way, material can be obtained through the court by means such as orders to produce financial transaction information, requests for factual inquiries and orders to produce documents.

How you organise the material also affects the outcome. Put assets and liabilities into a single table measured as at the same date, and note beside each entry the name of the supporting document. Consultations and case preparation move far faster that way. Setting out each item as a pair of columns, one for the assertion and one for the supporting record, is a habit worth adopting.

9. Tax - Property Division and Alimony Are Treated Differently

Transferring the same apartment produces different tax outcomes depending on whether it is property division or alimony. That is the second reason not to bundle the two. The table below summarises the taxation explanation given by Korean government legal information.

ItemProperty divisionAlimony
Recipient - gift taxNot charged (treated as liquidation of joint property)Not charged (treated as damages)
Recipient - income taxNot chargedNot charged
Recipient - acquisition tax and relatedAcquisition tax, local education tax and special rural development tax apply on receiving real estateAcquisition tax, local education tax and special rural development tax apply on receiving real estate
Transferor - capital gains taxNot charged, as the transfer is not treated as a transfer for considerationCan be charged. Paying in real estate is treated as satisfaction in kind and so as a transfer
Exception such as a sham divorceWhere the arrangement amounts in substance to a gift, such as a sham divorce for the purpose of tax evasion, gift tax may be imposed, according to the same source

The item most often misunderstood is the acquisition date. National Tax Service interpretive guidance treats the acquisition date of an asset received through the exercise of a property division claim as the day the transferring spouse acquired it, while for real estate received in satisfaction of a debt or of alimony the acquisition date is the day the transfer registration was received. That changes the holding-period calculation when the property is later sold, so the recipient also benefits from keeping the characterisation on the record.

Tax outcomes nonetheless turn heavily on the composition of an individual's assets, residence requirements and whether other homes are owned. What this article establishes is the broad structure only; the actual tax and whether any exemption applies require confirmation from the National Tax Service or a tax professional. Do not settle a detailed calculation on the strength of this text alone.

  • State the heading and the amount separately in the settlement. It is the starting point of any tax analysis.
  • If you are transferring real estate, model the capital gains tax that arises if it is characterised as alimony in kind.
  • The receiving side should agree at the negotiation stage who bears the transfer costs such as acquisition tax.
  • Tax law is amended annually, so re-check as at the date of the actual transfer.

10. What This Article Does Not Cover - Child Support, Custody and Inheritance

Child support is a separate axis from alimony and property division. In practice the Seoul Family Court's child support calculation table is widely used as a reference. The current edition is the one the Seoul Family Court announced on 22 December 2021 and brought into effect on 1 March 2022 (the table was first established in 2012 and revised in 2014, 2017 and 2021). The structure works by locating a standard support figure from the parents' combined pre-tax income and the child's age band, then adjusting the total by increase and decrease factors such as the parents' asset position, the region of residence, the number of children and unusually high medical or education costs, and finally apportioning that total between the parents. Note also that the standard figure is an average per child based on a four-person household with two children.

The figures from the table are not reproduced here for two reasons. The table can be revised, and numbers lifted out of it are easily misread once the adjustment factors are stripped away. Korean government legal information also states that the table is a reference and not legally binding. Check the current table and its commentary directly at the Seoul Family Court notice. Enforcement where payments are not made is covered in the guide to enforcing unpaid child support.

Who receives custody and parental authority, and how visitation is designed, are judged on different criteria from money questions. Those turn on the welfare of the child and are handled separately. See the guide to custody in divorce and the guide to enforcing visitation rights. The overall flow of the divorce procedure is in the complete guide to divorce procedure, and a practical feel for how property division and child support are calculated is in the guide to property division and child support.

Inheritance is also outside this scope. A spouse's right of inheritance is extinguished by divorce, so the order of the divorce date and the date the succession opens determines the result. The minimum share disputes among heirs are covered in the guide to the statutory reserved portion, and the ranking of heirs and statutory shares in the guide to the order of inheritance.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

We divorced by agreement and never discussed assets. Can I still claim?

If fewer than two years have passed since the divorce there may be room for property division, and if fewer than three years, for alimony. What matters is that the two years for property division is an exclusion period, so a petition must be filed with the family court inside it. Merely exchanging messages with the other side does not extend the period.

I already signed a settlement and then found there were more assets.

The answer depends on how far the settlement reached and whether assets were concealed, so it cannot be stated in general terms. What is certain is that the clock keeps running, so it is safer to consult a lawyer as soon as you find out and establish what time and options remain.

I think the other side is hiding assets.

Once litigation is under way, mechanisms such as orders to produce financial transaction information and requests for factual inquiries are available through the court. Accessing the other party's accounts directly, by contrast, can create separate legal problems, so discuss the method first.

I was a full-time homemaker. Will my contribution be recognised?

Korean government legal information states that childcare and household work are recognised as cooperation in building the assets. The specific proportion nonetheless varies with the length of the marriage, how the assets were built and each party's role, so it requires a case-by-case assessment.

Ours was a de facto marriage. Do the same rules apply?

Property division principles can apply to the dissolution of a de facto marriage as a matter of practice, but such cases frequently begin with a dispute over proving when the relationship formed and when it ended. Because fixing the start of the period is especially difficult here, confirm the position with a lawyer.

Conclusion: Three Things You Can Do Today

The point of this article is simple. The money side of a divorce rests on three axes - deadline, scope and evidence - and only one of them is irreversible. Scope and evidence can be shored up late; a period that has run cannot be revived by any means.

So there are three things to do today. First, fix the dates. Confirm the divorce report date or the date the judgment became final, and mark the two-year and three-year points in your calendar. Adding the three-year advance claim window for the divided National Pension is better still. Second, build the list. Put assets and liabilities into one table measured as at the same date and attach the name of a supporting document to each line. Third, write them separately. Whatever you agree, record alimony and property division as distinct items and specify the timing and method of payment.

Then, with those three done, see a professional. Arriving with a prepared list and confirmed dates buys far more from the same consultation hour. If cost is a barrier, the Korea Legal Aid Corporation's free consultation is a reasonable first step.
