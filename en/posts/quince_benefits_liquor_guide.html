Introduction: an ugly fruit with a long history of use

Quince (mogwa in Korean) is nicknamed the "ugliest fruit" for its lumpy skin, yet it is an autumn fruit that has long been used as a medicinal ingredient. Online, however, it is often called a cure-all, and much of that has no human evidence behind it.

This article separates what is confirmed from what is not, and explains how to make quince syrup, tea and liquor. For the liquor it also covers the risks of alcohol and the limits of Korean liquor law, and it does not conclude that a medicinal liquor is good for you.

What is quince?

It is the fruit of the quince tree (Chaenomeles sinensis Koehne, also written Pseudocydonia sinensis in recent taxonomy), a member of the rose family.

  • Season: an autumn fruit, generally harvested from September to November
  • Taste: very sour and astringent, hard to eat raw, so it is usually made into tea, syrup or liquor
  • Crude drug specification: listed as "Mokgwa" (木瓜) in the Korean standards for herbal medicines outside the Korean Pharmacopoeia

A listed specification means there are standards for its origin and appearance as a crude drug. It does not mean its effect on any disease has been proven in clinical trials.

Quince in traditional use

The Korean Traditional Knowledge Portal entry describes quince as warm in nature and sour, said to remove dampness, relax muscles and open the meridians. It records traditional use for beriberi (leg swelling), pain in the lower back and knees, and muscle cramps from vomiting and diarrhea, and warns that excess damages teeth and bones and that it is unsuitable for people with a weak digestive system or constipation from food stagnation.

Benefits: what is confirmed and what is not

You often see claims such as "works wonders for coughs" or "relieves joint pain", but a 2024 review (Kostecka-Gugała, Antioxidants) concludes that research on quince bioactivity is mostly in test tubes and animals with almost no human evidence. The table sorts common claims by level of evidence.

  • If joint or muscle pain continues, getting the cause diagnosed comes first
  • If you take medication, for example for diabetes, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before drinking quince syrup or tea regularly

Before quince liquor: the risks of alcohol

Even if the ingredient is fruit, what you drink is alcohol. WHO Europe states that there is no safe amount of alcohol that does not affect health and that the risk starts from the first drop of any alcoholic beverage; it also says alcohol is a Group 1 carcinogen (IARC) and causes at least seven types of cancer, including bowel cancer and female breast cancer.

The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) health portal links alcohol to head and neck, esophageal, liver, breast, colorectal and pancreatic cancer, and says alcohol is the second leading cause of liver cirrhosis and the third of liver cancer in Korea. It cites the Korean Association for the Study of the Liver as advising that liver damage risk rises above 40g a day for men and 20g for women; one standard drink (14g of pure alcohol) is about 90mL of soju.

By calculation, 100mL of 25% quince liquor holds about 20g of pure alcohol (25% x 100mL x 0.79g/mL), which already reaches the women's threshold above. This article therefore does not recommend "a little every day as medicine". Quince liquor is a drink, not a drug.

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding: there is no safe amount, so do not drink it
  • Minors: do not give or offer it; alcohol is a youth-harmful substance under Korea's Youth Protection Act
  • Before driving or operating machinery: Korea's Road Traffic Act treats a blood alcohol level of 0.03% or more as drunk driving; do not drive on a day you have drunk
  • Liver disease, medication, or a history of alcohol problems: do not drink and talk to a doctor

Even if none of these apply, set your amount in advance, and consult a professional if you are worried about your health.

Home-infused liquor and Korean liquor law: can I make it, can I sell it?

Article 6 of Korea's Punishment of Tax Evasion Act punishes making or selling liquor without a liquor licence with up to 3 years in prison or a fine, while excluding "manufacture for personal consumption". This is the gist of the provision as confirmed through search; the article text itself could not be opened this time, so check the original on the Korean Law Information Center.

  • Making it to drink yourself needs no licence
  • Selling it, or giving it in exchange for payment, is not allowed, including flea-market and social media sales
  • The exact scope of personal consumption could not be confirmed (amount, sharing with acquaintances, serving at gatherings). If in doubt, ask your local tax office
  • The method here is infusing fruit in store-bought soju. Brewing liquor by fermenting grain or fruit juice is different and must be checked separately

This section is not legal advice. If you cannot decide, consult the tax office or a professional.

Quince syrup and tea: enjoying it without alcohol

Making quince syrup

You need 3 to 4 quinces and sugar or honey at a 1:1 ratio with the fruit.

  1. Wash the quinces and wipe them completely dry
  2. Slice thinly and remove every seed
  3. In a sterilized glass jar, layer quince and sugar (or honey) alternately and cover the top with sugar, then seal
  4. Refrigerate and start using it after 2 to 3 weeks
  5. Once the sugar dissolves, remove the fruit and keep only the syrup

About half of the syrup is sugar, so keep the added sugar in mind, and never give honey to infants under 1 year.

Brewing quince tea

  1. Wash the quinces, slice them thinly and remove the seeds
  2. Put 10 to 15 slices into 1L of water
  3. Simmer on low heat for 30 to 60 minutes
  4. Add honey or sugar to taste. You can also stir quince syrup into warm water

No official recommended intake could be found. It is very sour, so treat it as a casual drink and start with a small amount.

Making quince liquor: for personal consumption

The method below assumes you make only the amount you will drink yourself. Selling or giving it away follows the limits in the previous section.

Ingredients

  • Quince: 5 to 6, large and fresh
  • Soju: 2L, 25 to 35% alcohol
  • Sugar or honey: 0 to 700g, optional (ratios in the table below)
  • Glass jar: 3 to 4L, sterilized with boiling water or similar

Steps

  1. Wash the quinces and dry them completely (overnight in the shade)
  2. Cut into quarters or eighths and remove every seed. Seeds left in the alcohol can release their compounds
  3. Put the quince in the jar and pour in soju until fully submerged
  4. Seal and keep in a cool place out of direct sun. Aging for 3 to 6 months is a common custom; no evidence was found that this period determines safety or effect
  5. Write the date and alcohol strength on the jar and keep it out of children's reach

Precautions when consuming

Always remove the seeds

⚠️ Seed toxicity: Seeds of quince-type fruit are reported to contain the cyanogenic glycoside amygdalin, with amounts varying by species (Kostecka-Gugała, 2024). The same review describes a study that detected up to 118 μg/mL in an ethanol extract of the seeds, meaning seeds in liquor can release it. The European Food Safety Authority assessed that amygdalin in apricot kernels turns into cyanide during digestion (an assessment of apricot kernels, not quince seeds).

  • Whether syrup, tea or liquor, use only seedless flesh
  • Check that no seed broke up and mixed in while cutting
  • If you feel unwell, stop at once and see a doctor

Teeth and stomach

  • Organic acids can irritate tooth enamel, so use a straw and rinse your mouth with water afterward
  • Do not brush immediately after drinking; wait a while
  • It can irritate people with excess stomach acid or acid reflux, and the traditional description also says it does not suit people with a weak stomach or constipation

When to consult a professional

  • If pregnant or breastfeeding, do not drink the liquor, and ask a doctor first about the syrup or tea as well
  • If you have diabetes or liver disease or take medication, talk to a doctor or pharmacist
  • Stop if you develop an allergic reaction such as a rash or itching

Choosing and storing

  • Pick large, heavy, firm fruit without blemishes
  • Strong fragrance and a bright yellow or greenish-yellow color are good signs
  • Fresh quince kept at room temperature gives off its fragrance and ripens slowly
  • Refrigerate the syrup and discard it if you see mold, foam or a sour fermented smell. No confirmed storage-time standard was found

What could not be confirmed

  • The vitamin C figure for quince: the original national food composition table could not be checked, and only media-quoted values exist
  • The amounts of saponins and minerals: the sources opened this time gave no figures
  • The exact scope of personal consumption and the original text of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act: only the gist was confirmed through search
  • A recommended intake for quince tea and syrup, and storage times for syrup and liquor: no official standard was found
  • Human evidence for cough, digestion, fatigue and liver function: none was found in the sources this time

Conclusion: fragrant, but not a cure-all

Quince is a sour fruit with a long history of use, but human evidence for its benefits is still thin. Enjoy it with modest expectations and follow the safety rules.

  • Take the benefits only at the level of "traditionally used"
  • If you do not need alcohol, syrup and tea are enough
  • Quince liquor is alcohol: do not drink it if pregnant, breastfeeding, underage or before driving, and do not sell it
  • Remove the seeds in every form

If you have symptoms or take medication, consult a professional before deciding.

# Quince and Quince Liquor Checklist: Before You Make It and Before You Drink It

As of: 2026-09-29

## 1. When you read a benefit claim

| Claim | Evidence found | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Cough, phlegm, bronchi | No human clinical evidence found | Do not accept it as effective |
| Muscle cramps, lower back and knee pain | In the traditional description, no clinical evidence | Only "traditionally used" |
| Digestion, stomach | Organic acids can irritate excess stomach acid | Do not treat it as good for digestion |
| Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, blood sugar, immunity | Test-tube and animal stage | Not a proven effect in humans |

## 2. Do not drink quince liquor if

- [ ] You are pregnant or breastfeeding
- [ ] You are a minor
- [ ] You will drive or operate machinery today (0.03% blood alcohol or more counts as drunk driving in Korea)
- [ ] You have liver disease or take medication
- [ ] You have a history of alcohol problems

If any applies, do not drink it and talk to a doctor.

## 3. Checks before you make it

- [ ] Are you making only the amount you will drink? (Selling or giving it for payment is not allowed without a licence in Korea)
- [ ] Did you remove every seed? (Seeds in alcohol can release their compounds)
- [ ] Did you sterilize the jar?
- [ ] Did you write the date and alcohol strength on it and keep it away from children?
- [ ] Did you decide your amount in advance? (100mL at 25% holds about 20g of pure alcohol)

## 4. To enjoy it without alcohol

| Method | Key points |
|---|---|
| Quince syrup | Quince to sugar 1:1, seeds removed, refrigerate 2 to 3 weeks. Half of it is sugar, and never give honey to infants under 1 year |
| Quince tea | 10 to 15 slices in 1L of water, simmer 30 to 60 minutes |

## 5. When to see a doctor first

- A cough lasts a long time, brings up blood, or leaves you short of breath
- Joint or muscle pain continues
- You have diabetes or liver disease or take medication
- You get a rash, itching or another allergic reaction
