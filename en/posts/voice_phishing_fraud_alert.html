Major Voice Phishing and Emerging Fraud Types

1. AI Deepfake Voice Scams

Method: Scammers use AI to clone the voice of a family member or acquaintance and stage a fake emergency.

  • Voice data is harvested from social media posts or recorded phone calls to generate deepfake audio
  • Fabricated emergencies such as "I've been in an accident" or "I'm in the ER" are used to create panic
  • The cloned voice can be nearly indistinguishable from the real person

How to respond: When you receive an emergency call, always verify directly with the person. Establish a pre-agreed code word among family members.

2. Messenger Phishing (Advanced Smishing)

Method: Sophisticated scams conducted through messaging apps like KakaoTalk and Telegram.

  • Phishing links disguised as delivery notifications, government alerts, etc.
  • The trust factor inherent in messaging platforms makes people more susceptible
  • Stolen personal data is then used for follow-up scams

How to respond: Never click links from unknown sources. Always verify through official channels.

3. Cryptocurrency Investment Scams

Method: Victims are lured with promises of guaranteed high returns on crypto investments.

  • Recruited into "investment groups" via social media or messaging apps
  • Enticed with claims of "inside information" and "guaranteed profits"
  • Small initial returns are paid out to build trust before extracting large sums

How to respond: Any investment guaranteeing high returns is 100% a scam. Only use officially registered and licensed platforms.

4. Government Agency Impersonation Scams

Method: Scammers pose as police, prosecutors, or the National Tax Service, demanding money under the pretext of a criminal investigation.

  • Caller ID is spoofed to display the actual agency's phone number
  • Intimidation tactics like "Your account has been linked to criminal activity"
  • Victims are pressured to transfer money or disclose personal information as part of "cooperating with the investigation"

How to respond: Government agencies never demand money over the phone. Always verify by visiting the agency in person.

Essential Fraud Prevention Rules

Phone Call Precautions

  • Do not answer calls from unknown numbers
  • Hang up immediately if personal information is requested
  • Verify directly when someone claims an emergency
  • If someone claims to be from a government agency, visit in person to confirm

Financial Precautions

  • Never share account numbers or passwords
  • Consult family or friends before making any transfers
  • Be skeptical of any high-return investment offers
  • Only use officially licensed financial institutions

Digital Security Rules

  • Never click links from unknown sources
  • Only download apps from official app stores
  • Verify the URL before entering personal information
  • Immediately delete suspicious texts or emails

Protecting Your Social Circle

  • Establish emergency code words with family members
  • Minimize personal information shared on social media
  • Consult others when something feels suspicious
  • Participate in regular security awareness training

Latest Fraud Trends and Countermeasures

Looking at the new fraud trends emerging in 2025, scam techniques are evolving in lockstep with technological advances:

Increasing Abuse of AI Technology

  • Deepfake technology: Manipulating real people's voices and videos to boost credibility
  • Chatbot exploitation: Using AI chatbots to conduct prolonged conversational scams
  • Automated systems: Deploying automated fraud targeting large numbers of victims simultaneously

Exploitation of Messaging Platforms

  • Group chats: Investment groups that are actually organized scam operations
  • Direct messages: Personalized messages that increase perceived trustworthiness
  • File attachments: Malicious apps or documents used to steal information

Exploiting Changes in Payment Systems

  • Simplified payments: Scams exploiting the convenience of one-click payments
  • QR codes: Malicious QR codes that redirect to phishing websites
  • Cryptocurrency: Using hard-to-trace crypto for money laundering and fund transfers

💡 Remember!

No matter the situation, never do these 3 things:

  1. Provide personal or financial information over the phone
  2. Click unknown links or install unverified apps
  3. Transfer money without proper verification

How to Strengthen Your Personal Security

Communication Security

  • Use your carrier's spam blocking service
  • Enable caller ID display services
  • Record calls as potential evidence
  • Block suspicious numbers immediately

Digital Security

  • Change passwords regularly
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)
  • Install and keep security apps updated
  • Exercise caution on public Wi-Fi

Financial Security

  • Set up account transaction alerts
  • Establish daily transfer limits
  • Regularly review transaction history
  • Only use official financial institution apps

Family Security

  • Conduct security awareness sessions with family
  • Share emergency response procedures
  • Perform regular security checkups
  • Discuss and learn from real fraud cases

Conclusion

Voice phishing (boiseu pising) and emerging fraud schemes continue to evolve, and our vigilance must keep pace. The single most important thing is to cultivate the habit of questioning everything.

"Wait, something seems off."

That one moment of doubt could protect your hard-earned savings and personal information.

As technology advances, so do fraud techniques -- but sticking to basic security practices and maintaining a healthy sense of skepticism can go a long way toward prevention. If you believe you have been targeted by a scam, do not try to handle it alone. Report it to the appropriate authorities immediately to minimize the damage.
