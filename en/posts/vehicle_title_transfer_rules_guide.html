Vehicle Title Transfer in South Korea - Why 15 Days Matters More Than You Think

While the transfer sits unfiled, the road tax, the fines and the insurance duty all stay with the seller. Deadlines, documents, acquisition tax, liens and insurance timing

Sep 1, 2026 Life & Practical

This covers transfer of vehicle ownership under the law of the Republic of Korea. In a used car deal the money changes hands quietly; the trouble starts afterwards. The car is gone, but the name on the vehicle register stays the same for days, sometimes months. Through all of it the road tax bill, the parking fine notice and the compulsory insurance duty stay with the seller.

Transfer registration is not a closing formality. It is an application with a statutory deadline. Article 12(1) of the Motor Vehicle Management Act places the duty on the acquirer, and Article 26(1) of the Motor Vehicle Registration Decree splits the period by cause. What follows is that deadline, the arithmetic when it is missed, and where real deals come apart. Position as of September 2026, South Korea only.

General information about the South Korean vehicle registration system, not legal or tax advice on a particular transaction. Starting dates, documents and tax amounts vary by vehicle class, deal structure and locality, so confirm your own case with the competent vehicle registration office and your local tax division, and with a lawyer or certified tax accountant where needed.

The deadline comes first - and it differs by cause

Most people treat this as a paperwork problem. It is a calendar problem. Papers can be reissued; a date that has passed cannot, and the penalty attaches to the number of days late, not a missing form. Article 26(1) of the Decree sets the period separately for each cause, so a single memorised number gets gifts and inheritances wrong.

CausePeriodRuns fromBasis
SaleWithin 15 daysThe date of purchaseMotor Vehicle Registration Decree Art. 26(1)1
GiftWithin 20 daysThe date the gift was receivedSame paragraph, subpara. 2
InheritanceWithin 6 monthsThe last day of the month in which succession openedSame paragraph, subpara. 3
Any other causeWithin 15 daysThe date the cause aroseSame paragraph, subpara. 4

The inheritance start date is misread most often. Not six months from the date of death but six months from the last day of the month in which the death fell. Succession opening in mid May starts the clock on 31 May. Some local notices compress this to from the date succession opened, so where timing is tight, confirm with the registration office.

  • The period runs to the day you file, not the day it completes. Hence having documents ready in advance
  • The 15 days run from the purchase date, not the day the balance is paid. Where contract and handover dates are far apart, settle which governs first
  • Acquisition at a court auction or public sale falls under subparagraph 4, the residual category

Late filing accrues by the day

The late charge is simple. It starts flat, grows daily, stops at a ceiling. The basis is Table 2 of the Enforcement Decree, made under Article 84, and local registration offices publish the same figures.

Days lateChargeHow it is worked out
Within 10 days of the deadline100,000 KRWFlat amount
More than 10 days100,000 KRW plus 10,000 KRW per additional dayRises as the days accumulate
Ceiling500,000 KRWIt stops here however long the delay runs

Because the ceiling is 500,000 KRW, some read it as meaning further delay costs nothing more. Wrong: a criminal provision stands next to the administrative one. Article 80(1) makes a person who violates Article 12(1) by failing, without justifiable grounds, to apply, and Article 80(2) a dealer who violates Article 12(2), liable to up to 2 years or a fine of up to 20 million KRW.

  • The late charge is an administrative penalty; Article 80 is criminal. Different in kind, so one ceiling does not absorb the other
  • While the transfer sits unfiled, the acquisition tax deadline runs too. The two are counted separately
  • Even where the delay is the other side's doing, the charge falls on whoever owed the duty. Circumstances are for the objection process, not an automatic exemption

Why the seller is in more of a hurry

Keep one section and keep this one. The duty to meet the deadline sits with the buyer, but the person who loses when it is missed is usually the seller.

The reason is one line of Article 6. Acquisition or loss of ownership of a vehicle takes effect only upon registration. Sign the contract, take the balance, hand over the keys, and the legal owner has not changed until the register does. Almost every public duty on a car holds that name.

  • Road tax — a local tax on ownership. While the register shows the old owner, the bill goes out to them
  • Fine notices — fines that do not identify a driver, such as illegal parking, go to the vehicle owner
  • The compulsory insurance duty — Article 5 of the Guarantee of Automobile Accident Compensation Act requires a vehicle holder to carry it. A seller who cancels while the transfer is unfiled becomes an uninsured registered owner
  • Accidents and disputes — the first name looked up after a collision is the owner in the register

Skipping the intermediate name carries one of the heavier penalties here. Article 12(3) requires a person who acquired a vehicle and wishes to pass it on to register it into their own name first, and Article 79(1)-2 makes onward selling without doing so punishable by up to 3 years or a fine of up to 30 million KRW — heavier than simple failure to file (2 years, 20 million KRW). Which is why, for a seller, the deal ends on the day the transfer is confirmed, not the day the balance arrives.

If the buyer will not file, the seller can

There is something to do besides wait. Article 12(4) provides that where the acquirer has not applied, the transferor may apply in their place. Transferor means the owner shown in the register at the time. Article 12(5) then requires the Mayor or Provincial Governor to accept it.

The procedure is Article 27 of the Decree. Once the transferor files with the documents prescribed by Ordinance of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, the office fixes not less than 7 and not more than 15 days and demands that the transferee apply. If no objection is raised, the office updates the register.

  • Running without the buyer's cooperation makes it effectively the only self-help remedy a seller has
  • It is not automatic. Documents proving the transaction are needed, so keep a copy of the transfer certificate
  • It takes time, since the demand period must run. Tax and fines arising meanwhile are argued separately

Documents - where private deals and dealers diverge

The papers fall into three groups: those identifying the car, those proving the seller's intent, and those establishing the buyer's standing. Always required: the transfer registration application (Form 14 annexed to the Rules), the vehicle registration certificate, the vehicle transfer certificate, proof of insurance naming the acquirer as the insured, identification, and proof of the place of use such as a resident registration abstract.

  • Private sale — add the transferor's seal certificate, or a certificate of personal signature, paper or electronic. The transfer certificate is Form 15
  • Dealer sale — the proviso to Article 33(1)2 of the Rules waives the transferor's seal certificate where a registered dealer or an auction house operator sold or brokered the car. The transfer certificate is Form 16
  • Corporate party — add the corporate seal certificate, the registry extract and a copy of the business registration certificate
  • Inheritance — the deceased's basic and family relation certificates, plus an agreement on division of the estate where there are several heirs
  • Filing by proxy — a power of attorney and a copy of the principal's identification, even between family members

Dealer transactions bring in two more provisions. Article 12(2) requires a registered dealer who sold or brokered a vehicle to apply in place of the buyer — not the buyer's option but the dealer's statutory duty. The second is the disclosure duty in Article 58(1): four things must be given in writing before the contract is concluded.

  • Whether the vehicle has been flood damaged, and the performance and condition inspection results for its structure and devices — with inspection photographs, dated within 120 days
  • Whether an attachment or a mortgage is registered
  • The fees or charges received under Article 65(1)
  • Where the buyer asks for it, the results of the survey and appraisal of the vehicle's value

The fee categories are fixed too. Article 65(1) and Article 122(1) of the Enforcement Rules list the brokerage fee, the registration filing fee, the management cost of storing vehicles held for sale, and the valuation fee, each limited to actual cost. The management cost may not exceed the local public car park charge. Buying-stage checks are in the complete used car buying guide.

Acquisition tax - the vehicle class sets the rate

Tax follows the transfer. The standard rates are in Article 12(1)2 of the Local Tax Act, divided by passenger or not and business use or not.

CategoryStandard rateProvision
Non-business passenger car70/1,000 (7%)Local Tax Act Art. 12(1)2(a)
Light car within that category40/1,000 (4%)Proviso to item (a)
Motorcycles prescribed by Presidential Decree20/1,000 (2%)Item (b)
Other vehicles - non-business50/1,000 (5%), light cars 40/1,000Item (c)1)
Other vehicles - business use40/1,000 (4%)Item (c)2)
Vehicles other than the above20/1,000 (2%)Item (d)

Timing sits in Article 20. Paragraph 1 sets the rule at within 60 days of acquisition, extending inheritance to six months from the last day of the month in which succession opened, or nine months where an heir is domiciled abroad. But paragraph 4 requires payment by the day the application reaches the registration authority, so nobody uses the full 60. Pay before you go in to register.

  • An ambiguous class changes the whole rate. A car registered as a van or truck falls in the 5% band of item (c), not the 7% passenger band
  • Separately, some localities require purchase of urban railway or regional development bonds. Amounts are fixed by local ordinance and vary
  • Local education tax on acquisition tax does not apply to vehicles. Article 150(1) excludes Article 124 vehicles from that limb. Instead, under Article 150(7), it rides on the road tax for non-business passenger cars

What happens if you understate the price

One story circulates endlessly: that writing down a lower price lowers the acquisition tax. It does not move that way.

Article 10-5(1)2 of the Local Tax Act sets the value at acquisition for an onerous succession at the actual acquisition price, with a proviso. Where there is no filing or no stated figure, or where the declared figure is lower than the standard market value under Article 4(2), that standard value becomes the value at acquisition. The moment the declared figure drops below it, the tax base rises to that value. Nothing is shaved off; there is a floor.

  • The standard market value is maintained by local government from vehicle class, model year and the like. Not something an individual sets
  • Where the real price is above the standard value, the actual acquisition price is the tax base. Understating there is a false filing in itself
  • A false filing is not tax planning but a breach of the filing duty, and this article does not describe how. Declaring the actual amount is the rule
  • Keeping the contract and transfer records works in your favour. They are the evidence if the tax base is disputed
  • Reliefs run through separate application, not a smaller number. Conditions are in the Restriction of Special Local Taxation Act and local ordinances

Vehicles carrying an attachment or a mortgage

This is where deals blow up hardest. A car has no title deed register but a vehicle register, which records attachments and mortgages alongside ownership.

How an attachment gets there is in Article 14: entrustment by a court under the Civil Execution Act, by an administrative agency under the National or Local Tax Collection Act, and by a public institution under Article 4 of the Act on the Management of Public Institutions. Mortgages come from a different statute: a vehicle can be the object of one under Article 3(3) of the Act on Mortgage on Motor Vehicles and Other Specific Movables, and Article 5 makes acquisition or loss of it effective only when registered in the vehicle register.

The key is the order of money and paperwork. A mortgage is discharged only once the debt is settled; an attachment only once whoever placed it releases it. Sellers commonly propose taking the balance and clearing the debt with it, and in that sequence the buyer carries all the risk. Such a vehicle is in practice excluded from online filing and directed to the registration office.

  • Pull the register yourself before the balance moves. Read a copy the buyer obtained, not the seller's printout
  • Where one exists, write the release method, timing and cost into the contract. A verbal promise leaves nothing behind
  • Structure it as simultaneous performance where possible: payment, discharge and filing in one place
  • With several attachments, clearing one frees nothing. Check how many there are and who placed each

The disclosure duty carries a penalty. Article 80(6) makes a person who violates Article 58(1) by failing to disclose flood damage and inspection results, or whether an attachment or mortgage is registered, and Article 80(7) one who discloses those falsely, liable to up to 2 years or a fine of up to 20 million KRW. Such a provision exists because this is where things went wrong often.

Insurance - registration timing is not insurance timing

Because proof of insurance is a filing document, it is easy to assume registering settles the insurance too. They run on separate clocks. Registration is an act of the authority; insurance is a contract with an insurer.

The cover is not optional. Article 5 of the Guarantee of Automobile Accident Compensation Act requires a vehicle holder to carry bodily injury and property damage cover, and Article 46(2) makes operating an uninsured vehicle punishable by up to 1 year or a fine of up to 10 million KRW. Even undriven, the uninsured state attracts a charge.

CategoryWithin 10 days uninsuredPer day beyond 10 daysMaximum
Private car, bodily injury I10,000 KRW4,000 KRW600,000 KRW
Private car, property damage5,000 KRW2,000 KRW300,000 KRW
Motorcycle, bodily injury I6,000 KRW1,200 KRW200,000 KRW
Motorcycle, property damage3,000 KRW600 KRW100,000 KRW
Commercial, bodily injury I and II each30,000 KRW8,000 KRW1,000,000 KRW each

It goes wrong in the gap between cancellation and cover. If the seller cancels the day the car leaves and the buyer's cover starts the next, the register still shows the seller, so the uninsured window lands in his name. Cover scope and renewal are in the complete car insurance guide.

  • The buyer should have cover in their own name running before the filing date, since proof of insurance is a filing document
  • The seller should cancel only after confirming the transfer completed. Reversing that order opens the gap
  • A day or two of overlapping premium costs less than the uninsured charge. Overlap is the safer side
  • Whether it is a change of named insured or a new policy differs by insurer, so ask directly

Three routes through the procedure

Three. In person at the registration office, online, through a dealer. The deadline is identical; what changes is what you need and how fast it moves. The online counter is Car365, the public vehicle services portal run by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, and Government24 routes internet filings to it.

RouteWhat you needHandling
In person at the registration officeThe full document set, acquisition tax paidUsually same day. Missing papers can be fixed on the spot
Online via Car365Simple authentication, mobile identity verification, a financial certificate or a joint certificate, plus proof of compulsory insuranceImmediate, but only completes within weekday hours from 09:00 to 18:00
Through a dealerBuyer's identification, proof of insurance and similarArticle 12(2) puts the filing duty on the dealer. An agency fee applies

The fees are small. The revenue stamp is 1,000 KRW, or 1,500 KRW when filing in a city or province other than the place of use, and the stamp duty is 3,000 KRW. Most of the cost is the acquisition tax, the bonds and the plates.

What matters is that the online route is closed more widely than people expect. Car365 excludes transfers by gift or inheritance, joint ownership, applicants wanting to buy the bonds, exemption and relief cases, corporate and business vehicles, vehicles once registered commercially, vehicles carrying an attachment or mortgage, garage requirement areas, and vehicles whose change of use cannot be traced after a chassis number change. A good share of real deals lands there.

  • Check the online route is open before assembling documents. Reverse that order and you do the work twice
  • Delegating does not shift the deadline. Responsibility stays with you, so follow up on progress
  • Confirm completion in the register whichever route you take. A filing receipt is not proof of completion

Inheritance and gift - different clock, different papers

A vehicle arriving by inheritance runs on a different track. The period is six months, and the paperwork shifts towards proving relationships between people. Article 26(2) of the Decree provides that where the owner has died, the office may give guidance on filing for transfer by inheritance under standards published by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport. Guidance may arrive; the filing is not automatic.

  • The acquisition tax deadline stretches to match. Article 20(1) gives inheritance six months from the last day of the month in which succession opened, nine months where an heir is domiciled abroad
  • With several heirs an agreement on division of the estate is required. Without it the process stops here
  • If renunciation or qualified acceptance is under consideration, filing may be an act readable as acceptance of the inheritance. That judgement calls for a lawyer
  • A gift runs on 20 days, and its acquisition tax follows the gratuitous acquisition rules, not the onerous ones. The computation differs, so take tax advice
  • Both are excluded from online filing, so plan on attending in person

How scrapping and export deregistration fit in

Transferring is not the only way a car leaves your hands. One that will not be driven again goes to deregistration, which erases the registration rather than changing the owner. Article 13(1) lists the grounds on which an owner must surrender the certificate and plates and apply; subparagraph 1 is requesting scrapping by a registered dismantling and recycling business, subparagraph 6 exporting the vehicle.

  • The deadline differs. Article 31 of the Decree sets deregistration at within one month of the cause arising, or six months from the last day of the month in which succession opened where the applicant is an heir
  • The applicant changes too. Article 13(2) has the dismantling and recycling business file for scrapping and the exporter for an export, in the owner's place
  • An export does not end at deregistration. Article 13(8) requires a report on whether the export was carried out, and Article 32 of the Decree sets that at one year from the deregistration date
  • Bringing a deregistered vehicle back is new registration, not transfer (Article 13(10))
  • Article 84(4), subparagraphs 5 to 7, make failure to file the deregistration, to report on the export, and to apply for new registration on re-entry each subject to a charge of up to 1,000,000 KRW
  • A vehicle written off as a total loss by an insurer carries a condition on the transfer itself. Article 12(6) accepts the application only where the repair inspection under Article 43(1)5 has been carried out

What matters in practice is sequence and title. Deregistration proceeds against the owner in the register, so a vehicle whose title was never sorted out gets stuck again at scrapping. And motorcycles fall outside that route: Article 2(9) expressly excludes them from the definition of the dismantling and recycling business.

Where things routinely go sideways

Some things run differently even with the deadline met and the papers in order. Usually a rule changed while the instinct did not, or two statutes each move on their own basis.

  • Plates carry over by default — a transfer does not change the number. Article 16 of the Act, Article 24(1) of the Decree and Article 29(2) of the Rules list the grounds, one being where the acquirer wants a change within 60 days of filing. Do it inside that window or keep the number
  • Regional plates are different — an old style regional plate is switched to a nationwide one at the transfer and surrendered. The plate charge varies by local government
  • The plate seal is gone — the sealing system, long treated as the car's registered seal, has been abolished. Per the Ministry of Government Legislation's statement of reasons, Act No. 20339, abolishing it and removing the temporary operation permit display requirement, was promulgated on 20 February 2024 and took effect on 21 February 2025. Some local pages still describe the old regime
  • Road tax is apportioned, not inherited — the assessment dates are 1 June and 1 December, and whoever the register shows then is the taxpayer. On a mid period handover Article 128(5) of the Local Tax Act apportions it by day against the transfer date. File late and the apportionment closes late. The computation is in how road tax is assessed and paid
  • Fines do not pass across — the Act on the Regulation of Violations of Public Order recognises succession only on death (Article 24-2(1)) and corporate merger (Article 24-2(2)); a transfer of ownership is not a ground. Article 160(3) of the Road Traffic Act charges the employer or equivalent where the driver cannot be identified, and on an untransferred car the person still in that place is the seller. His remedy is a civil claim against the buyer, not an objection
  • Motorcycles run on a different system — not registration but a use report, filed with the head of the city, county or district rather than a Mayor or Provincial Governor (Article 48). The record is a motorcycle ledger, not the vehicle register. A person acquiring ownership reports under Article 48(4), and if they do not, the proviso lets the transferor report in their place. The deadlines match: Article 101 of the Enforcement Rules gives 15 days from purchase, 20 for a gift, six months for an inheritance and 15 for any other cause. Changes of place of use, name or resident registration number run on 30 days

What to read in the register before you sign

The register is the car's curriculum vitae. Article 8(2) of the Decree splits it into vehicle register (part A) and (part B), the gap and eul sheets. Article 7(6) of the Act sets what goes on them: registration and chassis numbers, model name, place of use, owner, engine type, class and use, structural and device modifications, inspection validity, and matters concerning the vehicle mortgage. Inspection and issue of copies rest on Article 7(4) and Article 12 of the Rules; on Government24 a copy is 300 KRW and an abstract 100 KRW.

  • Owner — is the person you are contracting with the owner in the register? If not, what establishes their authority?
  • Attachments — how many, which body placed each, and on what condition released
  • Mortgage — whether one exists and the maximum secured amount. It is the basis for the discharge sequence in the contract
  • Chassis number and engine type — check against the actual car. A mismatch blocks everything downstream
  • Registration history — repeated recent changes of name are worth asking about
  • Unpaid tax and fines — these often show up as attachments, and are a common reason a transfer stalls

Add the check afterwards. About ten days after the balance date the seller should pull the register again and see whether the name changed. Confirming before the 15 days elapse leaves room for the transferor's own application under Article 12(4).

Summary

The frame reduces to three sentences. First, the deadline depends on the cause: 15 days for a sale, 20 for a gift, six months from the last day of the month in which succession opened for an inheritance, 15 for anything else. Second, the charge accrues by the day: 100,000 KRW within 10 days, rising 10,000 KRW a day and stopping at 500,000 KRW — but the criminal provisions in Articles 79 and 80 stand outside that ceiling.

The third actually bites. When the transfer does not happen, the loss lands on the seller. Ownership moves only on registration (Article 6), and road tax, fine notices and the insurance duty all follow the name in the register. The rest is sequence: insure first, check attachments and mortgages first, pay the acquisition tax before registering, and check again after handing the car over.

Once more, plainly. General information about South Korean law, not legal or tax advice on a particular transaction. Starting dates, documents, tax amounts and relief conditions vary with vehicle class, deal structure, locality and personal circumstances. Confirm with the competent vehicle registration office and your local tax division before filing, and consult a lawyer or certified tax accountant where an attachment, an inheritance or a dispute is involved.

What I Could Not Verify

  • The text of Table 2 of the Enforcement Decree could not be opened. The 100,000 KRW, 10,000 KRW per day and 500,000 KRW ceiling appear at identical values in the Ministry of Government Legislation's Easy Law service and in several local notices. The bands for new and change registration differ between notices, so they are not stated
  • No express provision prohibiting transfer registration where an attachment or mortgage exists could be located. Confirmed are the basis for registering an attachment (Article 14), the effect of registering a mortgage (Act on Mortgage on Motor Vehicles and Other Specific Movables, Article 5), and the Car365 exclusion from online filing. The text stays within that range
  • The provision dividing what is recorded on part A versus part B could not be identified. Only that Article 8(2) of the Decree splits it into two forms
  • Whether dealer transactions can be filed online could not be settled. The portal guidance is inconsistent with itself
  • The full list of subparagraphs in Article 33 of the Rules and the text of Article 34 could not be confirmed. Only confirmed subparagraph and form numbers appear
  • The exact effective date of the regional plate changes could not be confirmed, so no year is given. Bond amounts and relief categories are ordinance matters, so no figures either
