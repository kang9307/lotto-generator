Introduction: Why Korea's First-Time Buyer System Is Worth Understanding

Buying a home in Korea for the first time can feel like navigating a maze. Housing subscription accounts, special supply quotas, LTV caps, DSR ratios, acquisition-tax reductions — each term represents a distinct policy lever, and understanding how they connect can mean the difference between winning a coveted apartment allocation and missing the window entirely.

This guide covers Korea's key first-time buyer programs — all of which apply to the Korean domestic market — in plain language: the housing subscription account, tier-1 subscription eligibility, the first-time special supply quota, LTV and DSR benefits, acquisition-tax reduction, and additional government programs. A recurring caution: Korean housing policy changes frequently with economic conditions and government priorities. The figures below are general guidance; the official public notices and your financial institution are the authoritative source.

1. The Housing Subscription Account — Your First Step

In Korea, every applicant hoping to subscribe to a new apartment must hold a Housing Subscription Comprehensive Savings account (주택청약종합저축, often called the "cheongak account"). This single account covers both public housing (national housing, 국민주택) and private housing (민영주택) subscriptions.

1.1 Opening an Account

Any Korean resident may open one account, regardless of age. Even minors can hold one. The account is available at nine designated banks, including KB Kookmin, Woori, Shinhan, Hana, NH NongHyup, IBK, Busan, Daegu, and Gyeongnam banks. Opening online or at a branch takes minutes.

1.2 How Much to Contribute

Monthly contributions may range from KRW 20,000 to KRW 500,000. However, the amount recognised for subscription scoring purposes has a ceiling. Since November 2024, the monthly recognised ceiling was raised from KRW 100,000 to KRW 250,000. Contributing KRW 250,000 per month maximises the score credit for each payment period. Contributions above that are deposited but do not add additional scoring credit.

1.3 Public vs Private Housing Logic

For public housing (national housing), the number of monthly payments and tenure matter most — consistent, uninterrupted payments are rewarded. For private housing, meeting a regional deposit threshold for your target area and unit size is key. For example, the commonly cited threshold for units larger than 85 m² in Seoul and Busan is KRW 15 million. First-time special supply applications are generally governed by payment-count rules, making consistency essential.

Key takeaway: Open your subscription account as early as possible and contribute KRW 250,000 monthly without gaps. Check current thresholds and payment-count requirements at Cheongyakhom before applying.

2. Tier-1 Subscription Eligibility

Korea's subscription system uses tiers (순위, sun-wi). Tier 1 applicants are served first and have the best odds in competitive draws. Understanding Tier 1 requirements is useful even for first-time special supply applicants, because it contextualises the overall system.

2.1 Private Housing Tier 1

Under commonly cited rules, private housing Tier 1 requires:

  • Account tenure: 12 months or more (24 months in speculative or overheating zones)
  • Deposit balance: meeting the regional and unit-size threshold
  • Household status: head of household (or eligible household member) without a home

2.2 Public Housing Tier 1

For national housing, Tier 1 requires at least 12 months of tenure and at least 12 monthly payments (24 months / 24 payments in speculative or overheating zones). The household must be entirely home-free (no member may own any residential property).

3. First-Time Special Supply Quota — Key Eligibility

The First-Time Special Supply (생애최초 특별공급) reserves a portion of units for households purchasing a home for the very first time. Competition is typically lower than for general allocation, giving buyers with fewer subscription points a genuine chance.

3.1 Core Eligibility Criteria

Under commonly cited rules, applicants must meet all of the following:

  • No prior home ownership — every household member must have zero history of owning residential property (including inheritance and gifts)
  • Currently home-free — the entire household must not own a home at the time of application
  • Income-tax payment history — the applicant (or spouse) must have paid income tax as an employee or self-employed person for at least 5 cumulative years
  • Marital / family status — generally, must be married or have dependent children; single-person households may only apply for units of 60 m² or less in certain categories
  • Income ceiling — for public housing, household monthly income must be at or below 130% of the urban-worker average monthly income; for private housing, 160% or below (families with children born on or after 28 March 2023 may benefit from a relaxed threshold)
  • Asset ceiling — real estate assets must generally be below KRW 331 million; vehicle value limits also apply (varies by scheme)
Income ceilings, asset thresholds, single-person household eligibility, and other criteria vary by project type (LH, SH, private developer), unit size, and region. Read the individual project notice (공고문) carefully and check your eligibility at Cheongyakhom's self-check tool.

3.2 Selection Method

For public housing, selection is often split between a priority group (lower income first) and a general group (lottery). For private housing, first-time special supply typically uses a lottery. Check the specific notice for the development you are applying to.

4. Mortgage Benefits for First-Time Buyers

Korea provides meaningful mortgage concessions to first-time buyers, though tighter DSR rules since 2026 have reduced effective borrowing capacity across the board.

4.1 LTV Benefit

Under commonly cited rules, first-time buyers may access an LTV ratio of up to 80% even in regulated zones (speculation zones, overheating zones, and adjustment zones), where standard buyers face lower caps. The maximum loan amount in the Seoul metropolitan area is generally reported at KRW 600 million. In non-regulated areas, LTV limits can be even more generous. Actual approval depends on the lender, the property's appraised value, and the borrower's income.

4.2 DSR Cap and Stress-DSR Phase 3

The DSR (Debt-Service Ratio) cap limits total annual loan repayments to 40% of gross annual income at banks (50% at non-bank lenders) for any borrower whose total loan balance exceeds KRW 100 million. In 2026, Stress-DSR Phase 3 took full effect nationwide. It adds a simulated interest-rate stress buffer to the actual rate when calculating how much a person can borrow. Reports indicate this reduced effective borrowing capacity by more than KRW 100 million for a household earning KRW 100 million annually — a significant constraint for all buyers, including first-timers.

4.3 Policy Loan — Didimdol

The Korea Housing Finance Corporation (HF) and the Housing Urban Guarantee Corporation (HUG) administer the Didimdol Loan (디딤돌 대출), a subsidised mortgage product with preferential rates for first-time buyers. Eligibility requirements include income ceilings, asset ceilings, and home-price limits. For current rates, limits, and income criteria, check the Housing Urban Fund portal at enhuf.molit.go.kr.

Loan checklist: LTV 80% benefit (first-time), DSR 40% cap, Stress-DSR Phase 3 impact on capacity, Didimdol subsidised loan eligibility.

5. Acquisition Tax Reduction

When buying property in Korea, acquisition tax (취득세, chwi-deuk-se) applies. First-time buyers receive a reduction that can significantly lower upfront costs.

5.1 Standard Reduction

Under commonly cited rules, first-time buyers purchasing a home valued at KRW 1.2 billion or less can receive an acquisition-tax reduction of up to KRW 2 million. There is no income limit for this reduction. However, a 3-year owner-occupancy requirement applies: selling or leasing out the property within 3 years will trigger clawback of the full tax benefit.

5.2 Deadline Extension

Under the local-tax law amendment reportedly passed for 2026, the policy deadline is extended from 31 December 2025 to 31 December 2028. Purchasers should confirm this at the relevant local government office, as legislative details may differ from early reports.

5.3 Small / Affordable Home Bonus

For units of 60 m² or less located in areas designated as depopulation zones, an enhanced reduction of up to KRW 3 million may apply. However, under the 2026 amendment, this enhanced benefit is reportedly narrowed to depopulation-designated areas only — check whether your target location qualifies.

Acquisition-tax rules are subject to local tax legislation. Confirm the applicable rate and exemption ceiling with your local government tax office (시/군/구청) or a certified tax professional before filing. This is information only, not tax advice.

6. Other Support Programs

6.1 Jeonse (Deposit-Lease) and Monthly-Rent Loans

While working toward ownership, first-time buyers may use HF- and HUG-backed jeonse loans and monthly-rent deposit loans, which carry preferential rates for young adults and newlyweds.

6.2 Newlywed and Youth Special Supply Quotas

Beyond first-time special supply, Korea operates newlywed (신혼부부), multi-child (다자녀), and, in select projects, youth (청년) special supply quotas. Applicants may generally apply under only one category per project — choose the category most beneficial to your situation.

6.3 Nyu:Home (Public Sale Program)

The Ministry of Land's Nyu:Home (뉴:홈) program offers three types of public-sale housing: Sharing Type (나눔형, discounted price with profit-sharing on resale), Choice Type (선택형), and Standard Type (일반형). Sharing Type units, which are below-market-price condos, are heavily allocated to first-time buyers. Check the official site at 뉴홈.kr for schedules.

7. Step-by-Step Application Process

  1. Open and fund a subscription account — start as early as possible; contribute KRW 250,000 monthly
  2. Check eligibility — use Cheongyakhom's self-check tool (applyhome.co.kr) to verify home-free status, payment count, and special-supply eligibility
  3. Monitor project notices — watch Cheongyakhom and developer / LH sites for upcoming subscription schedules
  4. Prepare documents in advance — income confirmation certificate (국세청), family register, household register, marriage certificate, withholding tax statement (employees) or comprehensive income return (self-employed)
  5. Apply online — log into Cheongyakhom with your certified ID, select the target project's first-time special supply category, and submit before the 17:30 deadline on the designated day
  6. Submit physical documents after winning — winners must submit originals within the specified deadline; failure or ineligibility leads to cancellation and a subscription restriction penalty
  7. Sign the purchase contract and arrange financing — finalise your mortgage and plan the acquisition-tax filing within 60 days of the contract date

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Does living in a jeonse (deposit-lease) apartment disqualify me from first-time buyer status?

No. Jeonse is a tenancy arrangement; you do not own the property. First-time buyer status is forfeited only if you — or any household member — have at any point legally owned residential property, including by inheritance or gift (with limited exceptions for short-duration inherited properties). Confirm your specific situation at Cheongyakhom's eligibility checker.

Q. Can a single-income household apply?

Yes. The 5-year income-tax payment requirement may be met by either the applicant or the spouse. Income for the ceiling test is the combined household income. Single-income households that meet the income ceiling are eligible.

Q. What is the difference between the general subscription points system and first-time special supply?

The general subscription points system (가점제) awards points for home-free duration, number of dependants, and subscription account tenure — buyers with more points rank higher. First-time special supply is a separate quota reserved only for eligible first-time buyers; subscription points are not the deciding factor, making it accessible to younger buyers with shorter account histories.

Q. How do I claim the acquisition-tax reduction?

File for acquisition tax within 60 days of the purchase date at your local government tax office (시/군/구청) or online via Wetax (wetax.go.kr). Include a first-time buyer acquisition-tax reduction application (생애최초 취득세 감면 신청서). Retain documentation of your home-free status. The 3-year occupancy obligation starts from the acquisition date.

Q. Are there resale restrictions on first-time special supply units?

Yes, resale restrictions (전매 제한) apply to many new apartment developments, especially in regulated zones and public-sale programs like Nyu:Home. The restriction period varies by project type and location. Nyu:Home Sharing Type units also carry profit-sharing obligations on resale. Always check the specific project notice for restriction details.

Conclusion — Know the System, Seize the Opportunity

Korea's first-time homebuyer ecosystem offers genuine advantages: a reserved quota that bypasses the points competition, an LTV uplift to 80% in regulated zones, an acquisition-tax cut of up to KRW 2 million, and subsidised Didimdol loans. Used together, these benefits can meaningfully reduce upfront costs and improve affordability.

The price of those benefits is preparation. Subscription accounts must be opened early and fed consistently. Eligibility rules must be verified against the latest official notice — not summarised articles. And with Stress-DSR Phase 3 tightening credit capacity, buyers need a realistic budget that accounts for the new borrowing limits before falling in love with a property.

This article summarised general rules as of June 2026. Korea's housing policies change with market and regulatory conditions. Before any subscription, loan application, or tax filing, verify current rules at Cheongyakhom (applyhome.co.kr), MOLIT (molit.go.kr), FSS (fss.or.kr), and your bank or a licensed professional.
