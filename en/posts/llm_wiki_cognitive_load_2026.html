LLM Wiki and Germane Load: The Hidden Trap in AI-Era PKM

When AI builds your wiki, who builds your thinking? A cognitive load theory analysis of the PKM paradox

2026-06-25 Tech

Introduction: The Convenience Behind the Problem

In April 2026, Andrej Karpathy published a GitHub gist called llm-wiki. The idea is deceptively simple: an AI agent reads raw documents and automatically builds and maintains a structured Markdown wiki. Within two weeks it had crossed 5,000 stars, spawned dozens of re-implementations, and inspired commercial SaaS products. AI note automation, auto-generated wikis, the "AI second brain" — different names, same direction. AI will organize your knowledge for you.

We should pause here. This article does not argue that LLM Wiki is bad or that you should not use it. It raises a more fundamental question: when AI handles knowledge organization on your behalf, what happens inside your mind? To answer that, I turn to cognitive load theory (CLT) from educational psychology. The theory is precise, and its implications are uncomfortable.

As of June 2026, AI note tools are mature and their market is exploding. Yet discussion of their cognitive cost remains conspicuously thin. That conversation needs to begin now.

1. The Three Loads of Cognitive Load Theory

John Sweller developed cognitive load theory from the late 1980s onward, placing human working memory's capacity limits at the center. Working memory can process only a tightly bounded amount of information simultaneously — Miller's magical number seven, Cowan's four-chunk limit. Exceed that limit and comprehension collapses. CLT identifies three types of cognitive demand.

1.1 Intrinsic Load

Intrinsic load comes from the inherent complexity of the material being learned. Calculus carries higher intrinsic load than arithmetic. The more tightly the elements of a concept interact, the heavier the intrinsic load. Crucially, this load diminishes as a learner builds expertise: what is hard for a novice is automatic for an expert, because stored schemas reduce the number of elements that must be held in working memory simultaneously.

1.2 Extraneous Load

Extraneous load stems from unnecessary complexity in how information is presented — rambling explanations, split-attention layouts where text and diagrams are separated, irrelevant details that compete for attention. This load is wasteful: it consumes working memory without contributing to learning. Good instructional design, and AI tools that clean up formatting and noise, legitimately reduce extraneous load.

1.3 Germane Load

This is the critical one. Germane load is the cognitive effort invested in integrating information into long-term memory schemas — not merely receiving information, but connecting it to prior knowledge, abstracting patterns, and constructing mental structure. It is effortful and sometimes uncomfortable, but it is the process through which genuine understanding forms. Educational psychologists sometimes call it a "desirable difficulty."

A 2014 PMC study empirically validated germane load's distinctiveness. Simply reading information versus actively restructuring it and re-expressing it in one's own words produced measurably different outcomes in memory and comprehension. Not what you know, but how you processed it, determines the depth of your understanding.

2. What LLM Wiki Actually Does

Consider Karpathy's LLM Wiki pattern concretely. You feed raw documents to the AI; it writes summary pages, creates cross-references between concepts, detects contradictions, and updates related pages. The central claim is that AI solves the canonical PKM failure: "maintenance burden outpaces value, so humans eventually quit."

Translated into CLT vocabulary, list the tasks the AI performs: reading raw sources and extracting key points; finding connections between concepts; writing structured summaries; integrating entries into the existing wiki; identifying gaps and contradictions. These are precisely the activities where germane load concentrates. Once AI performs them all, what remains for the user is largely reading the finished wiki.

Yes, the user directs the system and asks questions. But the core cognitive work of schema formation — connecting elements, integrating with prior knowledge, building structure — was performed by the AI. The user ends up consuming a synthesis that AI already digested.

3. Cognitive Offloading vs. Cognitive Outsourcing

A crucial distinction: cognitive offloading and cognitive outsourcing are not the same thing.

3.1 Cognitive Offloading: The Productive Kind

Using a calculator for repetitive arithmetic; a calendar for date management; a shopping list to externalize memory. These forms of offloading reduce extraneous load, freeing working memory for higher-level reasoning — which can actually increase the germane load that matters. Using AI to automate document formatting, fix typos, or fetch references falls into this category. It is productive.

3.2 Cognitive Outsourcing: The Problematic Kind

Delegating comprehension itself to AI. Asking "summarize the key claims of this paper" and treating the AI's answer as your understanding. In this case, germane load does not occur. A 2025 mixed-methods study of 666 participants found that heavy AI tool use was associated with decreased critical thinking ability, mediated by cognitive offloading. An MIT Media Lab EEG study found that AI-assisted writers showed lower cognitive engagement signatures and, crucially, performed worse when subsequently doing the same task unassisted.

LLM Wiki's architecture slides easily toward cognitive outsourcing. The AI delivers a polished wiki; the user "uses" it but is, in practice, borrowing the schema AI constructed rather than building one internally.

4. The Expert–Novice Asymmetry

A 2025 PMC paper, "The extended hollowed mind," identifies a key asymmetry. AI functions as an amplifier for experts and a leveler for novices.

Experts already hold rich schemas. Reading an AI-generated wiki entry, they compare it against existing mental models, catch errors, and generate new connections. Schema exists, so AI output gets processed critically — germane load still fires. This is active, productive consumption.

Novices lack schemas to compare against. With no prior structure to trigger comparison or skepticism, they tend to accept AI output. Without engaging intrinsic load, germane processing cannot occur, and the result is an illusion of understanding rather than genuine comprehension: the sense of knowing without the cognitive substrate to support it.

This asymmetry has a direct implication for when to introduce AI into PKM. Only after you have built sufficient schema in a domain does AI become a true amplifier. Before that point, delegating schema formation to AI produces rapid accumulation of hollow knowledge.

5. The Second Brain Paradox

Tiago Forte's "Second Brain" methodology originally aimed to store information externally so the biological brain could focus on creative connection-making. Tools like Obsidian, Notion, and Roam embodied this philosophy. Critically, in the original methodology, the user writes the notes, makes the connections, drafts the summaries. That process is the germane load that builds understanding and memory.

LLM Wiki era PKM represents a paradigm shift: AI writes the notes, makes the connections, crafts the summaries. The user "has" a second brain but was absent from the cognitive work that should have accompanied its construction. There is a beautifully structured external knowledge system, but no guarantee it is integrated with any internal schemas.

Imagine AI doing your workouts so you feel as if you have muscles — without actually developing any. PKM automation pushed to the extreme hits this paradox: a rich knowledge repository externally, atrophied cognitive muscle internally.

6. The Cognitive Bypass Problem

The 2025 paper "The extended hollowed mind" explicitly addresses the cognitive bypass. The more frictionless and complete AI's answers, the stronger the pull toward bypassing slow System 2 thinking — logical reasoning, critical analysis, deep comprehension. The authors call it movement toward the "path of least cognitive resistance."

LLM Wiki is architecturally optimized for this bypass: zero friction, instant result, high polish. From a user's perspective, opening an AI wiki is always easier than reading the source and thinking. Short-term productivity appears to rise, but the eliminated friction is also eliminated germane load. Research consistently warns that the schema formation lost in that trade-off does not come back automatically.

An important caveat: this concern is not unique to LLM Wiki. Search engines, Wikipedia, and e-book highlighting have all attracted similar critiques. LLM Wiki is notable because its completeness and convenience exceed any prior tool, making the bypass more tempting than ever.

7. Design Principles for AI-Aware PKM

So what should we actually do? Three principles guide my thinking.

7.1 Intentionally Protect Germane Load

When integrating AI into PKM, explicitly decide which cognitive tasks to delegate and which to keep. Extraneous load — formatting, spell-checking, reference retrieval, deduplication — is fair game for AI. But concept linking, writing your own interpretation, drafting the first summary, answering "why does this matter" — these belong to you. Use AI to review your draft, not to originate it.

7.2 Never Invert the Sequence

"Ask AI first, then review" versus "think myself first, then verify with AI" are cognitively different processes with different outcomes. The latter preserves germane load. A five-minute personal memo on a concept, written before opening the AI wiki, is a small intervention that maintains schema formation even in a heavily AI-assisted workflow.

7.3 Use AI as an Amplifier Only Where You Have Schemas

In domains where you already carry rich expertise, AI wikis become genuine amplifiers: you critically process AI output through your schemas, catch errors, and generate connections. In domains you are just entering, resist delegating structure to AI before doing your own cognitive struggle first. It is slow and uncomfortable — that discomfort is the germane load doing its job.

8. The Policy Context: What Norway Signals

In early 2026, Norway announced restrictions on AI tool use in parts of its primary and secondary curriculum. Among the justifications: cognitive load theory, specifically the concern about germane load. Just as letting students use calculators while still learning arithmetic prevents arithmetic skill from forming, allowing AI to handle cognitive structuring during foundational learning prevents the corresponding capability from developing.

This logic applies equally to adult PKM. AI delivers the most value when accelerating what I already know how to do well. When AI knows something on my behalf that I have not yet internalized, I acquire the illusion of knowing without the reality of it.

9. Conclusion: Do Not Kill the Friction

LLM Wiki is an excellent tool for dramatically reducing the extraneous load of knowledge maintenance. Handing AI the wiki upkeep that humans inevitably abandon is a genuine and practical win. But this convenience carries a quiet cost. When AI handles the schema-formation process itself, the user is left with a beautifully organized external system while the internal processing that should have accompanied it was skipped.

Cognitive load theory has established this over decades of research: no pain, no gain applies in the cognitive domain. Germane load — the good friction of schema building — is not a nuisance to eliminate but a resource to protect. The central design challenge of AI-era PKM is to reduce extraneous load without killing germane load.

Knowing what to give AI and what to wrestle with yourself — that distinction is the core of maintaining thinking ability in the age of AI. No matter how powerful the tool, the thinking muscle only grows when you use it.
