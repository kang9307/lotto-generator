Safety is the top priority when living alone

Have you ever felt scared while living alone? Strange sounds at night, someone knocking on your door unexpectedly... When you live alone, you need to pay extra attention to safety.

This article compiles essential safety tips for solo dwellers.

1. Door Security

Basic Rules

  • Double locks: Use both deadbolt and chain
  • Check locks: Always verify before bed and when leaving
  • Door stopper: Cheap way to add extra security
  • Cover gaps: Make sure inside isn't visible from outside

Smart Locks

  • Change passcode regularly
  • Avoid easy codes like birthdays or phone numbers
  • Cover keypad when entering code

Intercom/Doorbell

  • Never open door immediately for strangers
  • Always verify through intercom first
  • Be wary of fake delivery/maintenance claims - call company to verify

Changing a lock, adding a video doorbell or installing a camera can depend on your lease and your landlord's or building manager's consent. It varies case by case, so read the lease and ask first. Shared areas such as entrances and corridors are usually managed by someone else.

Always ask "Who is it?" before opening! And never say you live alone.

2. Don't Reveal You Live Alone

Daily Life

  • Shoes: Keep men's shoes by the door (psychological deterrent)
  • Laundry: Hang men's clothes on balcony
  • Packages: Use initials or generic names
  • Mailbox: Empty regularly (piled mail = away from home)

Treat these as extras. We did not find official evidence that they work, so they do not replace basics like locking up and checking who is at the door.

Social Media Caution

  • Don't share real-time location
  • Be careful about posting interior photos (address exposure)
  • Don't post "I'm on vacation!" in real-time

3. Nighttime Safety

Coming Home

  • Take well-lit streets
  • Don't wear earphones in both ears (need to hear surroundings)
  • If someone's following, go into a store
  • Check behind you before entering building

At Home

  • Check all doors/windows before sleep
  • Keep phone charged (for emergencies)
  • Keep flashlight by bed

4. Fire Safety

Prevention

  • Turn off appliances when leaving (heater, iron)
  • Close gas valve after cooking
  • Don't overload power strips
  • Know where fire extinguisher is

In an emergency, call your local number: 911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 across the EU. On move-in day, also ask your landlord or building manager what smoke alarms and extinguishers the building provides and who checks them. Step-by-step fire and gas procedures are in Part 19: Emergencies.

In Case of Fire

  • Call emergency services
  • Stay low to avoid smoke
  • Cover nose/mouth with wet towel
  • Use stairs - NEVER elevator
  • Know your escape route in advance

On move-in day, check the fire escape route and extinguisher location!

5. Emergency Contacts

Prepare in Advance

  • Family contacts on speed dial
  • Close friend's number
  • Building manager/landlord contact
  • Nearest hospital and pharmacy locations

Location Sharing

  • Share your address with trusted family/friends
  • Set up location sharing app (trusted people only)

6. Useful Safety Apps

  • Safe walk apps: share your route when walking home; availability differs by city and provider
  • Emergency SOS: a phone feature that can contact emergency services and share location; check how it works in your country
  • Disaster alert apps: use your local authority's official alerts

7. Safety Equipment

Recommended Items

  • Door stopper: Prevents forced entry
  • Window locks: Extra security
  • Fire extinguisher: Small home-use type
  • Flashlight: For power outages
  • First aid kit: Basic emergency supplies

If You Suspect You're Being Stalked

The same person repeatedly following you, contacting you against your wishes, or waiting near your home or work may be stalking. Laws and reporting routes differ by country and region.

  • Danger right now, or someone at your door: do not open it, call your emergency number (911 / 999 / 112)
  • Not urgent: contact your local police non-emergency line (101 in England and Wales) or a local victim-support service
  • Keep records: do not delete messages, call logs or photos; they make it easier to explain what happened

You do not need to be certain before asking for advice. Emergency numbers and first steps are in Part 19.

8. Safety Checklist

Daily

  • ☐ Check door lock before leaving
  • ☐ Check doors/windows before bed
  • ☐ Keep phone charged

Regularly

  • ☐ Change door lock code
  • ☐ Update emergency contacts
  • ☐ Review escape route

## 1. Which number

| Situation | Number | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Danger now, or someone at your door | 911 (US) / 999 (UK) / 112 (EU) | Do not open the door |
| Fire or medical emergency | Same emergency number | Use stairs, not the elevator |
| Not urgent, want to talk to police | 101 (England and Wales) | Otherwise your local police line |
