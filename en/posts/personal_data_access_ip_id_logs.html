Introduction: when the reply does not answer the question

Guides to the right of access are easy to find, and almost all of them stop at the same place: you may make a request. The hard part begins after that. The request goes out, a reply comes back, and there is often no way to tell whether the reply answers what was asked. Records for a period other than the one named arrive. A list pulled on a different key arrives, with no note that the key was different. A short summary table arrives and the underlying record is never mentioned at all.

One sentence runs underneath everything below.

What cannot be verified is not evidence.

It surfaces in two forms. The first is you cannot say a period was normal without a record of it: a record is the means by which normality is shown, so the statement that no record exists and the statement that everything was normal have no room to stand together. The second is material you cannot check is not compliance: where there is no way to establish which criteria produced the extract, what was left out, and whether it matches the source, the reply has not reached the state the right of access asks for.

The same principle has a technical counterpart. Why You Cannot Trust the Client - Trust Boundaries and Web Security Controls works through the systems side: when identification controls have broken down, a user ID sitting in a log does not establish who acted. This article takes the other side, the rights side - what a data subject may ask for, and how to check what comes back. The technical ground is not repeated here.

Six parts follow. Part one is what you can ask for, part two is procedure, part three is how to verify what arrives, part four is what logs do and do not prove, part five is the status of IP addresses, and part six is what happens on refusal and how the same material looks from the operator's side. Of these, section 4 and section 9 carry the practical weight.

1. The right reaches all your personal data, not a file called the log

The most common way a request narrows itself is asking for a filename. Send me the access log. Send me the logs. Phrased that way, the recipient only has to produce whatever it calls the access log, and what goes into that name is the recipient's choice, not yours.

GDPR Article 15(1) gives the data subject the right to obtain confirmation as to whether personal data concerning him or her are being processed and, where that is the case, access to the personal data, and Article 15(3) requires the controller to provide a copy of the personal data undergoing processing, the first copy free of charge. California Civil Code section 1798.110 requires a business, on a consumer request, to disclose the specific pieces of personal information it has collected about that consumer alongside the categories, sources, purposes and third parties. Neither text is organised around files or tables. Both are organised around the personal data itself, and an access log is one kind of thing that sits inside that.

Korea's Personal Information Protection Act reaches the same place from the other direction. Article 35(1) provides that a data subject may request the personal information controller to grant access to his or her personal information being processed by that controller, so the object is again the data rather than a named file. What makes the Korean framework useful to an English-speaking reader is the next paragraph, because it is where the log itself becomes a statutory object rather than an operational nicety.

1.1 Where the access right and the security duty meet

Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 requires a personal information controller to take the technical, administrative and physical measures necessary to ensure safety - such as establishing an internal management plan and retaining access records - as prescribed by Presidential Decree, so that personal information is not lost, stolen, leaked, forged, altered or damaged. The phrase retaining access records sits inside the statutory text as a named example. That matters: the access record is not an optional by-product a team may or may not keep, it is a record the statute presupposes will exist.

  • The access provisions - GDPR Article 15, Civil Code section 1798.110, Korea Article 35 - define what a person may ask for, and in each case the object is the personal data as a whole
  • Korea Article 29 defines a duty on the controller, and names the retention of access records among the safety measures that duty covers
  • The meeting point of those two kinds of provision is this article's subject. Where a record must be kept, the part of it that concerns you falls inside what you may ask to see
  • So a request should describe the nature of the information rather than a filename: records concerning me that include the date and time of access, information on the point of access, and the work performed
  • Which system holds that information, and what the organisation calls it internally, is the organisation's business. A requester does not have to guess the name correctly to be asking for the right thing

2. The items the law lists - data, purposes, retention, recipients, source

Asking for all my personal data sounds open-ended, but the three regimes an English-speaking reader is most likely to be working under each publish a list. GDPR Article 15(1) enumerates the accompanying information at points (a) to (h): the purposes of the processing, the categories of personal data concerned, the recipients or categories of recipient, the envisaged storage period or the criteria used to determine it, the right to request rectification or erasure or restriction, the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, any available information as to the source where the data were not collected from the data subject, and meaningful information about the logic involved in automated decision-making including profiling. Article 15(2) adds the right to be informed of the appropriate safeguards where data are transferred to a third country, and Article 15(3) adds the copy. California Civil Code section 1798.110 lists five disclosures: the categories of personal information collected, the categories of sources, the business or commercial purpose for collecting or selling it, the categories of third parties with whom the business shares personal information, and the specific pieces of personal information collected. Korea's Enforcement Decree Article 41(1) lists five items a data subject may ask to see: the items and content of the personal information, the purposes of collection and use, the retention and use period, the status of provision to third parties, and the fact and content of consent to processing.

3. Three axes worth separating - IP, identifier, log

What people actually want back usually falls into three kinds. They behave differently, which means that asking for them in one sentence lets each of them hide the others. Separate them in the request and they come back separated.

4. How to write the request - pin the scope to period, key and item

This is the most practical part of the article. Most guidance stops at you may make a request. What actually separates a usable answer from an unusable one is how the request is written, and that comes down to nailing the scope to three axes.

A request with no scope is a request whose scope the recipient sets. And the scope the recipient sets is not usually disclosed to the requester either, so it cannot even be argued about.

4.1 Period - both ends, and the time zone

  • Do not write a month, write the endpoints with times and a time zone. July 2026 can be read as starting on the first at midnight or as the thirty days before the request, and those are different sets of rows
  • The workable form looks like from 2026-07-01 00:00:00 UTC to 2026-07-31 23:59:59 UTC: both ends plus the zone, so nothing is left to interpretation
  • System records are frequently stored in coordinated universal time while a reply is rendered in local time, so add a line asking that the time basis of the delivered material be stated. An offset that crosses a day boundary moves a whole interval
  • A wider window is not automatically better. Width invites the response that the volume is large and only part is being supplied. Narrow and unambiguous beats broad and vague
  • Where you need two or more intervals, put each on its own line. Two intervals inside one sentence frequently come back with only one of them processed

4.2 Criterion - which key the lookup runs on

The axis that goes missing even more often than the period is the lookup key. Within the same window, pulling on the account ID, on an internal serial number, on an address or on a session value produces entirely different rows. Leave the key unstated and the recipient picks one, and the one picked is frequently not recorded anywhere in the reply.

  • Name the key. On my account ID, or on the internal serial number mapped to my account - write the key as a key, by its name
  • You can ask for two or more lookups. Adding please deliver the result of each lookup separately keeps the outputs from being merged into one undifferentiated list
  • Where you do not know what keys exist, write please state in the reply which key the lookup was run on. That one sentence is what makes the material interpretable later
  • Asking for a lookup on an address means the result may contain rows belonging to other users of the same connection. The handling that calls for is the masking discussed in section 19
  • Recording in the request that different keys produce different results has a second effect. If the reply later explains that something was pulled on a different basis, that explanation can be placed inside or outside the scope that was asked for

4.3 Items - which fields have to be present

  • Carry the enumerated items across numbered and in the statute's own words. Where the request wording matches the provision wording, there is less room for a reply to treat the item as never having been requested
  • Add the nature of the record. Date and time of access, information on the point of access, information on the data subject processed, and the work performed are the elements Korea's notification names, and they read unambiguously in any regime
  • List authentication records separately. Sign-in success and failure, session creation, renewal and destruction, password changes and privilege changes are each written in a different place
  • If you want change history, write the value before and the value after. Change history on its own can come back as a list of timestamps with no values attached
  • Ask for a table showing, per item, whether it was provided. That turns the reply itself into a checklist against your own request

5. The intake channel is published before you need it

An access request is not a general enquiry that can be sent anywhere. It is a step in a statutory procedure, which means the place that receives it is meant to be identified in advance rather than discovered by asking around.

Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 30 requires a controller to establish and disclose a privacy policy, and lists what it must contain: the processing purpose, the processing and retention period, provision to third parties, destruction procedures, the data subject's rights and how to exercise them, and the name of the privacy officer or the name and contact details of the department that handles privacy work and related grievances. That is the clearest statutory statement of the idea, but the practical point generalises well beyond one jurisdiction: controllers publish contact details, and designate and publish a data protection officer where one is required, so where to send a request is information that exists before you need it. Reading the published policy first is what turns a request into a dated, addressed, traceable act rather than a message into a general queue. The specific provision that imposes the contact-details duty under the GDPR was not verified for this article and is listed in section 21.

5.1 Five lines worth reading in the privacy policy before you write

  • The privacy officer or the responsible department. This goes straight into the addressee line of the request
  • The contact details for grievances. The address and phone number published for privacy matters are what make the receipt provable later
  • How rights are exercised. Whether the intake route is postal, email, or an in-product form is stated here, and using the stated route removes an argument about whether the request was properly made
  • The retention and processing period. This gives you the realistic outer bound when choosing the window to ask about
  • Provision to third parties. Reading this before you write tells you what to expect back on the recipients family in Table 1, and whether to ask for named recipients or categories

Sending to the published channel has a value independent of the answer. It is what allows that the request was received and when it was received to be established from the route itself rather than from anyone's recollection.

6. What falls apart when the request travels off that channel

In practice other routes get used. A general support chat, a direct message to whoever answered first, a phone call. These are usually chosen to move things along, and the intention is fine, but four things blur at once.

  • The processing record. A request arriving on the published channel runs through a structure where intake, review and reply are written down. An informal route sits outside that structure, so what passed between the parties either is not recorded or is recorded on one side only
  • Who is answering. The reason a privacy policy names an officer and a department is to settle in advance who occupies the answering seat. An answer from outside that seat leaves whose answer it is unresolved
  • Where the personal data ended up. An access reply contains personal data by definition. Moving it through an uncontrolled route is itself a further processing operation, and nobody can later say where copies came to rest
  • The start of the clock, and proving it. Statutory deadlines run from receipt - 45 days from a verifiable consumer request under California Civil Code section 1798.130, 10 days from the day the request was received under Korea's framework. Where the date of receipt cannot be established, it cannot be said that the period has started at all

Procedure exists not to make the result arrive faster but to make what happened checkable afterwards. Handling that cannot be checked leaves nothing behind on which to say it was handled.

  • Practically, even where a conversation began informally, sending the same content once more through the published channel is what creates a datable starting point
  • When sending by email, send it in a form that preserves the send time, and where there are attachments, list the filenames and sizes in the body so the enclosure set is fixed in text
  • If guidance came over the phone, write it up and send it back to the same channel as a confirmation request. Even without a reply, the fact that it was sent remains
  • A request made through a representative needs documentation of that authority. Which form is expected is usually stated in the privacy policy or the rights page

7. Deadlines and refusal grounds are fixed by statute

California Civil Code section 1798.130 requires a business to disclose and deliver the required information free of charge within 45 days of receiving a verifiable consumer request, and permits that period to be extended once by an additional 45 days when reasonably necessary, provided the consumer is notified of the extension within the first 45-day period. Two features of that design are worth naming: the clock runs from receipt of a verifiable request, and an extension is an event that must be communicated, not a silence that lengthens by itself.

Korea runs a much shorter clock and splits the obligation in two. Personal Information Protection Act Article 35(3) requires access to be granted within the period prescribed by Presidential Decree, and the Enforcement Decree sets that period at 10 days, while allowing access to be postponed for justifiable cause on notice to the data subject and requiring access without delay once the cause ceases. The Enforcement Decree also requires a refusal or a postponement to be notified within 10 days of receiving the request, together with the reason and the method of objection. So there are two deadlines rather than one: a deadline to grant access, and a deadline to say why access is not being granted. A complete absence of any reply may therefore fall short of the second obligation as well as the first. The GDPR's response-deadline provision was not verified for this article and appears in section 21 rather than being asserted here.

7.1 Refusal grounds are enumerated, not open

Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 35(4) enumerates the cases in which access may be restricted or refused, and the shape of that enumeration is a useful reference point even outside Korea, because it shows what an exhaustive-list design looks like.

  • Where access is prohibited or restricted by statute - the ground has to sit in another law, not in an internal policy
  • Where there is a risk of harm to another person's life or body, or of unjustly infringing another person's property or other interests - the purpose is protection of third parties, which is the same interest GDPR Article 15(4) protects when it says the right to obtain a copy shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others
  • Where a public institution would suffer serious obstruction in performing certain duties - tax assessment, collection or refund; examinations relating to academic credentials, skills or recruitment; the calculation of compensation or benefits; and audits or investigations
  • The exhaustive form is the point. A ground that is not on the list is not a ground under that provision, whatever else it may be
  • Explanations such as internal policy does not allow it, the volume is too large, or the person who handles this is away do not map onto any enumerated item. Whether they might support a postponement is a separate question with its own notice requirement
  • Because a refusal must itself be notified with the reason and the route for objecting, an unexplained refusal and unexplained silence are not two different problems but two faces of the same one
  • The third item concerns public institutions, so a construction in which a private controller relies on it does not readily hold together
  • Partial provision is still an omission, so which part was withheld and why falls inside what must be communicated. That is the hinge that connects to the reasons-for-omission sentence requested up front in section 9

8. An answer to a different question is not compliance

There is a third state that is neither refusal nor silence: a reply arrived, and it is not a reply to what was asked. Formally the procedure has moved, because correspondence exists. Substantively the requested scope is still unanswered, and nothing in the file says so.

8.1 The five substitutions, and what they have in common

  • Period substitution. A calendar month was requested and the last thirty days arrive. The overlap makes it look like an answer, but the earlier part of the requested window is missing
  • Key substitution. A lookup on the account ID was requested and rows pulled on some other key arrive. The row count differs, and the fact that it differs is not stated anywhere
  • Item substitution. Date and time of access, point of access and work performed were requested, and a list containing only timestamps arrives
  • Format substitution. The native record was requested and a screen capture or a rewritten table arrives, with no statement of how it relates to the underlying record
  • Question substitution. What is recorded was asked, and an assurance that there was no problem arrives. An assurance is not a record

These look different and behave identically. In each case the scope the requester set has been reset by the responder, and the resetting is not shown on the face of the reply. That is precisely why the two sentences from section 4 - state which key the lookup used, and state the reason for any item not provided - work as a structural defence rather than a courtesy. With them in the request, a substitution becomes visible as a substitution.

  • In a follow-up, quote the original request verbatim and attach a table mapping each sentence of it to the part of the reply that corresponds, leaving the non-corresponding rows blank
  • Re-request only the unmatched items. Re-sending the whole request tends to produce the whole of the same answer again
  • Record both the original request date and the follow-up date. In any later discussion of deadlines the two dates carry different meanings and should not be collapsed

9. What to demand up front - native format, hash value, reasons for omission

Asking is this the original once the file has arrived is already too late. At that point only the form the other side produced exists, and there is no instrument left for relating that form to the source. Verification has to start inside the request.

If you cannot establish whether what arrived is complete and whether it matches the source, the reply has not reached the state the right of access asks for. The verification conditions are requested before the material, not after it.

9.1 Ask for the native format

  • What you are asking for is the shape the system recorded. A text log as a text log, structured data in its structure, with field names intact
  • A screen capture is not a native record. The crop boundaries and the sort order were the capturing party's choices, and those choices are not visible inside the image
  • Receiving a summary is not itself the problem. Receiving only a summary is. Requesting the summary and the underlying record together lets you see where the summary came from
  • Large exports may be split. Ask for the splitting rule - what the files were divided on - to be stated, so that the set can be checked for completeness
  • Ask for the character encoding and the line-ending convention as well. That is what separates a rendering problem on your machine from a property of the delivered file

9.2 Ask for a hash value alongside the file

A hash value is a fixed-length summary of a file's contents that changes completely if a single character changes. So if the hash at the moment of delivery is written into the correspondence, anyone can recompute it later on the same file and confirm whether this is the file that was delivered then.

# Compute the hash of the file you received and compare it with the value in the reply
# Windows (PowerShell)
Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 .\access_export_2026-07.csv

# macOS / Linux
shasum -a 256 access_export_2026-07.csv

# Sample output (invented value)
# 3f1c9a...  access_export_2026-07.csv
  • Name the algorithm. Something standard and widely available such as SHA-256 means both sides can compute the same value with tools they already have
  • A hash does not establish that the file is genuine. It establishes that it has not changed since. Keeping that distinction straight prevents overclaiming later
  • Its function is to fix the statement this was the content at the time of delivery. If a differently populated file later appears under the same name, the mismatch is what shows it
  • Where there are several files, ask for a value per file. A single hash over one archive says nothing about the identity of the individual files inside it
  • Ask for the values in the body of the reply. A hash that lives only in a separate side file can change along with the file it describes
  • Compute your own value on arrival and record it on your side with the date. Once the same value exists on both sides, that value itself becomes the point of comparison

9.3 Ask for the reason behind anything not provided

Of the three sentences this is the one that changes the most. Without it, silence does the work of an answer. Where five items were requested and three arrive, there is nothing to distinguish between the remaining two being absent because they do not exist, because they were withheld, and because they could not be produced.

  • Keep it short. Where any requested item cannot be provided, please identify that item and state the reason and the provision relied on.
  • The reason for asking for the provision is section 7. Where refusal grounds are enumerated, mapping a stated reason onto a provision immediately shows whether the reason sits inside the enumeration
  • If the reply is that the material does not exist, asking why it does not exist in the same breath leads directly into the discussion in section 14
  • If the reply is that it was destroyed because the retention period expired, you can ask for the date of destruction and the basis for it. Comparing that against the retention period published in the privacy policy is the first step
  • Group the three sentences into one paragraph at the end of the request under a heading such as requests as to the manner of provision, so they do not get mixed into the substantive items and lost

10. How to check that what arrived is intact and original

Once material arrives there is an order to work through. No specialist tooling is needed, and most of it is done by opening the file and looking.

  • Compare the hash. Put the value stated in the reply next to the one you computed. A mismatch means either damage in transit or a different file, and either way it is a question to put in writing
  • File timestamps. Creation and modification times are context, not proof, because copying and transfer change them. But where they diverge sharply from the times written inside the content, that divergence is worth asking about
  • Headers and row counts. For tabular material, check whether the column names in the first row correspond to the items requested. Where a requested item has no column at all, that item did not arrive
  • Sort order. A time series not in time order means it was re-sorted. Re-sorting is not itself a problem, but what processing was applied should be stated
  • Traces of processing. A single column empty throughout, or a string replaced uniformly, indicates the material was worked on. Masking can be entirely proper, which is exactly why it should be written down where it happened
  • Duplicates and gaps. Repeated rows or skipped sequence numbers suggest a merge or a filter step somewhere between the source and the file you hold

What these checks establish is not whether the material is true but whether it explains itself. When the criteria used to extract it and the processing applied to it arrive with it, the material is in an interpretable state. Without that, no volume of rows can be said to show anything in particular.

11. How to tell whether the material was picked for you

Three things are visible without specialist knowledge: continuity, internal consistency, and the gap between what was asked and what came. All three compare the material against itself or against your own request, so no outside knowledge is required.

11.1 Continuity - does the time series break

  • Read down the timestamp column and mark intervals where the spacing is unusually wide. You are the person best placed to say whether that was a time you were inactive or a time you were not
  • Where there is a sequence or serial column, look at where numbers skip. A skipped number may belong to someone else, so it is not a conclusion on its own, but it is a question
  • Check that paired events pair up. Five sign-in records against one sign-out or session-end record raises the question of where the other four terminations were written
  • Where the material stops at a date boundary, suspect a time zone effect first. That is why section 4.1 asks for the time basis up front

11.2 Consistency - do the rules change inside one file

  • A timestamp format that changes partway through can mean material from two different sources was concatenated
  • An identifier format that is an account ID on some rows and an internal number on others means the extraction did not happen in one pass
  • Check whether the masking rule holds. If some addresses are partially hidden and others appear in full, the processing standard is varying row by row
  • A column count that changes mid-file is a merge artefact. Merging is ordinary, but the fact of the merge should be stated
  • Where the same event appears in two files, check whether the two timestamps agree. If they do not, ask which one is the reference

11.3 The gap - requested scope against delivered scope

  • Write the period from your request and the timestamps of the first and last rows delivered side by side. Where both ends sit inside the requested window, that difference is what went unanswered
  • Map the requested item list against the delivered column names in a table. Whatever leaves an empty cell becomes the follow-up request without further work
  • Where a difference exists, check whether the reply explains it. With an explanation, the next discussion is about that explanation; without one, the discussion is still simply that the request was not answered
  • Keeping all three on one page assembles the follow-up document by itself. Four columns are enough: what was asked, what arrived, the difference, and whether it was explained

The single word log covers several records with quite different properties. Deciding what to ask for starts with separating what each of them can tell you.

13. A web access log does not meet the statutory access-record standard

Sometimes the substance behind we do keep records turns out to be a web server access log and nothing else. The two hold different things. An access log records the outward shape of a network request. A statutory access record - taking Korea's notification as the concrete benchmark, because it is the standard that spells the elements out - records an act of processing personal data. The distinction generalises: any regime that expects a controller to be able to say who did what to whose data is asking for the second kind of record, not the first.

A standard access log line looks roughly like this.

# One access log line (invented values). The address is from the RFC 5737 documentation range.
203.0.113.41 - - [14/Jul/2026:09:12:03 +0000] "GET /account/profile HTTP/1.1" 200 5312 "-" "Mozilla/5.0 ..."

# What is here: point of access - time - method - path - status code - response size - referrer - user agent
# What is not:  which account the request was authenticated as - the request body - which data it read or changed

  • That observation is not itself a conclusion that anything was infringed. The missing elements may be carried in another system. If they are, though, where that other record lives is what the reply needs to say
  • Listing the five elements verbatim in the request means that when only an access log comes back, what is missing is visible as a table rather than as a suspicion
  • We have logs and we hold access records containing the specified elements are two different sentences. The follow-up should ask the second one
  • Configuring an access log to also record the authenticated account is technically ordinary. So an answer that it is not there is a statement about a configuration choice, not about an impossibility

14. There is no such thing as selective logging

Logging is not a decision a person makes per event. It is an automatic behaviour that runs as a request passes through. Whether something is recorded is settled once, at configuration time, and then applied unconditionally. One consequence follows directly: a design in which one particular user's one particular action alone goes unrecorded does not arise in an ordinary deployment.

14.1 Four reasons an interval goes empty, and what each leaves behind

  • Service outage. If the process died or restarted, requests in that interval were not served at all. The traces are error logs, process restart records, gaps in monitoring metrics, incident alert histories, and above all the fact that other users' records for the same interval are missing too
  • Rotation or collection pipeline failure. Loss can occur when log files are periodically rotated and archived, or in a central collection stage. The traces are the rotation configuration and file listing, error records from the collection agent, and discontinuities in file sizes
  • Exhausted storage. When a disk fills, writing stops. The traces are disk usage graphs, capacity warnings, and the circumstance that other logs stopped at the same moment
  • Deliberate deletion. Unlike the first three, this is an act whose purpose is to leave no trace elsewhere. It is therefore what remains as a possibility when the first three are not each accounted for by their own traces

The first three causes leave marks in other records by their nature. That is why the sentence there are no logs for that interval is not self-contained: it calls for the follow-up material that explains why.

  • Having established that an interval is empty, the thing to ask for is not confirmation that it is empty but the reason and the other records that support that reason
  • Asking whether a different kind of log exists for the same interval is where cross-checking starts. It works because the five kinds in section 12 live in different systems
  • If the reply is that the feature is configured not to log, the next questions are since when and whether that configuration change is itself recorded
  • If the reply is that the retention period expired, compare it against the retention period published in the privacy policy and against the retention standard discussed in section 16
  • None of this section is aimed at asserting a conclusion. Its purpose is to produce a list of answerable questions; the assessment belongs to the competent authority and the courts

15. Whether a claim that everything was normal can be checked

A particular shape recurs in access replies: a question about records answered with a statement about a state. The session was maintained normally throughout the period. There was no problem with access controls. The sentences are definite, and nothing arrives with them by which the definiteness could be checked.

The principle from the introduction applies without modification here.

A record is the means by which a period is shown to have been normal. So the statement that no record exists and the statement that the period was normal have no room to stand together.

15.1 Turning an assurance back into a request for records

  • The session was maintained normally to a request for the authentication log carrying session creation, renewal and destruction times. Normal maintenance is visible only as the arrangement of those three
  • There was no problem with access control to a request for the authorisation failure and success records for the period. That there were no failures also has to be shown by a record
  • Nobody else accessed the account to a request for the complete access records for your account, including the point-of-access column. Showing an absence is also the work of a record
  • That feature does not behave that way to a request for the execution history of that feature and the records of its outcomes. A design description does not stand in for evidence of what ran
  • Our internal check found nothing unusual to a request for the output of the check itself: what was queried, on what criteria, and the resulting material
  • The five conversions share one move. They replace evaluative words with record words. Normal, no problem and nothing unusual are judgements, and a judgement can only be examined when its basis travels with it
  • If the records genuinely do not exist, the honest reply is they do not exist. That is itself information, and it leads into the questions in section 14
  • It exists but is not being provided and it does not exist are entirely different states. The reasons-for-omission sentence from section 9.3 is what separates them
  • An assurance about a state with no record behind it cannot be examined at all, whether it is true or false, because there is no material to examine
  • This is where the rights axis meets the technical one. In a system with weak controls there is a further problem - the record may exist and still not identify the actor - and that is set out in the article on trust boundaries and web security controls

16. Tamper protection and how long records are kept

That a record exists and that a record can be relied on are separate questions. Korea's notification on standards for ensuring the safety of personal information addresses both at once: how long the record is kept and whether it stays unchanged over that period. It is cited here as a Korean standard, but as a benchmark it travels, because any regime that treats logs as evidence has to answer the same two questions.

The notification defines an access record as an electronic record, of the work performed by a person who accesses a personal information processing system, containing the identifier, the date and time of access, information on the point of access, information on the data subject processed, and the work performed. Having the elements inside the definition is what makes it practically useful: a requester can know what ought to be present without having to be told by the party answering.

16.1 Retention, review, and what each implies

  • The notification sets the baseline at at least 1 year of retention and management for access records
  • At least 2 years applies to systems processing the personal information of 50,000 or more data subjects, systems processing unique identifying information or sensitive information, and telecommunications business operators
  • The notification requires access records to be reviewed at least once a month, and where a download of personal information is identified, the reason for it must be confirmed
  • The notification requires measures to retain access records safely so that they are not forged, altered, stolen or lost
  • Retention and review sitting together is what makes this useful in practice. If reviews are running, the reviews have outputs, and outputs are material that can be asked about
  • Where a reply says the period expired and the records were destroyed, you can ask whether the applicable period was one year or two and what drives that classification, since it turns on the nature of the data processed
  • If something was destroyed, there is a date of destruction. Whether the request fell inside or outside the retention period is decided there
  • The concrete form of tamper protection varies by deployment. Asking which method is used is itself a question that sets the range within which the answers can be relied on
  • The hash request in section 9.2 has the same character as this. The difference is direction: that one concerns immutability after delivery, this one concerns immutability during retention

17. When an IP address is personal data and when it is not

The argument about addresses is usually posed as is an IP address personal data, and none of the major regimes answers that question in that form. They look at the situation rather than the category of data, which is why the same string can be personal data in one set of hands and not in another.

GDPR Recital 30 records that natural persons may be associated with online identifiers provided by their devices, applications, tools and protocols, such as internet protocol addresses, cookie identifiers or other identifiers such as radio frequency identification tags, and that this may leave traces which, in particular when combined with unique identifiers and other information received by the servers, may be used to create profiles of the natural persons and identify them. Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 2(1) states the same test as an operational rule: information that cannot identify a specific individual on its own is still personal information where it can be easily combined with other information to identify that individual, and whether combination is easy is judged by reasonably considering the availability of the other information, and the time, cost and technology required.

17.1 The test asks whose hands the data is in

  • The same address string is assessed differently depending on what else the holder holds alongside it
  • An address inside a log that already stores the account ID and the access time is already attached to a specific account at that location. No separate combination step is needed
  • An address written on its own with no context, held by someone with no means of reaching subscriber information, sits at the other end of the same scale
  • The factors are availability, time, cost and technology. The question is not is it theoretically possible but what does a reasonable assessment show
  • So both an IP address is personal data and an IP address is not personal data are incomplete sentences on their own. Whose address, held in what context is the part that is missing
  • In an access context the implication is direct. Where a controller can query its own logs for addresses on a given account key, those addresses are, in that controller's hands, already joined to the account

18. The regimes side by side - GDPR, the Breyer ruling and the rest

For a reader in the European Union or the United States, the identification test is home ground rather than comparative material, and its clearest judicial statement comes from a single ruling of the Court of Justice of the European Union.

18.1 What Case C-582/14 settled

The Court of Justice of the European Union held in Case C-582/14 that a dynamic IP address registered by an online media services provider when a person accesses a website that the provider makes accessible to the public constitutes personal data in relation to that provider, where the latter has the legal means which enable it to identify the data subject with additional data which the internet service provider has about that person.

  • The core of it is that not all of the information has to sit in one pair of hands. The question is whether identification can be reached with the help of another party
  • The standard applied is means likely reasonably to be used either by the controller or by any other person, which is where the assessment gets its shape
  • The Court set the limit in the same breath. Identification that is prohibited by law, or practically impossible on account of the fact that it requires a disproportionate effort in terms of time, cost and man-power, so that the risk of identification appears in reality to be insignificant, falls outside
  • Korea's formulation - reasonably considering availability and the time, cost and technology required - is structurally the same test. The wording differs and the question asked does not
  • The ruling concerned the definition in the earlier data protection directive, and the General Data Protection Regulation now applies, but the structure of the identifiability assessment carries through

18.2 The GDPR right of access, item by item

Read next to the ruling, Recital 30 supplies the classification and Article 15 supplies the entitlement. Recital 30 names internet protocol addresses among the online identifiers a natural person may be associated with; Article 15 sets out what a controller has to hand over once that data is in scope.

  • Article 15(1) gives the right to obtain confirmation as to whether personal data concerning the data subject are being processed and, where that is the case, access to that data
  • The same paragraph enumerates what comes with it: the purposes of the processing, the categories of personal data concerned, the recipients or categories of recipient, and the envisaged storage period or, if that is not possible, the criteria used to determine it
  • It continues with the right to request rectification, erasure or restriction of processing, the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, any available information as to the source where the data were not collected from the data subject, and meaningful information about the logic involved in automated decision-making including profiling
  • Article 15(2) adds, where data are transferred to a third country, the right to be informed of the appropriate safeguards; Article 15(3) requires the controller to provide a copy of the personal data undergoing processing, with the first copy free of charge
  • Article 15(4) provides that the right to obtain a copy shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others. That provision connects directly to the masking discussion in section 19
  • The overlap with the other regimes is substantial. Purposes, categories, recipients and storage period point at the same four things whether they are read out of Article 15(1), out of California Civil Code section 1798.110, or out of Korea's Enforcement Decree Article 41(1)

19. The argument that fails on both branches, and masking third parties

One shape of reply is structurally interesting: an IP address is not personal data, so it cannot be released. Before it is a question of fact it is a question of argument structure, because the conclusion is lost on either reading of its own premise.

Where a position loses its conclusion on every available premise, it has stopped working as an argument before any fact needs checking.

  • Branch one: the address is not personal data. The restrictions on disclosure in data protection law attach to personal data. If this is not personal data, those restrictions do not attach to it, and the stated ground for withholding it disappears
  • Branch two: the address is personal data. Then it falls inside the right of access, so the part concerning you is within scope. And at the same time, other people's addresses left unmasked in the reply raise a separate question of their own
  • So on branch one there is no basis for refusal, and on branch two your rows must be provided and other people's rows must be protected. Neither branch yields therefore nothing can be provided
  • The move generalises. Split the premise a position depends on into its available readings and check whether the conclusion survives each one - that is the whole technique
  • That the two branches fail differently is itself informative. On one the basis vanishes; on the other an additional obligation appears
  • Sometimes the premise moves within a single document, treated as personal data in one passage and not in another. Where that happens, two sentences in the same reply contradict each other, and the contradiction is visible without any external material

19.1 When third-party information is mixed in

Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 17(1) permits a controller to provide personal information to a third party where the data subject has consented, or in other enumerated cases. An access reply is a movement of information, so it does not sit outside that rule. GDPR Article 15(4) states the same interest from the other side: the right to obtain a copy shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others. Between them they explain why other people's data appearing in your reply creates a distinct problem rather than a bonus.

  • A lookup keyed on an address can pull in rows belonging to other users of the same connection. On shared connections that is ordinary rather than exceptional
  • The handling that calls for is masking of third-party identifying values: keep the rows that concern you, obscure the values that identify anyone else
  • Where masking was applied, it should say so. Without that note a requester cannot tell an obscured value from a missing one
  • Where the masking rule varies row by row it becomes the inconsistency described in section 11.2. One rule should hold across one file
  • GDPR Article 15(4) is doing exactly this work when it limits the copy right by reference to the rights and freedoms of others. It stands your right and third-party protection up together rather than trading one against the other
  • So the practical form of the request becomes this: provide all rows concerning me, mask third-party identifying values, and state that masking was applied and the rule used
  • Written that way, the reply that third-party data is mixed in so nothing can be provided has much less room to operate, because the request already resolves the problem it raises

20. If you are refused, and why operators want the same records

When a request is refused or goes unanswered, the next steps fall into three broad kinds. This article does not recommend a choice among them; it sets out what each mechanism is for.

  • Objection. Where a regime requires a refusal or postponement notice to state the method of objection, that method is written on the notice itself. It is the route that takes the matter through the same channel once more
  • Dispute mediation. Korea's Personal Information Protection Act establishes a mechanism for mediating personal data disputes, which attempts a resolution between the parties rather than an adjudication
  • Infringement reporting. A separate route exists for reporting personal data infringements. Its purpose and its handling differ from mediation, and the two are not interchangeable
  • On any route, the record is the material. When and where what was requested, and what came back, has to exist in writing. That is why sections 5 and 6 matter practically rather than formally
  • Follow-ups and objections should quote the original request verbatim. The document has to show that the request did not change, or the discussion drifts
  • How a particular set of facts is assessed depends on those facts, and the assessment rests with the competent authority and the courts. This article does not predict it

20.1 On the operator's side, proper records are the cheapest defence

None of this is useful only to data subjects. Seen from the service side, having the records in place is the least expensive defensive position available.

  • With the five access-record elements in place, an incoming request means the answer is the material. Nothing has to be reconstructed or characterised after the fact
  • Without records there is also no way to show that something did not happen. Demonstrating an absence is equally the work of a record, so the asymmetry runs both ways
  • A deployment running on access logs alone sits at roughly half the elements in the comparison in section 13. Recording the account and the object of processing alongside is usually a matter of configuration and a few lines of code
  • A monthly review requirement looks like overhead until a request arrives. Where reviews are already running, their outputs are the answer material
  • Turning access-request handling into a defined internal procedure converts a 10-day or 45-day deadline from pressure into a schedule. Checking that the published intake channel actually works is the first step
  • That the evidential weight of a record depends on the system's controls is a separate subject. Trust boundaries and web security controls covers that part

Frequently asked questions

Does a data access request get me my own access logs?

The right reaches all of the personal data processed about you, not a file that happens to carry the word log in its name. GDPR Article 15(1) gives the right to obtain confirmation as to whether personal data concerning you are being processed and, where that is the case, access to that data, and Article 15(3) requires a copy of the personal data undergoing processing with the first copy free of charge. California Civil Code section 1798.110 lists the specific pieces of personal information collected among the required disclosures. Access records fall inside those descriptions to the extent they hold data about you. Korea supplies the clearest anchor for the log itself: Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 names retaining access records among the required safety measures, and the notification on standards for ensuring the safety of personal information defines an access record as containing the identifier, the date and time of access, information on the point of access, information on the data subject processed, and the work performed. So the request is sharper when it describes the nature of the record rather than a filename: records concerning me that include the date and time of access, information on the point of access and the work performed, for the period from 2026-07-01 00:00:00 UTC to 2026-07-31 23:59:59 UTC, queried on my account ID.

How long does an access request take, and what if there is no reply?

It depends on the regime. California Civil Code section 1798.130 requires disclosure and delivery free of charge within 45 days of receiving a verifiable consumer request, extendable once by an additional 45 days where reasonably necessary, provided the consumer is notified within the first 45-day period. Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 35(3) requires access within the period prescribed by Presidential Decree, and the Enforcement Decree sets that at 10 days, with a refusal or postponement also having to be notified within 10 days together with the reason and the method of objection. That produces two obligations rather than one: a deadline to grant access, and a deadline to explain why access is not being granted, so silence can fall short of both. Because every clock starts at receipt, sending to the published intake channel and keeping the acknowledgement is what fixes the starting point. After the deadline, the objection route named on the notice, the dispute mediation mechanism and the infringement reporting route are the next steps. The GDPR response deadline was not verified for this article and is listed among the unconfirmed items. Assessment of any particular case rests with the competent authority and the courts.

I was told an IP address is not personal data and cannot be released. Is that right?

Read on either branch, the position loses its own conclusion. If the address is not personal data, the disclosure restrictions in data protection law do not attach to it, so the stated ground for withholding disappears. If it is personal data, it falls inside the right of access, so your own rows are in scope, and at the same time other people's addresses left unmasked in the reply raise a separate question - Korea's Personal Information Protection Act Article 17(1) allows provision to a third party only on consent or another enumerated basis, and GDPR Article 15(4) states that the right to obtain a copy shall not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others. On classification, GDPR Recital 30 names internet protocol addresses among the online identifiers a natural person may be associated with, and the Court of Justice of the European Union held in Case C-582/14 that a dynamic IP address constitutes personal data in relation to an online media services provider that has the legal means to identify the data subject with additional data held by the internet service provider, applying the standard of means likely reasonably to be used. Korea's Article 2(1) frames the same test as easy combination, judged by reasonably considering availability, time, cost and technology. In a log that already stores the account ID and the access time, the address is already attached to an account.

How can I tell whether the material I received is everything?

Checking after delivery is late. Verification starts with three sentences in the request. First, provide the material in the native format the system recorded. Second, compute a hash of each delivered file with a standard algorithm such as SHA-256 and state the values in the body of the reply. Third, where an item cannot be provided, identify it and state the reason and the provision relied on. The third matters most: without it, silence does the work of an answer, and a missing item cannot be distinguished between does not exist, was withheld, and could not be produced. Once material arrives, three checks need no specialist knowledge: whether the time series has unexplained gaps, whether conventions such as timestamp format and identifier format stay consistent inside the same file, and whether any difference between the scope requested and the scope delivered is explained in writing. Laying those out as four columns - what was asked, what arrived, the difference, whether it was explained - assembles the follow-up document by itself.

I was told there are no logs for that period. Is that possible?

Logging is an automatic per-request behaviour configured once and then applied unconditionally, so a design in which one user's one action alone goes unrecorded does not arise in an ordinary deployment. When a whole interval is empty, the causes are generally four: a service outage, a rotation or collection pipeline failure, exhausted storage, and deliberate deletion. The first three leave marks elsewhere by their nature - error logs, gaps in monitoring metrics, alert histories, disk usage graphs, deployment records - and above all other users' records for the same interval should be missing too. So the sentence there are no logs for that interval is not self-contained; it calls for the follow-up material explaining why. One more thing follows. Saying no record exists while also saying the interval was normal cannot stand, because a record is the means by which normality is shown. What to ask for is therefore not confirmation of the absence but the reason and the other records that support that reason. How any particular set of facts is assessed remains a matter for the competent authority and the courts.

Conclusion - four propositions

Four propositions instead of a summary. The section numbers after each one are where the support sits.

  • Proposition 1. The right of access reaches personal data, not files. GDPR Article 15(1) fixes the object as personal data concerning the data subject, Article 15(3) adds the copy, California Civil Code section 1798.110 lists the specific pieces along with categories, sources, purposes and third parties, and Korea's Enforcement Decree Article 41(1) enumerates five items. So a request should carry the nature of the information and the enumerated items, not a filename. → section 1, section 2
  • Proposition 2. A request without a scope is a request whose scope the recipient sets. Fix the period, the key and the items, and require the reply to state which key the lookup used, so that when a substitution happens the substitution itself becomes visible. → section 4, section 8
  • Proposition 3. The verification conditions are requested before the material. Native format, a hash value, and a stated reason for anything not provided are what make it possible to say what the delivered material is. Asking after it arrives is too late. → section 9, section 10, section 11
  • Proposition 4. You cannot say a period was normal without a record of it. A record is the means by which normality is shown, so the two statements have no room to stand together. By the same structure, an IP address is not personal data so it cannot be released loses its conclusion on both branches. → section 14, section 15, section 19
