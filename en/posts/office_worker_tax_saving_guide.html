Korea Year-End Tax Settlement: A Practical Saving Guide for Salaried Workers

What you decide in July moves your refund more than what you file in January

Jul 28, 2026 Finance & Economy
Before you read — This article is general information and not tax advice. It describes the South Korean year-end tax settlement system only, and does not apply to any other country. Korean tax law is revised every year and contains many temporary measures, so the figures here are valid as of July 2026 only. Results vary widely with income structure, family status and housing situation. For your specific case, consult a licensed tax professional or the National Tax Service counselling centre (126, Korean-language service; English guidance is available on the NTS English website). This article does not describe how to claim deductions whose requirements are not met.

Introduction: the settlement is not a January task

Every January, salaried workers in Korea log into Hometax, download their simplified statements, and scramble for the receipts that did not come through. Yet at that point the only thing left to do is report accurately what has already happened. The decisions that actually change the size of the refund — which card to pay with, how much to contribute to a pension account, when to register a change of address — were all settled months earlier.

So this article inverts the usual order. It is not a January filing manual but a July-to-31-December order of operations. We are a little past the halfway mark of the year, five months remain, and far more can be done in those five months than in the whole of January.

The scope is deliberately narrow. There is no primer on what income tax is or why the state collects it (that sits in our guide to how taxes are structured), and no walkthrough of filing dates and procedures (see the year-end settlement procedure guide). Product structure and management of pension savings and IRP accounts are covered separately in the pension account tax-saving article. Instead this piece concentrates on the current caps and rates by item, how much a single extra 10,000 won actually returns at each income level, what only mid-year action can fix, which data the simplified service misses, how dual-income couples should split claims, and the request-for-correction procedure that reopens the past five years.

Every figure below was taken from primary Korean sources: the National Tax Service, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and the National Law Information Center. Where a value could not be confirmed at the primary source, no number is printed and the text says confirmation with the NTS is required. In tax writing, the most dangerous move is to print last year's number as if it were this year's.

1. Deduction versus credit: why the same 1 million won is not the same money

The first thing to establish about any Korean tax break is which stage of the calculation it attaches to. Employment income tax is computed roughly in this order.

  1. Subtract the earned income deduction from total salary to get employment income
  2. Subtract personal deductions, pension premium deductions, special income deductions and other income deductions to get the tax base
  3. Apply the rate schedule to the tax base to get computed tax
  4. Subtract tax credits and tax reductions to get determined tax
  5. Compare determined tax with tax already withheld, then refund or collect the difference

Income deductions act at step 2; tax credits act at step 4. Because their positions differ, so does the character of their effect.

1.1 Income deductions scale with your own rate

A 1 million won income deduction lowers the tax base by 1 million won. What you save is whatever tax that slice would have attracted, so the saving is directly proportional to your marginal rate. A junior employee with a tax base of 14 million won or less saves 60,000 won (66,000 won including local income tax), while someone above the 88 million won tax base saves 350,000 won (385,000 won including local income tax). The identical item is worth nearly six times as much to one person as to another.

The main income deductions are credit card and equivalent spending, housing subscription savings, and principal-and-interest repayment on a housing lease loan. The basic personal deduction of 1.5 million won per dependant is also an income deduction.

1.2 Tax credits return a fixed ratio regardless of income

A tax credit is subtracted straight from computed tax. For a pension account credit, 15% or 12% of the contribution comes off the tax bill regardless of income level. Local income tax is assessed on the determined national income tax, so when a credit reduces determined tax the local tax falls with it. As a rule of thumb the real-world effect is about 1.1 times the headline credit rate.

Credits carry one decisive limitation. Once determined tax reaches zero, there is nothing left to return. For a low-income worker whose determined tax is already zero after the earned income credit, child credit and standard credit alone, no amount of pension contribution creates a refund at settlement time. This point returns later in the discussion of dual-income couples.

1.3 Items with a threshold and items without

The third axis is whether a minimum spending threshold exists.

  • Credit card and equivalent spending: only the amount above 25% of total salary is eligible. Spending below the threshold is irrelevant to the deduction
  • Medical expenses: only the amount above 3% of total salary is eligible
  • Pension accounts, protection-type insurance premiums, education, donations and monthly rent: no threshold. The rate applies from the first won

Thresholds make an enormous practical difference. Spending more on an item you have not yet cleared the threshold for has a return of zero in tax terms, while an item already past its threshold gives you the full rate on the very next won. Knowing which side of the threshold you sit on therefore matters more than memorising caps.

One change in the table deserves particular attention. The credit card deduction was amended on 23 December 2025 (Act No. 21223) so that its sunset moves to 31 December 2028 and the base cap now rises with the number of dependent children. The amended provision took effect on 1 January 2026. Exactly which tax year's spending first reflects the higher cap depends on individual circumstances, so anyone planning to use the cap in full should re-check current NTS guidance.

Something also disappeared. The provision granting an extra deduction for spending growth over the previous year has always been designed as a temporary measure attached to a specific year. The growth clause still visible in the statute targets 2024 spending, and provisions of this kind are created and retired annually, so you cannot assume a special measure described in last year's article still exists. The 40% rate on donations above 30 million won, which ended with the 2024 tax year, is the same story.

Credit rates alone convey very little. This section computes how many won of tax reduction a single extra 10,000 won of spending produces.

What matters here is not the individual figures but the order of magnitude between them.

  • Card spending is not a tax-saving instrument. Even in the 50 to 88 million won tax base band, an extra 10,000 won on a credit card saves 396 won and on a debit card 792 won. Not spending the 10,000 won leaves you with 10,000 won; spending it returns roughly 8%. Card deductions are a question of how to pay for money you were going to spend anyway, never a reason to spend more
  • Credit items dominate on efficiency. Pension accounts, medical expenses and rent return 1,300 to 1,900 won per 10,000 won almost regardless of income band — two to four times a debit card
  • The lower your income the more credits matter; the higher your income the more deductions catch up. At a tax base of 14 million won or less a 40% deduction item yields 264 won while a pension account yields 1,650 won. Above the 88 million won base, the 40% traditional market item reaches 1,540 won and overtakes the pension account's 1,320 won
  • The 30% infertility treatment rate is the highest credit band an individual can reach. For households in that situation, securing the evidence deserves a high priority
  • All of the above is a marginal effect that holds only while headroom remains. If you have already contributed 9 million won to pension accounts, the 9,000,001st won is worth zero
  • The columns are keyed to the tax base, but pension accounts and rent set their rates on total salary. The two do not map one to one, so re-check pension accounts against a total salary of 55 million won and rent against 55 and 80 million won

3.3 A worked example

Assume a single-person household with a total salary of 55 million won, a tax base of roughly 28 million won (the 15% band) and no dependants. Suppose this person acts as follows from July through December.

  • Contributes 500,000 won a month to pension savings for six months, 3 million won in total → 15% applies because total salary is 55 million won or less, saving about 495,000 won including local income tax
  • Pays 600,000 won of monthly rent for six months while meeting all requirements (7.2 million won on an annual basis) → 17% applies at that salary level, saving about 1,346,000 won on the annual figure
  • Having already cleared the card threshold, routes 2 million won of second-half spending to a debit card → deduction of 600,000 won at a 16.5% marginal rate, saving about 99,000 won

The difference in character is stark. Switching payment method is worth about 100,000 won, funding a pension account about 500,000 won, and satisfying the rent requirements over 1.3 million won. That single comparison decides where your attention should go. These values hold only under the stated assumptions; if determined tax is small or other deductions are already large, the actual saving will be lower.

4.1 The card threshold and your fourth quarter

The National Tax Service opens a year-end settlement preview on Hometax around October each year. It pulls in January-to-September card spending and last year's deduction data, estimates the tax, and lets you type in expected October-to-December spending to simulate the outcome. Ultimately it tells you two things.

  • Have you cleared the 25% of total salary threshold? If not, changing your payment method in the fourth quarter achieves nothing. Until the threshold is crossed, every card produces the same result
  • Have you already used up the base cap? If so, switching methods again achieves nothing. From that point only the additional-cap categories such as traditional markets and public transport matter

In other words, the window in which use a debit card is sound advice is narrower than people assume. It holds only when the threshold has been cleared and headroom remains. Rearranging payment methods without checking is close to wasted effort.

4.2 Why pension contributions belong to now, not December

The pension account credit is judged on the date of contribution, so a single deposit on 31 December produces exactly the same credit. In practice, though, many people fail to fill the cap because they cannot find 9 million won in December. Starting monthly instalments now, with five months left, reaches the same target with far less strain.

That said, a pension account is a long-horizon retirement vehicle. Early withdrawal can carry tax disadvantages, so money you will need soon does not belong there. This article does not recommend any specific financial institution or product; product selection and investment approach depend on your own funding plan and risk tolerance.

5. Checklist of data the simplified service misses

The simplified service opens on 15 January each year and gathers most deduction data automatically. But not everything arrives on its own. The following items recur in National Tax Service guidance and counselling cases as uncollected or partly collected.

  • Prescription eyeglasses and contact lenses — some retailers do not file the data. The receipt must identify the user and confirm the corrective purpose
  • School uniforms and gym kit for middle and high school students — obtain a separate receipt from the retailer
  • Academy and sports facility fees for preschool children — request an education expense payment certificate from the provider
  • Assistive devices and hearing aids for people with disabilities, purchased or rented — a vendor receipt is needed
  • Donations to some religious bodies and small organisations — request the donation receipt directly
  • Monthly rent evidence — submit a copy of the lease and bank transfer records yourself
  • Part of postpartum care centre costs — it is safer to keep the receipt from the centre
  • Children's overseas education expenses — not domestic data, therefore not collected
  • Records relating to parents living elsewhere — nothing appears without completing the data-sharing consent

The reverse trap also exists: appearing in the simplified data does not make an amount deductible. Medical costs reimbursed by indemnity insurance, and amounts refunded later under the out-of-pocket ceiling scheme, were not borne by you and must be removed from the medical total. The simplified statement is a starting point, not a deductible amount.

One practical note: the file downloaded on opening day, 15 January, can be incomplete because some institutions file late. Unless your employer's deadline is tight, re-checking a few days later reduces the risk of omission.

6. Splitting claims between working spouses: one rule and three exceptions

The most common question in dual-income households is whose return a dependant should go on. Start with the part that is not negotiable.

Claiming the same dependant on both spouses' returns is not permitted. Nor can siblings each claim the same parent. The National Tax Service displays a duplicate-claim warning during the simplified stage and, when a review finds one, collects the tax with penalty tax attached.

6.1 The default rule: the higher marginal rate

As shown earlier, an income deduction saves in proportion to the marginal rate. Therefore the same income deduction saves more when attached to the spouse in the higher band. A basic deduction of 1.5 million won is worth about 578,000 won including local tax to a spouse above the 88 million won tax base, and about 99,000 won to one at 14 million won or less.

6.2 Exception 1: threshold items can favour the lower earner

Medical expenses count only above 3% of total salary. A spouse earning 80 million won must clear 2.4 million won; one earning 30 million won needs only 900,000 won. If the family's medical spending totals 2 million won, the higher earner's eligible amount is zero while the lower earner's is 1.1 million won. Card spending has the same structure at 25% of total salary.

There is a constraint, however. To claim a dependant's medical costs you must be the spouse who took that person's basic deduction. Medical expenses cannot be peeled off and handed to the other spouse. Splitting therefore has to be evaluated as whole-household combinations rather than item by item.

6.3 Exception 2: loading a spouse with zero determined tax achieves nothing

Credits only help if there is tax to remove. If one spouse's determined tax is already zero, piling credits there does not raise the refund by a single won. That is why each spouse's determined tax is the first number to check in any split simulation.

6.4 Exception 3: loading the lower cap gets you cut off at the cap

The card base cap is 3 million won for total salary of 70 million won or less and 2.5 million won above. A lower-earning spouse clears the threshold more easily but also runs into the lower cap. When household card spending is large, splitting to use both caps can be the better answer. The outcome turns on amounts, so no general rule settles it.

The National Tax Service publishes tax-saving guidance for dual-income workers on Hometax. Once both spouses consent to data sharing, it compares who should claim dependants, education, donations and card spending. Running the combinations through that tool is faster and more accurate than trying to compute the answer by hand.

Dependant eligibility is worth re-checking too. To qualify for the basic deduction, annual income must be 1 million won or less (or total salary of 5 million won or less if employment income is the only source). If a parent realised even a modest business or capital gain, they may fall outside the requirement, and claiming without checking becomes the over-claim discussed next.

7. Ten items that are easy to miss

  1. Income tax reduction for SME employees — RSTA article 30. A young worker joining an SME receives a 90% reduction of income tax for five years from the hire date, capped at 2 million won per tax year. The provision applies to hires through 31 December 2026. Rates and periods differ for other categories such as those aged 60 and over, people with disabilities and women returning after a career break, so confirm with the NTS. Nothing applies unless the application form is filed with your employer
  2. Combining income after a job change — without it the tax is computed incorrectly and a May comprehensive income filing becomes mandatory
  3. Basic deduction for parents living elsewhere — living apart does not disqualify you if you genuinely support them and no sibling is claiming them. Coordination among siblings is a precondition
  4. The tax definition of disability — it is not limited to those registered under the Act on Welfare of Persons with Disabilities. Patients requiring constant treatment for a serious illness can obtain a disability certificate from a medical institution, which brings both the additional deduction and the removal of the medical expense cap
  5. The housing subscription no-home certificate — opening the account and never filing the certificate is a recurring cause of lost deductions
  6. Timing of the move-in registration — because the lease address must match the registration, a late filing removes that entire period
  7. Postpartum care centre costs — included within medical expenses up to 2 million won per birth, and often absent from the simplified data
  8. Hometown love donations — the first 100,000 won receives a 100/110 credit, so almost all of it comes back, and the return gift raises the effective value further
  9. The marriage credit — 500,000 won once in a lifetime for registrations from 2024 to 2026. It turns on the registration date, so it is not a December decision
  10. Repayment of your own student loan — the taxpayer's own education expenses carry a 15% credit with no cap, and student loan repayments count here

Foreign employees in Korea have one additional route to consider. Article 18-2 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act allows a 19% flat tax election on employment income for up to 20 years from the first day of work in Korea. The trade-off is significant: electing the flat rate means forgoing most income tax exemptions, deductions and credits, which is precisely what this article is about. Someone with large medical, rent, pension or dependant claims can easily be worse off under the flat rate, so the two outcomes should be compared numerically before electing. Non-resident status, tax treaty relief and foreign tax credits raise further questions that belong with a tax professional or the NTS.

8. Where saving ends and over-claiming begins

This may be the most important section here. Claiming every deduction whose requirements you satisfy is tax saving; claiming one whose requirements you do not satisfy is an over-claim. The latter always gets settled eventually.

8.1 The tax office reviews afterwards, by computer

After the settlement season closes, the National Tax Service extracts suspected over-claims through data analysis and reviews them. Reported cases indicate tens of thousands of workers reviewed in a single year. The patterns flagged most often are these.

  • Duplicate claims — both spouses claiming the same child, or siblings each claiming the same parent
  • Claiming a dependant whose annual income exceeded 1 million won
  • Continuing to claim a dependant who has died
  • Claiming the rent credit at an address where the taxpayer does not actually live
  • Claiming a donation credit on a receipt for money that was never donated
  • Including a sibling's card spending in one's own claim — siblings are outside the card deduction

Read this list as patterns to avoid. This article does not explain how to do any of them, and could not. In particular, a receipt for a donation that never happened, or a rent claim on a property where the taxpayer does not live, is not treated as a simple mistake.

8.2 Getting caught costs more than the principal

When an over-claim is confirmed, paying the underpaid tax is not the end of it. Under-reporting penalty tax and late payment penalty tax are added. On National Tax Service guidance, the late payment penalty accrues at 22 per 100,000 per day on the unpaid amount for the number of days elapsed. Annualised that is around 8%, and the longer detection takes the larger it grows.

The under-reporting penalty rate differs sharply depending on whether the under-reporting was ordinary or achieved by improper means, with a much higher rate applying in the latter case. Because the exact bands turn on the category of the case, no figure is asserted here. If you need the number, ask the National Tax Service counselling centre (126) about your own situation.

8.3 Fixing it yourself reduces the burden

If you realise a deduction was wrongly claimed, correcting it voluntarily by the May comprehensive income tax deadline of the following year is the best available move. The National Tax Service states that correcting within that window can reduce the penalty burden. You can correct a settlement your employer processed.

To summarise: saving is the work of checking requirements and securing evidence, and over-claiming begins where that checking is skipped. When an item's eligibility is ambiguous, verifying first is always cheaper than claiming and worrying later.

9. Past settlements can be reopened for five years

If you discover a missed deduction after the fact, two routes exist.

9.1 The May comprehensive income tax return

You can reflect the omission when filing a comprehensive income tax return in May of the following year. Someone with employment income only has no filing obligation, but filing voluntarily to add a missed deduction is permitted.

9.2 Request for correction: a five-year window

If May has also passed, there is the request for correction. Article 45-2 of the Framework Act on National Taxes provides that a person who filed a tax base return by the statutory deadline may ask the head of the competent tax office for a determination or correction within five years of that deadline. The same article lists omissions arising during a withholding agent's settlement as grounds. The year-end settlement is exactly such a settlement.

In practice the sequence runs as follows.

  1. Identify the missed item and the tax year it belongs to
  2. Obtain that year's employment income withholding receipt (available on Hometax)
  3. Gather evidence for the missed item — often this means asking institutions to reissue old records
  4. File the request for correction through Hometax or at the tax office
  5. The tax office reviews, notifies the result, and refunds if the claim is accepted

The practical advantage of the request is that it does not go through your employer. Medical items you would rather not disclose at work, a dependant deduction discovered late, or an SME reduction you never knew about can all be handled personally.

One misunderstanding to avoid. A request for correction recovers something you were entitled to but did not receive; it is not a channel for adding a deduction whose requirements were never met. Without evidence and eligibility, the request will not be accepted.

10. Conclusion: what to do today, in December, and in January

Compressed into an execution order, the article says this.

10.1 Today, in five minutes

  • Open last year's withholding tax receipt and find your tax base and determined tax. Every judgement above depends on those two numbers
  • If you rent, confirm that your registered address matches the lease address
  • If you hold a housing subscription account, ask the bank whether the no-home certificate was filed
  • If you changed jobs this year, confirm the previous employer's withholding receipt reached the current one

10.2 August to December

  • Begin pension contributions as monthly instalments. The rate is the better 15% if total salary is 55 million won or less
  • From October, use the Hometax preview to check the card threshold and cap, then set your fourth-quarter payment method
  • If both spouses work, consent to data sharing and run the split simulation
  • Collect receipts in advance for items the simplified service misses — glasses, uniforms, academy fees, donations
  • Complete pension deposits and donation payments before 31 December

10.3 January to May

  • Download the simplified data in mid-January, then re-check the final version a few days later
  • Deduct medical costs reimbursed by indemnity insurance from your medical total
  • Re-verify dependant eligibility — income of 1 million won or less, and no duplicate claim
  • If the March withholding receipt shows a missing item, add it through the May return
  • If you recall a deduction missed within the past five years, consider a request for correction

One point deserves emphasis at the end. Manufacturing spending in order to save tax is not saving. As the arithmetic showed, an extra 10,000 won on a card typically returns a few hundred won. By contrast, meeting the requirements attached to spending and circumstances you already have — the address registration, the certificate, the reduction application, the receipt — costs nothing extra yet moves the outcome by hundreds of thousands of won. For salaried workers, almost all of the available saving sits in that second category.
