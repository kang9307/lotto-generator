Small Claims Court Complete Guide 2026 — Procedure, Costs, Documents & E-Filing

From filing your complaint to collecting the judgment — a practical step-by-step guide based on Korea's court system

2026-06-24 Law & Legal

Introduction: Resolving Small Monetary Disputes Through Court

When someone owes you money — whether from an unpaid loan, an undelivered service, or a broken contract — taking the matter to court can feel overwhelming. Many people assume litigation requires an expensive attorney and months of complex paperwork. In reality, for disputes of KRW 30 million or less (as of June 2026, subject to change), Korea's Small Claims Trial Act (소액사건심판법) provides a streamlined, affordable process that individuals can navigate without a lawyer.

This guide explains, step by step, how to file a small claims case in Korea in 2026: the eligibility threshold, how the process compares to a payment order (dok-chok), how to prepare your complaint, how to file electronically, how fees are calculated, and what to do after winning to actually collect the money. This is general information only — not individual legal advice. Laws and court rules may differ by jurisdiction and can change over time.

1. What Is Small Claims Court in Korea?

Korea's small claims procedure is governed by the Small Claims Trial Act, which establishes special rules within the civil procedure framework to allow district courts to handle low-value monetary disputes quickly and informally. The key features are: cases are heard by a single judge, hearings are normally completed in one session, and the court may issue a "compliance recommendation decision" (이행권고결정) as an alternative to a full trial — effectively functioning like a fast-track enforcement mechanism.

1.1 Eligible Claim Types and Amount

As of June 2026, claims eligible for the small claims procedure must:

  • Seek payment of money, fungible goods, or securities
  • Have a claim value (訴價) of KRW 30 million or less (general rule since January 2017; subject to legislative change)
  • Be a first-instance civil case filed at a district court or branch

Splitting a claim that exceeds KRW 30 million in total into smaller pieces to qualify for the small claims procedure is not permitted. Common eligible disputes include: personal loans, unpaid goods or service payments, partial security deposit refunds, and minor damages claims.

If the debtor is unlikely to dispute the claim, a payment order is cheaper and faster. If the debtor is expected to fight back, or if you want to lock in the outcome with a court hearing, the small claims lawsuit is safer. Note: if a payment order is contested and the claim is KRW 30 million or less, it automatically converts to a small claims case.

3. Before You File: Preparation Checklist

3.1 Verify the Correct Court

File at the district court (or branch) that has jurisdiction over the defendant's address or place of business, or over the place where the obligation is to be performed. If the contract specifies an agreed venue, that court applies. You can confirm the correct court using the electronic filing portal (ecfs.scourt.go.kr) search tool.

3.2 Check the Statute of Limitations

Most general civil claims expire after 10 years under Korea's Civil Act, though shorter periods apply to certain transaction types (e.g., 3 years for commercial claims, 2–3 years for some professional service fees). Filing a lawsuit stops the clock. If your dispute is long-standing, consult a professional to assess whether your claim is still valid.

3.3 Gather Evidence

  • Loan agreement, contract, receipt, or tax invoice
  • Text messages, KakaoTalk chats, or emails confirming the debt
  • Bank transfer records showing payment made
  • Photos or videos documenting damage or breach
  • Witness contact information

4. Writing the Complaint (Complaint / Sojang)

The complaint (소장) is the document you submit to initiate the lawsuit. The Supreme Court's self-help litigation site (pro-se.scourt.go.kr) offers free templates and guides. The electronic filing portal also provides guided form entry.

4.1 Required Information

  • Parties: Full name (or company name), address, and contact information for both plaintiff and defendant
  • Relief sought (청구취지): A concise statement of what you want the court to order — e.g., "Defendant shall pay plaintiff KRW [amount]."
  • Statement of claim (청구원인): A factual narrative of the dispute in chronological order — who, when, what was agreed, what was paid or owed, and what remains unpaid
  • List of evidence: An index of all documents you are attaching
  • Date and plaintiff signature/seal

4.2 Tips for Writing the Statement of Claim

Keep the statement factual and chronological. The judge needs to understand the dispute at a glance. Avoid emotional language. Be precise about dates, amounts, and the basis for your claim. The goal is to make it clear that you have a right to the money and the defendant has not paid it.

5. Filing Your Case: In Person vs. Electronic Filing

5.1 In-Person Filing

Visit the civil filing counter (민사접수과) at the relevant district court. Submit the original complaint and copies of all evidence. Pay the stamp duty (인지대) by revenue stamp or cash. Pay the service fee (송달료) by cash or bank transfer. You must also bring enough copies of the complaint for each defendant.

5.2 Electronic Filing via ecfs.scourt.go.kr

Korea's court electronic filing portal allows you to submit all documents online without visiting the courthouse.

  1. Access ecfs.scourt.go.kr and log in with a Korean digital certificate (공동인증서 or 금융인증서)
  2. Select "Document Submission" → "File Complaint (소장 제출)"
  3. Choose case type: Small Claims (소액사건)
  4. Enter party information and type your claim
  5. Attach scanned evidence files (PDF recommended)
  6. Pay stamp duty and service fees online (credit card or bank transfer)
  7. Receive a case number and track progress in real time

Electronic filers receive a 10% discount on stamp duty. Initial complaint submission must be made on a PC; subsequent filings and status checks can be done via the mobile app.

6.2 Service Fees (송달료)

Service fees = number of parties × 10 rounds × KRW 5,500 per round (subject to change). For one plaintiff and one defendant: 2 × 10 × 5,500 = KRW 110,000. Unused portions are refunded after the case closes.

7. How the Case Proceeds

7.1 Compliance Recommendation Decision (이행권고결정)

Upon receiving the complaint, the court typically issues a compliance recommendation decision — an order directing the defendant to pay the claimed amount. The decision is served on the defendant, who has two weeks to file an objection. If no objection is filed, the decision becomes final and has the same enforcement power as a court judgment. If the defendant objects, the court schedules a hearing.

7.2 Hearing (변론기일)

If the case goes to a hearing, the court summons both parties. Small claims cases aim to finish in one hearing session, after which the judge may immediately announce a verdict. The judge may question both parties directly and examine evidence. Failing to attend without notice can result in a default ruling against you.

7.3 Judgment and Appeal

The court issues a judgment for the plaintiff (full or partial), or dismisses the claim. An appeal must be filed within two weeks of receiving the written judgment. Note that appeals in small claims cases are limited to grounds specified by law — you generally cannot appeal simply because you disagree with the outcome.

8. After Winning: Enforcing the Judgment

8.1 Enforcement Title and Execution Clause

A final judgment or a finalized compliance recommendation decision constitutes an "enforcement title" (집행권원). To begin compulsory enforcement, you must obtain an execution clause (집행문) from the court clerk's office at the court that issued the judgment.

8.2 Methods of Compulsory Enforcement

  • Bank account garnishment: Most common. Apply to the court to attach and collect from the debtor's bank deposits.
  • Wage garnishment: Apply to the court to garnish up to one-half of the debtor's monthly wages from their employer.
  • Real property forced auction: Longer process; file with the court to force sale of the debtor's real property.
  • Movable property seizure: A court bailiff seizes and auctions the debtor's personal property.

8.3 Locating Hidden Assets

If you cannot identify the debtor's assets, you may apply for a property disclosure order (재산명시신청), which compels the debtor to submit a sworn list of their assets to the court. Non-compliance can result in civil detention (up to 20 days). Alternatively, a court-directed asset investigation (재산조회) authorizes the court to query financial institutions and government agencies for asset information.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

9.1 Do I need a lawyer?

No. Korea's small claims system explicitly supports self-representation. The Supreme Court operates the free self-help litigation website (pro-se.scourt.go.kr) with templates, guides, and tutorial videos. If the opposing party hires a lawyer or the case becomes complex, free legal assistance is available from the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (132, lak.or.kr).

9.2 How long does the process take?

If the defendant does not object to the compliance recommendation decision, the case can close within a few weeks to two months. If a hearing is required, the process typically takes three to six months. Court workload and case complexity affect timing.

9.3 Who pays the court fees if I win?

The losing party generally bears the court costs (stamp duty, service fees, and a portion of attorney fees if applicable). In partial victories, costs are split in proportion to the result. You must apply separately for a court costs determination order (소송비용 확정 신청) to recover costs from the other side.

9.4 What if the defendant cannot be located?

If you cannot find the defendant's address, you can attempt to obtain it through official channels (e.g., the resident registration system through the court). If service cannot be achieved otherwise, the court may allow publication-based service (공시송달). This process is more complex; consult the court or a legal aid professional for guidance.
