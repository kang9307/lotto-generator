G7 50th Anniversary Kananaskis Summit: At the Crossroads of Global Order

A Pivotal Moment Amid Trump's Return and Escalating Geopolitical Crises

G7 at 50: A Convergence of Crises

From its origins at Rambouillet in 1975, the G7 arrives at its 50th anniversary confronting a historic turning point in the Canadian Rockies at Kananaskis.

The 51st G7 Summit, held from June 15 to 17, 2025, in Kananaskis, Alberta, Canada, is far more than a routine annual gathering. It represents a critical inflection point that could shape the direction of the global order -- taking place at the most complex and challenging moment in the group's 50-year history.

Key Crisis Factors in 2025

  • Trump's Return: Unpredictable U.S. leadership
  • Russia-Ukraine War: Ongoing military tensions
  • Israel-Iran Conflict: Instability across the Middle East
  • Competition with China: Technological hegemony and economic security
  • BRICS Expansion: Construction of an alternative international order

The Significance of Choosing Kananaskis: Security and Isolation

Canada's choice of Kananaskis as the venue was no accident. The site successfully hosted the G8 Summit in 2002 in the wake of the September 11 attacks, and its remote, high-security environment symbolically reflects the internal and external pressures the G7 currently faces.

2002 vs. 2025: Kananaskis Then and Now

2002 Context

  • Post-9/11 security concerns
  • Anti-globalization protest response
  • G8 framework (Russia included)
  • Focus on economic recovery

2025 Context

  • Multiple geopolitical crises
  • Expected Trump-related protests
  • G7 framework (Russia excluded)
  • Debate on reshaping global order

The G7's Legitimacy Crisis

The G7 no longer possesses the economic power to unilaterally lead the global economy. Its raison d'etre must therefore be redefined around a new source of legitimacy: "solidarity among like-minded democratic nations."

Historical Evolution of the G7: A 50-Year Trajectory

The history of the G7 is a microcosm of changes in the global order. Starting as a body to respond to economic crises in the 1970s, it evolved into the political leadership of the Western bloc during the Cold War, and has now become an alliance defending democratic values.

1970s: Born in Crisis

1973 "Library Group" → 1975 Rambouillet G6 → 1976 G7 Complete

Launched as an informal economic forum in response to the collapse of the Bretton Woods system and the oil crisis. A "fireside chat" approach was adopted as a reaction to the rigidity of existing international institutions.

1980s: Cold War and Politicization

During the Reagan-Thatcher era, the agenda expanded from economics to politics and security. The 1983 Williamsburg Summit solidified Western bloc leadership with an agreement on anti-Soviet missile deployment.

1990s-2010s: The G8 Experiment and Its Failure

Russia was invited in 1997, launching the G8, only to be expelled in 2014 following its annexation of Crimea. The episode confirmed the limits of integration through transformation and the importance of shared values.

2020s: Redefined as a Democratic Alliance

Containing authoritarian powers like China and Russia and defending a "rules-based order" has emerged as the core mission. Economic governance has been ceded to the G20, with focus shifting to values-based solidarity.

The Future of the G7: Adaptation or Decline?

At its 50th anniversary, the G7's future is far from certain. However, several scenarios allow us to project its potential direction.

G7 Survival Strategies

Formalizing Partnerships

Building a structured "G7+" framework with India, South Korea, and Australia

Niche Governance

Focusing on setting standards in specialized fields like AI and cybersecurity

Values-Based Solidarity

Securing legitimacy through the defense of democratic values

The Expansion Debate: A Bigger Club vs. a Weaker One

The debate over G7 expansion fundamentally reflects philosophical differences about its purpose:

Arguments for Expansion

  • Increased legitimacy and representativeness
  • Expanded economic and technological influence
  • Strengthened democratic alliance
  • Asia-Pacific balance

Arguments Against Expansion

  • Reduced decision-making efficiency
  • Weakened intimacy and trust
  • Risk of becoming "G20-lite"
  • Potential amplification of regional conflicts

Conclusion: The G7 at a Crossroads

The 2025 Kananaskis Summit is a defining moment for the G7: will it rest on past glory, or find a role suited to the new era?

The G7 Today: Powerful Yet Anxious

At its 50th anniversary, the G7 reflects the Western world itself. Still powerful but facing relative decline; united by values yet divided by interests; uncertain of its role in a world it can no longer dominate.

Will the G7's next 50 years be a story of adaptation, or one of decline? The answer begins in the mountains of Kananaskis.

Key Outlook

The G7 must transition from being the conductor of the global economy to the guardian of democratic values. This is not a choice for survival -- it is a choice for prosperity.
