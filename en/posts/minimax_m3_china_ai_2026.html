What moved in two months - the conclusion first

Open a price sheet today and the numbers differ from June. The side that came down is not the Chinese one.

The original argument here was simple. Chinese models were delivering comparable results at single-digit percentages of Western flagship pricing, so price had stopped being a reason to stay with the incumbents. Two months on, half of that survives. Chinese list prices barely moved. The West moved beneath them.

Here is the two-month delta, compressed. Every figure below is what each vendor's own pricing or model documentation shows on 2026-08-04.

  • MiniMax-M3 pricing is unchanged. $0.30 input and $1.20 output per million tokens in the sub-512K tier, the same as at launch.
  • OpenAI came down. The GPT-5.6 family shipped on 2026-06-26, and after the cut on 2026-07-30, Luna lists at $0.20 input and $1.20 output. That is cheaper than M3 on input and identical on output.
  • The phrase 5-10% of a flagship now only holds against the top tier. Against GPT-5.5 at $5.00/$30.00, M3 sits at 4-6%. Against Gemini 3.1 Pro at $2.00/$12.00, it sits at 10-15%.
  • What actually moved on the Chinese side was licensing, not price. DeepSeek released the production V4-Flash under MIT on 2026-07-31, and Moonshot published Kimi K3 weights under a bespoke licence carrying a revenue threshold on 2026-07-27.
  • So the axis that decides today is not who is cheaper. It is whether the licence permits redistribution and resale, and whether your prompts may cross into that jurisdiction. Both rank above unit price.

The full cross-vendor price and benchmark tables live in the 2026 AI model comparison. This post carries only the figures its own axis needs, which is the distance between Chinese models and Western flagships. There is no reason to maintain the same table twice.

MiniMax-M3 - the specifications on record today

M3 shipped on 2026-06-01 and the weights appeared on Hugging Face on 7 June. Two months later it still sits at the top of MiniMax's model list. M2.7 remains in the current lineup; M2.5 and below have dropped into the legacy section.

2.1 Architecture - where sparse attention meets the price sheet

M3 is a Mixture-of-Experts model with roughly 428B total parameters and about 23B active per token. That much is standard for large models since 2025. The new part is attention. MiniMax built its own sparse attention scheme, MSA (MiniMax Sparse Attention), and the model card states a 9x prefill and 15x decode speedup over the M2 generation at a one-million-token context, with per-token compute down to roughly one twentieth.

Those numbers are not a performance boast. They are the reason the price works. In workloads that genuinely use a million-token window, cost is set by attention, not parameter count. Standard attention grows quadratically with context, and that growth lands directly on the unit price. Sparse attention flattens the curve. That is the mechanical reason M3 can keep a million-token window open and still hold a $1.20 output rate.

One judgement follows. If your contexts stay under 200K tokens, M3's structural advantage barely shows up on the invoice. In that band, M2.7 or a mid-tier model from another vendor may be cheaper or faster. The reason to pick M3 is a specific one: do you push long inputs repeatedly?

3.1 The layers the table does not show

A published rate is the cache-miss headline. The invoice is settled several layers below it.

  • Cache reads. M3 charges $0.06 (20% of input), GPT-5.6 Luna $0.02 (10%), DeepSeek V4-Flash $0.0028 (2%). If your architecture resends the same prompt prefix repeatedly, this column matters more than the headline rate.
  • Context tiers. M3 doubles above 512K. Gemini 3.1 Pro jumps to $4.00/$18.00 above 200K. GPT-5.6 Luna's long-context tier is $0.40/$1.80. Being able to open a million tokens and being able to afford a million tokens are different statements.
  • Batch and flex. Both OpenAI and Google apply roughly 50% off for batch processing. For work that is not time-critical, that discount alone halves the gap to the Chinese options.
  • Peak pricing. DeepSeek has announced a peak schedule at 2x list during 09:00-12:00 and 14:00-18:00 Beijing time. The start date has not been published.
  • Priority tiers. MiniMax's domestic platform charges 1.5x standard for priority processing.

Put all five layers in and rankings flip regularly. Take a structure that resends a shared prefix and reaches a 90% cache-hit rate: M3's effective input rate is not $0.30 but $0.30 × 0.1 + $0.06 × 0.9 = $0.084. Under the same conditions GPT-5.6 Luna comes to $0.20 × 0.1 + $0.02 × 0.9 = $0.038. The better your caching, the wider the gap opens in the direction opposite to the headline table.

Where caching barely applies, the headline rate is the invoice. Which layer dominates is set by your prompt structure, not by the model. So the calculation that belongs before any vendor comparison is an estimate of your own cache-hit rate. Plug in token volumes and hit rates with the LLM API price calculator.

3.2 M3's list price and its discount

The list price on the international platform is $0.60 input and $2.40 output. A standing 50% discount brings it to $0.30/$1.20. The domestic platform mirrors the structure: list ¥4.20/¥16.80, discounted to ¥2.10/¥8.40.

Standing or not, the list price is still in the documentation. Any organisation building a margin model on this rate should run the numbers once at undiscounted list. At $0.60/$2.40, M3 becomes twice the price of GPT-5.6 Luna at $0.20/$1.20.

The side that came down on price was the West

The largest event in this story between June and August did not come from China. It came from OpenAI.

On 2026-06-26 OpenAI shipped the GPT-5.6 family and changed how it packages capability. Instead of one model with a reasoning dial, there are now three tiers: Sol, Terra and Luna. On 2026-07-30 it cut Luna by 80% and Terra by 20%. Post-cut, Luna lists at $0.20 input and $1.20 output.

Set that beside M3.

  • Input: Luna $0.20 < M3 $0.30 — the Western model is cheaper
  • Output: Luna $1.20 = M3 $1.20 — identical
  • Cache read: Luna $0.02 < M3 $0.06 — the Western model is cheaper

For the sentence Chinese models cost a tenth to hold, the comparison has to be against a top tier such as GPT-5.5 or GPT-5.6 Sol. Compare like tier for like tier and the gap is gone. This does not mean Chinese models got expensive. It means price has stopped being the axis that separates the two camps.

Terra's post-cut figure is worth a second look too. $2.00 input, $12.00 output. Identical to Gemini 3.1 Pro to the cent. When two vendors' list prices converge exactly in the middle tier, that band has moved from price competition to capability competition. What decides a choice there is multimodal coverage and platform integration, not the unit rate.

4.1 So why still look at Chinese models

Three reasons remain, and they do not carry equal weight.

  1. You can hold the weights. Luna exists only as an API. M3, DeepSeek V4, GLM-5.2, Kimi K3 and Qwen 3.6 all publish weights. For an organisation that cannot send data out, this is not a pricing question, it is a possible or impossible question.
  2. The long-context curve bends differently. Luna's long-context tier is $0.40/$1.80; M3 holds $0.30/$1.20 all the way to 512K. If you routinely push 300K-500K tokens, the decision turns here.
  3. Not being tied to one supplier has a value of its own. In the first half of 2026 alone, GPT-5 line pricing doubled once (2026-04-23) and fell 80% once (2026-07-30). Building a business on a single supplier across that amplitude is a pricing risk in itself.

The inverse also holds. If none of those three apply and the only reason you are on a Chinese model is that it is cheaper, recalculate now. That reason was valid two months ago and is not valid today.

5.1 DeepSeek

V4-Pro and V4-Flash arrived as previews on 2026-04-24, and the production V4-Flash was released under MIT on 2026-07-31. V4-Pro is 1.6T total with 49B active; V4-Flash is 284B with 13B active. Both carry a one-million-token context.

Its pricing is the floor of this table. V4-Flash at $0.14 input and $0.28 output, with cache hits taking input down to $0.0028. For bulk batch classification, summarisation and tagging, this is the cheapest verified option in August 2026. The background sits in the DeepSeek V4-Pro 75% permanent price cut analysis.

5.2 Qwen (Alibaba)

Qwen split into two branches during 2026: open weights and a closed flagship.

  • Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (released 2026-04-16) and Qwen3.6-27B (2026-04-22) ship under Apache 2.0. Commercial use carries no conditions.
  • Qwen3.7-Max and Qwen3.7-Plus do not publish weights. They are API-only, with Max at $2.50/$7.50.

The Qwen3.6-27B card states a native 262,144-token context, extensible to 1,010,000. At 27B dense it fits on a single workstation, and it carries the simplest licence terms of any option here. The local-deployment view is in Qwen 3.6 27B for local development.

5.3 GLM (Zhipu / Z.ai)

GLM-5.2 is MIT. The model card lists 753B parameters, a one-million-token context and 128K maximum output. The API is $1.40/$4.40 with cache reads at $0.26. It is text-only, so anything needing multimodal input rules it out.

Being MIT makes it the lowest-friction option for businesses that resell a model as part of a product. Details are in the GLM 5.2 release analysis.

5.4 Kimi (Moonshot)

Kimi K3 launched as a hosted service first, with weights published on 2026-07-27. It is 2.8T total with 104B active per token, routing to 16 of 896 experts, a 1,048,576-token context, and a MoonViT-V2 vision encoder for native image input. The weights come as 96 safetensors shards totalling around 1.56TB.

It is the most expensive Chinese option here: $3.00 input, $15.00 output, $0.30 cached input. This is not a model you choose on price. The deployment question is covered in Kimi K3 open weights.

5.5 The three labs in one line

DeepSeek took the price floor, GLM took the unencumbered licence, Kimi took scale. MiniMax landed between them on the long-context price curve. There is no longer any reason to treat Chinese models as one bloc when choosing. The China LLM top three comparison covers the earlier part of that split.

6.1 Read the revenue clauses as contract text

MiniMax and Moonshot both set the same $20M threshold. The wording differs.

  • MiniMax keys on annual revenue of the related products or services and requires prior written authorisation above the threshold, with a single notice below it.
  • Moonshot keys on a business that offers the model as a service, requiring a separate agreement if that business exceeds the threshold over 12 months. Embedding the model in your own product does not trip it.

The reach of those two clauses is not the same. MiniMax's is wider, because revenue from a product that merely embeds the model can plausibly read as revenue of a related product. Even well below the threshold, that sentence belongs in front of counsel.

In practice this splits three ways.

  • If you sell the model as part of a product (MaaS, resale, white label), look only at MIT and Apache. DeepSeek V4, GLM-5.2, Qwen 3.6. There are no conditional clauses, so review time is zero.
  • For internal use or embedding in your own product, a community licence stays in scope. The Built with MiniMax M3 obligation has to land in product documentation or an about page. Omitting it is a breach of condition, not a style choice.
  • If you are on a trajectory past $20M in annual revenue, check now. Prior-authorisation clauses are the kind that are hard to settle retroactively.

6.2 Where the attribution goes

Attribution obligations such as Built with MiniMax M3 are the item teams most often miss. The licence text points at a website, product interface, blog post, about page or product documentation, and asks for a prominent placement. A source-code comment or a line in a bundled licence file is unlikely to satisfy it.

Kimi K3's attribution clause has a different reach. It applies only to products above 100 million monthly active users. Most organisations never touch it, which is why the practical review point on the Kimi side is the MaaS revenue threshold rather than attribution.

To state it plainly: MiniMax attaches an attribution duty regardless of scale, and Kimi attaches one only at scale. For a product that cannot surface a third-party model name in its own branded interface, that difference decides the choice.

7.1 If you need video input, there are two candidates

Among the Chinese options, only M3 accepts video directly. Kimi K3 stops at images; DeepSeek, GLM and Qwen 3.6 are text-only. On the Western side, Gemini 3.1 Pro takes video.

So once video understanding is in the requirements, the field narrows to M3 and Gemini 3.1 Pro. In that condition the price spread is wide: M3 at $0.30/$1.20 against Gemini at $2.00/$12.00. Video workloads consume tokens heavily, so that multiple carries straight through to the invoice.

7.2 Will you actually use a million tokens

The context column is a maximum you may open. Three consequences follow.

  • The price tier bends (M3 at 512K, Gemini at 200K, GPT-5.6 at its long-context boundary).
  • Latency grows. A million-token prefill dominates time to first token.
  • Accuracy does not scale with length. Losing material in the middle of a long input is not vendor-specific.

The judgement. A million tokens is best used as no longer needing to split, not as put everything in at once. The real gain is that five 200K documents go in without a chunking and reassembly pipeline, and that gain arrives in the form of code you delete.

8.1 The international and domestic accounts are different services

MiniMax runs separate international and Chinese domestic platforms with separate price structures. One bills in dollars, the other in yuan, and converting at market rates does not reconcile them. The domestic platform also carries a priority processing tier at 1.5x standard and a different token package lineup.

From outside China the international platform is the default route. A gateway shows lower headline rates ($0.24 input, $0.96 output) but the actual serving provider can change between requests. Where tool-call accuracy or response latency feeds directly into product quality, a first-party API beats a gateway. For evaluation the reverse holds: one key across many models behind one interface is exactly what a comparison run needs.

8.2 Regional limits live in the licence text

Published weights do not imply universal use. GLM-5.2's model card states no regional limits explicitly. For the others, the licence body is the place to check. Availability and regional conditions are separate items, and they are written in the licence file rather than the summary page.

How realistic is running it yourself

With more weights published, download and run has become a real option. Put the scale in numbers.

  • Qwen3.6-27B — 27B dense. Quantised, it runs on a single high-spec workstation. This is the only band an individual or small team realistically carries.
  • Qwen3.6-35B-A3B — 35B total, 3B active MoE. Active parameters are small, but the full weight set still has to sit in memory. MoE cuts compute, not resident memory.
  • DeepSeek-V4-Flash — 284B total, 13B active. From here it is a multi-GPU server.
  • MiniMax-M3 — 428B total, 23B active.
  • GLM-5.2 — 753B.
  • Kimi K3 — 2.8T total, 104B active. The weight files alone are about 1.56TB across 96 shards, at MXFP4 quantisation.

The judgement. Self-hosting that starts as a cost-cutting exercise usually fails. Unless batch throughput keeps the GPUs busy, real cost per token overtakes API pricing easily. With a $0.14/$0.28 API on the table, the band where self-hosting wins on unit cost alone is narrow.

The conditions where self-hosting does win are different ones. Data that cannot leave, behaviour that has to be frozen, and fine-tuning. If any one of those applies, self-hosting is the answer regardless of the cost arithmetic; if none apply, the API is. There is not much middle ground.

Behaviour that has to be frozen deserves an explanation. API models are updated behind the endpoint. Calling the same model name can return subtly different output formats or refusal boundaries, and the parsers and post-processing built on top move with them. For a pipeline that must hold validated behaviour steady for months, holding the weights is what reproducibility means. That value never appears in a unit-price calculation, and for any team that has lived through a silent regression it is the largest line item.

By scale: 27B is an individual's territory, anything from 300B up brings a team and a budget, and weights past a terabyte are effectively an infrastructure business. Kimi K3's 1.56TB is a project just to download.

Data handling and regulation in practice

Even with pricing and licensing settled, jurisdiction comes first. Only confirmed items appear here.

  • Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission published findings on DeepSeek's service on 2025-04-24 and issued corrective recommendations. Personal data of domestic users had been transferred to five companies in China and the United States without disclosure, and no Korean-language privacy policy was provided. The recommendations covered establishing a lawful basis for cross-border transfer, destroying data already transferred, and appointing a standing domestic representative.
  • Korea's AI Framework Act took effect on 2026-01-22, with an amendment in force from 2026-07-21. Breaches of operator obligations carry a guidance period of at least one year.
  • Public-sector projects using generative AI go through a separate security review.

Translated into working sentences, that comes to the following. The specifics are Korean, but the shape repeats in most jurisdictions that have written cross-border transfer rules.

10.1 Jurisdiction first, unit price second

If personal data enters the prompt, disclosure and a lawful basis for cross-border transfer come before anything else. That constraint is independent of model quality and price, and a 90% discount does not change a regulation. What went wrong in the DeepSeek case was not model performance but an undisclosed transfer.

So the working order for regulated data is fixed. Settle jurisdiction, then compare unit prices among whatever clears it. Reverse the order and the work gets unwound later.

10.2 Open weights shorten the regulatory conversation

Put weights on your own infrastructure and the cross-border transfer does not occur. That single structural change removes a long list of review items. This is why the value of MIT and Apache options rose in summer 2026 independently of price.

The catch is that licence conditions travel with the weights. Self-hosting a community-licensed model to avoid a regulator, then landing on a revenue clause, relocates the problem rather than solving it. For regulated workloads on self-hosted weights, narrowing to MIT and Apache leaves the smallest total review surface.

10.3 Language quality is not in any of these tables

None of the benchmarks here measure performance in Korean, Japanese or any other language outside the evaluation sets. SWE-bench is code, MMMU is multimodal understanding, Video-MME is video. Summarisation quality, register and honorific consistency in a non-English language have no published figures.

That gap closes only through your own evaluation: 30 to 50 real tasks as a golden set, the same inputs across candidate models, results compared side by side. The markdown artefact attached below this post carries that procedure in a form you can copy straight out.

11.1 If you have to pick one

With no conditions known, set the defaults like this. Bulk text processing goes to DeepSeek-V4-Flash, anything that has to stay with a Western vendor goes to GPT-5.6 Luna, and long context mixed with video goes to MiniMax-M3. What held from June and what did not

The time comparison belongs on the record. For this post to stay useful two months from now, it has to mark which judgements survived and which did not.

12.1 What held

  • MoE architecture sets the price. Every low-cost model verifiable in August is MoE, and lower active parameter counts track lower rates. M3 at 23B active is $0.30; V4-Flash at 13B active is $0.14. No exception yet.
  • Published rates diverge from real invoices. If anything the layers multiplied. Tier splits (Sol, Terra, Luna), an announced peak schedule and a priority processing tier all appeared in these two months.
  • Benchmark leadership is not production advantage. M3's SWE-bench Pro 59.0% is still on the model card in August, and it still says nothing about summarisation quality in your language.

12.2 What did not

  • The generalisation that Chinese models cost 5-10% of Western ones. Tier for tier it does not hold. GPT-5.6 Luna is cheaper than M3 on input. The claim now survives only against the top tier.
  • The direction that price pressure runs from China outward. The pressure came back as a cut, and a large one: Luna down 80% on 2026-07-30. That pressure existed is a fact; that it is still running one way is a different claim.
  • Explaining the landscape through MiniMax alone. In June one model could stand in for the trend. In August, DeepSeek's MIT release, Kimi's threshold licence and Qwen's open-closed split are separate axes that no single model represents.

12.3 The one sentence for now

Rewritten for August 2026: price no longer separates the camps. What separates them is whether you can hold the weights, and what the licence lets you do with them.

How to read the benchmarks

Every benchmark figure in this post is a vendor-run measurement. That does not void them, but it does require a reading rule.

  • Vendor measurements and third-party measurements are different kinds of claim. Mixing them into one ranking breaks the comparison conditions.
  • Whether benchmark items leaked into training data cannot be checked from outside. This applies to every evaluation built on a public question set.
  • Averages are published; variance is not. A model that collapses one run in five and a model that lands consistently can post the same score.
  • The measurement configuration is not yours. Reasoning effort, output ceiling and tool availability all change the result.

One working rule. A benchmark lead is a reason to run your own evaluation, not a conclusion. If M3 leads on coding benchmarks and your team writes code, that is a signal to spend an afternoon on your own golden set. It is not a signal to swap providers next week.

The generational arc is in the six-month LLM progress recap, and the agentic axis is in the Qwen Conference write-up.

What could not be verified

These correspond to the blank cells above. Values without a source were left out rather than filled in.

  • Parameter counts for the M2 family. MiniMax's documentation publishes context and output speed only.
  • GLM-5.2's active parameter count. The model card carries 753B total with no per-token active figure.
  • Parameters and context for Qwen3.7-Max and Plus. Closed models with no published material.
  • Context ceilings for the GPT-5.6 family and Gemini 3.1 Pro. The pricing pages carry tier rates; the ceilings themselves sit across separate documents.

  • Real invoice totals. These follow cache-hit rates, retries and batch share, and cannot be derived from published rates.
  • Per-provider rates at open-weight hosting services. They vary by provider and change often, so no fixed value was placed in the table.

Frequently asked questions

Q. What does MiniMax-M3 cost right now?

As of 2026-08-04, the international platform lists $0.30 input, $1.20 output and $0.06 cache read per million tokens. Requests above 512K bill at double. List is $0.60/$2.40 with a standing 50% discount producing the figures above. The Chinese domestic platform prices separately at ¥2.10/¥8.40.

Q. Is the gap to Western flagships still tenfold?

Between top tiers, yes. Against GPT-5.5 at $5.00/$30.00, M3 is 4-6%. But GPT-5.6 Luna at $0.20/$1.20 is cheaper than M3 on input and identical on output. Against Gemini 3.1 Pro at $2.00/$12.00, M3 is 10-15%.

Q. Can I take the M3 weights into a commercial product?

Yes, under the community licence conditions. A Built with MiniMax M3 notice is mandatory, and prior written authorisation is required once related products or services pass $20M in annual revenue. For unconditional commercial use, the MIT-licensed DeepSeek V4 family and GLM-5.2, or the Apache 2.0 Qwen 3.6 family, are the right fit.

Q. Which Chinese option is cheapest?

On official pricing as of 2026-08-04, DeepSeek-V4-Flash at $0.14 input and $0.28 output is the lowest, with cache hits taking input to $0.0028. It is text-only, and once peak pricing starts it doubles inside the announced windows.

Q. What should a company check first?

Three things, in order. Which jurisdiction the prompts leave for, whether the licence carries revenue or attribution conditions, and how the rate bends in the long-context tier. Compare unit prices only among candidates that clear all three. Reverse the order and the work gets unwound.

# Model Selection Checklist — What to Check Before Unit Price

> Source: braindetox.kr — MiniMax-M3 and Chinese AI pricing in 2026

## 0. The order — reverse it and you will redo the work

1. **Jurisdiction** — which country do the prompts leave for
2. **Licence** — redistribution, resale, attribution, revenue thresholds
3. **Context-tier price** — what it costs at the length you actually use
4. **Unit price comparison** — only among candidates that cleared the first three
5. **Your own evaluation** — a golden set as the final check

Put price at step 1 and you get blocked at step 3; blocked at step 3 and you restart from step 1.

## 1. Jurisdiction check

- [ ] Does personal data enter the prompt
- [ ] If so, is the cross-border transfer disclosed with a lawful basis
- [ ] Is this a regulated sector (finance, healthcare, public)
- [ ] Does the service form require a designated local representative
- [ ] For public procurement, has the separate security review been confirmed
- [ ] If any box is ticked → evaluate **self-hosted open weights** first

## 2. Licence check

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Licence name | MIT or Apache 2.0 means no conditional clauses |
| Attribution duty | If present, it has to land in product docs or an about page |
| Revenue threshold | Prior-authorisation type differs from notice type |
| What the threshold applies to | Related product revenue, or model-as-a-service revenue |
| Regional limits | Written in the licence file, not the summary page |

**Judgement**: selling the model as part of a product means looking only at MIT and Apache. Internal use keeps community licences in scope.

## 4. Effective rate calculation

```
effective input rate = input_rate × (1 - h) + cache_read_rate × h
h = cache hit rate (0 to 1)
```

- If you do not know h, measure it first. Your logs show the shared prompt prefix length
- Once h passes 0.8, **the cache read rate matters more than the headline rate**
- Record what share of the workload can go through batch. Batch discounts are usually 50 percent

## 5. Evaluation harness

1. **A golden set of 30-50 items** drawn from real work. Roughly 40 easy, 40 medium, 20 hard
2. **Write the pass condition first.** Set it after seeing output and it drifts toward the output
3. **Pin the configuration.** Model name, version string, reasoning effort, output ceiling, tool availability
4. **Five runs per input.** Record each run separately. Averages hide variance
5. **Grade blind** so you do not know which model produced which output
6. **Log tokens and latency alongside quality.** Quality alone gives half a conclusion
