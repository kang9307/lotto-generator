Credit Recovery and Debt Restructuring Guide — Workout, Rehabilitation, and Bankruptcy in Korea

A complete comparison of Korea's four debt relief options: pre-workout, individual workout, personal rehabilitation, and personal bankruptcy

2026-06-29 Finance & Economy
Notice: This article provides general information about Korea's debt restructuring programs as of June 2026. Requirements and outcomes vary by individual circumstances. This content does not constitute legal or financial advice. For personalized guidance, contact the Credit Recovery Committee (1600-5500) or the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (132).

Introduction: When Debt Becomes Unmanageable

Sudden job loss, business failure, unexpected medical costs, or high-interest debt spiraling out of control — these are situations millions of people face. In South Korea, individuals struggling with debt have access to four main public debt relief programs: pre-workout (사전채무조정), individual workout (개인워크아웃), personal rehabilitation (개인회생), and personal bankruptcy with discharge (개인파산·면책).

These four programs share similar names but differ significantly in who administers them, eligibility requirements, duration, the extent of debt reduction, and the impact on credit. Choosing the wrong path can mean years of unnecessary hardship — or missing a program that would have provided far greater relief. This guide, based on Korean law and regulations as of June 2026, explains each option clearly so you can identify which path fits your situation.

1. Overview: Four Debt Relief Programs at a Glance

Korea's debt relief programs fall into two main tracks. The first track is administered by the Credit Recovery Committee (CCRS / 신용회복위원회), a quasi-public body that brokers voluntary agreements between debtors and financial institutions. The second track is managed by the courts under the Debtor Rehabilitation and Bankruptcy Act. CCRS programs are generally faster and simpler but offer more limited debt reduction. Court-based programs are more complex and time-consuming but can eliminate far larger amounts of debt.

Program Administered by Delinquency Requirement Debt Reduction Repayment Period Best For
Pre-Workout CCRS 31–89 days overdue Overdue interest reduced Up to 10 years Early delinquency, willing to repay
Individual Workout CCRS 90+ days overdue Principal up to 70–90% Up to 10 years (35 for secured) Long-term delinquency, some income

Personal Bankruptcy Court None required Full discharge (assets liquidated) Until liquidation complete No income or assets, total insolvency

2. CCRS Pre-Workout (Pre-Debt Adjustment)

2.1 What It Is

Pre-workout is a preventive measure for debtors who are 31 to 89 days past due. The goal is to restructure obligations before delinquency hardens into long-term default. The debtor benefits by halting credit deterioration; lenders benefit by reducing non-performing loan growth. It is available through CCRS member financial institutions — primarily banks, savings banks, and credit card companies.

2.2 Eligibility

  • Delinquency of 31 to 89 days
  • Total debt of 1.5 billion KRW or less (unsecured up to 500 million, secured up to 1 billion)
  • Debt owed to at least one CCRS member institution
  • New debt incurred in the last 6 months must be less than 30% of total principal

2.3 What You Can Expect

The primary benefit of pre-workout is reduction or waiver of overdue interest. The original principal and regular contractual interest are generally maintained. Repayment can be stretched up to 10 years (unsecured), reducing monthly burden. Significant principal reduction is not typically available at this stage. Consultation and application are free of charge.

3. CCRS Individual Workout

3.1 What It Is

Individual workout is the primary CCRS program for debtors 90 or more days past due. It allows for substantial principal reduction and is the most widely used CCRS relief tool. CCRS acts as a mediator between the debtor and member creditors to restructure the debt on affordable terms.

3.2 Eligibility

  • Delinquency of 90 days (3 months) or more
  • Total debt of 1.5 billion KRW or less (unsecured up to 500 million, secured up to 1 billion)
  • Debt owed to at least one CCRS member institution
  • New debt from the past 6 months must be less than 30% of total principal
  • Debt owed to non-member creditors must be less than 20% of total

3.3 Debt Reduction

The extent of reduction depends on the debtor's repayment capacity. For unwritten-off debt (still on creditor books), principal reduction ranges from 0 to 30%. For written-off debt (already charged off as losses), principal reduction can reach 20 to 70%. Socially vulnerable groups — including national basic livelihood beneficiaries, seniors 65 and over, and persons with disabilities — may receive up to 90% reduction on written-off principal.

3.4 Repayment Period

Unsecured debt can be repaid over up to 10 years; secured debt up to 35 years. Longer repayment periods reduce monthly payments but increase total interest paid, so choosing a term aligned with actual repayment capacity is important.

4. Court Personal Rehabilitation (개인회생)

4.1 What It Is

Personal rehabilitation is a court-supervised procedure under the Debtor Rehabilitation and Bankruptcy Act. An individual with regular income proposes a repayment plan to the court; after faithfully repaying for 3 to 5 years, the court discharges the remaining debt. There is no cap on total debt, unlike CCRS programs.

4.2 Eligibility

  • Must be an individual (sole proprietors included)
  • Must have regular, stable income (wages, pension, rental, business income, etc.)
  • No upper limit on total debt amount
  • Must face the risk of insolvency without court relief

4.3 How It Works

The court calculates disposable income — income minus the minimum living cost (approximately 1.54 million KRW per month for a one-person household in 2026). This amount is paid monthly under a court-approved plan for 3 years (extendable to 5 if needed). Upon completion and court confirmation of faithful performance, all remaining debt is discharged.

4.4 Pros and Cons

Key advantages include no debt cap, coverage of secured debt, and a freeze on creditor collection actions once the court approves the case. Drawbacks include the complexity of court filings, a 3-to-5-year commitment, court and attorney fees (which can range from tens to hundreds of thousands of KRW), and the risk of dismissal if income disappears or payments are missed.

5. Court Personal Bankruptcy and Discharge (개인파산·면책)

5.1 What It Is

Personal bankruptcy is the most extreme form of debt relief — a court-supervised liquidation of the debtor's assets, followed by discharge of all remaining debt. It is intended for individuals who are in a state of total insolvency: unable to repay debt now or in the foreseeable future.

5.2 Eligibility

  • Must be an individual (sole proprietors included)
  • Must be in a state of insolvency (persistent inability to meet debt obligations)
  • No cap on total debt amount
  • Income is not required — even employed individuals may apply if they cannot repay

5.3 The Process

After the court declares bankruptcy, a trustee is appointed to liquidate non-exempt assets and distribute proceeds to creditors. The debtor then applies for discharge. Once the court grants the discharge order, all remaining unpaid debt is legally extinguished. Certain debts are not dischargeable: tax obligations, fines, debt arising from fraud or gross negligence, and alimony/child support.

5.4 Grounds for Denial of Discharge

Discharge may be refused if the debtor concealed assets, created false liabilities, preferentially paid certain creditors within 1 year before filing, accumulated debt through gambling or extravagance, or received a discharge within the past 5 years.

6. Which Program Is Right for Your Situation?

The key factors are: length of delinquency, presence of income, total debt size, and degree of reduction needed.

Situation Recommended Program
31–89 days overdue, willing to repay in full, need extended schedule Pre-Workout
90+ days overdue, some income, need partial principal reduction Individual Workout
Regular income, large total debt (no upper cap), need major reduction Personal Rehabilitation
No income or assets, completely unable to repay Personal Bankruptcy
Debt concentrated with non-CCRS lenders (e.g., private lenders) Personal Rehabilitation or Bankruptcy

7. How to Apply and Required Documents

7.1 CCRS Programs (Pre-Workout / Individual Workout)

  1. Visit the CCRS website (ccrs.or.kr) or a local CCRS branch to apply in person or online
  2. CCRS reviews your debt profile and conducts a consultation
  3. Submit the application form with income and debt documentation
  4. CCRS negotiates terms with your creditors and confirms the restructuring plan
  5. Begin installment repayment under the agreed schedule

Required documents typically include: national ID, employment certificate or business registration, proof of income (pay stubs, tax withholding statements), and loan contracts. There is no application fee.

7.2 Court Programs (Personal Rehabilitation / Bankruptcy)

  1. Contact the local district court or Korea Legal Aid Corporation to prepare the application
  2. Compile a debt list, asset list, income/expense statements, and other required documents
  3. File at the court and pay filing fees
  4. Court reviews and issues a commencement order
  5. Repayment plan confirmed (rehabilitation) or bankruptcy declared (bankruptcy)
  6. After completing repayment or liquidation, apply for and receive discharge

Court filing fees are modest (roughly 10,000–50,000 KRW), but attorney or legal scrivener fees can add significantly to costs. The Korea Legal Aid Corporation (132) provides free legal assistance to those who qualify.

8. Beware of Illegal Lenders and Debt Relief Scams

People struggling with debt are frequently targeted by fraudulent operators. Recognize these red flags:

  • Anyone calling themselves a "debt relief specialist" who demands upfront payment or a fee
  • Operators impersonating government agencies or official programs
  • Brokers who refer you to unregistered lenders and charge a referral fee
  • Anyone promising "credit score erasure" or "debt deletion" — these do not exist legally

Public agencies — CCRS (1600-5500), Korea Legal Aid Corporation (132), and the Financial Supervisory Service illegal lending report line (1332) — are all free. They never charge advance fees. Report suspicious operators to the FSS or call police (112).

9. Rebuilding Credit After Debt Restructuring

9.1 How Long Records Stay

CCRS workout records can affect your credit for approximately 5 to 7 years after completion. Personal rehabilitation and personal bankruptcy records may remain on file for up to 10 years after the court's decision. During this period, obtaining new loans or credit cards will be difficult.

9.2 Steps to Rebuild

  • Make every installment payment on time as agreed
  • Open and maintain a small savings account to build a financial transaction history
  • Minimize unnecessary credit inquiries and close dormant accounts
  • Once CCRS procedures are complete, obtain a Debt Adjustment Completion Certificate to present to financial institutions
  • Build consistent income documentation — stable income history is the most powerful credit rebuilder

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Will my family be notified of my debt restructuring?

Neither CCRS programs nor court procedures notify family members separately. However, if a family member is a co-signer (guarantor) on any of your debts, they may be affected by the restructuring.

Q. Can I take on new debt while in a workout or rehabilitation?

Practically speaking, new credit is very difficult to obtain during a CCRS workout or personal rehabilitation. Under court rehabilitation, incurring new debt above certain amounts requires court approval.

Q. What if I change jobs or get a raise during my workout?

A job change itself is not a problem. If your income increases significantly, CCRS may revise the repayment terms. Report any material income changes to CCRS to keep your plan current and avoid complications.
