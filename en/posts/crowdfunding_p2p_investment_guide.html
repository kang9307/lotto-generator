Types, Regulation, Investor Limits, and the Risks You Must Understand

Introduction: The Risk Hidden Behind the Word Small

Phrases like "invest small amounts in a startup" or "earn more than a savings account" appear constantly when crowdfunding and P2P lending are introduced. These models genuinely opened a new channel for many individuals to pool small sums through an online platform and supply capital to companies, projects, or borrowers. But behind the impression of "small" and "simple" sits a risk structure entirely unlike a bank deposit. These are investments where principal can be lost, and in many cases funds are locked until maturity and hard to sell in the meantime.

This article covers how crowdfunding types differ (equity, reward, and lending), the concept of Korea's online investment-linked finance that formalized the lending type, how to confirm a registered operator and investor limits, and the risks of principal loss, delinquency, illiquidity, and platform failure. The angle is deliberate: instead of inflating returns, it focuses on what you can lose and what criteria you should judge by. 1. What Is Crowdfunding?

Crowdfunding means raising funding from a crowd, and broadly refers to many individuals pooling small amounts through an online platform to finance a specific company or project. For the party raising money, it is a channel to appeal directly to the public rather than through traditional routes like bank loans or venture capital; for participants, it is a way to provide capital in various forms - donation, investment, or lending. Because the character of the deal changes drastically depending on what you receive in return, distinguishing the types is where understanding begins.

2. The Four Types of Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding is commonly divided, by what you receive in return for your money, into donation, reward, equity (investment), and lending types.

2.1 Donation and Reward Types

The donation type is participation purely for support or charity, with no material return. The reward type repays backers with the finished product, a service, or merchandise, making it closer to a "pre-order" than an investment. These are not products designed for financial return, though the risk remains that a failed project may never deliver the promised reward.

2.2 Equity (Investment) Type

The equity (investment) type is where a company issues securities such as shares or bonds and investors acquire them to supply capital. If the company grows you may expect gains in equity value or dividends, but neither returns nor share-price appreciation is guaranteed, and principal loss is possible. Targets are frequently early-stage or unlisted companies, so growth can take a long time, and the lack of a ready market to resell the issued securities means liquidity is very low - a point that is often highlighted.

2.3 Lending Type (P2P)

The lending type is where a company or individual borrows from many investors through an online platform rather than a financial institution, and investors expect interest income through repayment of principal and interest. This lending type is what has been formalized by law in Korea as online investment-linked finance, commonly called P2P investment. In other words, P2P is one branch of crowdfunding.

3. P2P Investment and Online Investment-Linked Finance

P2P finance once operated without a clear legal framework, and after a string of defaults and fraud cases, the government enacted the Online Investment-Linked Finance Business Act (November 2019), which took full effect from August 2020. Under it, to broker P2P loans an operator must register with the Financial Services Commission as an online investment-linked finance business, subject to registration requirements and user-protection duties. The stated intent of formalizing the sector was to expand inclusive finance by promoting mid-rate loan brokerage for borrowers with moderate-to-low credit.

Formalization, however, does not equal safety. P2P loans still have a structure where, if a borrower cannot repay, the loss passes to investors, and the platform only links and brokers the loan rather than guaranteeing recovery of principal and interest. Even with a registered operator, the default risk of an individual product is borne directly by the investor.

4. Registration and Investor Limits - Non-Negotiable Checks

Before investing, at minimum confirm these two things.

  • Registered operator status: An online investment-linked finance business cannot operate without registering with the Financial Services Commission. Check registration and disclosed metrics such as delinquency and loss rates through Financial Services Commission and Financial Supervisory Service disclosures and the central record-keeping institution. Unregistered operators or those touting abnormally high returns carry a high risk of illegal fundraising or fraud.
  • Statutory investor limits: For investor protection, general retail investors face investment caps, and higher-risk categories such as real-estate-related products are understood to have a separate, lower cap. The exact amounts and conditions can change with the law in effect, so before investing always verify with the latest guidance from the Financial Services Commission and the central record-keeping institution.

5. Key Risks - What You Can Lose

5.1 Principal Loss and No Deposit Protection

The most fundamental risk is loss of principal. Equity crowdfunding and P2P investment are not bank deposits, so they are not covered by depositor protection. A borrower's default or the failure of a target company can cause you to lose all of the principal you invested.

5.2 Delinquency Rate and Loss Rate

Two metrics common in P2P: the delinquency rate is the share of amounts delayed in repayment by more than a month, while the loss rate is the share where recovery has become effectively impossible and principal loss is confirmed. Delinquency is not automatically a total loss, but a high delinquency rate at an operator or on a product can signal elevated risk of future loss. Rather than judging safety from a single low figure at one moment, review both metrics together. And because some operators have been criticized for hiding information unfavorable to investors or disclosing poorly, taking disclosures at face value is itself risky.

5.3 Liquidity Risk - Hard to Sell Midway

A bank deposit can be withdrawn any time, but crowdfunding and P2P investment generally lock funds until maturity. The equity type lacks a ready resale market, and the lending type is hard or restricted to redeem early. You must factor in the liquidity risk that even if you urgently need cash, you may not be able to recover funds when you want.

5.4 Platform Risk

Separate from the risk of the investment itself is the risk of platform (broker) failure. Incidents such as false disclosures, revolving-loan schemes, and misappropriation of funds have actually occurred, and if a platform goes bankrupt, managing repayments and recovering funds can become difficult in itself. That is why confirming whether an operator is registered and how funds are managed and held in custody matters.

6. The Investment Process - Generally This Flow

It varies by platform, but the general flow is described as follows. First, sign up on a platform run by a registered operator and complete the checks tied to investor type and limits. Second, review the disclosed products - borrower information, rate, term, presence of collateral or guarantee, delinquency and loss metrics. Third, after reading the product statement and risk disclosure, invest small amounts spread across deals. Fourth, monitor repayment progress and delinquency regularly. Throughout, the crucial habit is to weigh "how much could I lose in the worst case" before "the expected return."

7. Criteria That Reduce Risk

  • Spare-funds principle: Invest only money you can lose without disrupting your life, and only a portion of total assets.
  • Diversification principle: Do not concentrate on one product, borrower, or platform; split across many deals. Diversification is not a tool to amplify returns but a device to soften losses.
  • Registration and limits: Confirm the operator is registered and that you respect statutory investor limits.
  • Return skepticism: A return far above market rates can mean correspondingly high risk - or the possibility of fraud.
  • Liquidity awareness: Set your horizon on the assumption funds are locked until maturity.

8. Conclusion: Approach It Acknowledging High Risk

Crowdfunding and P2P investment opened a new channel for individuals to participate in varied assets with small amounts. But their essence is high-risk investment where principal can be lost, not covered by deposit protection. The character of the risk differs by type (equity, reward, lending), and it is safest to access the lending type (P2P) only through operators registered under the relevant law. The essentials are: confirm registration and disclosed metrics, respect statutory investor limits, spread across many deals within spare funds, and weigh the liquidity constraint and worst-case loss before anything else.
