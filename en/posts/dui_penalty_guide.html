Never Drink and Drive. Drunk driving kills and injures thousands of innocent people every year in South Korea. No amount of alcohol is safe before driving. If you drink, take a designated driver service, taxi, or public transport. There are no exceptions.

Introduction: Why Understanding DUI Law Matters

South Korea has one of the strictest drunk driving enforcement regimes in Asia. Following the 2018 death of university student Yoon Chang-ho — struck by a repeat drunk driver — the Korean National Assembly tightened DUI thresholds and penalties significantly. In 2026, the country is adding ignition interlock device requirements for habitual offenders.

This guide explains the 2026 South Korean DUI legal framework based on Article 44 and Article 148-2 of the Road Traffic Act and related statutes. Note: this article covers South Korean law exclusively. For other countries, consult the laws of that jurisdiction.

1. Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) Enforcement Threshold

Under Article 44(1) of the Road Traffic Act, no person may drive a motor vehicle while intoxicated. The statutory threshold is a BAC of 0.03% or higher. Prior to a 2019 amendment (part of the Yoon Chang-ho Act reforms), the limit was 0.05%.

1.1 Even One Drink Can Exceed 0.03%

For a 70 kg adult male, a single serving of soju (50 ml, 25% alcohol) can produce a BAC of 0.02–0.04% within 1–2 hours, depending on food intake and metabolism. Lighter individuals, women, and those who have not eaten may reach higher BAC levels from the same amount. If you have consumed any alcohol, do not drive.

1.2 Refusing a BAC Test

A police officer may require a breath or blood test if there is reasonable suspicion of drunk driving. Refusing the test without justifiable cause is itself a criminal offense carrying the same maximum penalties as the highest BAC category (0.2% and above).

2. Criminal Penalties Under Article 148-2 of the Road Traffic Act

South Korean DUI criminal penalties are tiered by BAC level. The figures below are statutory ranges (maximum and minimum set by law); actual sentences depend on prosecutors and courts.

BAC Level Imprisonment Fine (KRW) License Action
0.03% – under 0.08% Up to 1 year Up to 5,000,000 Suspension (100 demerit points)
0.08% – under 0.2% 1 to 2 years 5,000,000 – 10,000,000 Revocation
0.2% and above 2 to 5 years 10,000,000 – 20,000,000 Revocation + actual imprisonment standard (2026)
Refusal to test 1 to 5 years 5,000,000 – 20,000,000 Revocation

Starting in 2026, the Korean Sentencing Commission has established new guidelines for BAC 0.2%+ offenders and habitual drunk drivers, making actual imprisonment (rather than suspended sentences) the standard outcome. Even first-time offenders with very high BAC levels face a realistic risk of a prison term.

3. Administrative Penalties: License Suspension and Revocation

Administrative action on the driver's license operates independently from criminal prosecution. License suspension or revocation can take effect immediately upon apprehension, regardless of whether criminal charges have been filed.

3.1 License Suspension (BAC 0.03% – under 0.08%)

A BAC between 0.03% and 0.08% results in 100 demerit points, which translates to a 100-day suspension (1 day per point). Drivers may be able to reduce the suspension period through an appeal process or mandatory traffic safety education. The Road Traffic Authority (Korea Road Traffic Authority, KoROAD) administers these processes.

3.2 License Revocation (BAC 0.08%+ or Refusal)

A BAC of 0.08% or above, or refusal to take the BAC test, results in license revocation. Once revoked, the driver cannot reapply for a license until the statutory disqualification period expires.

4. License Disqualification Periods After Revocation

After a license revocation for DUI, the driver must wait through a mandatory disqualification period before reapplying.

Circumstances Disqualification Period
First DUI offense (no accident) 1 year
Repeat DUI offense or DUI with property damage 2 years
DUI accident causing personal injury 3 years
DUI accident causing death Up to 5 years

The disqualification period runs from the date of revocation. Driving during the disqualification period constitutes unlicensed driving, a separate criminal offense.

5. The Yoon Chang-ho Act and the Three-Strikes Rule

5.1 Background

In September 2018, Yoon Chang-ho, a university student in Busan, was struck by a drunk driver with multiple prior DUI convictions and fell into a coma. He died weeks later. Public outrage drove rapid legislative action. By late 2018, legislators passed amendments collectively known as the Yoon Chang-ho Act.

5.2 Key Changes the Act Introduced

The reforms lowered the DUI BAC threshold from 0.05% to 0.03%, significantly raised criminal penalties across all BAC tiers, and added a repeat-offender aggravated penalty provision under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes (AAPSC), commonly called the three-strikes rule. Under this provision, a second or subsequent DUI offense triggered far higher mandatory minimum sentences.

5.3 Constitutional Court Strikes Down Three-Strikes Provision

The Constitutional Court of Korea struck down the AAPSC repeat-offender aggravated penalty clauses through a series of decisions, ruling they violated the constitutional principle of proportionality between offense and punishment. As of 2026, the three-strikes aggravated penalty provisions are no longer in effect. However, the lowered BAC threshold (0.03%) and the heavier base penalties introduced by the Yoon Chang-ho Act reforms remain fully in force.

6. 2026 New Rule: Ignition Interlock Device (IID) Requirement

Effective October 24, 2026, a new conditional license system takes effect in South Korea. Drivers convicted of DUI two or more times within the previous five years who subsequently obtain a new license must install an ignition interlock device (IID) in their vehicle for a period equal to their disqualification period.

An IID requires the driver to provide a breath sample before the vehicle will start. If the BAC reading exceeds the set limit, the engine will not start. Tampering with or bypassing the device — or having another person provide the breath sample — is itself a punishable offense.

7. DUI Accidents: Civil Liability and Insurance

A DUI accident triggers civil liability for damages in addition to criminal and administrative consequences. Critically, standard Korean auto insurance policies contain exclusions or subrogation clauses for DUI accidents. The insurer may pay the victim first and then recover the full amount from the drunk driver. A serious DUI accident can therefore result in concurrent criminal penalties, license revocation, and a civil debt running into tens or hundreds of millions of won.

8. How to Prevent DUI: Practical Rules

  • Never drive after any amount of alcohol. There is no safe amount.
  • Plan your return trip before you start drinking — designated driver app, taxi, or public transit.
  • If you plan to drink, leave your car at home.
  • If staying is an option, choose accommodation nearby.
  • Actively intervene if someone you know plans to drive after drinking — call a designated driver service for them.
  • To report a drunk driver in South Korea, call 112 (Korea National Police Agency).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is drunk driving on a bicycle or electric scooter also a DUI offense in Korea?

Electric scooters (personal mobility devices) are subject to DUI enforcement under the Road Traffic Act. Cycling while drunk is also punishable by a fine. The safest rule is: do not operate any vehicle after drinking.

Q. Does a DUI fine create a criminal record in South Korea?

Yes. A DUI fine imposed through summary indictment (약식기소) is a criminal conviction and creates a criminal record. This can affect employment background checks, visa applications, and professional license eligibility. If charged, consult an attorney to understand your options before accepting any outcome.

Q. What if the other driver was drunk and hit me?

If you are the victim of a drunk driving accident, you have the right to file a criminal complaint and a civil damages claim. If the drunk driver's insurer pays you, the insurer can pursue the drunk driver for recovery. Consult an attorney to maximize your compensation.

Q. Can a foreigner in South Korea be prosecuted for DUI?

Yes. Korean traffic law applies to all drivers on Korean roads regardless of nationality. A foreign national convicted of DUI may also face immigration consequences including deportation or denial of re-entry.
