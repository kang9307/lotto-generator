Lease Renewal Refusal Grounds and How Tenants Should Respond

When the landlord says I am moving in - and then rents to somebody else

Jul 27, 2026 Law

Introduction: five words that end a tenancy

Four months before the lease expires, the tenant texts a renewal request. The reply is short: I am moving in myself. On the strength of those words the tenant books movers, transfers a child to a new school, and borrows more to cover a higher deposit somewhere else. Six months later, walking past the old building, the tenant learns that a stranger lives there - and pays far more rent than the previous tenant ever did.

This is the single most common and most bitter dispute surrounding the renewal request right in Korea. A landlord's genuine intention to occupy the home is a legitimate statutory ground for refusing renewal. The difficulty is that a tenant has no way to verify that intention at the moment it is asserted. Korean law therefore imposes damages on a landlord who evicts a tenant on self-occupancy grounds and then, without justifiable cause, leases the property to somebody else, and it also opens a channel for the tenant to check the facts afterwards.

Rather than introducing the renewal right in the abstract, this guide focuses on what a tenant should verify and do after a renewal request is refused: the full list of statutory grounds and the checkpoints for each, the requirements and arithmetic of a damages claim, the limits on rent increases, and the route from conciliation to litigation. Everything below reflects the law as of July 2026 and describes the system of the Republic of Korea. Basics such as opposing power and priority repayment rights are covered separately in our tenant rights and lease protection guide.

1. The framework: when, how often, how long

The renewal request right lives in Article 6-3 of the Housing Lease Protection Act, and the structure of that article is effectively the roadmap of every dispute. Paragraph 1 covers the request and the permissible refusals, paragraph 2 the frequency and term, paragraph 3 the conditions of the renewed lease, paragraph 4 termination after renewal, and paragraphs 5 and 6 the damages owed when a self-occupancy refusal turns out to be false.

1.1 The window: six months to two months before expiry

A tenant must request renewal between six months and two months before the lease term ends. Outside that window the right simply cannot be exercised. Note that the two-month figure applies to leases first concluded or renewed on or after December 10, 2020; older continuing contracts may still be governed by the earlier one-month deadline, so check when your contract was first signed and how it has been renewed since.

1.2 Once only, two years

The right may be exercised once, and the renewed lease is deemed to run for two years. This is the origin of the familiar two-plus-two pattern. If you have already used the right for one two-year extension, you cannot invoke the same right at the next expiry. A voluntary re-contract by agreement is a different matter and counts as a new lease rather than an exercise of the right.

1.3 The renewed lease keeps its original terms

A renewed lease is deemed concluded on the same conditions as the previous lease, except that rent and deposit may be adjusted within the range set by Article 7. In other words the landlord cannot rewrite the substance of the contract and may only propose a monetary change within the statutory limit. No new paperwork is legally required for the renewal to take effect, but recording the renewal and any revised figures in writing is far safer in practice.

One line to remember: request between six and two months before expiry, once, for two years. Miss those three numbers and nothing that follows in this article ever begins.

3. The nine grounds for refusing renewal

The default rule is that a landlord may not refuse a timely renewal request without justifiable cause. Article 6-3(1) then lists the exceptional grounds. Reasons outside that list - such as intending to sell, wanting to convert a deposit lease into monthly rent, or simply feeling the rent is below market - do not by themselves qualify.

3.1 The burden of proof sits with the landlord

Here is the principle every tenant should internalise. The grounds are exceptions to a prohibition. The rule is that renewal may not be refused; the subparagraphs are carve-outs. Whoever invokes an exception must prove it, so the landlord must establish that arrears reached two periods' rent, not the tenant that they did not. That is why the first response to a refusal should always be to demand that the ground be specified. The vaguer the stated reason, the weaker the landlord's position.

3.2 Three families of grounds

  • Tenant conduct (1, 2, 4, 5, 9): the tenant usually holds the rebuttal evidence - payment records, photographs, messenger threads.
  • Objective circumstances (3, 6, 7): agreement, destruction or redevelopment can be tested against documents such as settlement agreements, safety inspections and permits.
  • Landlord's own plans (8): self-occupancy exists only in the landlord's mind, so advance verification is impossible. That is precisely why the statute attaches a separate after-the-fact damages remedy.

4. Subparagraph 8: where the disputes cluster

Of the nine grounds, self-occupancy dominates real-world disputes, because every other ground can usually be settled with documents while this one turns on a stated intention about the future.

4.1 Who counts as the occupant

The text names the landlord and the landlord's lineal ascendants and descendants - parents and children, grandparents and grandchildren. Siblings, parents-in-law and children-in-law are not lineal relatives in this sense. If a landlord says a brother will move in, that may not satisfy subparagraph 8, so ask precisely who is moving in and keep the answer in writing.

4.2 Can a new owner rely on self-occupancy?

In a judgment rendered on December 1, 2022 (case 2021Da266631), the Supreme Court of Korea held that even after a tenant has requested renewal, a landlord may in principle, absent special circumstances and within the prescribed period, refuse the request under subparagraph 8 of the proviso to Article 6-3(1) on the ground of intended actual residence, and that a transferee who has succeeded to the landlord's position may likewise assert that ground within the refusal period if the transferee intends to reside there. So the argument that a new owner is automatically bound by an earlier renewal request does not always succeed. The judgment nevertheless presupposes action within the prescribed period, which makes the dates of transfer and of the refusal notice decisive.

4.3 There is a deadline for refusing too

A landlord's refusal is not open-ended. It must fall within the statutory window, and silence beyond that window may result in an implied renewal instead. Tenants should therefore log the exact date the refusal notice arrived, and landlords should preserve proof of when notice was given. A single date can flip the outcome.

A self-occupancy refusal is not automatically abusive. Most landlords who say they are moving in actually do, and the law squarely recognises that right. The problem is the false claim, and it can only be identified from what happens after the tenant leaves.

5. Damages when the home is let to somebody else

This is the heart of the guide. Article 6-3(5) provides that where a landlord refused renewal on the subparagraph 8 self-occupancy ground and then, before expiry of the period for which the lease would have been renewed, leases the home to a third party without justifiable cause, the landlord must compensate the loss caused by the refusal.

5.1 The four requirements

  1. The tenant made a valid renewal request within the statutory window.
  2. The landlord refused specifically on the self-occupancy ground - a refusal based on any other subparagraph falls outside this provision.
  3. The landlord leased the home to a third party before the renewed period, normally two years, expired.
  4. That letting was without justifiable cause.

The fourth element attracts the most litigation. A sudden overseas posting, illness or job loss forcing a sale can amount to justifiable cause, and the landlord may plead and prove it. Conversely, evidence that there was never any intention to live there and the aim was simply a higher rent works strongly in the tenant's favour. Courts look at the combination of objective facts: when anyone moved in, how long they stayed, and the terms of the new lease.

5.2 The greatest of three amounts

Paragraph 6 fixes the arithmetic. Unless the parties agreed on liquidated damages at the time of refusal, the award is the greatest of the following three figures.

BasisStatutory formulaIllustrative exampleCharacter
Item 1Three months of the converted monthly rent at the time of refusalConverted rent KRW 830,000 → about KRW 2.49 millionSimplest to compute; acts as a floor
Item 2Twenty-four months of the gap between the converted monthly rent obtained from the third party and the converted monthly rent at refusalNew KRW 1,000,000 less KRW 830,000 = KRW 170,000 x 24 = about KRW 4.08 millionGrows with the landlord's rent gain
Item 3The actual loss the tenant suffered from the refusalMoving costs, brokerage fees, extra loan interestPotentially the largest, but each item needs receipts

The figures above are assumptions for illustration, not outcomes of a real case. The structure, though, is clear: compute all three and claim the largest. Item 3 rewards documentation, so keeping receipts, contracts and interest statements from the move is literally worth money later.

5.3 What converted monthly rent means

In a deposit-heavy lease with little or no monthly rent, three months of rent would be a meaningless yardstick. The statute therefore uses converted monthly rent: the monthly rent plus, where a deposit exists, that deposit translated into a monthly figure using the lower of the rates in Article 7-2.

Article 7-2 caps the conversion of a deposit into monthly rent at the lower of (i) the rate prescribed by Presidential Decree, which is 10 percent per year, and (ii) the Bank of Korea's announced base rate plus the rate prescribed by Presidential Decree, which is 2 percent per year. In low-rate conditions the second limb usually governs, so check the base rate applicable at the relevant time before computing anything.

  • Assume a deposit of KRW 100 million, monthly rent of KRW 500,000 and a conversion rate of 4 percent per year.
  • Monthly value of the deposit = KRW 100 million x 4 percent / 12 ≒ KRW 330,000.
  • Converted monthly rent ≒ KRW 500,000 + KRW 330,000 = KRW 830,000.
  • Item 1 damages ≒ KRW 830,000 x 3 = about KRW 2.49 million.
The conversion rate moves with the base rate. The 4 percent above is an assumption for explanation only; a real calculation must use the base rate at the time of the refusal. Public conversion calculators run by the housing lease conciliation bodies can help.

5.4 Three things to assemble before claiming

  • Proof the stated ground was self-occupancy: texts, messenger threads, content-certified mail. Another ground takes the case outside paragraph 5.
  • The fact and timing of the third-party lease: whether it fell inside the period the lease would have run is decisive.
  • The arithmetic: the old contract, the terms of the new lease, and receipts for relocation costs.

6. How to check whether the landlord really lives there

Every damages claim starts with establishing that somebody else is in the home. Tenants have several routes.

6.1 Requesting lease information

Article 3-6 of the Act governs fixed-date stamping and the provision of lease information, and it allows a tenant whose renewal request was refused to request inspection or a written copy of information on lease contracts existing during the period for which the lease would have been renewed. The authority may not refuse such a request without justifiable cause. This provision is, in effect, a door the legislature opened so tenants can detect false self-occupancy. The exact scope and procedure are set by the Enforcement Decree, so confirm required documents with the office beforehand.

6.2 Checking who is registered at the address

Reviewing the resident registration status for the address is also common. If a third party rather than the landlord or a lineal relative is registered there, that is powerful circumstantial evidence. Eligibility to inspect and the supporting documents required are restricted by law, so ask the local community service centre first.

6.3 Other circumstantial evidence

  • Property register: check for a change of owner or new mortgages. A sale may contradict a stated plan to live there.
  • Brokerage platforms: see whether the same unit was relisted, and capture the screen with the posting date visible.
  • Building management records: changes in who is billed for maintenance charges can be indicative, though privacy rules may limit disclosure.
  • Mail and deliveries: only supporting material, never sufficient on its own.
Gathering evidence must not infringe anyone else's rights. Trespassing, covert filming and unauthorised collection or disclosure of personal data can themselves be unlawful. Prefer material obtainable through official channels, and when in doubt ask a professional about the method before acting.

7. How much can rent rise at renewal?

Plenty of disputes arise not because renewal was refused but because of the number attached to it.

7.1 The one-twentieth cap

Article 7 allows either party to seek an adjustment for the future where taxes, public charges or economic conditions have made the agreed rent or deposit inappropriate, while providing that an increase request may not exceed one twentieth of the agreed rent or deposit - that is 5 percent. Metropolitan cities, special self-governing cities and provinces may, taking local rental market conditions into account, set a lower ceiling by ordinance within that range. Local government can go below the statutory cap, not above it.

7.2 No second increase within a year

The Enforcement Decree bars an increase request within one year of the lease contract or of the last agreed increase. A demand for another rise a few months after a 5 percent renewal increase may run into this restriction.

7.3 Converting a deposit lease into monthly rent

A proposal to convert deposit into monthly rent can transform the tenant's real burden even when the headline sum looks similar. The Article 7-2 conversion ceiling discussed above applies here as well. Run the numbers, and if the implied rate exceeds the ceiling there is room to object.

7.4 If you agreed to more than 5 percent

Where the renewal followed a valid exercise of the renewal request right, the effect of an agreement exceeding 5 percent can itself be contested. Where instead the parties concluded a genuinely new contract by agreement, the cap is understood not to apply in the same way. So the first question is always whether this is a renewal or a new lease - and the answer turns on whether the renewal request right was exercised, not on the title printed on the paperwork.

8. Implied renewal is not the same right

Both routes extend the tenancy, but their legal character and the tenant's position differ. Crucially, an implied renewal is understood not to consume the renewal request right, which matters when deciding whether the right remains available at a later expiry.

9. Leaving after renewal - the three-month rule

Sometimes a tenant secures a two-year renewal and then needs to leave early. The tenant's position here is strong, because Article 6-3(4) applies Article 6-2 mutatis mutandis to the renewed lease. Under Article 6-2 the tenant may give notice of termination at any time, and the termination takes effect three months after the landlord receives that notice.

  • Because receipt is what counts, send notice by a method that fixes the date the landlord received it, such as content-certified mail.
  • The three months give the landlord time to find a replacement, and rent for that period is normally payable by the tenant.
  • Landlords sometimes insist the deposit will be withheld until a new tenant is found, or that the tenant pay the brokerage fee. Where this statutory termination right is being exercised after a renewal, the basis for such demands deserves scrutiny.
This three-month rule is the practical leverage a renewed tenant holds. Do not assume you are locked in for the full two years.

10. Resolving the dispute - from demand letter to court

10.1 Content-certified mail

Content-certified mail does not create rights by itself, but it publicly fixes what was communicated and when. It is useful at every stage: requesting renewal, demanding that a ground be specified, or claiming damages. Set out the facts chronologically, cite the relevant provisions, and state the demand and a deadline for reply. Emotional language tends to hurt rather than help.

10.2 Housing Lease Dispute Conciliation Committees

Committees established under Article 14 of the Act offer a faster and cheaper route than litigation. They sit at branches of the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, the Korea Land and Housing Corporation and the Korea Real Estate Board, and applications can be filed online, by post, by fax or in person. Where conciliation succeeds a conciliation document is drawn up, and an authenticated copy stating consent to compulsory execution is provided to have the same effect as an enforceable title. The limitation is that the process may not proceed if the other side declines to participate. Fees, timelines and jurisdiction vary by body and period, so check before filing.

10.3 Civil litigation

If conciliation fails, a damages action follows. For modest amounts the small claims procedure can cut cost and time. The material gathered earlier becomes the evidence, so a chronological file starting from the day you moved out makes any lawyer consultation dramatically more efficient.

10.4 Where to ask for help

  • Korea Legal Aid Corporation: dial 132 within Korea, or 82-54-132 from abroad. Telephone consultation is free regardless of income; in-person and video consultations are by online reservation. Confirm current operating hours with the agency.
  • Housing Lease Dispute Conciliation Committee: filing and procedural guidance.
  • Local government help desks: several municipalities run their own free lease counselling services.

11. Practical tenant checklist - records are the case

These disputes are usually won on preserved documentation rather than legal knowledge.

11.1 When requesting renewal

  • Mark six months and two months before expiry on a calendar.
  • Give notice in a form that leaves a record and state expressly that you are exercising the renewal request right.
  • Use a channel that shows the message was read, and add content-certified mail when the stakes are high.
  • After any phone conversation, summarise it by text and obtain a reply.

11.2 On receiving a refusal

  • Secure the date and the stated ground in documentary form.
  • If self-occupancy is claimed, record who will move in, since lineal kinship is an element.
  • Map the stated reason against the subparagraphs of Article 6-3(1).
  • Check whether the unit is simultaneously advertised for rent, and screenshot it.

11.3 While moving out

  • Remember that deposit return and handover of the property are concurrent obligations.
  • If you must move before the deposit is returned, consider a lease registration order first.
  • Keep receipts for moving costs, brokerage fees and additional loan interest - these support Item 3 damages.
  • Photograph or film the condition of the home just before leaving to pre-empt restoration disputes.

11.4 After moving out

  • Check the resident registration status and rental listings periodically for the two years the lease would have run.
  • Ask whether you may request lease information from the fixed-date authority.
  • Save any evidence of breach with the date visible in the capture.
  • Compute all three damages formulas before claiming and identify the largest.
  • Move quickly: evidence disappears, so seek advice and send a demand as soon as you know.

12. Landlords have legitimate rights too

This guide is written from the tenant's side, but a refusal is not inherently wrongful. Wanting to live in one's own home is the most basic exercise of property rights, and the statute recognises it directly. Most self-occupancy refusals are genuine - supporting ageing parents, a child's marriage, a return home after retirement. Plans also collapse for real reasons, which is exactly why paragraph 5 contains the qualifier without justifiable cause.

Landlords have their own discipline to observe. First, identify the ground precisely and give notice in writing. Second, if self-occupancy is the ground, actually live there and leave a trace of it. Third, if circumstances prevent the plan, keep documents proving why. Those three habits prevent most disputes from ever forming. The renewal regime is less a weapon for tenants than a set of rules letting both sides plan predictably.

13. As of July 2026 - the rules can change

Korea's renewal request right and rent cap have been the subject of continuing reform debate since they took effect. During 2025 the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport and state research institutes ran public discussions in which options such as retention, region-specific application, party autonomy and adjustment of the cap were floated. As of July 2026, however, this article assumes the current provisions described above remain in force.

Statutes are amended, ordinances can change the local cap, and accumulated case law shifts interpretation. If you are actually requesting renewal or have just received a refusal, verify the current text on the national legal information portal and pair that with professional advice.

Conclusion - a refusal can be the beginning, not the end

The renewal request right is powerful, but it is completed only by exercising it in time, testing the stated ground, and continuing to check after you leave. Because a self-occupancy claim cannot be verified on the spot, the legislature attached two devices: after-the-fact damages and a right to obtain lease information. Knowing about those two devices is the difference between merely feeling wronged and actually recovering.

Three things to remember. First, the request window is six to two months before expiry, once, for two years. Second, the grounds for refusal are only those listed, and the landlord must prove them. Third, if a tenant was evicted on self-occupancy grounds and the home was then let to somebody else without justifiable cause, damages equal the greatest of three months of converted rent, twenty-four months of the rent differential, and actual loss. All of it rests on records. One saved text message or receipt can be worth millions of won.
