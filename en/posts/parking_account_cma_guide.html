Introduction: Make Your Idle Cash Work

Keeping money in a standard demand deposit account that pays virtually no interest is a silent opportunity loss. In an environment where regular savings account rates are very low, two products stand out as efficient tools for earning interest while keeping your funds fully accessible: the parking account (파킹통장) and the CMA (Cash Management Account).

Both allow you to deposit and withdraw freely, and both earn interest daily — but they differ in important ways: the type of institution that offers them, whether your money is protected by deposit insurance, and the underlying investment mechanism. This guide explains both products — their concepts, key differences, subtypes, comparison with regular savings and time deposits, practical strategies, and pitfalls. It was first written in June 2026; the deposit insurance limit, protected products, Bank of Korea base rate, and interest tax were updated to September 29, 2026. All institutional figures refer to South Korea, where these two products come from.

Note: Interest rates, deposit limits, and conditions vary by financial institution, product, and market environment. Always verify the latest terms directly with the relevant institution. The content of this article is for general educational purposes only.

1. What Is a Parking Account?

The term "parking account" borrows from the idea of briefly parking a car — you temporarily park your cash, earn meaningful interest, and can move it out whenever needed. Unlike a regular demand deposit, a parking account typically pays significantly higher interest while maintaining complete liquidity.

1.1 Core Features

  • Free deposit and withdrawal: No maturity date — deposit and withdraw whenever you want
  • Daily interest accrual: Interest accumulates from day one, even if you deposit for only one day
  • Higher rate than demand deposits: Typically far above the near-zero rate of standard savings accounts
  • Deposit insurance: As a bank or savings bank deposit, principal plus prescribed interest on all deposits at one institution is protected up to KRW 100 million per depositor (since September 1, 2025; raised from KRW 50 million)

1.2 Who Offers Parking Accounts?

In South Korea, parking accounts are primarily offered by internet-only banks (e.g., Kakao Bank, Toss Bank, K Bank) and savings banks, which compete aggressively on rates. Some traditional commercial banks also offer similar products. Rates and conditions vary significantly, so comparing multiple products before choosing is important.

1.3 Variable Rates and Fine Print

Most parking accounts use variable interest rates, meaning the financial institution can adjust the rate at any time based on market conditions. Many products apply a tiered rate structure — a higher rate up to a certain balance limit, and a lower rate on the amount above that threshold. Promotional "bonus" rates often require meeting conditions such as using a specific card or setting up salary deposits.

2. What Is a CMA (Cash Management Account)?

A CMA (Cash Management Account), in the South Korean context, is an account offered mainly by securities firms that automatically sweeps customer deposits into short-term financial instruments to earn interest. The cash management note account offered by comprehensive finance companies is also called a CMA. It is especially useful for investors who want to earn returns on cash that is waiting to be deployed into stocks or other investments.

2.1 The Critical Distinction: Deposit Insurance

The Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation (KDIC) names the securities-firm CMA as an example of an unprotected product, and for securities firms (investment dealers and brokers) it also lists investment products such as MMFs, repurchase agreements (RP), and wrap accounts as unprotected. For comprehensive finance companies, by contrast, it lists the cash management note account (CMA) as a protected product, alongside issued notes and cover bills (checked 2026-09-29). In other words, two accounts both called CMA can differ in deposit insurance depending on which kind of company issues them. This is the most important difference from a parking account, and each CMA type also carries a different safety profile.

3. CMA Types Explained

There are four main CMA types, defined by how the underlying funds are invested. Always confirm which type you are signing up for.

3.1 RP Type (Repurchase Agreement)

The securities firm uses government bonds and high-grade corporate bonds as collateral, offering a fixed interest rate. When the term ends, the firm repurchases the securities (hence "repurchase agreement"). Because collateral is involved, this type is considered relatively safer among CMA options — but the KDIC lists repurchase agreements (RP) as unprotected, so it is not covered by deposit insurance.

3.2 MMF Type (Money Market Fund)

Customer funds are invested in a money market fund managed by an asset management company. The fund holds diversified positions in short-term government bonds, commercial paper (CP), and certificates of deposit (CDs). Returns are performance-based rather than fixed, so there is a theoretical possibility of loss in extreme market conditions. The KDIC does not protect financial products whose payout varies with investment performance. Not covered by deposit insurance.

3.3 MMW Type (Money Market Wrap)

The securities firm signs a discretionary (wrap) agreement with the client and invests the funds in short-term liquid assets such as deposits with Korea Securities Finance Corporation (KSFC). Korea's Financial Services Commission describes MMW as a wrap account that invests the entrusted assets in liquid assets such as deposits at financial institutions, commercial paper, call loans, RP, and bonds. Because the assets are short-term and highly liquid, the risk is generally regarded as low, but it is not a principal-guaranteed product. The KDIC lists wrap accounts as unprotected, so it is not covered by deposit insurance. The actual holdings differ by product, so check the product description.

3.4 Jonggeum Type (Comprehensive Finance Company)

This is the cash management note account (CMA) issued by a comprehensive finance company (종합금융사), which the KDIC lists as a protected product. Together with other protected products at the same company, it is covered up to KRW 100 million per depositor. However, few companies offer it, so the choice is limited in practice. Because it shares the CMA name with securities-firm accounts, checking the deposit insurance label in the product description is the reliable way to tell them apart.

4. Comparison: Demand Deposit, Parking Account, CMA, Time Deposit

Understanding how these products compare helps you choose the right tool for each purpose.

4.1 Demand Deposit (Regular Savings Account)

Standard bank savings accounts offer very low interest rates. They provide full liquidity and deposit insurance but offer minimal returns. Best used as a transactional account for salary deposits and automatic bill payments — not as a vehicle for storing idle cash.

4.2 Time Deposit (Fixed-Term Deposit)

Time deposits lock your money for a set period (e.g., 3, 6, or 12 months) in exchange for a higher fixed rate. Early withdrawal typically results in a significantly lower penalty rate. If you can commit your funds for a defined period without needing to access them, time deposits often offer better returns. For funds you may need at any time — like an emergency fund — parking accounts or CMAs are more appropriate.

5. Practical Strategies

5.1 Building an Emergency Fund

Financial educators commonly recommend keeping 3–6 months of living expenses in an emergency fund that can be accessed immediately (this is a common rule of thumb, not a legal or official standard, and exact amounts depend on individual circumstances). Keeping this in a demand deposit is essentially leaving money on the table. A deposit-insured parking account earns daily interest on your emergency fund without sacrificing accessibility. It is the simplest, most effective upgrade from a standard savings account for this purpose.

5.2 Managing Investment Standby Cash

For investors who frequently trade stocks, ETFs, or other securities, CMA is particularly well-suited. Funds in a CMA linked to a brokerage account automatically earn interest while waiting for the next investment opportunity — and can be deployed instantly. This is, in fact, the original design intent of the CMA product.

5.3 Splitting Across Institutions for Safety

In South Korea, deposit insurance applies per depositor per institution up to KRW 100 million. If your total deposits at one institution approach or exceed that limit, consider distributing funds across multiple institutions. For example: keep emergency funds in a parking account at one bank, and keep additional savings at another institution to maximize insured coverage. When deciding the split, remember that money in a securities-firm CMA is outside deposit insurance regardless of this limit.

5.4 Monitoring Rates Regularly

Parking account and CMA rates change frequently in response to central bank policy and market conditions. Reviewing the rates of leading products quarterly helps you avoid staying in a product that has become uncompetitive. Comparison tools such as Korea's Financial Supervisory Service financial product comparison site (finlife.fss.or.kr) can help.

6. Key Risks and Cautions

6.1 Variable Rate Risk

Both parking accounts and most CMAs use variable rates. A rate that looks attractive today may decrease tomorrow. Advertised promotional rates often require meeting specific conditions — always read the fine print.

6.2 Deposit Insurance Limits

In South Korea, the deposit insurance limit has been KRW 100 million per depositor since September 1, 2025. According to the KDIC, this covers principal plus prescribed interest on all deposits a person holds at one financial institution combined. Amounts above the limit are unprotected in case of institutional failure, and spreading funds across institutions maintains separate coverage limits. Defined-contribution and IRP retirement pensions, pension savings (trust and insurance), and accident insurance payouts are each protected separately up to KRW 100 million, even at the same institution (checked 2026-09-29).

6.3 CMA Without Deposit Insurance

RP, MMF, and MMW CMAs at securities firms are not covered by deposit insurance. While the underlying assets (government bonds, high-grade commercial paper) are generally considered low-risk, they are not risk-free in extreme market scenarios. Understand the trade-off: typically somewhat higher rates than fully insured deposits, in exchange for the absence of formal deposit insurance. For reference, the KDIC does list cash left uninvested in a brokerage account (not used to buy securities) as a protected product; this differs from CMA money that is invested in RP or MMFs.

6.4 Interest Withholding Tax

Interest income from both parking accounts and CMAs is generally subject to a 15.4% withholding tax in South Korea: according to the National Tax Service, 14% income tax plus local income tax. If annual financial income such as interest and dividends exceeds KRW 20 million, it becomes subject to comprehensive financial income taxation (checked 2026-09-29). This reduces the effective yield. Tax-exempt accounts have their own eligibility requirements.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Can I use both a parking account and a CMA at the same time?

Yes — and doing so is often more efficient. Keep your emergency fund in a deposit-insured parking account, and keep investment standby cash in a CMA linked to your brokerage account. Each product serves a distinct purpose; just remember that the securities-firm CMA side is outside deposit insurance.

Q. Can I use a CMA for everyday spending like a checking account?

Some securities firms offer debit cards linked to CMA accounts, enabling everyday purchases. However, services vary by firm, so verify available features before signing up.

Q. How often do interest rates change?

Rates can change at any time in response to central bank policy rate decisions and the financial institution's own product strategy. The Bank of Korea base rate stood at 2.50% from May 29, 2025, then rose to 2.75% on July 16, 2026 and to 3.00% on August 27, 2026 (checked 2026-09-29), so it is different now from when this article was first written in June. Institutions change their rates on no fixed cycle, so periodic comparison is worthwhile.

Q. Are there balance limits for the higher rate?

Many parking accounts apply higher rates only up to a certain balance, with lower rates on the excess. This rate cap is a separate concept from the deposit insurance limit (KRW 100 million). Always check the tiered rate structure in the product terms.

8. Conclusion: Don't Let Your Cash Sit Idle

Parking accounts and CMAs are two of the most practical tools for making idle short-term cash earn meaningful interest. The essential distinction is deposit insurance: parking accounts (bank products) are insured; securities-firm CMAs are not, though the underlying assets are generally low-risk.

For emergency funds, a deposit-insured parking account is the natural default. For investment standby cash linked to a brokerage, a CMA offers seamless integration and daily interest. Both products have variable rates and require active comparison to remain competitive. The best choice depends on your fund size, purpose, and risk tolerance — and may well be a combination of both.
