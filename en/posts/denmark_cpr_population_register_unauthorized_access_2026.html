This article restates what the notices published on October 5, 2026 by the Danish ministry responsible for higher education, science and digitalisation (UFM) and by the CPR administration (CPR-administrationen) say, in the notices' own terms. The notice states that the concrete course of events is still being investigated and that further mapping may lead to the specific information being consolidated. The notice does not say whether any company was at fault or who is behind the incident, and this article does not judge either.

Incident Overview and When Authorities Became Aware — Irregular Behavior in September and the Evening of October 2

On October 5, 2026, the Danish ministry (Forsknings-, Uddannelses- og Digitaliseringsministeriet) published a press release titled "Omfattende uautoriseret adgang til borgeres CPR-oplysninger" (extensive unauthorized access to citizens' CPR information). The news page of the Central Person Register (Det Centrale Personregister, hereafter CPR) carries an item with the same title on the same date, and that item links to the ministry's press release. The notice opens by saying that the CPR has established a serious security incident (alvorlig sikkerhedshændelse).

The Fakta section of the notice gives the following sequence. The notice dates only two of the points below; the others are given only as periods.

  • During September 2026: The notice says there was irregular behavior (uregelmæssig adfærd) in the CPR system during September. It does not say what that behavior consisted of or when it began.
  • Evening of Friday, October 2, 2026: The CPR administration became aware (blev opmærksom på) of the irregular behavior during September. The notice's wording stops at "became aware" and does not describe what prompted this or how it happened.
  • During that weekend: The notice says that during the weekend (i løbet af weekenden) the CPR administration learned that unauthorized persons had gained access to the names, addresses and CPR numbers of about 8.8 million registered persons. No day or time is given.
  • At the time of the October 5 notice: The notice states that the CPR administration has stopped the company's access, has reported the matter to the data protection authority Datatilsynet, and that the police are investigating together with relevant authorities. It does not give the date on which access was stopped or the date of the report.

The notice adds that the concrete course of events is still being examined together with relevant authorities, and it reserves that further mapping may lead to consolidation (konsolidering) of the specific information. Adding dates that are not in the timeline above would therefore mean saying something the notice does not say. This article uses the notice's own time expressions and does not fill gaps with estimated dates.

The Scale of 8.8 Million — How It Relates to a Register of About 11 Million

The central figure in the notice is "about 8.8 million" (ca. 8,8 mio.). The notice calls these "registered persons" (registrerede personer) and lists in parentheses the living (levende), the emigrated (udrejste), the deceased (døde) and "others" (m.fl.). On the notice's own wording, then, 8.8 million counts people recorded in the register, not only people who are alive and living in Denmark today.

  • Size of the register: The Fakta section says that the CPR system today holds about 11 million (ca. 11 millioner) registered citizens.
  • Who is in the register: The same sentence explains that, besides people who are alive and live in Denmark, the register also covers the deceased and citizens who have moved abroad.
  • Categories within the 8.8 million: The notice says the 8.8 million includes the living, the emigrated, the deceased and others. It gives no number for any category.

Putting the notice's two numbers side by side, 8.8 million is the number of people whose records were accessed within a register of about 11 million. This article does not compute and publish a ratio between the two. The notice gives 8.8 million as "about" (ca.) and gives the register size as "about" as well, so a quotient of two rounded figures is not something the notice states. Some readers will want to compare 8.8 million with Denmark's present population. The notice does not publish current population statistics, so that comparison lies outside the notice. What the notice does explain is that the register includes deceased persons and people who have moved abroad.

Exposed Fields and Excluded Groups — Names, Addresses, CPR Numbers and Protected Registrants

The fields the notice names as accessed are names (navne), addresses (adresser), CPR numbers (CPR-numre) and "etc." (mv.). The subtitle of the ministry's press release uses "bl.a." (among other things) before the same three items. The notice does not list any further fields beyond these three.

  • Included fields: Names, addresses, CPR numbers and whatever is covered by "etc." The notice does not spell out what "etc." stands for.
  • Excluded group: The notice says that "the review carried out shows" (Den foretagne gennemgang viser) that the unauthorized access does not cover the names and addresses of people who have chosen to be registered with name and address protection (navne- og adressebeskyttelse). This statement is presented in the notice as the result of a review.
  • Scope of the exclusion: What the sentence excludes is the "names and addresses" of protected registrants. Nothing can be read from this sentence about any other field relating to those registrants.
  • What the notice does not mention: The notice does not say whether any data beyond the three named fields and "etc." was within or outside the access. This article does not claim that any unmentioned item was included or excluded.

The most specific boundary in the notice concerns registrants with name and address protection. The notice does not explain the protection scheme itself; it only reports a review finding that the names and addresses of people who chose that registration are outside the access. How such protection is applied for and under what conditions it is granted is beyond the scope of this article.

Access Route — Misuse of One Company's Lawful Query Access

The notice describes the route in a single sentence. By misusing (misbruge) a Danish company's lawful access (lovlige adgang) to search for information in the CPR system, unauthorized persons (uvedkommende) gained access. Because the notice does not name the company, this article also refers to it only as "one company."

  • The notice's description of the route: Unauthorized persons gained access to names, addresses, CPR numbers and similar data by misusing one Danish company's lawful search access.
  • Scope of the access: The Fakta section states that the access took place within the framework of the information that private companies have access to (inden for rammerne af de oplysninger, som private virksomheder har adgang til).
  • Gist of Section 38 of the CPR Act: According to the notice, private companies with a legitimate interest (berettiget interesse) are entitled under Section 38 of the CPR Act to have information supplied from the CPR about a substantial, delimited circle of persons whom the company has identified individually in advance. The identification is made either by personal number, date of birth and name, or by name and address.
  • Further requirement and items: The same explanation adds that the private company must also be entitled to receive information under the data protection regulation (GDPR) and the Danish Data Protection Act. Which information private companies with a legitimate interest generally have access to is specified in Section 38(2), the notice says.

The notice's account ends there. How the misuse was carried out, what happened on the company's side, and by what procedure the searches were made are not in the notice, and this article does not fill those gaps with conjecture. The description of Section 38 is likewise only what the notice summarizes.

Measures Reported by the Authorities — Access Stopped, Report and Investigation, Parliamentary Committee, Security Review

The measures in the notice divide into those taken by the CPR administration and those stated by the minister. The minister for higher education, science and digitalisation, Christina Egelund, is quoted in the notice as calling this "a deeply serious incident" (en dybt alvorlig hændelse).

  • Company's access stopped: The notice says the CPR administration has stopped the company's access (har stoppet virksomhedens adgang) and, together with specialists and authorities, is mapping the course of events.
  • Report and investigation: The CPR administration has reported the matter (anmeldelse) to Datatilsynet, and the police are investigating in cooperation with relevant authorities. The notice says nothing about what Datatilsynet does after the report.
  • Parliamentary committee: The minister says she has informed (orienteret) the Danish Parliament's Committee on Business and Digitalisation (Folketingets Erhvervs- og Digitaliseringsudvalg) about the incident.
  • Preventing recurrence and security review: The minister says that initiatives have already been launched in relation to the CPR to prevent similar incidents, and that she has asked for a thorough security review of the CPR system. The Fakta section says this review is to form the basis for any further initiatives. The content of the initiatives already launched is not in the notice.

The minister says that, together with all relevant authorities, she is mapping the full extent of the incident, and she urges citizens to be attentive now and in the time ahead and to inform themselves at sikkerdigital.dk. Only the parts of the minister's statement that the notice quotes directly are reproduced here.

Official Advice — A Reminder About Callers Who Know Your Name, Address and CPR Number

In light of the course of events (I lyset af hændelsesforløbet), the notice reminds citizens of one thing. Even if someone who contacts you by telephone, e-mail or the like seems to know your name, address and CPR number, you should never hand over passwords or other confidential information (kodeord eller andre fortrolige oplysninger). This article reproduces only the items that the official guidance contains.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • The name and sector of the company whose query access was used. The notice does not name the company and refers to it only as "a private Danish company" (en privat dansk virksomhed).
  • The concrete way in which the access was misused. The notice has no description, and this article does not guess. The Ingeniøren (ing.dk) article of October 5 also quotes the same notice and adds no further facts.
  • The number of living, emigrated and deceased persons within the 8.8 million, and Denmark's current population statistics. For the latter, the Statistics Denmark population page was opened but no figure could be confirmed, so none is given here.
  • The text of Section 38 of the CPR Act. The legal database (retsinformation.dk) was opened but its text could not be read, so only the scope summarized in the UFM notice is stated.
  • What Datatilsynet did after the report, and whether it published anything. A separate notice on the Datatilsynet site could not be confirmed, and the fact of the report was verified only through the UFM and CPR notices. Who is behind the incident is not confirmed, since the notice says the investigation is at an initial stage.
