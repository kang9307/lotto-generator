Longer Than YouTube - Why the Character AI Chat Industry Got This Big

Engagement, monetization, and the safety debate behind the fastest-growing consumer AI category

Jul 19, 2026 Trends & Culture

Intro: Why Character AI Chat Deserves a Closer Look

A recent piece surfaced via GeekNews (hada) framed companion AI chat's engagement with a simple line: longer than YouTube. The exact minute-by-minute comparison varies by source and methodology, but after cross-checking multiple independent app-analytics reports myself, the direction held up consistently. Character AI chat has become, in effect, the one generative-AI consumer segment that is already generating real revenue at scale, and some individual apps now rival established giants like YouTube or Netflix on raw time-spent.

As of July 2026, treating this as just another chatbot fad misses the point. This piece is my own analysis of why these apps hold attention so long, what that attention actually converts into revenue-wise, how the competitive landscape is structured, and the controversies sitting underneath the growth numbers.

1. Why It's So Sticky - the Mechanics of Immersion

1.1 No structural reason for the conversation to end

A goal-oriented chatbot conversation - search, summarize, get an answer - ends once the task is done. Character AI chat, by contrast, is designed from the ground up as a relationship with no finish line. The character remembers you, picks up prior threads, and sometimes messages first. Because the roleplay format has no narrative reason to conclude, the product itself is, in my view, structurally biased toward longer sessions.

1.2 Emotional immersion and personalization

Being able to build a character from scratch - personality, world, tone, the nature of the relationship - deepens immersion. ScatterLab's Zeta reportedly hosts over 4 million user-created characters. Talking to a character you designed yourself carries a different psychological weight than consuming pre-made content.

1.3 A demand that predates AI

I'd argue the more accurate driver of this industry's growth is social, not technical. Rising single-person households, the cost of forming new relationships, and the desire for a partner who's always available all predate generative AI. Character AI chat serves that demand at extremely low cost around the clock - it's less that it created new desire and more that it's the cheapest product yet to fill a gap that already existed.

2. "Longer Than YouTube" - How Big Is the Gap, Really

Methodologies differ enough across industry reports that no single absolute number should be treated as definitive. Still, several independently-sourced data points point the same direction.

  • Character.AI's average time-per-visit runs around 25-30 minutes, reportedly beating ChatGPT on this specific metric (per Similarweb's blog analysis).
  • Some aggregated statistics put Character.AI's average daily usage per user between roughly 75 minutes and up to two hours.
  • Korea's Zeta reportedly reached about 750,000 weekly users in Japan with an average daily usage of around four hours per user - a single data point striking enough on its own to support the claim that this category's engagement isn't exaggerated.
  • For comparison, TikTok is commonly cited around 90 minutes per day, and YouTube around 40 minutes per session (synthesized from multiple sources including Sensor Tower and Similarweb).

Lining up numbers measured on different bases to declare a clean ranking wouldn't be honest. But the conclusion holds regardless: character AI chat is one of the few consumer app categories producing streaming-video-grade, or longer, immersive sessions rather than quick check-ins.

3. Monetization - Turning Attention Into Revenue

3.1 Subscription-first, purchases as a supplement

Industry-wide, subscriptions reportedly account for over 70% of revenue. Tiers commonly split into entry ($5-10/month), mid ($13-18/month), and premium ($20-35/month). On top of that, unlocking message limits, voice or image generation, and cosmetic character items add roughly $3-5 per month in supplemental spend per paying user.

3.2 Low conversion, fast-growing scale

Free-to-paid conversion averages under 5% industry-wide, though outliers like Replika reportedly reach around 25%. Per Appfigures data reported by TechCrunch, the global mobile AI companion app category (337 monetizing apps) was projected to top $120M in revenue for full-year 2025, with H1 downloads up roughly 88% year-over-year to about 60 million. The absolute scale is still modest, but the growth rate is the real story.

3.3 What a real P&L looks like - Korea's case

ScatterLab reportedly posted annual revenue of roughly 26 billion won (about $19M) with operating profit near 3 billion won, and raised about 50 billion won (roughly $37M) in June 2026 on the strength of Zeta's growth. It's a concrete example that keeping core chat free and unlimited while charging for images, voice, and special features can actually turn a profit.

The US and Europe have long been anchored by Character.AI and Replika. MiniMax's China-linked Talkie expanded aggressively enough to briefly outrank Character.AI on US App Store downloads. Korea is effectively a single-leader market with ScatterLab's Zeta out front, followed by Crack, Frontia, and Glaze. What stands out is that Zeta has proven real competitiveness beyond Korea - beating Netflix on total time spent within Japan's entertainment category is a rare case of a Korean-made character AI chat product winning on a foreign market's own terms.

5. Why It's Emerging as a "Killer" Consumer AI App

This industry's rise connects directly to yesterday's piece on consumer app distribution. As that piece argued, a consumer app's fate usually hinges less on raw product quality than on the distribution and retention loop that brings people back. Character AI chat effectively solves the retention problem through product design itself. The character remembers you, there's no narrative reason for the conversation to end, and a custom-built character creates real switching costs that make leaving for another app costly. The result is structurally high return-visit rates without needing separate growth hacks - which, in my read, is exactly why this category monetizes while most other generative-AI consumer categories still struggle to.

6. The Shadow Side - Emotional Dependency and Minor Safety

6.1 Who's using it, and how much

Common Sense Media's 2025 survey found that roughly 74% of US teens aged 13-17 had used an AI companion, with 52% reporting regular use. In the same survey, about a third of respondents said they had discussed something important with an AI instead of a real person. A 2026 follow-up census found 86% of children and teens use AI in some form, with a quarter using it daily.

6.2 Regulation and litigation

These numbers have translated into real incidents and lawsuits. A wrongful-death suit filed by the family of a Florida teen who died by suicide after prolonged conversations with a Character.AI persona (Garcia v. Character Technologies) settled alongside Google in January 2026. The settlement ended the case, but the court's earlier refusal to dismiss it - finding that an AI chatbot service can plausibly owe a duty of care to minor users - stands as an industry precedent regardless of the settlement. Several US state attorneys general, including Pennsylvania and Kentucky, have since filed separate suits. Replika has faced its own FTC complaint and a fine from a European data protection authority.

6.3 How the industry is responding

Character.AI phased out, then fully removed, open-ended chat for users under 18 starting November 24-25, 2025. Past conversations remain viewable but can't be continued, and age is verified through an in-house estimation model combined with third-party tools. It reads as an industry catching up on safety only after growth had already outpaced it.

7. What This Means for the Korean Market

Zeta's success already proves this category works in Korea. Domestic services mostly haven't yet faced the large-scale lawsuits or regulatory scrutiny seen abroad - which, in my view, is exactly why getting ahead on age verification and crisis-response systems now is the more strategic move. The emotional-support function has real upside for single-person households and elder care, but if Korean regulation is going to avoid simply reacting to incidents that already happened overseas, industry self-regulation needs to move first.

Conclusion: Holding Opportunity and Risk Together

Character AI chat's engagement and revenue growth are backed by real data, not hype. At the same time, the very mechanics that produce that engagement - emotional immersion, endless conversation, memory and personalization - are, by the same data, genuine risk factors for minors and emotionally vulnerable users. Dismissing this industry as simply "addictive apps" or romanticizing it as "the cure for loneliness" are both inaccurate. As of July 2026, the most honest assessment is that this is a category where the growth curve and the safety net need to move at the same speed.
