Student Loan Repayment in Korea - Start by Finding Out Which Loan You Hold

How the system is structured, how repayment works, and what you can do before falling behind

2026-07-31 Life / Practical

Introduction: graduated, and no idea when repayment starts

A few months have passed since graduation. Nothing is leaving the bank account, and it is impossible to tell whether that means nothing is due yet or something has been missed. When the loan was applied for at university it was a matter of ticking boxes on a form, and the memory of what was chosen has gone soft. Asking around produces two different answers. One person says it gets deducted automatically once you have a job, another says you pay every month starting a few years after graduation.

Both are correct. They are simply describing different loans.

The point of this article is not to say pay it off fast. It is to say start by finding out which type of loan you hold. In the Korean system, the loan type determines when repayment begins, what happens when income is low, and which relief and deferment programmes are available. A repayment plan built without knowing the type is likely to be pointing in the wrong direction.

And one more thing. The rules change every year. The threshold income is published annually and interest rates are announced each semester. So the aim here is not to make anyone memorise current figures. It is to hand over the list of items to check and the official channels for checking them. Every number below carries its verification date, and the current value must be re-checked.

1. Split the two tracks first - which one do you hold

This is the starting point for every other judgement. Student loans from the Korea Student Aid Foundation divide broadly into the income contingent loan and the general repayment loan. The first is often abbreviated to ICL.

The names describe the structure. The income contingent loan starts being repaid when income passes a threshold; the general repayment loan starts when the agreed period ends. One is tied to income, the other to the calendar.

1.1 How to check, first of all

  • Log in to the Korea Student Aid Foundation site and read the product name on your disbursement records. For most people that settles it
  • You may have taken different products in different semesters. Do not judge from one semester; check every semester in which you borrowed
  • Check tuition loans and living expense loans separately. They can be different products
  • Some borrowers genuinely hold both types at once. Each loan then follows its own rules, so a single blanket plan will drift off course
  • Do not fold non student loan debts into the same calculation. Card balances and personal credit loans follow entirely different rules

The third and fourth rows carry the weight. The income contingent loan moves with the semester rate and defers repayment when income is low; the general repayment loan fixes the rate at disbursement but must be paid as agreed whatever your income does. That difference opens up decisively in situations such as job loss or unpaid leave.

2. Income contingent - the threshold income

The first concept to grasp is the repayment threshold income. When the annual income amount exceeds it, mandatory repayment begins; below it, principal and interest repayment is deferred.

What matters is that this is not a fixed number. The threshold is published each year by the Minister of Education, taking the standard median income and inflation into account. It moves annually. That is exactly why a figure found in an online post cannot be taken at face value.

Income yearThreshold (annual income amount)Gross salary equivalentSource and verification date

2026NEEDS VERIFICATIONNEEDS VERIFICATIONCheck the Ministry of Education notice

The most common mistake. It is worth pausing on why two numbers are published side by side for the same year. The annual income amount and gross salary are different figures. Comparing the salary written on a payslip or employment contract directly against the threshold will send the judgement off course. The basis is the annual income amount, and the gross salary figure shown alongside it is a reference conversion. Your own income classification must be confirmed with institutional guidance and your income records.

So what happens while income stays under the threshold? Foundation guidance states that principal and interest repayment is deferred until income exceeds the threshold. There is one point that is easy to misread here.

  • Repayment is deferred; interest is not switched off. Guidance states that interest arises from the moment of disbursement and is calculated daily even before mandatory repayment begins
  • However, a separate interest exemption exists for borrowers under deferment for specific grounds. Section 7 covers it
  • The repayment obligation itself arises from disbursement. This is not money that is unrelated to you until you have a job; only the timing of repayment is tied to income
  • Guidance notes that mandatory repayment can begin even while still enrolled, if the income criterion is exceeded

3. How the mandatory amount is calculated

The formula itself is simple. As set out in Foundation guidance:

Mandatory repayment = (annual income amount － threshold income) × repayment rate － voluntary repayments made for that income year

Three conditions attach to it.

  • The repayment rate is 20% for undergraduate loans and 25% for graduate loans. At the same income, a graduate loan costs more per year
  • The minimum mandatory repayment is KRW 360,000 per year, or KRW 30,000 a month. Even when the computed figure falls below that, this amount is payable. Guidance states it does not apply in the inherited or gifted property case
  • Amounts paid voluntarily are deducted from the mandatory figure for the same income year. Money paid early is not lost

3.1 Worked on the 2025 threshold

The table applies the formula directly to the confirmed 2025 threshold of KRW 18.98 million. The amount actually billed must be confirmed through the tax authority notice. What follows is only an illustration of the structure.

Two things to read off. First, just above the threshold the minimum applies. At the 20% rate that band runs to roughly KRW 20.78 million of annual income amount, and at 25% to roughly KRW 20.42 million. Second, the rate applies only to the excess. It is not 20% of total income but 20% of the part above the threshold. Confusing the two leads many people to imagine a burden far larger than the real one.

And again: when the threshold changes, every figure above changes. The table exists to show the structure, not to be memorised.

4. General repayment - grace period and repayment period

The general repayment loan is closer to a conventional loan in shape. You elect a grace period and a repayment period, and pay according to that agreement. What was confirmed in Foundation guidance is as follows.

ItemConfirmed content (as of 2026-07-31)
Interest rate1.7% per year for the second semester of 2026 (fixed)
Maximum grace periodRemaining years of enrolment + 3 base years + 3 additional years
Maximum loan term20 years for university and graduate students (up to 10 years grace + up to 10 years repayment)
Choosing the termElected by the borrower in whole years, within the maximum
Repayment methodEqual principal or equal total payment, your choice
Living expense limitKRW 2 million per semester
Tuition limitFull required amount for the semester, with institutional caps of KRW 40 million to 120 million
Payment channelsAutomatic transfer and virtual account

4.1 Paying interest only, versus paying principal and interest

The two periods have different characters, and it is worth making that explicit.

  • The grace period is when interest only is paid. Principal does not fall. Guidance describes it as the interest payment period
  • The repayment period is when principal and interest are paid together. Only from then does the principal actually shrink
  • Electing a long grace period lowers the monthly outflow, but interest keeps accruing throughout it. Total burden and monthly burden move in opposite directions
  • Equal principal tends to be heavier at the start and lighter later, while equal total payment tends to keep the monthly figure steady with interest weighted toward the early years. Which suits you depends on the shape of your income
  • A longer term lightens the monthly figure but lengthens the period over which interest is owed, and both need weighing together

There is a point here where a flat verdict should be avoided. The advice to keep the grace period short is sound for someone with a stable income and slack in the budget, but if the monthly figure becomes unmanageable in the unsettled period right after graduation and the account slides into arrears, that costs far more. The choice comes out of the balance between total interest and the risk of falling behind.

5. Interest rates - why the mechanism matters more than the value

The interest rate is the fastest ageing item in this article. Understanding how it is set stays useful for longer than the value itself.

  • The general repayment loan is fixed rate. The confirmed value is 1.7% per year for the second semester of 2026, sourced from the Korea Student Aid Foundation general repayment loan page and verified on 31 July 2026
  • Fixed rate means the rate of the semester in which the loan was drawn continues to apply to that loan. So if you borrowed across several semesters you may hold several loans at several different rates
  • The income contingent loan is stated to carry a variable rate each semester, calculated as simple interest. The value moves semester to semester
  • The income contingent rates for the 2026 academic year could not be confirmed here. That is NEEDS VERIFICATION. The relevant semester notice has to be checked directly
  • The channel is Korea Student Aid Foundation loan guidance and the semester notices. A value quoted in a blog or a summary article may already belong to a past semester

In short: the rate on your loans differs by product and by disbursement semester. It cannot be reduced to one number, so any repayment plan should start from checking the rate loan by loan.

6. Is early repayment always the better move

This is the most frequent question and the hardest to answer flatly. Saying that paying it off fast is always better is difficult to support, because the balance tips differently with income, balance, rate and loan type.

Instead of a verdict, here are the criteria. Reading each row against your own situation shows the direction.

One combination deserves particular care: an income contingent loan where current income sits below the threshold and the borrower is simultaneously within the interest exemption. Rushing to repay in that position is hard to call clearly advantageous. Conversely, where the loan is general repayment, no higher rate debt exists, and the emergency fund is comfortable, the case for early repayment becomes relatively clear.

  • Whether a fee applies to the general repayment loan could not be confirmed. That is NEEDS VERIFICATION
  • Under the income contingent loan, voluntary payments are deducted from the mandatory amount for the same income year, so money paid early does not go out twice
  • General principles of saving and investing are outside the scope here. This article covers the student loan system and nothing else

7. Relief and deferment - what was confirmed, and how to apply

This is the area where not knowing costs money. Only confirmed programmes are described.

7.1 Interest exemption on the income contingent loan

  • Legal basis. Foundation guidance cites the Special Act on Student Loans Repayment Contingent on Income, Article 18(7), items 2 through 5
  • Eligibility by status. Basic livelihood and near-poverty recipients, multi-child households, self-reliance support recipients, and households at or below 130% of the standard median income
  • Eligibility by ground. Borrowers under repayment deferment for reasons such as business closure, job loss, retirement, parental leave, or residence in a declared disaster area
  • Scope. The normal contractual interest arising during the deferment period, stated to apply to interest arising on or after 1 July 2024
  • Application. Guidance states that the Foundation processes the exemption based on eligibility records, without a separate application from the individual
  • Verification is still yours. Even where processing is automatic, it is safer to confirm that your own eligibility record was in fact applied

There is a structure worth noticing. The interest exemption is tied to deferment. Qualifying on a ground and obtaining deferment comes first, and the interest of that deferral period is then waived. Which means that if you do not apply for deferment when a ground arises, the exemption does not follow either.

7.2 Items this article could not confirm

For accuracy, what could not be confirmed is recorded as such. All of the following must be checked directly through official channels.

  • The published threshold income for the 2026 income year → check the Ministry of Education notice and Foundation guidance
  • Income contingent loan rates for the 2026 academic year semesters → check the semester notices
  • The late payment damages rate and how it is calculated → check your loan contract and institutional guidance
  • The precise conditions for loss of benefit of time → check institutional guidance

  • Detailed eligibility for each Credit Counseling and Recovery Service programme → check with that body (its page could not be opened at the time of writing)

8. What changes if you fall behind - and what comes before that

The order is deliberately reversed here. What you can do before arrears matters more than what happens after them, because once arrears begin the options narrow sharply.

8.1 What you can do before arrears

  • If a due date is approaching and you cannot manage it, contact the Korea Student Aid Foundation before it becomes delinquent. That is the single most important line here
  • Check whether you match a deferment ground. Grounds given in guidance include business closure, job loss, retirement, parental leave, and residence in a declared disaster area
  • If you do, confirm the application procedure and the documents required
  • If deferment is granted, confirm whether you also fall within the interest exemption (see section 7)
  • If your income has fallen, check that the basis of the income contingent assessment is correct. A separate objection procedure is published
  • Check the balance in the automatic debit account. It is a surprisingly common cause of arrears
  • Update your registered phone number and address. Out of date details mean the notices never reach you

8.2 Once arrears begin (confirmed scope)

The following was confirmed on the Korea Student Aid Foundation delinquency notice page for the general repayment loan.

ItemConfirmed content
Credit registration triggerArrears of six months or more on a general repayment student loan lead to registration of delinquency information with the Korea Federation of Banks
Where maturity has passedThree months or more triggers registration
Disadvantages on registrationPossible restriction of dealings with other financial institutions, possible ineligibility for future student loans, possible disadvantage in employment
Other guidanceAdverse effect on personal credit standing, collection demands and claim preservation procedures

The second row is easy to miss. Where the maturity date has already passed, the trigger shortens from six months to three. In other words, arrears after maturity reach credit registration faster.

9. Debt adjustment - the official route, and spotting illegal advertising

Long-term arrears change the conversation. At that stage, where you turn makes a large difference to the outcome. Start with the official programmes.

9.1 Where the official routes are

  • Korea Student Aid Foundation credit recovery support. Because it addresses the student loan itself, this is the first place to look. An instalment repayment programme, a reduction programme for late payment damages, and support for restoring credit judgement information are described as being in operation
  • The instalment programme is described as being for cases where, on grounds such as six months or more of arrears, benefit of time has been lost so the full balance would otherwise fall due at once, allowing payment in parts to ease the burden. The detailed conditions must be confirmed directly with the institution
  • ★One condition deserves attention. Guidance states that arrears of three months or more on the instalment payments void the existing agreement and re-register the credit judgement information. Keeping the agreement matters more than signing it, which is precisely why the agreed figure has to be one you can actually sustain
  • The Credit Counseling and Recovery Service is the public body handling debt adjustment. However, its page could not be opened at the time of writing, so programme conditions are left as NEEDS VERIFICATION and must be checked on the body official website
  • The Financial Supervisory Service operates reporting and guidance channels for illegal financial advertising and illegal private lending
  • For consultation, always contact the institution yourself, on the number published on its official website

9.2 ★How to spot illegal or advertising channels

Searching about debt brings advertising to the front of the results, and a good share of it is not an official route. The wording is enough to filter it.

If the number the other party gave you differs from the one on the official site, stop the conversation at that point. This one habit filters out most impersonation.

10. Working out where you stand

Finally, the order of execution, split into what to check on the Foundation site and what to prepare before calling.

10.1 What to check on the site

  • Product name — income contingent or general repayment, for every semester
  • Disbursement date and amount, and the remaining principal
  • The applicable rate, and whether it is fixed or variable
  • Grace period, repayment period and maturity date (for the general repayment loan)
  • Repayment method — equal principal or equal total payment
  • The automatic debit account and its balance
  • Whether your registered phone number and address are current
  • For an income contingent loan, check the mandatory amount through the National Tax Service ICL channel. Foundation guidance points to that channel as well

10.2 What to prepare before calling

  • Recent income records — on an annual income amount basis, not gross salary
  • Whether you meet a relief or deferment ground, and the documents proving it
  • If in arrears, the start date and the number of months
  • The rates and balances of your other debts — needed to judge the order of repayment
  • Write your questions out as sentences beforehand. They are easy to lose track of mid-call
  • Record what you are told, with the date. When rules change, the date the guidance belonged to becomes important

If the distinction between annual income amount and gross salary is the confusing part, getting a feel for salary structure first can help. The net pay calculator in the tool list below is usable for that. Note, though, that its output is a reference figure and is not a basis for determining the threshold assessment. That determination requires institutional confirmation.

Frequently asked questions

Does repayment start automatically on graduation?

It depends on the loan type. For the income contingent loan the trigger is income, not graduation: mandatory repayment begins when the annual income amount exceeds the threshold. For the general repayment loan the trigger is the end of the elected grace period. So the experience of graduating and seeing nothing leave the account can arise under either, for entirely different reasons.

Can a repayment obligation arise while still enrolled?

For the income contingent loan, guidance states that the repayment obligation arises from the moment of disbursement and that mandatory repayment can begin even during enrolment if the income criterion is exceeded. The point to hold on to is that the trigger is income rather than enrolment status.

If my income is below the threshold, does interest stop too?

That is hard to support. Guidance indicates that repayment is deferred, not that interest stops accruing: interest arises from disbursement and is calculated daily even before mandatory repayment begins. That said, a separate interest exemption exists for borrowers under deferment on specific grounds, so it is worth checking whether you fall within it.

I borrowed across several semesters. Are the rates all different?

The general repayment loan is fixed rate, so the rate of the disbursement semester continues to apply to that loan. Borrowing across several semesters can therefore leave you holding several loans at several different rates. When planning repayment it is more accurate to check loan by loan than to average them into one figure.

If the rules change annually, how long are these numbers valid?

The figures here were verified on 31 July 2026, and each carries its date and source. The threshold income is published annually and interest rates are announced each semester. The purpose of this article is therefore not to have anyone memorise numbers but to leave behind which items to check and where. Before acting, confirm the current values through official channels.

In summary - memorise the channel, not the number

If one thing survives from this article, let it be this: find out which type of loan you hold before deciding to pay it off fast.

  • Split the type first. Income contingent and general repayment differ in when repayment starts, in what happens when income is low, and in relief and deferment
  • For the income contingent loan the threshold is the gate, and it is published annually. Confirmed values are KRW 17.52 million for 2024 and KRW 18.98 million for 2025; 2026 is NEEDS VERIFICATION
  • Do not confuse gross salary with the annual income amount. The basis is the annual income amount
  • Under general repayment you elect the grace and repayment periods. Monthly burden and total interest move in opposite directions
  • Early repayment is not automatically better. Weigh the emergency fund, the rates on other debts, the loan type and relief eligibility together
  • There are options before arrears. Deferment and adjustment offer more choices beforehand. If it is getting difficult, make contact before the account falls behind
  • Take debt adjustment through official routes. Advance fee demands, guaranteed deletion of credit information and promised write-offs are not to be trusted. Find the number on the official site and call back yourself
  • The rules change every year. Which is why the verification path outlives the numbers

## 2. ICL — the threshold income

The repayment threshold income is published annually by the Minister of Education, taking the standard median income and inflation into account. It therefore changes every year.

| Income year | Threshold (annual income amount) | Gross salary equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | KRW 17.52 million | KRW 26.79 million |
| 2025 | KRW 18.98 million | KRW 28.51 million |
| 2026 | NEEDS VERIFICATION (check the notice) | NEEDS VERIFICATION |

★ A common mistake: **annual income amount and gross salary are different figures.** That is exactly why two numbers appear side by side for the same year. Do not compare the salary figure on your payslip directly against the threshold.

### The mandatory repayment formula

```
Mandatory repayment = (annual income amount - threshold income) × repayment rate - voluntary repayments made for that income year
```

- Repayment rate: undergraduate loans **20%**, graduate loans **25%**
- Minimum mandatory repayment: **KRW 360,000 per year** (KRW 30,000 per month). It does not apply to the inherited or gifted property case
- If income does not exceed the threshold, no mandatory repayment arises; principal and interest stay deferred until it does

Once arrears begin, the available options narrow sharply. Order matters.

- [ ] A due date is approaching and you cannot pay → contact the Foundation **before** it becomes delinquent
- [ ] Check whether your situation matches a deferment ground (business closure, job loss, retirement, parental leave, declared disaster area residence, and similar)
- [ ] If it does, confirm the application procedure and the documents required
- [ ] If deferment is granted, also confirm whether you fall within the interest exemption
- [ ] If your income has fallen, check that the basis of your ICL assessment is correct; a separate objection procedure is published
- [ ] Arrears sometimes start from something as simple as an empty auto-debit account. Check the account first
- [ ] Outdated contact details mean notices never reach you. Update your registered information

## 7. What changes if you fall behind (confirmed scope)

- For a General Repayment student loan, arrears of **six months or more** result in registration of delinquency information with the Korea Federation of Banks
- However, where the maturity date has already passed, **three months or more** triggers registration
- Disadvantages stated on registration: possible restriction of dealings with other financial institutions, possible ineligibility for future student loans, and possible disadvantage in employment
- Also stated: adverse effect on personal credit standing, and collection demands and claim preservation procedures
- The specific late payment damages rate was not present on that notice page → **confirm against your contract and the institution**

## 8. Debt adjustment — where the official route sits

If you are already in long-term arrears, look at the official programmes first.

- **Korea Student Aid Foundation credit recovery support**: an instalment repayment programme, a reduction programme for late payment damages, and support for restoring credit judgement information are operated
- The instalment programme is described as being for cases where benefit of time has been lost, through six months or more of arrears among other grounds, so that the full balance would otherwise fall due at once
- ★Note: it is stated that arrears of **three months or more on the instalment payments void the agreement and re-register the credit judgement information**. Keeping the agreement matters more than signing it
- The **Credit Counseling and Recovery Service** is the public body handling debt adjustment. That page could not be opened at the time of writing, so its programme conditions are left as NEEDS VERIFICATION
- For consultation, always contact the institution through the number published on its **official website**

## 9. ★How to spot illegal or advertising channels

If you see any of the following, do not trust it. Verify directly with the official institution.

- ❌ Demands a **fee or retainer paid up front**
- ❌ Claims to **guarantee deletion of credit information**
- ❌ Promises **debt write-off or full forgiveness as a certainty**
- ❌ Impersonates a public body, or implies partnership or delegation from one
- ❌ **Approaches you first** by text, messenger, or phone and asks for personal, account, or authentication details
- ❌ Offers to hold your ID, certificate, or account password on your behalf
- ❌ Rushes the consultation and **gives you no time to think**
- ❌ Names a **specific product** and urges you to refinance into it
- ✅ How to check: do not call a number found through a search. Go to the institution's **official website directly** and call the number published there

## 10. Prepare this before you call (checklist)

- [ ] Product name (ICL or General Repayment), for every semester
- [ ] Disbursement date, amount, and remaining principal
- [ ] The applicable rate, and whether it is fixed or variable
- [ ] Grace period, repayment period, and maturity date (for General Repayment)
- [ ] Repayment method (equal principal or equal total payment)
- [ ] Recent income records, on an annual income amount basis
- [ ] Whether you meet a relief or deferment ground, and the documents proving it
- [ ] If in arrears, the start date and the number of months
- [ ] Your auto-debit account and its balance
- [ ] Whether your registered contact details are current

## 11. Official verification channels

- Korea Student Aid Foundation — repayment guidance, interest exemption, delinquency notice, credit recovery support
- National Tax Service ICL portal — checking and paying the ICL mandatory amount
- Ministry of Education — the annual threshold income notice
- Credit Counseling and Recovery Service — debt adjustment
- Financial Supervisory Service — reporting and guidance on illegal financial advertising and illegal private lending

Reaching each institution through its **official domain directly**, rather than through a search box, is the safer habit.

## 12. What not to do

- ❌ Rushing to repay without identifying the loan type — under ICL a low income may mean no mandatory amount at all
- ❌ Trusting numbers found in online posts — the threshold and the rates **change every year**
- ❌ Comparing gross salary directly against the threshold — the basis is the annual income amount
- ❌ Emptying your emergency fund to repay early — borrowing again may cost more
- ❌ Making contact only after falling behind — deferment and adjustment offer more options **before** arrears
- ❌ Handing personal details to whoever contacted you first — always call back on the official number yourself
