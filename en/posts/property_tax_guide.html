Korea Property Tax Guide: Jaesan-se and Jongbuse Explained

Tax base, rates, payment schedule, and one-home reliefs for Korea's two property-holding taxes

2026-06-17 Finance & Economy

Introduction: Two Taxes on Owning Property in Korea

Owning real property in Korea means paying at least one, and sometimes two, annual holding taxes. The first is jaesan-se (재산세), a local tax levied by the municipality on virtually all types of property. The second is jongbuse (종합부동산세, often abbreviated as 종부세), a national tax imposed on top of jaesan-se when the combined official price of all property held nationwide exceeds a threshold. Together these two make up Korea's boyu-se, or property-holding tax system.

This article explains both taxes in plain terms: what they cover, how the tax base is calculated, what the rates look like, when to pay, and what reliefs are available to one-household one-home owners. The focus is Korea's tax system as it is generally understood for 2026. Tax law changes frequently, so treat all figures here as indicative and always verify current rules with the relevant authorities before making any decision.

2. Jaesan-se — How It Works

2.1 Assessment Date: 1 June

The person who owns a property on 1 June each year is liable for that year's jaesan-se. This means that if you sell before 1 June, the buyer (who takes ownership on that date) pays; if you sell after 1 June, you still pay for the full year. In practice, the exact transfer date (the date of final payment / title registration) determines who is assessed. The 1 June cut-off is therefore a significant factor when negotiating sale timing.

2.2 Tax Base: Official Price × Fair Market Value Ratio

The tax base for jaesan-se is not the market price but the official price (gongsi gagyeok) published annually by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport — multiplied by the fair market value ratio (gongjeong sijang gaeaek biyul). For general housing the ratio is commonly 60%, while a lower special ratio (around 43–45%) has applied to one-household one-home owners in recent years. These ratios are set annually by the government and can change.

Tax base = Official price × Fair market value ratio (commonly 60% for general housing; lower special ratio for eligible one-home owners)

2.4 Payment Schedule

  • 16–31 July: Half of housing tax + all non-residential building, vessel, and aircraft tax
  • 16–30 September: Remaining housing half + all land tax
  • If the total housing tax is 200,000 KRW or less, the full amount is collected in July.

3. Jongbuse — How It Works

3.1 Who Is Subject to Jongbuse?

Jongbuse applies when the combined official price of all housing held nationwide as of 1 June exceeds the basic deduction. For most owners the commonly cited threshold is 900 million KRW; for a one-household one-home owner it is 1.2 billion KRW. Separate thresholds apply to different categories of land (generally 5 billion KRW for general aggregate land and 8 billion KRW for separately aggregated commercial land).

These deduction amounts are set by law and subject to change. The figures given here are those commonly cited as of 2026.

3.2 Tax Base

Jongbuse tax base = (Sum of official prices of all housing nationwide − Basic deduction) × Fair market value ratio (generally 60% as of 2026).

3.4 Reliefs for One-Household One-Home Owners

Beyond the larger basic deduction (1.2 billion KRW), eligible one-household one-home owners can claim tax credits for age and long-term holding, combined up to a maximum of 80%:

  • Age credit: approximately 20% (age 60+), 30% (age 65+), 40% (age 70+)
  • Long-term holding credit: approximately 20% (5+ years), 40% (10+ years), 50% (15+ years)
  • Combined cap: 80%
Credit rates and eligibility requirements may change. Verify with the NTS (hometax.go.kr or 1588-0060) before relying on these figures.

3.5 Payment and Filing

  • Payment period: Approximately 1–15 December each year
  • The NTS issues an assessment notice; taxpayers may also self-assess and pay voluntarily.
  • Instalment option: If the tax exceeds 2.5 million KRW, payment can be split over up to six months after the deadline.
  • Portal: Hometax (hometax.go.kr)

4. The Role of the Official Price

Both taxes are calculated from the official price (gongsi gagyeok), not the transaction or market price. This government-assessed value is published annually by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport as of 1 January. Historically set below market value, the gap between official and market prices has been an ongoing policy debate. You can look up the official price for any property at the Real Estate Public Price Information portal (realtyprice.kr).

Changes in either the official price or the fair market value ratio can raise or lower the tax burden even if market prices stay flat — a key dynamic to keep in mind when estimating holding costs.

5. Exclusion from Jongbuse Aggregation (Hapsan Baeje)

Certain properties — registered rental homes, unsold new homes, company housing — can be excluded from the count used to determine jongbuse liability under a scheme called hapsan baeje (합산배제). Applications are typically filed in September each year via Hometax. Missing the window means losing that year's benefit. Requirements are detailed and fact-specific; consult a tax professional before applying.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. I bought a home on 5 June. Do I pay jaesan-se this year?

No. The assessment date is 1 June. If ownership transferred after 1 June (i.e., the seller held it on that date), the seller is liable for the full year's jaesan-se. You become liable from next year. Confirm the exact transfer date with your legal adviser.

Q. Is there a simple way to check whether I owe jongbuse?

The NTS provides a mock calculation tool on Hometax each year. Input your properties' official prices, and the tool estimates your liability. The formal assessment notice arrives in late November or early December.

Q. Does jaesan-se paid get credited against jongbuse?

Yes. Korean tax law includes a provision crediting a portion of jaesan-se already paid against the jongbuse amount owed, preventing full double counting. The exact credit mechanics are set out in the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax Act.

Q. What if I own the property jointly with my spouse?

For jaesan-se, each co-owner is assessed proportionally to their share. For jongbuse, joint ownership creates complexity around the one-household one-home determination; in some configurations each owner's share is treated individually, affecting which threshold applies. This is one of the most frequently misunderstood areas — consult a tax accountant before drawing conclusions.

Conclusion: 1 June is the Key Date

Korea's property-holding tax calendar revolves around 1 June. Whoever holds a property on that date pays the full year's jaesan-se (due in July and September) and, if their combined holdings exceed the threshold, jongbuse (due in December). One-home owners have meaningful reliefs available under both taxes, but the rules change regularly. Make a habit of checking the latest official guidance each year before the assessment season begins.
