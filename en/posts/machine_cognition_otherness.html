How Much of an Alien Mind Is Observed - Sorting the Claims Into Three Layers

Observed behaviour, claims about model internals, and metaphor. Mix the three and the investigation stops

2026-09-07 Trends & Culture

On 6 September 2026 an essay titled An Alien Mind appeared on openai.com. Korean tech aggregators carried it as 「an unfamiliar intellect, different from ours」. Several outlets attributed the piece to Jakub Pachocki, chief scientist at OpenAI.

Writing of this kind tends to produce one of two reactions. The first is 「so it really is something we cannot know」. The second is 「it is picking the next word by probability, and the rest is overstatement」. The two look like opposites, yet they do the same work. Each covers the subject with a single sentence and then stops looking.

So the yardstick used below is not agreement or disagreement. There is one axis: within the claim that machine cognition is unfamiliar, what is confirmed by observation and what is metaphor. Hold that yardstick against the text and the handling changes sentence by sentence. Some sentences can be re-measured. Some have no numbers attached yet. Some were never candidates for numbers in the first place.

What follows sorts the essay's claims into layers and nothing more. There is no verdict on whether the essay is right or wrong, and no ranking of one company or model against another. Every figure quoted in the observational sections comes from a published paper or evaluation document, with the sample and conditions attached. Items whose wording could not be pinned down are gathered in a later section with the reason given.

What the word unfamiliar actually points at

The essay runs in six sections: an intellect we do not fully understand, teaching machines to love, monitoring generalisation, scalable defence, pacing recursive self-improvement, and what comes next. Even the headings are enough to place the genre. This is policy commentary, not an experimental report.

Naming the genre first matters. In policy commentary, observation, forecast and prescription flow into one another inside a single paragraph, and that is the grammar of the form rather than a defect in it. The trouble starts when the piece is carried onward. Whoever carries it tends to lift the strongest sentence, and the strongest sentence is almost always the metaphor. 「An alien mind」 survives; the conditions that stood beside it fall away. The same counting exercise applies here that applies whenever a headline sentence has to earn the conditions that make it a proposition.

And 「unfamiliar」 is not one kind of statement to begin with. At least three sit stacked under the word: a statement that behaviour unlike human behaviour is actually observed, a statement that the inside of a model is built unlike the inside of a person, and a statement that likens that difference to an alien or a stranger. The three are tested in entirely different ways. The first you measure, the second you can only handle once an operational definition exists, and the third was never a candidate for true or false.

Sorting the claims into three layers

The layers are drawn on one criterion: what would a third party have to do in order to handle this sentence at all.

  • Layer ⓐ, observed behaviour - anything that yields a number if you supply inputs, collect outputs and count. Benchmark scores, error distributions, and changes under altered conditions belong here. The method of adjudication is already inside the sentence.
  • Layer ⓑ, claims about internals - what sits where inside the weights and activations. To handle one of these you must first define the thing in a measurable form. Without a definition the question does not arise, let alone get answered.
  • Layer ⓒ, metaphor and figure - 「alien mind」, 「grown」, 「teaching machines to love」. These are aids to understanding, and useful as aids. The only thing to keep in view is that they are not objects to which values attach.

Applying that yardstick to the essay gives the table below. The left column restates a claim the essay makes; the right column says what a third party can do with it.

Observable ground one - where the errors fall

Down into layer ⓐ. The first place where 「different from people」 turns into a number is where the errors fall. That both people and machines make mistakes is no news. What differs is the distribution of the places where the mistakes happen.

Keep only the tasks that are easy for people and hard for machines

ARC-AGI was built to aim straight at that difference in distribution. Its design principle is literally easy for humans, hard for AI. The ARC-AGI-2 technical report, released in May 2025, nails the property down by attaching human testing results. Its abstract describes a benchmark that remains accessible to human intelligence while being difficult for current AI systems.

What matters is that the task selection criterion is public. The benchmark's own documentation states that a study with more than 400 participants was run in San Diego in early 2025, and that every task was solved by at least two humans in under two attempts. In other words, the tasks left in this problem set have had their low human difficulty verified in advance. If such a set still functions as a wall on the machine side, the wall is not a matter of 「hard」 but of 「the axis of hardness runs elsewhere」.

Add one more sentence and it collapses

The same property shows up in a far simpler form elsewhere. GSM-Symbolic, published in October 2024, converts a grade-school maths problem set into symbolic templates and mass-produces variants of the same question. The abstract reports two things. One is that changing only the numerical values in a question makes every model's performance decline. The other is that adding a single clause that looks relevant but does not affect the answer causes drops of up to 65 per cent.

It is worth reading precisely what the 65 per cent figure refers to. In the abstract it is the upper bound of the drop observed across several recent models. It does not mean every model falls that far. The direction is nonetheless clear. Values swing hard at exactly the place where a person would glance at the sentence and skip it.

Together the two say one thing. The human difficulty curve and the machine difficulty curve do not lie on the same axis. This is the first place where unfamiliarity appears as a value rather than a figure of speech. And at this place, 「unfamiliar」 is not an explanation but the name of a measurement result. That distinction is the whole point of this piece.

Observable ground two - sensitivity to surface form

The second place is whether the answer changes when the same thing is written differently. People are swayed by phrasing too. Results showing that item wording or option order moves scores have been in the educational measurement literature for a long time. The difference is magnitude.

A study of prompt format sensitivity published in October 2023 measures that magnitude head on. Constructing several semantically equivalent prompt formats and comparing performance across them produced a spread of up to 76 accuracy points on LLaMA-2-13B. The authors also released a tool for sweeping that spread quickly, named FormatSpread.

In a human examination, 76 points would be read as evidence that the test is broken. If the same content is asked but changing only the answer-sheet layout splits scores that far, we suspect the paper rather than the candidate. Here the direction is reversed. Format sensitivity is a property of the candidate.

  • An observation that a model cannot do something is usually an observation that it cannot do it in this format. A failure obtained under one format is not an absence of capability but an observation of one condition.
  • Evaluation comparisons only hold when the format is fixed. The moment two scores measured under different formats are set side by side, the comparison is of formats and not of capability.
  • In operations the format is part of the contract. When editing prompt wording is treated as tidying up phrasing, the reason a value moved never reaches the record.
ItemWhat the source states
What was variedPrompts identical in meaning and differing only in format
Reported spreadUp to 76 accuracy points
Conditions on that valueMeasured on LLaMA-2-13B in few-shot settings
Released alongsideFormatSpread, a tool for sweeping format variance quickly

Observable ground three - the reliability of self-report

The third place is whether a model's account of why it answered as it did points at the actual cause. Since the essay devotes considerable space to chain-of-thought monitoring, this ground overlaps precisely with its concerns.

A study published in May 2023 and presented at NeurIPS that year cuts the question down to a narrow, measurable form. The method is simple. A biasing feature is planted in the input, for instance by reordering multiple-choice options so that the correct answer is always the first one. Two things then happen at once. Accuracy drops by up to 36 per cent across 13 BIG-Bench Hard tasks, and the explanation the model produces never mentions the bias. The models covered were GPT-3.5 and Claude 1.0.

It is worth cutting the meaning of that result precisely. It is not a result showing that models lie. It is a result showing that an explanation can differ from the list of actual causes. Something similar has long been reported in people. The human literature calls it reconstructing the reasons for one's own behaviour after the fact, and it adds that the reconstruction comes out as a reasonably coherent story. Here the coherence of the story is preserved while one decisive input drops out of it entirely.

This is where the essay's claim and the published work meet. The essay says reliance on chain-of-thought monitoring is progressively diminishing, and gives as reasons the blurring of interaction boundaries, models getting better at handling their own reasoning process, and a growing share of reasoning that is not verbalised. The published work says something else. Even under the far simpler conditions of 2023, explanation and actual cause already came apart. The two statements differ in period and in scope, and neither refutes the other. What differs is that the second comes with a reproduction procedure attached and the first does not.

ItemWhat the source states
What was plantedBiasing features in the input, such as reordered answer options
Change in accuracyDrops of up to 36 per cent across 13 BIG-Bench Hard tasks
What happened in the explanationThe planted bias went unmentioned
Models coveredGPT-3.5 and Claude 1.0
What the result does not sayAnything about intent, or values for current-generation models

How far claims about internals have got

On to layer ⓑ. The sentence 「the inside of a model is built unlike the inside of a person」 belongs to interpretability research. Pinning down what that field actually holds settles which column the essay's internal claims go in.

What sparse autoencoders actually showed

A study published in September 2023 shows the shape of the field well. Reconstructing a language model's internal activations with a sparse autoencoder yields a feature set that is more interpretable and more monosemantic than what earlier methods produced. Interpretability was scored with an automated measure, and that is the part that came out as a value. On the narrow task of indirect object identification, the work went as far as identifying features that are causally responsible for the behaviour.

The presence of causal intervention is the important part. There is a difference in evidential weight between the correlational observation 「when this feature fires that output appears」 and the intervention 「switch this feature off and that output disappears」. Only the latter earns a feature the standing of an explanation.

So what has to be in place before a sentence claiming that something exists inside a model can be handled at all? Three things.

  • An operational definition of the thing - what would have to be observed for it to count as present has to be settled before the sentence is written. Without a definition there is no way to falsify it either.
  • Causal intervention - does switching the candidate off or on move the output as predicted. Correlation alone does not earn the name.
  • An explicit scope - which model, which layer, which task did it hold for. The moment something established on a narrow task is carried over as a property of the whole model, it turns back into metaphor.
What is in hand todayWhat is not in hand yet
A procedure for extracting interpretable feature sets from internal activationsAn account of the whole computation of a model
An automated-measure comparison showing greater monosemanticity than earlier methodsGeneralisation that carries directly to arbitrary tasks
Identification of causally responsible features on narrow tasksWhere higher-level notions such as intent or goal sit
A methodology that confirms a feature's role through intervention experimentsAn indicator that reads alignment directly off the internals
Tooling that proposes names for features automaticallyAny guarantee that such a name points at the same thing a human concept does

So the essay's four internal claims go not in a column marked wrong but in one marked 「this is the only form in which it can currently be handled」. The sentence saying that value alignment is a more intrinsic property of the model is, until intrinsicness has something that measures it, a proposal for a definition rather than an observation. That is not a criticism; it is writing down what kind of sentence it is. The way to separate several possible causes behind one symptom is the same method used when a model suddenly starts answering that it does not know and the cause splits four ways.

When a metaphor replaces an explanation

Layer ⓒ. The metaphor itself is not the problem. 「An alien mind」 is a good figure. It binds a set of unhuman failure patterns into one picture and works as a warning that human intuitions should not be carried over unchanged. That much is gain.

The trouble comes when the figure takes the seat of the explanation. The moment 「because it is an alien mind」 arrives as the answer to 「why did this model get that easy question wrong」, four things happen together.

  • It fills the causal slot - an answer has arrived, so the next question does not get asked. Nobody asks whether the format was varied, what entered the context, or whether the same input was tried again.
  • It erases the falsifying condition - nobody writes down what observation would make 「unfamiliar」 false. A sentence with no falsifying condition survives any result, and survives at the cost of predicting nothing.
  • It moves responsibility - if an unknowable thing did it, the choices made in design and operation drop out of the discussion. The system prompt, the tool definitions and the ordering of retrieval results were all set by people, and that layer disappears.
  • It removes the measurement plan - nothing budgeted attaches to a thing declared unknowable. What to measure next quarter never gets decided, and so next time the same sentence answers again.

The structure is not new. Whether an organisation is likened to a newsroom or to a cathedral and a bazaar, the point where a figure stops solving and starts working as magic always sits in the same place. A figure is a tool when it points at where to look, and an obstacle when it says stop looking there.

The same figure, two usesUsed as a toolUsed to replace an explanation
「An alien mind」A warning against transferring human intuition. Prompts a separate measurement of the error distributionThe answer to why it was wrong. Stops anything being measured
「Grown」A summary of the fact that this is not a system whose rules were written by handA conclusion that the inside is unknowable. Interpretability research drops out of the discussion
「Teaching it to love」The opening of a distinction between pursuing a goal and judging by valuesRead as a claim about a model's feelings, which cuts against the essay's own argument

The opposite reduction - 「just statistics」

The sentence pointing the other way does the same work. 「In the end it is only choosing the probability of the next word.」 As a description of the training objective this is accurate. The problem is what comes after. Once that accurate description is used as a reason to stop observing, the four effects of the previous section follow identically.

Set the two reductions side by side and the symmetry shows. One says 「it is unknowable, so stop looking」; the other says 「it is fully known, so stop looking」. The conclusions are opposite and the behaviour is the same. Both cancel the next observation.

AxisThe 「alien mind」 reductionThe 「just statistics」 reduction
ClaimIt is fundamentally unlike us, so our concepts do not applyIt is probability arithmetic, so no separate explanation is needed
The part that is rightFailure distributions genuinely do differ from human onesAs a description of the training objective it is accurate
Where it overreachesUses the difference as a conclusion without measuring its degree or scopeJumps straight from the objective function to behaviour
The identical outcomeWhat to measure next never gets decidedWhat to measure next never gets decided

The GSM-Symbolic result cited earlier is a good test bench. It sounds convenient to both camps. One side reads it as 「people are not swayed but machines are」, the other as 「so it is not reasoning at all」. But what the abstract actually states is a value: performance changed this much under this particular variation. The road from that value to 「reasoning is present or absent」 lies outside the abstract's wording, and what carries you across that road is metaphor again.

In production, what can you actually hold

There is one place where all of this distinction gets used. A model produced a strange output and somebody asks why it did that. Answer with 「because it is an alien mind」 or with 「because it is probability」 and the result is the same. Nothing gets fixed.

What you can hold and what you cannot

What you can holdWhy you can hold itWhat you cannot hold
The full user inputIt is in the recordThe path of the computation inside the weights
The system prompt and its revision historyThe operator set itThe single reason that answer appeared
Tool definitions and schema versionIt is in the codeWhat the model intended
Sampling settings and the random seedThey are call argumentsWhat would appear under different conditions
The documents that entered the context and their orderThe retrieval layer builds itWhich of them the model actually used
The model identifier and version stringIt is in the response metadataWhen the provider changed something internally
Request timestamp and retry historyIt is in the logsServer-side state at that same moment
The output distribution when the same input is rerunIt is a value you can generate yourselfWhether that distribution holds next week

The right-hand column shows why the essay's layer ⓑ claims are hard to use directly in production. The things you cannot hold are mostly questions about the inside of the model. So the way to get values on the ground is not to look inward but to fix one external condition at a time and run it again.

Six questions that discriminate

What to askWhat a divergent answer means
Does the same input with the same settings produce the same thingIf not, sampling or context assembly is the first candidate cause
Does the value change when only the format changesIf it does, this is surface sensitivity rather than a capability problem
Removing context documents one by one, where does it flipThe document at the flip point is a direct cause
Delete the reason the model gave and ask again; is the answer the sameIf it is, that reason was an explanation and not a cause
Is the failure somewhere people also find hard, or somewhere people do not failThe latter is an error-distribution problem and needs measuring on its own
Is the model version string the same as last timeIf not, every other comparison is void

Those six lines are a device for turning 「why did it do that」 into 「what has to change for it to differ」. The first question yields no answer and the second one does. And answering the second requires a record. The following is the minimum set worth keeping for a single anomalous output.

model      : model identifier and version string
timestamp  : request time and time zone
system     : full system prompt, or its hash and length
input      : full user input
tools      : list of tool definitions and schema version
sampling   : temperature, top_p, seed, max_tokens
context    : identifiers of the documents that entered, in order
output     : full response
retries    : retry count and the result of each attempt
  • A hash is enough - if storing the full system prompt is impractical, a hash and a length still separate the same one from a different one.
  • Order is a value - for documents inserted by the retrieval layer, the order matters more than the list. The same set in a different order is a different input.
  • Without a seed, record the distribution - where a setting cannot be fixed, keep the distribution across several runs. A single output is one sample.
  • Log the version string every time - provider-side changes arrive without notice. If the version differs, comparison with earlier records does not hold.
  • Do not discard the retries - keeping only successful responses erases the failure distribution, and causes turn up more often on the failure side.

The reader's criterion sits in the same place. Rather than working out whether a text was written by a person or a machine, it is more useful to ask whether its content can be checked, and that shift from verdict to verification applies here unchanged. Only the object has moved, from prose to output.

Questions that can be examined and questions that cannot

The phrase 「alien mind」 almost always leads to the next question. What is in there. Does it feel. Does it want. These are serious questions, but in their present form no observation procedure has been defined for them. Writing that there is something and writing that there is nothing are, without a procedure, statements of the same kind.

What can be separated out is something else: among the questions that arise from the same interest, which ones admit an operational definition and which do not.

QuestionCan it be handled nowWhat would be needed to handle it
Does self-report match actual behaviourYesPlant a bias and count whether the explanation mentions it
Does the explanation point at the actual causeYesDelete or substitute the stated reason and see whether the output moves
Does avoidance behaviour increase under certain conditionsYesDefine the conditions and the behaviour in advance, then count frequency
Are there internal representations matching human conceptsPartlyDefine a feature per concept and carry it through to causal intervention
Is there subjective experienceNot in the present formThe observation procedure itself would have to be defined first

Writing the bottom row as 「not known」 is different from writing it as 「none」. And the essay itself keeps that distinction. The section heading is the figure 「teaching machines to love」, while the body translates it into the term value alignment. The essay opens the door with a metaphor and continues the argument in operational terms. What most often falls away in transmission is precisely that second step. When only the figure at the door survives and the term inside disappears, the reader receives the metaphor as the claim.

What writing down the layer leaves you with

To gather it up: of the essay's seventeen sentences, five have the form of observation and one of those five is confirmable from outside as it stands. Four are claims about internals, and interpretability research has currently reached the point of identifying causally responsible features on narrow tasks. Four are metaphor and two are normative.

And the places where 「different from people」 comes out as a value are currently three, all published. A wall that remains even after keeping only tasks people find easy; values that swing when only the surface changes; explanations that omit a decisive input. All three come with samples and conditions, and all three can be run again.

  • Writing down the layer settles what to ask next. At the observational layer you ask for conditions, at the internal layer for definitions, at the metaphorical layer what the figure is hiding.
  • A sentence with no value is not false; it is a sentence with no value yet. Mixing the two columns leaves the reader disoriented when the numbers eventually appear.
  • Both directions of reduction cost the same thing. Call it unknowable or call it fully known, either way the next observation is cancelled.

There is no need to discard the word unfamiliar. When it is the name of a measurement result it is the precise word. Error distributions differ from human ones, values swing on a change of phrasing, self-report omits a decisive input. As a name binding those three together, 「unfamiliar」 is well chosen. It is when the word begins to stand in for the measurement that the same word starts working in reverse. The difference is whether a table follows it.

What could not be confirmed

The items below were kept out of the body because their wording could not be pinned down in a primary source. The reason is given with each.

  • Direct retrieval of the original page - the address on openai.com returns 403 to a direct request. Quotation in the body was therefore kept to a minimum, and short direct phrases follow the form quoted by third-party reporting. They were not collated word by word against the original.
  • The alignment comparison between the newest and previous model - no evaluation name, sample, condition or score appears in the essay. The table therefore records only the shape of a comparative evaluation result.
  • Support for the cybersecurity capability claim - no evaluation is offered behind the statement that capability at breaking in and defending has passed the human level.
  • Public documentation of the activation-monitoring technique - for the technique the essay names as a complement to chain-of-thought monitoring, no public document setting out method and performance could be located.
  • Per-model scores and the human panel average for ARC-AGI-2 - absent from both the technical report abstract and the benchmark's introductory page. What the introductory page does carry is the participant count and the statement that every task was solved by at least two humans in under two attempts.
  • The number of models and the dataset size in GSM-Symbolic - the abstract says only several recent open and closed models. The specific drop from changing numerical values alone is likewise not in the abstract.
  • The wording of the conclusions in the GSM-Symbolic body - any reading beyond the abstract's values requires the full paper. The body was not examined, so only the abstract's values are used above.
  • Task and format counts in the prompt-format study - the abstract carries the 76 accuracy points and LLaMA-2-13B, but not the number of tasks, the number of formats, or per-model values elsewhere.
  • Model names and stated limitations in the sparse autoencoder work - the abstract names no model and states no limitation. The right-hand column of that table therefore reflects the field's current reach rather than that paper's own caveats.
  • Publication time and edit history of the original - the date lines up as 6 September 2026 across several reports, but the time of day and any later revision could not be established.
