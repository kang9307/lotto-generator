Introduction: Who Needs to File Korea's Comprehensive Income Tax?

Every May, millions of Koreans receive notices about Jonghap Sogeukse (종합소득세) — Korea's comprehensive income tax. Unlike salary earners whose taxes are handled through year-end settlements (yeomaljeongsang), freelancers, YouTubers, instructors, people with side income, and landlords must actively file their own tax returns.

This guide is based on the Korean National Tax Service (NTS) official materials and covers the essentials for the 2025 tax year (income earned January–December 2025), filed in 2026. It walks through who must file, income types, rates, deductions, business expenses, the Hometax procedure, and penalties, in that order.

This article was first written in June 2026 and updated on September 29, 2026. Everything that changed is collected in the update history near the end. Tax law is subject to change and applies differently to each person. Please consult official NTS sources or a tax professional before filing.

What Is Comprehensive Income Tax? Types of Income

Korea's comprehensive income tax aggregates most categories of personal income for a single annual return. Under the Income Tax Act, income is broadly classified as follows.

Income Included in Comprehensive Taxation

  • Interest income: Bank deposit interest, bond interest, etc. Subject to comprehensive taxation if combined with dividend income it exceeds KRW 20 million/year
  • Dividend income: Dividends from stocks, funds, etc. Combined with interest income above KRW 20 million triggers comprehensive taxation
  • Business income: Self-employed individuals, freelancers (personal services), real estate rental. Comprehensive taxation regardless of amount, in principle
  • Employment income: Wages and bonuses. No separate filing is needed once year-end settlement is done, except, for example, when you worked for 2+ employers without a combined settlement or must combine salary with other income
  • Pension income: Public pensions (National Pension, public sector pensions) need no separate filing once year-end settled. Private pensions above KRW 15 million a year (KRW 12 million through December 31, 2023) are taxed comprehensively, or you may elect 15% separate taxation when filing
  • Other income: Lecture fees, manuscript fees, prizes, consulting fees, etc. If the other income amount (after necessary expenses) is KRW 3 million or less a year and tax was withheld, you may choose separate or comprehensive taxation (items subject to mandatory separate or comprehensive taxation excluded)

Income Taxed Separately (Not Included)

  • Retirement income: Taxed separately under retirement income tax rules
  • Capital gains: Taxed separately under capital gains tax rules
  • Separately taxed financial income: Interest plus dividends of KRW 20 million or less — withholding at source is final

When You Do Not Need to File

You generally do not need to file if one of the following applies (assuming no other income).

  • You have only employment income and your employer completed year-end settlement
  • You have only public pension income and year-end settlement was completed
  • Your financial income (interest + dividends) totals KRW 20 million or less a year
  • Your other income amount (revenue minus necessary expenses) is KRW 3 million or less, tax was withheld, and you chose separate taxation

Filing Deadlines and Payment

Comprehensive income tax must be filed and paid within a fixed period each year; missing the deadline triggers penalty taxes.

General Deadline

  • Filing period: In principle May 1–31 of the following year. When the deadline falls on a Saturday or public holiday it moves to the next day, so for the 2025 tax year (May 31 was a Sunday) the deadline was Monday, June 1, 2026
  • Sincere Report Confirmation (성실신고확인서) filers: June 30 of the following year (June 30, 2026 for the 2025 tax year)
  • Payment: Due by the filing deadline. For the 2025 tax year, the NTS extended the payment deadline to August 31, 2026 on its own authority for certain taxpayers, such as those whose VAT payment deadline had been extended

Installment Payment (Bunap)

If the tax you owe exceeds KRW 10 million, you may pay part of it within 2 months after the payment deadline. The amount that can be deferred depends on the size of the tax due, so follow the installment amount shown on the filing screen and in NTS guidance.

Late (Post-Deadline) Filing

If you miss the deadline, a late return reduces the failure-to-file penalty. Article 48 of the Framework Act on National Taxes provides only three reduction bands after the statutory deadline: 50% within 1 month, 30% from over 1 month to 3 months, and 20% from over 3 months to 6 months. Filing as early as possible is therefore always better.

Major Income Deductions and Tax Credits

Deductions reduce the tax you actually pay. They are split into income deductions (reducing taxable income, which lowers tax indirectly) and tax credits (subtracted directly from calculated tax).

In this section, the child tax credit and the Yellow Umbrella deduction limits are values as of September 2026; the other amounts and rates are values as of June 2026 (not re-verified in this update). Each deduction applies to specific income types (employment, business, etc.), so check with the NTS whether it applies to you.

Key Income Deductions

  • Personal deduction: KRW 1.5 million basic deduction for the taxpayer, plus KRW 1.5 million per qualifying dependent (spouse, lineal ascendants/descendants, etc.). Additional deductions for seniors, persons with disabilities, single parents, etc.
  • Pension contribution deduction: National Pension and occupational pension contributions are fully deductible
  • Special income deduction: Health and employment insurance premiums (for employment income earners)
  • Yellow Umbrella deduction (노란우산공제): Contributions to the small business owner mutual aid scheme. Limit: KRW 6 million if business income is KRW 40 million or less, KRW 4 million if over KRW 40 million up to KRW 100 million, KRW 2 million if over KRW 100 million (per the NTS tax consultation center)
  • Overall deduction ceiling: Certain income deductions, such as the credit card usage deduction, are capped at KRW 25 million combined per year

Key Tax Credits

  • Standard tax credit: KRW 130,000 for employment income earners; KRW 70,000 for business income earners (excluding Sincere Report Confirmation filers)
  • Child tax credit: KRW 250,000 for one qualifying child, KRW 550,000 for two, and for three or more KRW 550,000 plus KRW 400,000 per child beyond two. For a birth or adoption in the year: KRW 300,000 (first), 500,000 (second), 700,000 (third or later) extra (Easy Law Korea, reference date 2026-08-31)
  • Education expense credit: 15% of qualified education expenses (a special tax credit limited to eligible filers such as employment income earners)
  • Medical expense credit: 15–30% of qualified medical costs exceeding 3% of total income (a special tax credit with limited eligibility)
  • Donation credit: 15–30% depending on the donation type and amount band
  • Electronic filing credit: KRW 20,000 when taxpayers file electronically themselves

What Changes From the 2026 Tax Year — Enacted Changes vs. Government Proposals

  • Already enacted: The age threshold for the child tax credit rises step by step — age 9+ in 2026, 10+ in 2027, 11+ in 2028, 12+ in 2029, then 13+ (Easy Law Korea). The National Law Information Center shows the Income Tax Act as partially amended on December 23, 2025 (Act No. 21221, effective July 1, 2026)
  • Still a government proposal: The government's 2026 tax reform plan, finalized on September 1, 2026, includes relaxing the income test for spouse/dependent basic deductions (income amount KRW 1 million or less → KRW 3 million or less; KRW 5 million → KRW 7.5 million in total salary for salary-only dependents), preferential monthly-rent tax credits for young people, and abolishing the extra public-transport portion of the credit card deduction. Content and timing may change during National Assembly review
  • Unchanged: The rate table applied to 2025 tax-year returns (8 brackets, 6–45%) was not mentioned as a change item in the article introducing the reform plan

Business Expense Deductions for Self-Employed and Freelancers

For business income, taxable income = gross revenue minus necessary expenses (필요경비). Expenses are handled in one of two ways: bookkeeping, which proves actual costs with books, or estimated filing, which applies NTS-set ratios.

Bookkeeping Method (Gijang)

If prior-year revenue is below the industry threshold (for example, KRW 300 million for wholesale/retail, KRW 150 million for manufacturing, KRW 75 million for real estate rental), you may use simplified bookkeeping; at or above it, double-entry bookkeeping (recording every transaction as debit and credit) is required. Books let you deduct all actual expenses — rent, labor, materials, marketing, etc. — which pays off when costs are high. Without required books, a bookkeeping-failure penalty (무기장가산세) may apply, calculated as "calculated tax × (unrecorded or under-recorded income ÷ comprehensive income) × 20%", with small-scale businesses exempt.

Estimated Filing (Simple and Standard Expense Rates)

If you file without books, the NTS applies expense rates set for each industry.

  • Simple expense rate (단순경비율): For small operators whose prior-year revenue is below the threshold. Revenue multiplied by the simple expense rate is recognized as expenses
  • Standard expense rate (기준경비율): Key costs (purchases, rent, payroll) must be documented with actual receipts; remaining expenses use the standard rate

Pre-Filled Filing (Modu Chaeum)

The NTS pre-fills income and deduction data and sends a notice. It applies to people with personal-service income (freelancers, instructors, etc.); if everything looks right, you can submit as is. Any additional deductions must be edited or added yourself.

How to File on Hometax (Step by Step)

Electronic filing via Hometax (www.hometax.go.kr) is the most common method. In-person filing means submitting a return at a local tax office.

Hometax Filing Steps

  1. Log in to Hometax: Use a joint (public) certificate, simple authentication (Kakao, Naver, PASS, etc.), or a financial certificate
  2. Open the filing menu: Go to the comprehensive income tax filing menu. Menu names and locations can change with Hometax redesigns, so follow the on-screen guidance
  3. Select filing type:
    • Pre-filled / simple expense rate: for small-scale filers, pre-populated by the NTS
    • Bookkeeping return (general or simplified books): actual expenses from books
    • Financial income return: when financial income exceeds KRW 20 million
  4. Enter income and deductions: Fill in revenue, expenses, income deductions, and tax credits. Use the auto-calculation
  5. Submit and pay: Submit, check the payment slip, and pay via virtual account, internet banking, or card
  6. File local income tax: According to the NTS, after finishing the national return on Hometax you can continue to the local income tax return via the "move to local income tax filing" button at the top of the screen. Local income tax is 10% of income tax

Mobile Filing (Sontax App)

Simple filings such as pre-filled returns can also be completed on the Sontax (손택스) mobile app. Complex returns (bookkeeping, financial income) are best handled on a PC.

Penalties for Late or Incorrect Filing

If you do not file and pay accurately on time, penalty taxes apply. Amounts differ by type, and more than one can apply.

Summary of Penalty Taxes

Penalty TypeTriggerRate (General Standard)
Failure-to-file penaltyNot filing by the deadline20% of the unreported tax (40% if fraudulent)
Under-reporting penaltyReporting less tax than actual10% of the under-reported tax (40% if fraudulent)
Late payment penaltyNot paying by the deadlineUnpaid tax × days elapsed × 2.2/10,000 (0.022% per day)
Bookkeeping-failure penaltyBooks not kept or under-recordedCalculated tax × (unrecorded or under-recorded income ÷ comprehensive income) × 20%; small-scale businesses exempt

If both the failure-to-file and the bookkeeping-failure penalties apply, only the larger one is imposed; if they are equal, the failure-to-file penalty applies (NTS comprehensive income tax penalty guide).

Penalty rates and how they apply are subject to legal revision. Consult the NTS or a Korean tax accountant for exact penalty calculations.

Voluntary Late Filing Reduction

If you voluntarily file a late return after the deadline, the failure-to-file penalty is reduced by 50% within 1 month, 30% within 3 months, and 20% within 6 months of the statutory deadline (Article 48(2)(2), Framework Act on National Taxes). An amended return — correcting a return that was filed on time — has its own bands, from 90% within 1 month down to 10% within 2 years, applied to the under-reporting penalty (Article 48(2)(1)). Do not mix the two schedules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Do freelancers with 3.3% withholding still need to file?

Yes. The 3.3% is withholding, not the final tax. The difference between it and the tax determined by your actual income and deductions is refunded or paid, so filing is mandatory. With expenses and deductions properly claimed, you may get part of the 3.3% back.

Q2. I have a side job in addition to my salaried position. Do I need to file?

If you have business income on top of settled employment income, or an other income amount (after necessary expenses) above KRW 3 million, you must file a combined return. If the side income is other income of KRW 3 million or less with tax withheld, you may choose separate taxation.

Q3. Do I pay local income tax separately?

Yes. After the comprehensive income tax return, local income tax (10% of income tax) must also be filed and paid. From the Hometax completion screen you can move straight on to the local income tax return.

Q4. What if I cannot afford to pay the tax?

File on time first — that avoids the failure-to-file penalty. If payment is difficult, you can use installment payment (tax due over KRW 10 million, within 2 months) or apply to the NTS for a payment extension or collection deferral (징수유예). Check eligibility and procedures on the NTS website.

Q5. Is it hard to file without a tax accountant?

If you qualify for the simple expense rate or a pre-filled return, filing on Hometax is relatively easy. For complex business income or when books are required, hiring a licensed tax accountant (세무사) to capture legitimate savings is a sound option.

Summary: Filing Right the First Time

Korea's comprehensive income tax looks complicated, but the most important thing is to know which filing type applies to you and file on time. Freelancers, the self-employed, and side earners in particular should claim expenses and deductions carefully so they do not overpay.

Remember these three points.

  • Meet the deadline: May 31 (general; the next day if it falls on a weekend or holiday — June 1 for the 2025 tax year), June 30 for Sincere Report filers. If late, file within 6 months, ideally within 1 month
  • Maximize legitimate deductions: Do not miss items that apply to you, such as personal deductions, pension contributions, the child tax credit, and the Yellow Umbrella deduction
  • Use professionals: In complex situations, consult a tax accountant for lawful tax planning

Tax law can change every year. Always check the latest NTS guidance or consult a tax professional when filing.

## 1. Do I have to file?

| My income | Filing required? |

## 2. Deadline checklist

- [ ] General filing and payment deadline: May 31 of the following year (next day if Saturday or holiday — June 1, 2026 for the 2025 tax year)

- [ ] Check whether the NTS extended your payment deadline ex officio (August 31, 2026 for certain taxpayers, 2025 tax year)
