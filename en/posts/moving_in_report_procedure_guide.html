What the move-in report is - legal basis, deadline and filing office

The move-in report (jeonip singo) is the notification that tells the head of the local government at the new address that a resident has changed residence. Article 16(1) of the Resident Registration Act provides that when all or some of the members of a household move, the person obliged to report under Article 11 or 12 must file the move-in report with the head of the city, county or district of the new address within 14 days of the day of moving in. In practice the counter is the community center (eup, myeon or dong office) of the new address, and the internet application on Government24 is another route into the same report.

The first thing to read in the provision is the starting point of the deadline. It is not the day the contract was signed or the day the boxes were packed. It is the day of moving into the new residence, and the 14 days run from that day. The same sentence also names who must file. Who that person is gets settled in Articles 11 and 12, so the filer has to be identified before any form is filled in.

The word move-in covers two different cases. A whole household may move together, or only some members may move. Article 23(1) of the Enforcement Decree says that, for residents, the report follows Annex Form No. 15 or Annex Form No. 15-2 depending on whether the entire household moves, and for overseas Koreans Annex Form No. 15-3. The Government24 service page likewise lists the application form in three kinds: move-in report (whole household), move-in report (part of the household and others) and move-in report (overseas Koreans and overseas residents).

  • Subject - all or part of one household changing residence (Article 16(1))
  • Deadline - within 14 days of the day of moving in at the new address
  • Where - the head of the city, county or district of the new address; in practice the eup, myeon or dong community center accepts it
  • Method - Article 18(1) of the Act says reports are made orally or in writing; the online application on Government24 follows its own service guidance

One report can do more than one job. Article 17 says that a move-in report made on a change of residence is treated as also being the move-out and move-in report required by the Military Service Act, the Framework Act on Civil Defense, the Seal Impression Certification Act, the National Basic Living Security Act, the National Health Insurance Act and the Welfare of Disabled Persons Act. Under Article 16(2), when the head of the new local government receives the report, it promptly informs the former address and requests transfer of the resident registration record and related public documents. The structure means no separate move-out report has to be made at the former address.

Filing twice is blocked. Article 10(2) says nobody may file the report in duplicate, and Article 37 lists duplicate filing and reporting false facts about resident registration as offenses punishable by up to three years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million. This is why writing down an address where the person does not actually live is not a mere clerical slip.

Who files - the head of household and household confirmation

Article 11(1) provides that the head of the household files within 14 days of the date the reportable event occurred. Where the head cannot file, the person who manages the household, the person concerned, or a spouse, lineal relative, spouse's lineal relative or lineal relative's spouse who has the head's authorization may file. Residents of dormitories, care facilities and similar shared accommodation are covered by Article 12, under which the manager of the accommodation files.

The place where household confirmation actually bites is Enforcement Decree Article 23(2). When the head of household (or household manager) at the new address differs from the head at the former address, for example when moving into a home where another person is already the head, two things are required. One is confirmation by the person moving in (item 1), and the other is presentation of that person's identification (item 2). Both items say in brackets that where the person moving in is a minor, the legal representative (a person with parental authority or a guardian) is meant. If confirmation is hard to obtain, a fact-finding inquiry by the head of the eup, myeon or dong, or of the branch office, may substitute for it. Item 1 of the version in force since April 28, 2026 contains no wording about an annexed form. The amended version scheduled to take effect on October 29, 2026 changes the bracket in item 1 to 「where the person moving in is a minor, by the legal representative confirmation of Annex Form No. 15-4」; that is a scheduled change and not a filing requirement as of today.

There is one exception to the identification requirement. If the person moving in is the filer's own spouse or a lineal relative, presenting identification may be omitted. In that case the head of the local government must confirm the filer's family relationship certificate through shared use of administrative information, and if the filer does not consent to that confirmation, the document is requested from the filer (Decree Art. 23(3)). That is why the documents requested differ when a family member goes to the counter on another person's behalf.

The purpose of the confirmation steps can be read from the structure of the provisions. A move-in report changes the composition of the household at the new address, so when a person enters a household that already exists, the counter screens whether that person is the one actually moving in. The receiving official checks the identity of the filer, checks the number of move-in households registered at the new address, and then records the report date, the reason for moving in and the official name in the resident registration record and its index (Decree Art. 23(5)). One report ends not when the filer hands over the papers but when the receiving office has made the record.

How to file - community center visit or Government24

There are two routes: visiting the community center that has jurisdiction over the new address, or applying over the internet on Government24. The Government24 move-in report service page (marked last modified December 9, 2025) lists the application methods as internet and in-person, the eligible applicant as the person or an agent (no agent applications online), and the processing time as 「immediately (3 hours within working hours)」 with no fee, and describes the service as a report to be made within 14 days of moving in. It shows the service as open to members and non-members alike. The inquiry line is 1588-2188.

The last cell of the table reflects the Government24 move-in guidance screen. It says that a fixed date on a housing lease can be applied for on the Internet Registry Office or in person at the community center with jurisdiction over the new address, and adds that no application is needed where the lease was submitted at the move-in report stage or attached to a housing lease report. The advantage of Government24 is that the report can be completed without going to an office; the report is accepted within the deadline just as an in-person one is. Online applications cannot be made through an agent, however, so a family member filing on someone else's behalf has to use a visit.

The first question on the filing screen is whether the entire household moves or only part of it. Decree Article 23(1) divides the forms on that basis, and the Government24 page too splits the application form into whole household, part of the household and others, and overseas Koreans. Whether the head of household changes and whether the person joins an existing household decide whether the household confirmation step of the previous section is added, so the household composition should be sorted out before anything is entered. The Government24 page lists a frequently asked question on how the head-of-household confirmation works for an online report, but its answer text was not confirmed.

  • For a visit, bring identification and go to the community center that has jurisdiction over the new address. The Government24 page says the filer and the persons moving in must all bring identification, but where the persons moving in are a spouse or lineal relatives only the filer needs to. For a self-filed application it lists one of the following: resident registration card, youth ID, driver license, national merit recipient card, disability registration card (only if it contains the resident registration number) or passport, within its validity period
  • For the online route, apply on the Government24 move-in report screen as a member or non-member; the details of the input items are not covered here
  • On either route, the receiving office records the report in the resident registration record together with the report date and the reason for moving in (Decree Art. 23(5))

What to bring - identification, filing by proxy and the lease

The law fixes the range of persons obliged to report, the confirmation by the person moving in, the presentation of identification, and the check of family relationship. The detailed document list is set out in the Government24 service page, so the list below separates what can be read from the provisions from what that page confirms.

  • Identification - corresponds to the identity check by the receiving official (Decree Art. 23(5)) and the identification requirement for the person moving in. For a self-filed application the Government24 page lists presentation of identification (one of resident registration card, youth ID, driver license, national merit recipient card, disability registration card or passport) and a consent form for shared use of administrative information (Enforcement Rule Annex Form No. 1-16)
  • Household-related confirmation - if the filer is not the head of household, or the heads of household at the new and former addresses differ, confirmation by the person moving in (for a minor, the legal representative) is needed (Decree Art. 23(2), item 1)
  • Materials for a proxy filer - for an application by an agent the Government24 page lists the identification of both the delegating and the delegated person, a power of attorney (Enforcement Decree Annex Form No. 15-2 or 15-3) and the consent form for shared use of administrative information. Agents cannot apply online
  • Family filing on another person's behalf - if the person moving in is the filer's spouse or a lineal relative, identification may be omitted, but the family relationship certificate is checked through shared use of administrative information
  • The lease document - the fixed date is the one on the lease document (Housing Lease Protection Act Art. 3-2(2)). The Government24 page says that where the lease was submitted at the move-in report stage, no separate fixed date application is needed
  • The exact new address - check the building name, unit block and unit number against the wording of the contract

The last two items follow from the Government24 page and the provisions. The receiving official checks the number of move-in households registered at the new address and records the report date and the reason for moving in (Decree Art. 23(5)), so it is safer to compare the building name, block and unit number with the contract before filing.

The preparation can be reduced to three steps. First, before the move, decide who is filing and, if an authorization is needed, prepare the power of attorney and the identification of both persons in advance. Second, confirm who is the head of household at the new address and whether that person is the same as at the former address. Third, keep the original lease and the identification in a bag separate from the moving boxes. According to the Government24 page, a lease submitted at the filing makes a separate fixed date application unnecessary.

Moving day sequence - from delivery to the fixed date

Tenant protection starts from the day after both requirements, delivery of the dwelling and resident registration, are met, so whichever of the two is completed later sets the date. The flow of moving day can be set as follows.

  1. Receive the keys on moving day and take delivery (possession) of the dwelling. This is the day the delivery requirement is met, and it becomes the move-in day
  2. Write down the move-in day. It is the day from which the 14 days run, and later it anchors the fixed date and tenant protection dates
  3. Decide who files. Check whether it is the head of household, a family member with authorization, or whether the heads of household at the new and former addresses differ
  4. File the move-in report by visiting the community center or through Government24. Compare the unit number and the household composition on the new address before entering them
  5. If the lease document is in hand, request the fixed date at the same community center. The fixed-date ledger records the location of the dwelling, the date granted, the rent and the deposit
  6. After filing, use a certified copy of the resident registration to check that the new address and household composition were recorded correctly

The point of the sequence is to finish steps one to five in one go on moving day. The 14 days are only the reference for the fine and not the reference for deposit protection. Filing on any day within the 14 days avoids the fine, but tenant protection starts correspondingly later. Tenant protection in general is covered in the tenant rights guide under the Housing Lease Protection Act.

The statutory standard is the same when the move-in date differs from the date written in the contract or when the move takes several days. The starting point under Article 16(1) is the day of moving in at the new address, and tenant protection under Article 3(1) of the Housing Lease Protection Act is judged from the time both delivery and registration are complete. Which day counts as the move-in day in an ambiguous case is not judged here and is a matter to confirm with the community center.

Fixed date and tenant protection - where the dates split

The move-in report, the fixed date, tenant protection and the priority repayment right are four separate effects resting on different provisions, even when they are handled one after another at the same counter on the same day. The table below separates the four by provision.

ItemBasisWhat must be in placeEffect
Move-in reportResident Registration Act Art. 16Report within 14 days of moving in at the new addressThe resident registration moves to the new address. Article 3(1) of the Housing Lease Protection Act treats the registration as made at the time of the move-in report
Tenant protection (opposability)Housing Lease Protection Act Art. 3(1)Delivery of the dwelling plus resident registrationThe lease takes effect against third parties from the next day
Fixed dateSame Act Art. 3-6The lease document, and one of the town or township office, community center or branch office where the dwelling is located, the district court or registry office, or a notaryThe fixed-date ledger records the dwelling location, the date granted, the rent and the deposit
Priority repayment rightSame Act Art. 3-2(2)The tenant protection requirements plus a fixed date on the lease documentThe right to be repaid the deposit from the auction or public sale proceeds of the leased dwelling (including its site) ahead of subordinate rights holders and other creditors

The part most often confused is the next day. Article 3(1) of the Housing Lease Protection Act says that once the tenant has completed delivery of the dwelling and resident registration, the lease takes effect against third parties from the next day, and its second sentence adds that the resident registration is deemed made at the time the move-in report is filed. So the registration counts from the time of the move-in report and the effect starts the day after. Even when the move-in report is completed on the day of the move, tenant protection starts on the next day, and filing on day 14 pushes it back by that much. The provision contains no rule that the effect starts at the beginning of the filing day or at the time of acceptance. Details of the case law on this timing could not be confirmed.

A date example makes it easy to see. If the keys are received on October 5 and the report is completed the same day, applying the statute text as it stands, the calculated effect starts on October 6. If the tenant moves in on October 5 and files on October 15, the requirements are met on the 15th, so the effect starts on the 16th. If another right is established over the dwelling during those ten days, the order between them becomes an issue. The ranking in a concrete dispute is a matter for the individual case and should be checked with a court or a professional.

Tenant protection and the priority repayment right are different rights and should be kept apart. Tenant protection is the effect that lets the tenant assert the lease against a new owner even when the landlord changes; the priority repayment right is the right to be repaid the deposit first from the auction or public sale proceeds of the leased dwelling (including its site). The latter needs a fixed date added to the tenant protection requirements. A comparison with registered lease rights is in lease right registration compared with move-in report and fixed date, and a contract-stage check is in the jeonse and monthly rent contract checklist.

Deadline and fine - when 14 days pass

Article 40(4) provides that a person who, without justifiable grounds, fails to make a report or application under Articles 11 to 13, Article 16(1) or the first sentence of Article 24(4) within the period is subject to an administrative fine of up to KRW 50,000. Three points can be read from the wording, and a fourth concerns a separate penalty.

  • Scope - not only the move-in report under Article 16(1) but also the reports under Articles 11 to 13 sit in the same paragraph
  • Conditions - two conditions apply together: without justifiable grounds and failing to act within the period. The range of justifiable grounds is not listed in the provision
  • Ceiling - up to KRW 50,000, imposed and collected by the head of the city, county or district as set by Presidential Decree (Article 40(5)). Article 58-2 of the Enforcement Decree makes Annex 4 the guideline for the amount, and for a report not made within the period under Article 16(1) and the others Annex 4 sets KRW 5,000 within 7 days after the period, KRW 20,000 within one month, KRW 30,000 within three months, KRW 40,000 within six months and KRW 50,000 beyond six months
  • Separate penalty - a false report or a duplicate report is subject to the criminal penalties of Article 37, not to a fine

Even after the deadline has passed, reporting late is better than not reporting. Annex 4 raises the amount the longer the delay runs, from KRW 5,000 within 7 days to KRW 50,000 beyond six months. Whether there were justifiable grounds is judged separately at the acceptance and imposition stages, and a person with a reason can explain it to the community center when filing. Which circumstances are accepted as justifiable grounds is not enumerated in the provision, so no examples are invented here.

The larger cost than the fine is the protection gap during the delay. Tenant protection starts from the day after registration is completed, so during the period without a report that requirement is empty. Separately, Article 20 of the Act provides for fact-finding and ex officio measures where a report has not been made or does not match the facts. Putting off the report leaves the address record out of step with reality regardless of the fine.

After the report there is a step left to confirm the result. A certified copy of the resident registration shows the address, the head of household and the household members, so the unit number and household composition can be checked against what was entered. If there is an error, a correction is requested from the receiving office. The details of the correction procedure are not covered here.

Eight common mistakes

Putting the provisions next to the service guidance shows where mistakes cluster. The items below were picked because each follows directly from the grounds in the earlier sections.

  • Misreading the starting point - the 14 days run from the day of moving in at the new address, not from the contract date or the moving date
  • Assuming the same day means the same-day effect - tenant protection starts from the day after delivery and registration are complete
  • Treating the 14 days as the filing target - the 14 days is the fine reference, and for deposit protection filing on the day of entry comes first
  • Skipping the household confirmation - when the heads of household at the new and former addresses differ, confirmation by the person moving in and identification are added (Decree Art. 23(2))
  • Forgetting the fixed date - the move-in report alone does not create the priority repayment right; both the requirements and the fixed date are needed
  • Trying to file a separate move-out report - under Article 17 the move-in report is treated as the move-out and move-in report under six laws
  • A one-unit difference in the address - if the unit number or building name differs from the contract, align them before filing
  • Reporting an address where no one lives - a false report is subject to Article 37 penalties, and duplicate reporting is prohibited

Of the eight, the first three concern dates, and they hang directly on values fixed in the provisions: the 14 days (Article 16(1)) and 「from the next day」 (Housing Lease Protection Act Art. 3(1)). Filing on the day of the move picks the earliest day for both the deadline and the start of tenant protection. For documents, the Government24 page lists the submissions separately for self-filing and agents, and checking against that list before filing is enough.

What could not be confirmed

  • The details of when the tenant protection effect begins - case law or interpretation on matters such as the time of acceptance or a start-of-day basis could not be confirmed. The basis stops at the statutory words 「from the next day」 and 「the resident registration is deemed made at the time the move-in report is filed」 in Article 3(1)
  • Processing time of the fixed date procedure - the processing time of the move-in report on the Government24 page (immediately, 3 hours within working hours) was confirmed, but that of the fixed date was not. The online head-of-household confirmation method and any consent form requirement were not confirmed either, as only the title of the related question was seen
  • The fixed-date fee, the details of the application documents and the reference time that decides priority - not confirmed from the statute text
  • How the reduction rule in Annex 4 is applied in practice, and actual imposition cases - not confirmed. Only the statutory ceiling of KRW 50,000 and the period-based amounts in Annex 4 were confirmed
  • Enforcement rule form details, office-by-office acceptance methods such as reservations, and move-in reports by foreign nationals or overseas Koreans - outside the scope
