What Changes When You Force Technical Docs Into a Controlled Language

Penalty 74 down to 3, vocabulary down 34 percent - and why this must not go on every document

2026-08-01 IT/Tech

Opening: two people read the same sentence and did the opposite

Suppose a procedure contained this line. 「The cable must be disconnected before the cover is removed.」 One technician pulled the cable first. The other opened the cover first. Both had read that sentence, and both believed they had read it correctly. The sentence was not wrong. It was simply a sentence that permitted two readings.

After an incident like that, the usual prescription is to write the document in more detail. But detail makes sentences longer, and longer sentences have more places to fork. There is a different prescription. Shrink the set of words and structures a writer is allowed to use in the first place. Expressions that can fork are simply unavailable. This is what a controlled language does.

This article is about that method, but it will not tell you to write in plain English. It does two things instead.

  • It measures. I built a checker and ran the same procedure paragraph through it before and after the controlled rewrite, comparing sentence length, passive ratio, vague terms and terminology variance. The penalty fell from 74 to 3, and unexpectedly total word count dropped by 40 percent
  • It proves where not to use it, with the same checker. A well written conceptual paragraph broke the rules in 60 percent of its sentences. That paragraph is not bad writing. It is writing with a different purpose

Environment. Every number below comes from running a checker I wrote myself on Windows 10 Pro (10.0.19045) with Node.js v22.16.0 (win32). No external libraries were used. The full checker source is in section 3 and you can copy it and run it as is.

I did not open the specification itself. The best known standard in this space, ASD-STE100, is distributed as a PDF through a request form on its official site. I read the official overview page and the official FAQ, plus one public explanatory page, and I did not read the specification text. So this article contains neither the full list of the 53 rules nor the contents of the roughly 900 approved words. The figures I quote are the ones the official site and public explanations state, and every concrete check rule shown below is a rule of my own checker, not a rule of the standard.

All examples here are fictional. The devices, procedures and documents do not exist. Problems with the document format itself were covered in Korea's HWP document format in the AI era. This article is about sentences, not formats.

1. What a controlled language actually is

A controlled language is not a style guide. A style guide suggests that you prefer the active voice. A controlled language makes the passive voice unavailable. The difference is not between advice and prohibition. It is between having a choice and not having one.

The best known case came out of aircraft maintenance documentation. The official site states the motivation plainly. Complex technical instructions can be misunderstood, and misunderstandings can lead to accidents. European airlines asked for a solution and the specification was created in the 1980s. Within what I verified, its structure is as follows.

  • 53 writing rules arranged in 9 sections, covering grammar and style
  • About 900 approved words. As a rule each word carries one meaning and one part of speech
  • About 1,200 unapproved words listed with alternatives. The widely quoted example is that start is approved while begin and commence are not
  • The current edition is Issue 9, January 2025, maintained by a dedicated group formed in 1983
  • Although it began in aerospace, the official FAQ states that it is also used for land and sea vehicles, renewable energy, and medical devices

One detail is easy to miss. The 900 approved words are not the whole vocabulary. The official FAQ explicitly allows industry specific technical nouns and technical verbs. Terms drawn from drawings, official documentation or a company glossary are permitted even when they are outside the approved list. So a controlled language does not cage you inside 900 words. It fixes the general vocabulary at 900 and lets the specialist vocabulary grow only through a documented list. Teams that adopt it without understanding this distinction collapse in week one under complaints that a needed word is banned.

2. What is forbidden, and what kind of misreading each one creates

You do not need all 53 rules. In practice four or five things cause almost all of the damage. What matters is the kind of misreading each one produces.

ForbiddenMisreading it createsBeforeAfter
Long sentences (over 20 words in instructions, 25 in description)Modifiers attach in several ways. Nothing in the sentence says which noun they belong toDisconnect the cable and the sensor that is loose.Disconnect the cable. Disconnect the sensor if the sensor is loose.

2.1 The real reason the passive is dangerous

If you think the passive is banned because it reads awkwardly, you have half the story. Write the same instruction three ways and the problem surfaces.

--- one instruction, three forms ---
(1) The cable must be disconnected before the cover is removed.
(2) Disconnect the cable. Then remove the cover.
(3) Remove the cover after disconnecting the cable.

                          (1)          (2)            (3)
who performs it           unstated     the reader     unstated
what comes first          the cable    the cable      the cable
reading order vs work     reversed     identical      reversed
instructions per sentence 2            1              2
who the must binds        unclear      not needed     unclear
  • In (1) and (3) the reading order is the reverse of the work order. Under time pressure a reader who acts as they read will invert the steps. Only (2) can be followed literally
  • The must in (1) never says whose obligation it is. Operator, design, or software, the sentence gives no basis to decide
  • after disconnecting in (3) is grammatically clean, but a gerund can carry both a time relation and a condition. Readers split between after you disconnect it and only if you disconnect it
  • The practical value of banning the passive is not stylistic. It is that the writer is forced to name the agent. With an imperative the agent becomes the reader automatically

3. I built a checker - what it counts and how

Describing rules in prose is different from showing how much they move a document. So I wrote a checker. It implements no published specification. It counts only the four things a controlled rule set constrains: sentence length, passive voice, unclear reference, and terminology consistency.

// ste_check.js — Node 22, no dependencies.  run: node ste_check.js doc.txt proc
const LIM = { proc: 20, desc: 25 }, PARA = 6, NOUN = 3;

const PASSIVE = /\b(?:is|are|was|were|be|been|being)\s+(?:not\s+|often\s+|normally\s+)?([a-z]+(?:ed|en)|done|set|put|kept|sent|made)\b/gi;

const VAGUE = ['as required', 'if necessary', 'as appropriate', 'properly', 'correctly',
  'regularly', 'periodically', 'normally', 'usually', 'typically', 'should', 'may',
  'might', 'could', 'various', 'relevant'];

// one group = one concept, which the document must express with one word only
const SYN = {
  start:  ['start', 'begin', 'commence', 'initiate', 'launch'],
  stop:   ['stop', 'halt', 'cease', 'terminate', 'abort'],
  check:  ['check', 'verify', 'confirm', 'validate', 'ensure', 'make sure'],
  remove: ['remove', 'detach', 'disconnect', 'unplug'],
  fix:    ['fix', 'repair', 'correct', 'rectify']
};

The three functions below carry the weight. Skip inflection handling and the checker fails silently. My first version appended ed to commence and searched for commenceed, so it reported zero terminology violations in a paragraph that visibly mixed three synonyms. When a checker returns zero, the document is not necessarily clean. The checker may simply be broken.

// inflection — needed so that commence also matches commenced
const forms = v => { const o = new Set([v, v + 's', v + 'ed', v + 'ing']);
  if (v.endsWith('e')) o.add(v + 'd');
  if (v.endsWith('y')) o.add(v.slice(0, -1) + 'ied'); return [...o]; };

// sentence split — cut only when a capital or a digit follows the stop
const sents = t => t.replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim()
  .split(/(?<=[.!?:])\s+(?=[A-Z0-9(])/).filter(Boolean);

// noun clusters — broken by punctuation, function words and digits
function runs(s) { const out = [];
  for (const c of s.split(/[,;:.()]+/)) { let cur = [];
    for (const t of c.split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean)) {
      const l = t.toLowerCase().replace(/[^a-z0-9]/g, '');
      if (!l || /\d/.test(l) || STOP.has(l) || /(?:ed|ing|ly)$/.test(l)) {
        if (cur.length > NOUN) out.push(cur.join(' ')); cur = [];
      } else cur.push(t); }
    if (cur.length > NOUN) out.push(cur.join(' ')); }
  return out; }

// penalty — the weights are arbitrary, use it only to compare one doc before and after

  • I deliberately kept seven separate metrics rather than one composite. A single total tells you a document is bad without telling you which rule to fix
  • The two modes proc and desc exist because procedures and explanations must not share a limit. That distinction is the technical basis for the conclusion in section 8
  • Under two hundred lines of code is enough to move a documentation argument onto numbers. That is the single decisive value of tooling here

4. Measured - the same procedure paragraph, before and after

I wrote a fictional firmware update procedure two ways. The content is identical and only the sentence rules differ. Here is the familiar version first.

--- A. written without control (the common house style) ---
Before the firmware update is started, it should be verified that the unit is powered
from the primary supply and that the redundant supply cable is disconnected, since the
update procedure cannot be commenced while both supplies are active. The configuration
backup file, which is normally generated by the maintenance console during the previous
session, must be copied to the removable media before proceeding, and it is recommended
that the operator confirms that the file size is not zero. If the status indicator is
flashing, the controller has not finished the previous write cycle and the operation
should be halted until the indicator becomes steady. After the media is inserted, the
update is initiated from the service menu, and the process, which typically takes about
eight minutes, must not be interrupted. The device will be restarted automatically; if
it is not restarted within two minutes, power should be removed and the unit should be
started again manually. Any configuration entries which were not migrated correctly can
be corrected later using the console, but the relevant log files should be retained for
analysis as required.
--- B. same content, controlled rules applied ---
Start this procedure only when the primary supply gives power to the unit.

Disconnect the redundant supply cable. Make sure that the backup file on the removable
media has a size of more than 0 bytes. If the status indicator flashes, wait until the
indicator is continuously on.

Put the removable media in the slot. Start the update from the service menu.
Do not stop the update. The update takes 8 minutes.

The unit starts again automatically. If the unit does not start again in 2 minutes,
remove the power. Then start the unit manually.

Keep the log files. You can correct the configuration entries with the console after
this procedure.

4.1 Where the checker was wrong - admit the false positives first

One violation survives in the controlled paragraph B. It is a defect in my checker, not in the document.

  B still flags: ["remove"]
  A flags       : ["start","check","remove"]

  the two words flagged in B
    "Disconnect the redundant supply cable."   -> disconnect
    "remove the power. Then start the unit."   -> remove

  my SYN entry:  remove: ['remove', 'detach', 'disconnect', 'unplug']
  in a real spec: remove and disconnect are separate approved words
                  with different meanings (pulling a cable is not
                  the same operation as detaching a component)
  • My synonym group put two different concepts in one bucket. In an actual controlled vocabulary disconnect and remove are distinct approved words with distinct meanings
  • The lesson is direct. When a checker reports a violation, suspect the checker before the document. Synonym rules in particular must come from your own glossary. Copy somebody else's list and false positives pour in
  • The failure ran in the other direction too. As noted in section 3, the early version that skipped inflections reported zero terminology variance in paragraph A, a paragraph with obvious mixing. A zero from a checker is not evidence

5. Translation gets easier - what a 34 percent vocabulary cut means

Translation is the most frequently cited reason to adopt a controlled language. The official FAQ states it directly: translation becomes easier because variation in source texts is minimized. Turn that sentence into a number and you get the distinct column from section 4. 107 words down to 71. A third of the separate expressions a translator has to decide about are gone.

  • Translation is priced by variance more than by volume. Translation memory scores each segment by similarity to previously translated text. Repeating structures raises exact and fuzzy match rates, which lowers the share that must be translated fresh
  • Three machine translation failures disappear under control. Attaching a modifier to the wrong noun in a long sentence, inventing an agent for a passive, and binding a pronoun to the wrong antecedent. None of the three has anywhere to occur when sentences are short, active, and pronoun free
  • Terminology consistency compounds in translation. If the source mixes start and commence, the translator assumes the difference is meaningful and picks two different target terms. Meaningless variance in the source hardens into meaningful difference in the target
  • This gain is paid for by writing the source under rules. It does not come from swapping engines or adding post editing. The cost lands on the author

One point of honesty is owed here. The official FAQ states that ASD does not endorse or certify automated tools that claim compliance, and that no tool can replace the standard itself. That applies to my checker too. It is a ruler for the rules, not the rules.

An easy mistake. The conclusion that a controlled language lowers translation cost holds only for documents that ship in more than one language. Carrying that argument into a Korean-only internal procedure has no basis. There the benefit is not translation spend but fewer misreadings, and that can only be demonstrated from incident records. If you build a business case on evidence that does not apply to your organisation, the first objection destroys it.

6. Where Korean documents sit - dropped particles, missing subjects, stacked nouns

Korean technical writing has failure points that appear reliably on the way into English. Three habits combine. I took one fictional line and pushed it straight through the checker.

--- Korean source (fictional) ---
점검 후 전원 차단 및 케이블 분리 작업 진행하고 이후 교체 부품 장착 상태 확인할 것

--- D. rendered into English word by word ---
After inspection completion power supply blocking and cable separation work progress
is required and afterward replacement part mounting state confirmation must be
performed by the operator.

  • cable separation work progress does not mean anything in English. Yet the Korean equivalent reads naturally. This is the classic case of a defect invisible in the source and exposed by translation
  • E uses fewer words than D, 24 against 26. Unpacking noun stacks into verbs feels like it should expand the text, but the packaging such as is required and must be performed by disappears
  • The practical move is to invert the order of work. To raise English quality, fix the Korean authoring stage rather than the translation stage. Applying only one instruction per sentence, an explicit subject, and no dropped particles to the Korean source drags the English metrics down on its own
  • Conversely, do not apply English controlled rules to Korean source text. A 20 word limit is an English number. Korean needs its own limits stated in phrases and clause elements

7. The price of automated enforcement - good writing gets penalised too

This is the most important experiment in the article. I put a well written conceptual paragraph through the same checker. Not a deliberately bad sample, but the kind of paragraph written carefully to persuade a reader.

--- C. conceptual paragraph (descriptive mode, limit 25 words) ---
Controlled language is often misunderstood as a style guide, but it is closer to a
contract between the writer and every reader who will ever open the document. When you
accept that contract, you give up a great deal: the rhythm you would use to keep a
reader interested, the aside that explains why a decision was made, and the well-placed
metaphor that makes an unfamiliar mechanism suddenly obvious. What you get in return is
narrow and valuable. Two people who read the same sentence in different rooms, in
different years, with different amounts of English, will act on it the same way.

  sents 5 / words 143 / avg 28.6 / max 42
  over 3  (60% of sentences past the limit)
  passive 3   pron 3   vague 0   synVar 0   cluster 0
  score 18       (six times the 3 scored by the controlled procedure B)

This paragraph does not need fixing. The parts that break the rules are exactly the parts doing the work. The contract metaphor, the colon followed by three items, the long final cadence are devices for persuading a reader. Cut them to 20 words and the meaning survives while the persuasion dies.

  • Enforce globally and writing like this gets blocked in CI. Authors then do one of two things. They chop sentences mechanically to satisfy the rule, or they switch the checker off. Both outcomes make the tool pointless
  • Of the three passives, a decision was made hides the agent on purpose, because who decided is not the point. What is a defect in a procedure is a feature in an explanation
  • The three instances of it work the same way. In explanatory prose a pronoun creates cohesion with the previous sentence. Turn them all back into nouns and the paragraph reads like a list
  • So the checker must be enabled per directory only. Rule strength is a property of the document type, not a single organisation wide setting

8. Where to use it and where not to

The decision reduces to one question. What does a misreading cost? If it costs equipment or people, apply the rules. If it costs one extra read, do not.

Document typeApplyModeReason
Work procedures and maintenance instructionsEnforceproc (20 words)Inverting the order causes accidents. Reading order must equal work order
Warnings and safety labelsEnforceproc (stricter)Read once under pressure. No subordinate clauses allowed
Installation and deployment runbooksEnforceprocFollowed literally at 3am by someone seeing it for the first time
API reference (parameters, errors)EnforceprocFed straight into machine translation and search summaries. Entries must share one shape
UI strings and error messagesEnforceprocTranslated and width constrained. Variance is cost, directly
Policies and contract clausesPartialproc, vocabulary onlyTerminology must be fixed, but legal phrasing cannot be freely shortened
Release notesWarn onlydescChange summaries benefit from brevity, but cutting the background makes them useless
Conceptual and architecture documentsDo not apply-See section 7. The rule breaking part is the functioning part
Tutorials and onboardingDo not apply-Half the value is context and motivation. Leave only imperatives and readers quit
Design decision records and postmortemsDo not apply-The body of the document is why. Controlled rules have no slot for why
  • The top five rows share one property. The reader moves their hands while reading. The bottom three are read only. In practice that is the cleanest boundary available
  • Partial and warn only exist for a reason. All or nothing adoption fails. Enforcing vocabulary while leaving sentence length free is the combination that survives longest in real teams
  • Separating by path inside one repository is the cleanest implementation. Hook only the procedures directory and do not run the checker anywhere else
  • When a single page mixes both kinds, split the procedural part into its own file. Only then can rules be applied per file

9. Adoption sequence - glossary, warn mode, enforce mode

Get the order wrong and it fails. Turning the checker on first is the most common failure. A tool that assigns penalties to other people's writing using rules nobody agreed to gets switched off in week one.

StageWhat you doDone whenFailure signal
1. GlossaryCollect every phrase your existing docs use for the same concept and keep oneThe top 30 concepts each have a chosen word and banned alternativesThe list was copied from elsewhere. Section 4.1 false positives flood in
2. ScopeSplit directories using the table in section 8. Decide what is excluded firstPath rules are written in a config fileYou start with everything enabled and plan to carve exceptions later
3. Warn modeReport penalties and let the build pass. At least four weeksYou know the penalty distribution of existing docsYou set a threshold without measuring the distribution
4. ThresholdStart near the median of that distribution and lower it each quarterMore than half of existing docs passYou pick an ideal number first and fail everything
5. Enforce modeBreak the build, in the procedures path onlyNew documents arrive already compliantSuppression comments multiply. That means the rules are wrong
6. MaintenanceTake false positive reports and change the rulesThe glossary is refreshed each quarterYou respond to reports by telling people to change the document

9.1 From warn mode to enforce mode

The simpler the hook, the longer it survives. Split the mode by path and keep exactly one threshold.

# procedures path — enforced. break the build above the threshold
for f in docs/procedures/*.md; do
  node ste_check.js "$f" proc | tee -a report.txt
done
node ste_gate.js report.txt --max-score=10 || exit 1

# explanatory path — warn only. never breaks the build
for f in docs/concepts/*.md; do
  node ste_check.js "$f" desc
done

# excluded paths are simply never run
#   docs/tutorials/  docs/adr/  docs/postmortems/
  • Set the threshold per document. A repository wide total fails automatically as the repository grows
  • Always ship an escape hatch. Allow a comment that exempts a paragraph, then count how many times it is used. If the count climbs, the problem is the rule, not the document
  • Enforce on new documents only and let existing ones be fixed when they are touched anyway. Demanding a full sweep first kills the adoption outright
  • Success or failure is decided at stage one, the glossary, not by the tool. The checker only enforces the glossary, and with no glossary there is nothing to enforce

10. Checklist and things not to do

If you are considering adoption, run your own organisation against these items first.

  • You listed the document types you will not cover, first
  • The glossary's chosen words came from your own documents, not somebody else's list
  • Procedures and explanations get different limits
  • The checker exposes per metric results, not one composite total
  • You ran warn mode for four weeks or more and measured the existing distribution
  • More than half of existing documents pass at your threshold
  • A false positive channel exists, and reports lead to rule changes
  • You did not use translation savings as justification for single language documents
  • Korean source rules and English rules are defined separately
  • When the checker reported zero violations you suspected the checker first

10.1 Things not to do

  • Turning on enforce mode repository wide - conceptual docs get blocked and people disable the checker
  • Importing somebody else's synonym list - as with remove and disconnect in section 4.1, distinct concepts land in one bucket
  • Judging documents by absolute penalty - the weights are arbitrary. Only before and after comparison is usable
  • Applying English limits to Korean text - 20 words is an English number
  • Fixing translation quality at the translation stage - the noun clusters in section 6 were manufactured in the Korean source
  • Inventing rules without reading the specification - this article did not open the specification either, which is why it lists no 53 rules
  • Running controlled rules over tutorials and postmortems - the rules have no slot for why

The conclusion returns to the opening scene. Two people read the cover and cable line differently not because the document was hard to read, but because two readings were permitted. What a controlled language does is not make documents easy. It removes the range of interpretation. So there is one test. Is the purpose of this document that it gets read, or that it gets read the same way? If the former, do not control it. If the latter, control it. It is not for every document.

## 0. The premise

**If the purpose of the document is that it gets read, do not control it.
If the purpose is that it gets read the same way, control it.**
It is not for every document.

## 1. Sentence rule table

| Rule | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction sentence | **20 words or fewer** | Removes the room for modifiers to fork |
| Descriptive sentence | **25 words or fewer** | Same reason, looser limit |
| Paragraph | **6 sentences or fewer** | One topic per paragraph |
| Noun stack | **3 words or fewer** | From four words the modification is ambiguous |
| Instructions per sentence | **1** | The single biggest cause of inverted steps |
| Passive voice | **forbidden** | The agent disappears |
| Unanchored pronoun | **forbidden** | it, this, that bind differently for every reader |
| One concept | **one word** | Never mix start / begin / commence |
| Vague terms | **forbidden** | properly, as required, regularly → use numbers |

## 2. The checker (Node 22, no dependencies)

```js
// ste_check.js   run: node ste_check.js doc.txt proc
const LIM = { proc: 20, desc: 25 }, PARA = 6, NOUN = 3;
const PASSIVE = /\b(?:is|are|was|were|be|been|being)\s+(?:not\s+|often\s+|normally\s+)?([a-z]+(?:ed|en)|done|set|put|kept|sent|made)\b/gi;
const VAGUE = ['as required','if necessary','as appropriate','properly','correctly','regularly',
  'periodically','normally','usually','typically','should','may','might','could','various','relevant'];
const SYN = { start:['start','begin','commence','initiate','launch'],
  stop:['stop','halt','cease','terminate','abort'],
  check:['check','verify','confirm','validate','ensure','make sure'],
  remove:['remove','detach','disconnect','unplug'],
  fix:['fix','repair','correct','rectify'] };
const STOP = new Set(('a an the and or but if then of to in on at by for with from into over under is are was were be been ' +
  'being do does did has have had will shall can may must should would could not no this that these those it its they them ' +
  'their you your we our as than so when while after before during until up down out off about again also only very each ' +
  'who which whom whose what where why how all any some both more most other such there here one two three').split(' '));

const forms = v => { const o = new Set([v, v+'s', v+'ed', v+'ing']);
  if (v.endsWith('e')) o.add(v+'d'); if (v.endsWith('y')) o.add(v.slice(0,-1)+'ied'); return [...o]; };
const sents = t => t.replace(/\s+/g,' ').trim().split(/(?<=[.!?:])\s+(?=[A-Z0-9(])/).filter(Boolean);
const wc = s => s.toLowerCase().replace(/[^a-z0-9' ]/g,' ').split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean);

function runs(s) { const out = [];
  for (const c of s.split(/[,;:.()]+/)) { let cur = [];
    for (const t of c.split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean)) {
      const l = t.toLowerCase().replace(/[^a-z0-9]/g,'');
      if (!l || /\d/.test(l) || STOP.has(l) || /(?:ed|ing|ly)$/.test(l)) {
        if (cur.length > NOUN) out.push(cur.join(' ')); cur = [];
      } else cur.push(t); }
    if (cur.length > NOUN) out.push(cur.join(' ')); }
  return out; }

function analyze(text, mode) {
  const lim = LIM[mode || 'proc'], paras = text.trim().split(/\n{2,}/), ss = paras.flatMap(sents);
  const lens = ss.map(s => wc(s).length), total = lens.reduce((a,b) => a+b, 0);
  const low = ' ' + text.toLowerCase().replace(/[^a-z0-9' ]/g,' ').replace(/\s+/g,' ') + ' ';
  const has = w => low.includes(' ' + w + ' ');
  const passive = (text.match(PASSIVE) || []).length;
  const pron = (text.match(/\b(?:it|they|them)\b/gi) || []).length;
  const vague = VAGUE.filter(has);
  const varied = Object.entries(SYN).filter(e => e[1].filter(v => forms(v).some(has)).length > 1).map(e => e[0]);
  const cl = ss.flatMap(runs), over = lens.filter(n => n > lim).length;
  const longP = paras.filter(p => sents(p).length > PARA).length;
  return { sents: ss.length, words: total, distinct: new Set(ss.flatMap(wc)).size,
    avg: +(total/ss.length).toFixed(1), max: Math.max(...lens), over, passive, pron,
    vague: vague.length, varied, clusters: cl.length,
    score: over*3 + passive*2 + vague.length + pron + varied.length*3 + cl.length*2 + longP*2 };
}
module.exports = { analyze };
if (require.main === module) { const fs = require('fs');
  console.log(analyze(fs.readFileSync(process.argv[2], 'utf8'), process.argv[3])); }
```

## 3. Measured result (same procedure paragraph, before and after)

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sentences | 6 | 13 | split apart |
| Words per sentence | 30.7 | **8.5** | -72% |
| Longest sentence | 40 | **18** | inside the limit |
| Sentences over limit | 6 (100%) | **0** | fully cleared |
| Passive constructions | 19 | **0** | -100% |
| Unanchored pronouns | 3 | **0** | -100% |
| Vague terms | 6 | **0** | -100% |
| Concept groups varied | 3 | 1 | the remaining 1 is a checker false positive |
| **Total words** | 184 | **111** | **-40%** |
| **Distinct words** | 107 | **71** | **-34%** |
| Penalty | 74 | **3** | **-96%** |

**Key point: sentences were split and the total word count still fell.**
What disappeared was padding, not content.

**Control group — a well written conceptual paragraph through the same checker:**
60% of sentences over the limit, passive 3, pronouns 3, score 18. **That paragraph needs no fixing.**

## 4. Scope decision table

| Document type | Apply | Mode | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work procedures, maintenance | **Enforce** | proc (20 words) | Inverted order causes accidents |
| Warnings, safety labels | **Enforce** | proc (stricter) | Must be read once, under pressure |
| Installation, deployment runbooks | **Enforce** | proc | Followed literally by a first time reader |
| API reference | **Enforce** | proc | Fed to machine translation and search summaries |
| UI strings, error messages | **Enforce** | proc | Variance is translation cost, directly |
| Policies, contract clauses | Partial (vocabulary) | proc | Legal phrasing cannot be freely cut |
| Release notes | Warn only | desc | Cutting the background makes them useless |
| Conceptual, architecture | **Do not apply** | — | The rule breaking part is the functioning part |
| Tutorials, onboarding | **Do not apply** | — | Half the value is context and motivation |
| Decision records, postmortems | **Do not apply** | — | The rules have no slot for why |

**The boundary: does the reader move their hands while reading?**
If yes, enforce. If no, do not apply.

## 5. Fix the Korean source first

| Habit | What happens in English | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dropped subject | No agent, so the clause turns passive | Name the actor in the Korean source |
| Dropped particles | Turns into a bare noun sequence | Restore the verb |
| Stacked nouns | Four word phrase, ambiguous modification | Unpack it with a verb |
| Chained connectives | Several instructions in one sentence | One instruction, one sentence |
| Non finite endings | Obligation and recommendation blur | Imperative for mandatory, if clause for conditional |

**Translation quality is decided at the authoring stage, not the translation stage.**

## 6. Adoption checklist

- [ ] Listed the document types you will **not** cover, **first**
- [ ] Glossary words came from **your own documents**, not somebody else's list
- [ ] Procedures and explanations get **different limits**
- [ ] Checker output is **per metric**, not one composite total
- [ ] Ran warn mode for **four weeks or more** and measured the distribution
- [ ] **More than half** of existing documents pass at the threshold
- [ ] A false positive channel exists and reports lead to **rule changes**
- [ ] Did **not** use translation savings to justify single language documents
- [ ] Korean source rules and English rules defined **separately**
- [ ] When the checker reported zero, you **suspected the checker first**

## 7. Things not to do

- ❌ Turning on **enforce mode repository wide** — people disable the checker
- ❌ Importing **somebody else's synonym list** — distinct concepts land in one bucket
- ❌ Judging documents by **absolute penalty** — the weights are arbitrary
- ❌ Applying the English 20 word limit to **Korean text**
- ❌ Fixing translation quality **at the translation stage**
- ❌ **Inventing rules** without reading the specification
- ❌ Running controlled rules over tutorials and postmortems — no slot for why
- ❌ Treating **a zero from the checker as evidence** — one missing inflection produces a zero
