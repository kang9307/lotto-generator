John Carmack: "Don't Be the Out of Touch Kung Fu Master" - What Hand-Coding Skills Mean in the AI Era

From Miyamoto Musashi's swordsmanship to modern martial arts and AI programming: preserving practical discernment amid relentless abstraction

2026-09-14 Tech & IT

In the annals of computer science, certain legendary figures come to embody entire technological philosophies. John Carmack (id: ID_AA_Carmack), co-founder of id Software and the mastermind behind landmark titles such as DOOM and Quake, has long stood as an icon of uncompromising technical craftsmanship and low-level hardware optimization. Having built his reputation on meticulously tuning assembly routines, squeezing memory alignments, and shaving individual clock cycles off raw silicon, Carmack's recent reflections published on X (formerly Twitter) sent ripples across the global software engineering community.

Carmack recounted reading an English translation of Miyamoto Musashi's classic treatise, The Book of Five Rings, and observing the historical progression of swordsmanship and martial arts. Over centuries, techniques that originated as life-or-death battlefield necessities gradually transformed into competitive sports, historical curiosities, and personal hobbies. Carmack pointed out that an identical current is now sweeping through the software engineering discipline. While countless developers still pride themselves on the fine art of writing code entirely by hand, relentless waves of abstraction and the rapid ascent of generative AI tools are systematically pushing manual syntax crafting from the front lines of commercial utility into the realm of cultural discipline and recreational art.

His sharpest warning was both vivid and unvarnished: "Don’t be the out of touch Kung Fu master, heir to lifetimes of tradition, that gets mauled by an amateur MMA fighter." Mistaking sentimental attachment to legacy craft for genuine market competitiveness is a trap that leads directly to obsolescence. This article examines the exact context of Carmack’s message on X (Twitter) and explores how software engineers must redefine the role and value of hand-coding skills in the era of artificial intelligence.

This analysis is based on primary statements published by John Carmack on his official X (Twitter) account (@ID_AA_Carmack) on September 11, 2026, alongside verifiable public discussions. It avoids speculative metrics and unsubstantiated claims, focusing strictly on architectural evolution, engineering history, and practical survival strategies for professional software developers.

John Carmack's Proposition on X (Twitter) - Programming Through the Lens of Musashi's Five Rings

In the opening passages of his post on X (Twitter), Carmack shared his thoughts after reading the introduction to a translation of Musashi's Book of Five Rings. The introduction detailed how swordsmanship and martial arts evolved over time from pragmatic combat necessities into formalized athletic sports, historical curiosities, and recreational pastimes.

A Realistic Warning from an Industry Titan

Carmack's engineering pedigree is virtually unmatched in the interactive software and gaming industries. As the architectural driver of id Software, he pioneered real-time 3D rendering algorithms and engine architectures that defined entire eras of computing. Because his historic achievements were rooted in a profound, intimate command of computer hardware, some might have expected him to mount a spirited defense of traditional manual craftsmanship. Instead, Carmack has consistently operated as a ruthlessly honest technological pragmatist.

In his post, Carmack confessed: "The poignant undercurrent for me is that I can see many programming skills following that path." He recognized with clear-eyed sobriety that the manual coding skills software engineers have spent decades honing are steadily losing their status as essential frontline weapons in commercial software delivery.

Where Martial Arts History Intersects with Modern Code

Carmack underscored this transformation with a compelling historical example. During the post-World War II Allied occupation of Japan, when martial arts were strictly banned, practitioners adapted to survive: Judo was presented to authorities as equivalent to Western wrestling, and Kendo later aligned itself with Western fencing.

By shedding their original lethal intent and rebranding into structured sporting games with standardized rules, these traditions preserved their lineage. However, their position as practical, lethal combat doctrines was irrevocably extinguished. Carmack observed that the meticulous manual construction of code statements is reaching an analogous turning point in technological history.

The Historical Metamorphosis of Martial Arts - From Lethal Battlefield Tool to Spiritual Discipline

Studying the evolution of martial disciplines illuminates how the fundamental purpose of any technical craft shifts when external environments and tools transform. This historical cycle illustrates how practical survival techniques crystallize into cultural traditions.

From Practical Combat 'Jitsu' to Spiritual Cultivation 'Do'

In traditional Japanese martial traditions, the suffix -jitsu (術) denoted practical, applied techniques designed to neutralize opponents on chaotic battlefields. Kenjutsu and jujutsu were brutal, outcome-driven combat methods aimed at cutting down armor-clad foes or breaking joints. When protracted civil wars concluded and modern firearms revolutionized warfare, these practical skills lost their direct combat function and morphed into -do (道)—paths of personal discipline, mental refinement, and physical character building.

Carmack captured this conceptual shift with playful wordplay: "AI is making many other programming skills much less critical. We aren’t there yet, but carefully writing code completely by hand is moving from a -jitsu to a -do. Code-do? Codo?" The act of handcrafting every line of source code is rapidly moving from an indispensable commercial prerequisite to a discipline pursued for self-fulfillment and intellectual gratification.

How Advanced Armaments Displaced Physical Craftsmanship

Swordsmanship did not fade because swordsmen ceased training diligently. It receded because gunpowder, muskets, and artillery fundamentally rearranged the mechanics of conflict. A lifetime of disciplined sword training could not overcome a conscript soldier who received a few days of firearm instruction. Breakthroughs in tool paradigms inevitably establish insurmountable productivity advantages that redefine combat effectiveness.

Software engineering has witnessed identical cycles. Whenever compilers, garbage collectors, or higher-level frameworks appeared, seasoned practitioners frequently cautioned that abstracting away memory registers and system calls would compromise quality. Yet history consistently rewarded the tools that delivered higher developer velocity, rapid iteration, and manageable maintenance overheads.

The Irreversible March of Computing Abstraction - From Hex Machine Code to Modern AI

Computer science has always been an uninterrupted march toward higher levels of abstraction, progressively isolating human problem-solving from the tedious mechanical details of underlying hardware. Carmack contextualized current AI developments by citing precedents from his early engineering career.

Manual Hex Assembling and Assembly Programming Across Decades

Carmack reflected on the earliest days of microcomputing: "There are probably dozens of people reading this that remember hand assembling opcodes to hex and still have some magic numbers burned into their memory." Early developers routinely translated processor instructions directly into hexadecimal opcodes with pencil and paper, burning bytes straight into EPROM chips without assembler assistance.

Yet as Carmack pointed out: "even the small group of people still programming in assembly today (hey, @FFmpeg !) don’t work at that primitive level now." Even the elite contributors maintaining FFmpeg's performance-critical multimedia routines rely heavily on modern macro assemblers, compiler intrinsics, and automated profiling tools. Translating opcodes to hexadecimal by hand vanished decades ago into the realm of nostalgic curiosity.

The One-Way Ladder of Abstraction and Shifting Industry Baselines

Transitions from machine code to assembly, from assembly to C, and from C to managed, garbage-collected environments have proven entirely irreversible. While early C compilers faced skepticism regarding binary bloat and performance penalties, optimizing compilers eventually surpassed the register allocation capabilities of all but the most exceptional human programmers.

Generative AI and autonomous coding agents represent the newest rung on this abstraction ladder. The labor-intensive task of transcribing mental algorithmic logic into rigid programming syntax is being elevated to high-level intent specification, architectural boundaries, and automated synthesis.

The Trap of the "Out of Touch Kung Fu Master" - Tradition versus Reality

The most striking passage in Carmack's commentary highlights the perils of placing dogmatic faith in traditional martial forms when facing modern, practical combatants. It cuts directly to the psychological vulnerability of practitioners who confuse historical reverence with practical efficacy.

The Disillusionment of Traditional Arts in the MMA Arena

Carmack took care to validate technical enthusiasm: "That’s ok! The retro computing scene is delightful, full of people building and exercising old skills for the love of it." Cultivating vintage skills purely for the intrinsic pleasure of the craft is a joyful, legitimate pursuit. But his adjoining caveat was uncompromising:

"But don’t be the out of touch Kung Fu master, heir to lifetimes of tradition, that gets mauled by an amateur MMA fighter."

In modern martial arts history, traditional stylists frequently claimed their complex forms and venerable lineages conferred superior fighting prowess. However, when tested in open mixed martial arts contests against cross-trained fighters using fundamental boxing, wrestling, and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, dogmatic stylists were repeatedly overwhelmed. Over-refining traditional forms without subjecting them to unvarnished empirical testing inevitably breeds vulnerability.

Musashi and Modern Weaponry - Unapologetic Pragmatism

Carmack concluded his commentary with a poignant observation: "Musashi would probably have been pretty enthusiastic about assault rifles."

Miyamoto Musashi was no dogmatic mystic. He famously carved a wooden oar into a lengthened sword to overcome Ganryu Kojiro's reach advantage and strategically positioned himself with the sun at his back to blind his adversaries. During Musashi's lifetime, firearms were already transforming Japanese battlefields, notably demonstrated at the Battle of Nagashino. Had the legendary swordsman walked into the 21st century, he would not have sentimentally restricted himself to cold steel; he would have eagerly mastered the most reliable, decisive firearms available.

Does Hand-Coding Still Matter? - Four Enduring Pillars of Low-Level Competence

Carmack's warning must not be misconstrued as a decree that software fundamentals and manual coding fluency have become useless. Just as special operations soldiers master hand-to-hand combat and rigorous physical conditioning while deploying modern firearms, low-level coding experience serves as the foundational substrate that allows engineers to orchestrate AI tools with commanding authority. Four critical dimensions illustrate why manual coding competence remains indispensable:

Detecting Hallucinations and Auditing Subtle Edge-Case Integrity

Large language models generate statistically plausible code, but they regularly harbor subtle off-by-one errors, concurrency race conditions, and phantom API assumptions. Developers who have never implemented complex data structures or traced memory mutations manually easily fall prey to superficial correctness. Deep hand-coding experience provides the critical analytical lens required to spot algorithmic flaws that automated linters miss.

Root-Cause Triage, System Observability, and Debugging Intuition

When enterprise systems encounter severe production degradation—such as silent memory leaks, thread pool exhaustion, or database deadlocks—generic AI prompts cannot replace diagnostic intuition. Engineers who possess a visceral mental model of operating system scheduling, memory paging, socket lifecycles, and garbage collection behavior can formulate precise hypotheses and isolate faults rapidly.

Architectural Boundary Setting and Managing Domain Context

While AI generates localized functions and boilerplate code effortlessly, it struggles to maintain cohesive domain models and bounded contexts across distributed services. Deciding where transaction boundaries lie, how state mutability is partitioned, and which components own specific responsibilities remains the domain of human architects. Only developers who have implemented systems from scratch understand the operational costs hidden beneath abstraction layers.

Preserving True Engineering Autonomy Independent of Tool Failures

A developer completely reliant on automated generation becomes paralyzed when AI services face outages, API quotas expire, or model versions drift. Engineers grounded in low-level coding fundamentals retain complete agency. When crises arise, they can inspect raw assembly, analyze network packets, and author immediate hotfixes, maintaining full command over their technological domain.

Ecosystem Debates and Friction - Genuine Productivity versus Foundational Decay

Carmack's observations ignited vigorous discussion across international engineering platforms. While many seasoned practitioners endorsed his pragmatic vision, others raised serious structural concerns regarding the long-term vitality of the software profession.

Skepticism Over Net Productivity and Unchecked Code Volume

Numerous senior architects question whether the flood of generated code translates into genuine business productivity or merely creates mountains of unmaintainable technical debt. When teams use AI primarily to inflate code volume rather than elevating design rigor, systems become bloated, brittle, and impenetrable to human maintainers over time.

Critics also express concern that frictionless code generation encourages superficial problem-solving. True software engineering entails deeply understanding complex domain realities; relying on AI completions can short-circuit the intellectual struggle necessary to achieve clarity and architectural elegance.

The Threat of Foundational Knowledge Atrophy Among Junior Engineers

A more profound anxiety centers on technical education and the mentorship of early-career programmers. Today's senior engineers learned their craft when building software required direct engagement with algorithms, memory models, and compilation chains, forging robust mental representations of how computers actually function.

If incoming cohorts rely exclusively on generative assistance from day one, they risk missing the vital trial-and-error experiences that build diagnostic intuition. An industry populated by developers who can prompt applications into existence but cannot troubleshoot the underlying runtime engine faces alarming systemic fragility when unexpected outages occur.

Practical Takeaways for Software Engineers - Embracing Pragmatic Realism

John Carmack's commentary provides clear guidance for developers navigating this technological watershed, urging a healthy reconciliation between historical appreciation and contemporary commercial demands.

Separating Romantic Craftsmanship from Economic Value Creation

Software development communities have long celebrated intricate language features and elegant esoteric patterns. However, commercial software exists primarily to resolve user problems and create sustainable economic value, not to serve as a showcase for self-indulgent artisanal code.

End users and stakeholders derive zero utility from the fact that an engineer wrote thousands of boilerplate statements entirely by hand. Refusing high-leverage tools in the name of purity risks confusing technical nostalgia with commercial engineering.

Loving the Old Craft while Brandishing the Sharpest Modern Weapons

The optimal mindset is embodied in Carmack’s balanced philosophy: savor the beauty of manual craftsmanship, cherish retro computing, and study low-level systems with boundless curiosity, but enter the commercial marketplace armed with the most formidable tools available.

Engineers must emulate Musashi's pragmatic spirit—valuing martial discipline while instantly reaching for modern firepower on the battlefield. By combining deep systems intuition with the rapid leverage of AI generation, modern developers can achieve levels of productivity and architectural resilience that previous generations could scarcely imagine.
