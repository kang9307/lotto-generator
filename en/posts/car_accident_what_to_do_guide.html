Car Accident in Korea: Complete Guide on What to Do

Scene safety, emergency calls, evidence, insurance filing, fault ratio, settlement and injury claims — step by step

Introduction: Why Knowing the Steps Matters Before an Accident Happens

A car accident can happen to anyone, regardless of how experienced a driver you are. Korean traffic statistics show more than 200,000 traffic accidents occur each year in Korea alone. The problem is not just the collision itself — it is the panic that follows. Most people freeze up and forget what to do next, which can turn a simple fender-bender into a hit-and-run charge, an unfavorable insurance settlement, or a missed injury claim.

Knowing the correct sequence of actions in advance is one of the most practical things you can do. This guide covers every stage from the moment of impact to final settlement, based on information from the Korea Non-Life Insurance Association (KNIA), Korean traffic regulations, and established insurance practices as of 2026.

1. Immediate Actions — Scene Safety Comes First

The first few minutes after a car accident are the most critical. Secondary accidents caused by other vehicles hitting the stopped cars are often more fatal than the original collision. Follow these steps in order.

STEP 1 — Turn on hazard lights and move to safety

Activate your hazard flashers immediately and move your vehicle to the roadside or a safe area. On highways, get completely beyond the guardrail. If your car cannot move, place a reflective emergency triangle about 50–100 meters behind the vehicle and keep the hazard lights on. Staying in a live lane is one of the most dangerous things you can do.

STEP 2 — Check for injuries and call 119

Check yourself, your passengers, and the occupants of the other vehicle for injuries. If anyone is hurt, call 119 (fire and EMS) immediately. Do not move a person with a suspected spinal injury — wait for trained rescuers. Follow the 119 dispatcher's instructions for first aid until help arrives.

1.1 Never Leave the Scene

Leaving the scene without providing aid when someone is injured can be prosecuted under Korea's Act on Special Cases Concerning Traffic Accidents as hit-and-run (뺑소니), which carries severe criminal penalties including imprisonment. Even in a minor accident, you must exchange contact information and notify your insurer before driving away.

1.2 Stay Calm and Watch What You Say

Avoid heated arguments with the other driver. More importantly, do not admit fault at the scene. Statements like "It was my fault" can work against you even if the actual fault ratio ends up being shared. Fault is determined later based on objective evidence such as dashcam footage, photos, and witness accounts — not on-the-spot admissions.

2. Who to Call — 112 vs. 119

Agency Number When to Call
Fire & EMS 119 Any time someone is injured
Police 112 Injuries, fault disputes, drunk/uninsured driver, public property damage
Insurer 24-hr call center or app Report the accident and request roadside assistance

2.1 When Police (112) Is Required

  • Any personal injury or fatality
  • Other driver suspected of drunk driving or no insurance
  • Parties cannot agree on fault
  • Damage to public property (guardrails, traffic lights, etc.)
  • Other driver flees the scene

2.2 Handling Minor Accidents Without Police

For minor property-damage-only accidents where both drivers are insured and agree on fault, the KNIA's Traffic Accident Quick Settlement Form (교통사고 신속처리 협의서) allows both parties to document the accident and submit it to their insurers without a police report. The form is available at consumer.knia.or.kr. When in doubt, call the police — documentation always helps.

3. Gathering Evidence — Do This Before Police Arrive

Evidence is the foundation of every insurance claim and fault dispute. Collect as much as possible while the scene is intact.

3.1 Photo and Video Checklist

  • Both vehicles from front, sides, and rear (with license plates visible)
  • Close-up shots of all damage
  • Wide-angle shots showing vehicle positions relative to road markings
  • Traffic signal status, lane markings, intersection layout
  • Skid marks, debris, or other road evidence
  • Surrounding CCTV cameras (note their locations)

3.2 Secure Dashcam Footage Immediately

Dashcam footage is the single most important piece of evidence in most accidents. Remove the memory card right away to prevent it being overwritten, or manually trigger the dashcam's emergency save function. For parked-car incidents (hit-and-run), check the parking-mode recording. Act fast — once footage is overwritten, it cannot be recovered.

3.3 Exchange Information with the Other Driver

  • Full name and contact number
  • Insurance company name and policy number
  • Vehicle license plate and make/model
  • Witness names and contact numbers (if available)

4. Filing an Insurance Claim

Contact your insurer as soon as possible — ideally from the scene. Most major Korean non-life insurers operate 24-hour accident hotlines and mobile apps for immediate reporting.

4.1 Information to Give Your Insurer

  • Date, time, and exact location of the accident
  • Brief description of what happened
  • Other driver's license plate and insurer name
  • Whether anyone was injured
  • Whether police were called

4.2 Emergency Roadside Assistance

If your car is undriveable, request your insurer's emergency roadside service. Most Korean auto insurance policies include towing, flat tire, battery jump-start, and lockout assistance. Coverage scope and usage limits vary by policy, so check your policy details in advance.

5. Fault Ratio — How It Is Determined and How to Dispute It

Fault ratio (과실비율) is the percentage of fault assigned to each party. It determines how much each side's insurer pays and affects the compensation calculation.

5.1 The KNIA Fault Ratio Standards

The Korea Non-Life Insurance Association publishes the Automobile Accident Fault Ratio Recognition Standards, the industry-wide reference used by insurance adjusters. It incorporates court precedents, laws, and dispute resolution cases. Adjusters review dashcam footage, photos, witness statements, and CCTV to apply these standards to each accident type.

5.2 Disputing Your Fault Ratio

  • KNIA Fault Ratio Dispute Resolution Committee (accident.knia.or.kr): Submit additional evidence for independent review
  • Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) Financial Dispute Mediation Committee: File a complaint against your insurer
  • Civil litigation: Seek a binding court judgment if mediation fails

6. Settlement — Take Your Time

A settlement agreement ends your right to claim further compensation. Do not rush into one, especially before you fully understand the extent of your injuries or property damage.

6.1 The Risk of Settling on the Spot

Whiplash (cervical sprain) and soft tissue injuries often produce no pain at the moment of impact but develop significantly over the following 24–72 hours. Accepting cash and signing a release at the scene can leave you unable to recover treatment costs that arise later. Always get a medical evaluation before settling.

6.2 What to Verify Before Signing

  • Full vehicle repair estimate
  • Current and projected future medical expenses
  • Lost income compensation (if applicable)
  • Potential for lasting disability or ongoing treatment
  • Whether treatment is fully concluded

6.3 Settlement Agreement Clauses to Watch

Settlement forms often include a clause waiving all future civil and criminal claims. Signing this makes subsequent claims extremely difficult. If the settlement amount is significant or you are unsure of the terms, consult an attorney before signing.

7. Injuries and Long-Term Symptoms

7.1 Visit a Hospital Within 72 Hours

Even if you feel fine, visit a doctor (orthopedic or neurology/ER department) within 72 hours of the accident. Obtain a medical certificate and submit it to your insurer. The longer you wait, the harder it becomes to establish a causal link between the accident and your symptoms for compensation purposes.

7.2 Residual Disability Claims

If you are left with permanent functional impairment after treatment concludes — limited range of motion, chronic pain, neurological symptoms — you can apply for a residual disability (후유장해) assessment. The disability grade determines additional compensation from the at-fault party's insurer. This is why it is important not to settle until treatment is complete.

8. Special Situations

8.1 Uninsured or Drunk Driver

If the other driver has no insurance, your own policy's uninsured motorist (무보험차 상해) coverage can cover your injuries. For drunk driving cases, the at-fault insurer pays out but retains the right to recover from the intoxicated driver. Make sure police measure and document the driver's blood alcohol level on the scene.

8.2 Parked-Car Hit-and-Run

If you return to your parked car and find it damaged, check nearby CCTV and your own dashcam's parking-mode footage. Ask police to help identify the other party. If the driver cannot be found, claim through your own own-vehicle damage (자차) coverage. Note that using this coverage may affect your insurance premium at renewal.

8.3 Highway Accidents

On Korean expressways, the risk of secondary collisions is extremely high. After a highway accident, immediately get out of the car and move completely beyond the guardrail or to the far edge of the emergency lane. Call the Korea Expressway Corporation emergency line (1588-2504) or use roadside emergency phones. Remaining inside your stopped vehicle on a highway is one of the most dangerous decisions you can make.

9. Quick Reference Checklist

  • Turn on hazard lights and move to safety
  • Check for injuries — call 119 if anyone is hurt
  • Call 112 (police) when required
  • Photograph the scene thoroughly
  • Secure dashcam footage immediately
  • Exchange information with the other driver
  • Call your insurer's 24-hour hotline
  • Visit a hospital within 72 hours
  • Do not settle before treatment is complete
  • Dispute unfair fault ratios through the KNIA committee
