This article is based on the published retrospective essay 「Plan mode is dead」 by Ayman Nadeem, creator of Nuanced and formerly a senior engineer at GitHub. It focuses on the verified experiential observations and architectural assessments recorded in the source text, strictly excluding unverified commercial metrics and arbitrary evaluations of proprietary products.

1. The Rise of Plan-Centric Coding Apps and the Genesis of a Retrospective

As artificial intelligence coding agents advanced into everyday engineering workflows, a novel form of cognitive disorientation emerged across development environments. With modern frontier models capable of synthesizing thousands of lines of source code in mere minutes, developers frequently inherit massive structural maintenance burdens long before determining why a feature matters or how it should be shaped. When automated synthesis outpaces human capacity to inspect, comprehend, and reason about architectural changes, foundational abstractions degrade and erroneous assumptions become prematurely hardened into the codebase.

Motivated by this growing tension, an ambitious software venture attempted to place planning at the very center of AI-assisted engineering. Ayman Nadeem, a former senior engineer at GitHub building Nuanced, committed deeply to this hypothesis. As Nadeem recounted in the original essay: "My belief in this idea was so strong that I built and launched an entire desktop coding app around it." Nuanced stemmed from the acute observation that while artificial intelligence had radically amplified the speed and volume of code generation, the user interfaces designed to manage this heightened tempo remained tethered to legacy paradigms. As the author rotated through the Claude Code CLI, to Conductor, and finally Codex when it launched, plans were meticulously shaped and whittled without a clear home to iterate on them, and pieces of a plan had to be copied into new chat messages to revise them. That clunky cut-and-paste workflow, and the wish to give plans a home, motivated Nuanced.

Yet, following the full implementation and commercial launch of the desktop application, practical realities diverged sharply from initial expectations. The author openly acknowledged that the product's foundational premise had fallen short. Furthermore, that realization cast light on a broader shift occurring across modern engineering tools. Nadeem observed: "Nuanced's approach to planning failed, but it also revealed to me how plan modes more broadly aren't as useful anymore."

Crucially, the declaration that 「plan mode is dead」 does not suggest abandoning architectural deliberation or engineering discipline. The object of criticism is neither the cognitive act of planning nor the rigorous evaluation of trade-offs. Rather, the critique targets 「plan mode」 as an isolated, rigid user interface abstraction and the massive AI-generated specification artifacts it routinely produces. As Nadeem put it: "There is still an enormous amount of planning happening inside that loop, but it doesn't necessarily need to appear as a document called “the plan”."

  • Identification of the cognitive dissonance between exponential code generation velocity and human inspection bandwidth
  • Development of Nuanced, a dedicated desktop coding application designed around structured planning modes
  • Empirical failure of the dedicated plan mode architecture, acknowledged transparently through production retrospective
  • Clarification that the critique refutes rigid interface abstractions and static documents rather than the act of planning itself

2. The Two Historical Purposes of Plan Modes

To diagnose why plan modes have lost their efficacy, Nadeem delineated the two historical functions that dedicated planning interfaces originally evolved to serve. Conflating these distinct engineering objectives obscures the root cause of current workflow friction.

The author defined these functional pillars with precision: "Historically, plan modes served two purposes: (1) they specified instructions that were sufficiently precise enough for an agent, and (2) they helped humans understand what they were building." The former functioned as an execution contract ensuring machine compliance, while the latter served as a cognitive aid maintaining human orientation.

Historical Purpose of Plan Modes Author's Assessment Direct Source Citation
Purpose 1: Specifying precise instructions for an agent Rapidly becoming obsolete as model reasoning improves "I think #1 is rapidly becoming obsolete as models get better."
Purpose 2: Helping humans understand system architecture Matters more than ever, yet plan modes are the wrong abstraction "I think #2 matters more than ever, but plan modes are the wrong abstraction for it"

2.1 The Obsolescence of Explicit Agent Instructions

During the early iterations of autonomous coding agents, developers could rarely rely on brief natural language instructions. Early models regularly misconstrued project boundaries, hallucinated non-existent dependencies, or derailed across multi-file refactorings. Forcing the generation of an exhaustive, step-by-step instructional blueprint prior to execution served as an essential guardrail to prevent catastrophic regressions.

However, recent frontier models demonstrate unprecedented in-context comprehension, autonomous verification, and adaptive problem-solving. Contemporary models infer implicit domain constraints, fill architectural gaps intelligently, and self-correct through execution feedback. Consequently, the necessity for human engineers to curate exhaustive instructional blueprints before running an agent has diminished precipitously. The first historical objective is rapidly becoming obsolete as models improve.

2.2 The Escalating Need for Human System Comprehension

Conversely, the second objective—enabling human engineers to maintain a coherent mental model of what is being built—has escalated into an existential challenge. Agents now operate across entire repositories, modifying disparate modules at once. The author writes of feeling mentally disconnected and zombie-like, especially as coding apps like Conductor and Codex made it possible to run more agents in parallel.

Nadeem identified this understanding dilemma as an enduring question for software engineering: how can humans preserve a coherent mental model of a system when machines modify it faster than human attention can review? While this need is more critical than ever, the author concluded that plan modes represent a fundamentally flawed abstraction for achieving that comprehension.

3. Four Fundamental Causes Behind Nuanced's Failure

The author does not say the original ideas about gaps in existing tools were wrong, but that the implementation did not deliver the expected solution, and lists four reasons: "I conflated planning with a plan / models got really good / no one wants to read AI-generated text / we separated planning from building in a way that was disruptive".

First, the product conflated the ongoing cognitive act of planning with the physical generation of a plan document. Engineering deliberation requires evaluating trade-offs, questioning premises, and exploring architectural variants. Nuanced mistakenly reduced this dynamic thought process into a static textual artifact.

Second, underlying model capabilities advanced faster than interface design anticipated. As models got better at understanding large codebases through context and memory, the need to explicitly instruct them to produce a well-thought-out result "had shrunk."

Third, human developers universally demonstrated an unwillingness to consume voluminous AI-generated text. Early users showed surprisingly little appetite for exhaustive specifications ("But early users had surprisingly little appetite for that spec."). The author experienced the exact same cognitive fatigue firsthand: "My eyes kept glazing over." An attempt to mitigate this with a "Spec Tour" feature that walked users through the important parts merely added another layer of superficial complexity: "But that only bolted on an additional layer of complexity".

Fourth, bifurcating planning and building into distinct execution modes disrupted engineering flow. Forcing developers to toggle manually between a "plan state" and an "implementation state" added to cognitive load. Nadeem concluded that this distinction should be handled dynamically by the AI itself, leveraging context it already possesses: "This felt like the kind of distinction AI should make for you based on context it already has".

  • Conceptual error of reducing continuous architectural deliberation to a static documentation artifact
  • Rapidly shrinking utility of prescriptive specifications as models improve
  • Profound human reading fatigue and disinterest when confronted with extensive AI-generated prose
  • Artificial segregation of planning and execution modes causing severe disruption to natural developer focus

4. Contrasting Linear Spec Pipelines with Iterative Discovery Loops

The initial architecture of Nuanced embodied a classical, linear waterfall pipeline. Developers engaged in disambiguation dialogues, generated comprehensive specifications, reviewed and approved documents, and finally supervised implementation. In contrast, the author came to see that a simple chat interface where planning happened as needed was actually pretty great.

The author contrasted the initial pipeline with a different loop that became possible as agents improved, using verbatim workflow definitions. This structural comparison illuminates the philosophical transition from rigid documentation to agile exploration.

Workflow Paradigm Sequential Execution Phases (Verbatim Source) Human Intervention Points (Source Grounded)
Nuanced's Linear Pipeline chat → disambiguate by answering questions → generate spec → review spec → revise spec → approve → implement → review code Answering questions, reviewing and revising specs, approving, final code review
Iterative Discovery Loop understand → act → inspect → clarify → adjust → act again The source does not specify human intervention points for this loop (it describes the loop as enabled by agents that can inspect and revise their own work)

4.1 The Fragility of Nuanced's Eight-Stage Linear Pipeline

Nuanced's original pipeline relied on total upfront specification. The developer initiated a chat, answered clarifying questions to remove ambiguity, triggered automated spec generation, reviewed the generated draft, revised details, formally approved the document, waited for agent implementation, and finally performed code review. This rigid pipeline assumed that requirements could be completely finalized before writing a single line of executable logic.

In practice, software design choices rarely solidify in the abstract. True architectural clarity arises when developers observe runtime feedback, interface layouts, and concrete edge cases. The source says the interface forced users to prematurely "finish thinking" so they could start building, and once implementation began, returning to the earlier chat-based reasoning felt like moving backward: "There was no going back up the waterfall."

4.2 The Agility of the Six-Stage Iterative Loop

Conversely, the source observes that in how Codex currently works, the boundary between planning and execution is disappearing, and that as agents got better at acting autonomously, testing their own work, and revising their approach after inspecting results, a different loop became possible: understand the context, take an action (act), inspect the resulting artifacts (inspect), clarify ambiguities exposed by execution (clarify), adjust directional parameters (adjust), and proceed with renewed action (act again).

Within this loop, substantial planning still occurs continuously, as the quotation in Section 1 states. However, this planning is integrated seamlessly into conversational dialogue and verifiable code modifications rather than frozen into an isolated document. As Nadeem concluded, the simplicity of a conversational interface where planning was performed on demand rather than enforced by default proved markedly superior: "the simplicity of a chat interface where planning was done as-needed rather than by default, was actually pretty great."

# Traditional Linear Specification Pipeline
chat → disambiguate by answering questions → generate spec → review spec → revise spec → approve → implement → review code

# Iterative Discovery Loop
understand → act → inspect → clarify → adjust → act again

5. The Costliest Mistake — Replacing Understanding with Static Artifacts

At the center of Ayman Nadeem's retrospective stands a direct, uncompromising assessment of the project's central architectural failure: "I think the biggest mistake I made was turning the plan into an artifact instead of designing a process for improved human understanding."

This admission pinpoints a fundamental trap in AI tooling design. The ultimate goal of software tooling must be augmenting human cognitive clarity—equipping developers to maintain an accurate mental model of system boundaries, invariants, and side effects. Nuanced mistakenly substituted that cognitive objective with the production of tangible documentation artifacts.

The reasons the source gives are specific. AI-generated text is very difficult to read because of its pacing and overly structured nature: "There's something about the pacing and overly structured nature of AI-generated text that makes it very difficult to read." The source also notes that working through decisions in chat feels more intuitive, but keeping that understanding up to date as the system changes is still an open problem.

Nadeem reinforced that setting aside static plan modes does not diminish the profound value of engineering deliberation: "Thinking through what to build, why it matters, and evaluating decisions remains important. People need to build a coherent mental model of what's going on, but I don't think a large body of AI-generated text is the right interface for that." Evaluating product decisions and architectural trade-offs remains paramount; voluminous AI text is simply the wrong medium for facilitating that thought process.

  • Failure to distinguish between enhancing human mental models and generating static document artifacts
  • AI-generated text is hard to read, and keeping understanding current as the system changes remains unsolved
  • Affirmation that evaluating software purpose and architectural integrity remains essential to engineering
  • Rejection of extensive AI prose as an ineffective medium for human architectural comprehension

6. Unresolved Challenges and Human Attention in the Multi-Agent Era

Recognizing the obsolescence of plan modes does not imply that the underlying problem of human comprehension has been resolved. Nadeem writes that this remains unsolved: "We still haven't solved how to help people stay oriented as hundreds of increasingly capable agents change a system at once."

As tooling moves from one or two assistant agents toward dozens or hundreds of increasingly capable agents modifying backends, frontends, and infrastructure in parallel, the author writes that the problem gets harder, and that a way to help human developers keep their bearings has not yet been found.

To navigate this emerging reality, Nadeem defined the fundamental design imperative for future coding agents: "Agents need to identify the fewest places where human attention can have the greatest impact and surface enough context to make that attention useful."

Human attention is an inherently scarce, non-scalable resource, whereas machine synthesis capacity is effectively unbounded. An intelligent agent should not flood developers with comprehensive textual updates or multi-page roadmaps. Instead, agents must pinpoint the precise leverage points where human editorial judgment, security assessment, or product alignment yields maximum impact, surfacing strictly the minimal context required to empower that decision.

  • Escalating risk of developer disorientation in multi-agent environments operating concurrently across repositories
  • Failure of existing text-heavy interfaces to preserve developer mental models under parallel agent refactorings
  • Mandate for agents to identify minimal leverage points where human intervention yields maximum systemic impact
  • Requirement to surface distilled, high-signal contextual information rather than exhaustive narrative logs

7. Reconciling with Living Spec Frameworks — Divergent Premises, Not Direct Conflict

The distinction below is this article's interpretation. At first glance, Ayman Nadeem's critique appears to directly contradict contemporary methodologies advocating spec-driven development. Prominent frameworks such as the structured multi-stage proposal workflows examined in the OpenSpec framework analysis, mathematical state invariant verification in TLA+ formal modeling, and verifiable contract definitions in agent behavior specifications all emphasize committing explicit specifications to the repository.

However, framing this divergence as a simplistic conflict over whether to write specifications misunderstands the differing premises underlying each approach. They address fundamentally distinct phases and operational scales within the software lifecycle.

Spec-driven frameworks like OpenSpec focus on team-level collaboration, asynchronous agent coordination, and long-term repository governance. In those contexts, committing living specifications to version control provides an indispensable, durable contract ensuring that distributed human contributors and autonomous agents operate against an immutable source of truth.

In contrast, Nadeem's retrospective focuses on helping people keep their understanding and bearings as the number of parallel agents grows. The source qualifies its judgment on purpose 2 with "especially as the number of parallel agents we run increases" and notes that the problem gets harder as you go from five agents to hundreds. In that setting, having people read and approve large AI-generated specifications is not the right interface for understanding. Thus, in this article's interpretation, while shared specification files serve as contracts for team governance and auditing, helping humans understand the system calls for a different abstraction than a fixed plan mode.

Evaluation Dimension Spec-Driven Frameworks (e.g., OpenSpec) Plan Mode Retrospective (Ayman Nadeem)
Primary Problem Focus Loss of architectural rationale across ephemeral chat sessions Cognitive exhaustion caused by consuming AI-generated plan text
Nature of Specification Durable repository artifact for team-wide alignment Disruptive UI abstraction hindering real-time development flow
Optimal Operational Context Multi-agent delegation and long-term codebase maintenance Keeping humans oriented as the number of parallel agents grows
Core Strategic Objective Immutable architectural auditability and verifiable contracts Maximizing the leverage of human attention at minimal critical points
