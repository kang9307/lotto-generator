This article relies on the sentences in the announcement Mistral published on October 6, 2026, titled Introducing Mistral Large 4, and on the model page and pricing page in the Mistral documentation. Every benchmark figure is a value Mistral wrote in that announcement, and any number that does not appear in the sources is marked as a calculation.

The Trigger - Between a Public Preview and Weights by the End of the Month

Mistral published an announcement titled Introducing Mistral Large 4 on October 6, 2026, and GeekNews carried it the same day. The byline is not a person but simply By Mistral. The opening paragraph separates three things. What launches today is a public preview of Mistral Large 4, the preview API can be tried today on Mistral Studio, and the weights drop at the end of this month. The short name of the model is ML4, and the nickname the announcement gives it is le Chonk.

The announcement says ML4 pushes the frontier of open-weight performance, and the model page in the documentation also introduces the model as open-weight. Yet on the Weights tab of that same page, both the weights cell and the license cell read Coming soon. As of October 6, 2026, what can be used is an API operated by Mistral, and there is no weight file to download yet. At this point the word open-weight is a commitment about the near future, and the announcement itself puts the schedule as by the end of the month.

That splits the material into what can be read now and what has to wait. The specifications, the pricing, the form of availability and the benchmark figures Mistral chose to publish are in the announcement and the documentation. The weight files, the license terms, the architectural details, additional benchmarks and the post-training methodology are items the company says it will share as it works toward releasing the weights. How to read a large MoE model that is announced while its weights are still pending was covered in the analysis of the Reflection Beam 501B announcement, so the sections below stay with the values written in the ML4 announcement and documentation.

  • Date and publisher. The announcement is dated October 6, 2026, and its byline reads By Mistral rather than naming a person.
  • What is available now. A preview API on Mistral Studio. The model page in the documentation carries the labels Public Preview, Open and v26.10.
  • Weights. The announcement says they will be released by the end of the month, and the Weights tab in the documentation says Coming soon. Nothing can be downloaded on the announcement date.
  • Until then. The announcement says the model is being red-teamed in real-world settings with cybersecurity leaders, vetted partners, and state authorities.

Specifications - 1 Trillion and 49 Billion in the Post, 1.05T and 52B in the Docs

The specification numbers are written differently depending on the document. The body of the announcement describes ML4 as a 1 trillion-parameter natively multimodal model with 49 billion active parameters. The model page in the documentation says it features 52B active parameters and 1.05T total parameters, and a 1.6B vision encoder, and the table on the Weights tab repeats those values as Parameters (T) 1.05, Active (B) 52 and Vision Encoder (B) 1.6. The GeekNews summary, which speaks of 1.05 trillion total parameters with 49 billion active, places the total from the documentation and the active count from the announcement in a single sentence.

The word both documents use for the structure is Mixture-of-Experts. The documentation calls it a granular Mixture-of-Experts architecture, and the model card at the end of the announcement summarizes the model as an open-weight hybrid instruct-and-reasoning MoE with multimodal input. Neither document goes further than that. The announcement states twice that further details on the model architecture will be shared as the weights are released.

The context length does not appear in the body of the announcement and is given only in the documentation. The Context field on the model page reads 1M, and Context Size (tokens) on the Weights tab also reads 1M. The glossary text in the Mistral documentation defines this value as the maximum number of tokens a model can process in a single request, including input (prompt and files) plus output combined. The modalities are shown as four labels, namely Text input, Image input, Reasoning output and Text output.

Pricing and Access - $1.36 and $4.18, and the Original and Sale Labels in the Docs

The model card at the end of the announcement gives the price in two lines. Input costs $1.36 per million tokens and output costs $4.18 per million tokens. The currency symbol is the dollar and the unit is written as /M tokens. The announcement does not say whether these values apply only during the preview period or are the regular price.

The documentation shows two layers of values. The model page displays $1.36 and $0.68 side by side, and the pricing page writes Original price: $1.36 and Sale price: $0.68 in the same cell. Cached input is $0.14 as the Original price and $0.07 as the Sale price, and output is $4.18 as the Original price and $2.09 as the Sale price. In all three items the Sale value is half of the Original value (calculation). On the pricing page, Mistral Large 4 is the only row that carries a Sale label.

The same pricing page offers values that can be read alongside. The previous generation, Mistral Large 3, is listed at $0.5 for input and $1.5 for output, and Z.ai GLM 5.3, a third-party model hosted by Mistral, is listed at $1.4 for input and $4.4 for output. The Original values for ML4 sit slightly below GLM 5.3 and above Large 3. The pricing page has Batch and Priority tabs in addition to Standard, and the values above are those shown on the Standard tab.

How to call the model is described on the Usage tab of the documentation. It says to send a request to the Chat Completions API with the mistral-large-4 model name, and the sample code sets reasoning_effort to high. The names shown on the model page are mistral-large-4 and mistral-large-4-0.

  • Announcement values. The model card at the end of the announcement lists Input (/M tokens) at $1.36 and Output (/M tokens) at $4.18.
  • Documentation values. Input from $1.36 to $0.68, cached input from $0.14 to $0.07, output from $4.18 to $2.09. The first value is the Original price and the second is the Sale price.
  • Model names. The model page in the documentation shows two names, mistral-large-4 and mistral-large-4-0.
  • When comparing cost. The Original and Sale values differ by a factor of two, so state which one was used. The order in which to check the denominator behind cost claims about open-weight models is laid out in reading cost and benchmark claims about open-weight models.

Training Infrastructure and Regions - 3,800 GPUs and More Than 160 Languages

The announcement says ML4 was trained from scratch on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral’s own datacenters in Europe. The public preview is served on that same infrastructure. The announcement calls this a significant milestone in the company's long-term investment across infrastructure, research, and product development.

There is one sentence about the training data. A significant share of the training data was multilingual, spanning more than 160 languages, including every official language of the European Union. The share per language and the size of the data are not given. The announcement also says Mistral worked closely with leading enterprises in finance, engineering, manufacturing, logistics, pharmaceuticals, science, shipping and the public sector to train ML4, and adds that the model uses the same training, customization, and RL environment the company offers its customers through Mistral Forge.

Post-training comes with numbers in the reinforcement learning passage. At the current scale, written as 3k GPUs, a single training run produces roughly 33 billion tokens per day, of which around 16 billion are trainable completion tokens after filtering and masking. Post-training is described as two stages, supervised fine-tuning and RL. The announcement says the reinforcement learning run behind this preview is still in flight and shows no signs of saturation, and that the company expects large and rapid improvements in the weeks and months to come. The benchmark figures at preview time are values that rest on that premise.

On regions, the announcement says the model will be available across multiple regions worldwide. That includes a European deployment that Mistral operates end-to-end, independently of other digital service providers and under European law. The tense is future, will be available.

  • Training hardware. According to the announcement, 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral’s own datacenters in Europe, with the model trained from scratch, and with the public preview served on that same infrastructure.
  • Languages. More than 160, including every official language of the EU. The model card at the end of the announcement writes it as natively fluent in 160+ languages.
  • Reinforcement learning scale. Roughly 33 billion tokens per day at 3k GPUs, of which around 16 billion are trainable completion tokens.
  • Funding. The announcement describes ML4 as the first milestone on the roadmap funded by its €3 billion Series D.

The three coding figures correspond to software engineering, repository understanding, and complex terminal workflows. The announcement says the combined Coding Agent Index score of 49.8% places ML4 ahead of DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 and Qwen3.8 Max. The evaluation with Surge AI is of a different kind. Professional annotators rated the coding quality of model outputs on a 1–5 scale with model identities hidden, and ML4 Preview ranked second of five models. First place went to Claude Opus 5 at 4.22.

For agentic workflows and knowledge work, the figures given are 59.9% on AutomationBench and 1,393 Elo on AA-Briefcase. Law and finance come with statements only. The announcement says Mistral evaluated ML4 through third party evaluators (vals.ai) on representative legal and financial tasks and found that the model exceeds GPT-6-Astra in both cases, and that on HarveyAI’s Legal Agent benchmark ML4 outperforms all open-source models. Neither sentence carries a score.

On the vision side, the figure given in text is 42% versus 41% on Dense 200. The wording in the announcement is that ML4 is one of the most capable models Mistral tested, for instance surpassing GPT-6-Astra on Dense 200. The gap between the two values is one percentage point (calculation). ChartQA Pro and GDP.pdf appear only as chart titles. On the science side, the announcement says ML4 is state of the art on SciCode-Verified among open-weight models and gives no score.

The generalizing sentences at the top of the announcement have separate scopes. Against the strongest open-source models globally the performance is described as competitive, while against any open-weight model developed in the US or Europe the model is described as significantly outperforming. For cybersecurity, finance and law the wording is that Mistral finds it to be state-of-the-art among open models, which states the company's own assessment. The sentence about surpassing even frontier closed models is limited to some domains such as visual grounding.

  • How the combined index is built. 49.8% matches the arithmetic mean of 61.7, 59.4 and 28.3 (calculation).
  • The comparison differs by metric. The coding index is set against DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 and Qwen3.8 Max, the blind evaluation against Claude Opus 5, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.2, and grounding against GPT-6-Astra.
  • Items with statements only. The legal and financial tasks through vals.ai, the HarveyAI Legal Agent benchmark and SciCode-Verified come with ranking statements and no scores.
  • Items shown only as charts. CyberGym-E2E, ChartQA Pro, GDP.pdf, Finance Agent v2 and Finch (FinWorkBench) appear as chart titles.
  • Preview label. The subject of the blind evaluation is written as ML4 Preview, which ties that score to the preview-stage model.

The Cybersecurity Passage - What the Post Says and Who Says It

This is the area the announcement covers at greatest length. Mistral writes that ML4 is one of the world's strongest AI models for cybersecurity. The support it gives is the Artificial Analysis Cyber Index. The announcement introduces the index as an independent evaluation of how well AI models find and fix security flaws in real software, and says ML4 ranks among the top five models globally and leads open-weight models developed outside China by a wide margin.

The 82% is not the score for the index as a whole. It is the score on one of the index's tests, which asks a model to reproduce a real vulnerability in open-source software and then patch it, and the announcement says this is the highest of any model. On Cybench the announcement says ML4 solves 93% of the challenges, a set of 40 exercises drawn from security competitions, and describes that as one of the highest scores reported for an open-weight model.

The sentence about closed models should be read together with its attribution. The announcement says several leading closed models, including Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, score near zero on the same test, and gives the reason as because they refuse to perform the task. That sentence is not an assessment of what those models are capable of. It is Mistral's description of refusal behavior on that particular test. The argument of the announcement lies in the sentence that follows. Defending software often starts with proving that a flaw is real, and that is exactly the kind of work safety filters in closed models can block. Elsewhere the announcement says provider-level refusals can block legitimate vulnerability research and incident response, and that losing access to a capability mid-incident can itself become a critical security risk.

The wording of the red-teaming passage is as follows. The announcement says that until the weights are released the company is red-teaming the model in real-world settings with cybersecurity leaders, vetted partners, and state authorities, who will access the same model with reduced moderation and expanded cyber capabilities. This reads as saying that the configuration those parties access is not the same as the general preview API, but the announcement does not say what differs or how.

The safety section contains a figure that points the other way. The announcement says that despite strong performance on Cyber benchmarks, the average refusal rate of the model on cyber prompts from JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT, and AgentHarm is higher than all OSS models. Placing the two passages side by side, what Mistral puts forward is a distinction in which defensive work is performed and malicious requests are refused (interpretation). Where that boundary falls in actual use is something that can be examined after the weights and the additional material are out.

  • AA Cyber Index. Among the top five models globally and leading open-weight models developed outside China, according to the announcement.
  • 82%. The score on one test within the index, the one that asks a model to reproduce a real vulnerability in open-source software and then patch it, and not the score for the index as a whole.
  • Cybench. The announcement says ML4 solves 93% of the challenges in a set of 40 exercises drawn from security competitions.
  • Internal testing. The announcement says the model proved useful for analysing malware, prioritising vulnerabilities, and writing detection rules, and describes these as capabilities that extend beyond what it was explicitly trained for.
  • Self-deployment. The announcement says, in the future tense, that it will be able to run on private cloud or on-premise.

Safety Measures and Human Evaluation

The Model Safety section of the announcement states three things. The first is robustness to indirect prompt injections. Mistral writes that ML4 has saturated its benchmarks in this area and names GLM-5.2, GLM-5.3, Kimi-K2.6, Kimi-K3 and DS-V4-Pro-0813 as the comparison. On Lakera’s public B3 AI Security Benchmark, it says ML4 resists 93.3% of attacks and that the company sees no higher scores among competitors.

The second is the KORA Benchmark. The announcement says ML4 scored 1.691 and that this is the highest measured score among the OSS models Mistral evaluated. The maximum of the scale is 2, and the name of that grade is Exemplary. The third is the refusal rate on cyber prompts discussed in the previous section.

There are two separate kinds of human evaluation. One is the blind coding-quality evaluation run with Surge AI, and the other is an internal evaluation at Mistral. In the internal one, expert annotators across coding, computer-aided design (CAD), finance, mathematics and physics compared Mistral Large 4 with GLM-5.3. The announcement says ML4 was preferred in CAD and STEM, while performing on par or close to GLM-5.3 in finance and coding. The only comparison model in the internal evaluation is GLM-5.3.

Sorted by evaluator, there are three groups. There are measures built outside the company that Mistral cites or ran (the AA Cyber Index, Lakera B3, KORA and the HarveyAI Legal Agent benchmark), evaluations run with an outside party or through outside evaluators (Surge AI and vals.ai), and work done inside Mistral (the expert comparison, the indirect prompt injection benchmarks and the STEM evaluation). In every group the party publishing the figure is Mistral, and independent reproduction outside the announcement is something that follows the release of the weights.

  • Lakera B3. According to the announcement, ML4 resists 93.3% of the attacks in Lakera’s public B3 AI Security Benchmark.
  • KORA. A score of 1.691 on a scale where 2 is the maximum.
  • Refusal rate on cyber prompts. A statement that the average across JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT and AgentHarm is higher than all OSS models, with no figure given in text.
  • Internal human evaluation. Preferred over GLM-5.3 in CAD and STEM, on par or close in finance and coding.

A result measured against the preview API is a result about the API at that moment. Because the announcement says reinforcement learning is still in progress, it is hard to assume that the behavior seen in the preview and the behavior of the weights released at the end of the month will be the same (interpretation). Anyone running their own evaluation during the preview stage will find later comparison easier if the date and the model name are recorded together with the results.

The terms are also worth reading separately. The announcement uses open-weight, open-source, open models and OSS interchangeably. While the license cell says Coming soon, the conditions for commercial use and redistribution cannot be read from the documentation. The point that open-weight and open-source are not the same word is laid out in State of Open Source AI, July 2026.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • Why the parameter figures differ. Neither document explains why the announcement says 1 trillion and 49 billion while the documentation says 1.05T and 52B.
  • License. The License cell in the documentation says Coming soon, and no license name appears in the announcement. The conditions for commercial use could not be confirmed.
  • Architectural details. The number of experts, the routing method and the structure of the vision encoder are not in the sources. The announcement says they will be shared as the weights are released.
  • Conditions of the Sale price. The period and conditions of the Sale values, and whether the $1.36 and $4.18 in the announcement are limited to the preview period, are not written on the pricing page or in the announcement. The ML4 values on the Batch and Priority tabs were not included in the sources either.
  • Maximum output tokens. The model page gives only the 1M context length, and no item for an output limit was found.
  • Individual chart values. The values in the charts for CyberGym-E2E, ChartQA Pro, GDP.pdf, Finance Agent v2, Finch and Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark are not written in text. The original publications from Artificial Analysis, vals.ai, HarveyAI and Lakera were not included in the sources.
  • Whether the preview and the weights are identical. The announcement does not say whether the model served by the preview API is the same checkpoint as the weights to be released at the end of the month, or how the configuration used by the red-teaming parties differs from the general preview.
  • Release date and location. The exact date of the weight release and where the weights will be distributed are not stated beyond the phrase by the end of the month.
