Age Standards for Youth and Children in Korea: Cheongsonyon Bohobeop vs. Adong Bokjibeop

Understanding the Different Age Criteria Under Korea's Youth Protection Act and Child Welfare Act

2025-06-30 Lifestyle

Key Takeaway

In Korea, the age definitions for "cheongsonyon" (youth) and "adong" (child) differ from one law to another, which frequently causes confusion. Knowing the difference between the Cheongsonyon Bohobeop (Youth Protection Act) and the Adong Bokjibeop (Child Welfare Act) is genuinely useful in everyday life.

Age Criteria by Law

Cheongsonyon Bohobeop (Youth Protection Act)

Under 19 (int'l age)
  • Ages 0 through 18
  • Blocks access to harmful environments for youth
  • Regulates harmful media and materials
  • Establishes youth-protection zones

Adong Bokjibeop (Child Welfare Act)

Under 18 (int'l age)
  • Ages 0 through 17
  • Promotes overall child welfare
  • Creates a healthy growth environment
  • Prevents child abuse

Why Do the Age Criteria Differ?

1. Different Legislative Purposes

The Cheongsonyon Bohobeop (Youth Protection Act) is primarily designed to shield young people from harmful social environments, so it covers a broader age range -- everyone under 19 (international age).

The Adong Bokjibeop (Child Welfare Act) focuses on ensuring basic welfare and healthy development for children, targeting the relatively younger group of those under 18 (international age).

2. Alignment with International Standards

The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child defines a child as anyone under 18, and Korea's Child Welfare Act follows this same standard.

Real-Life Examples

Gaming and Internet Access

  • 18-year-olds are still covered by the Youth Protection Act, meaning they are subject to game shutdown systems and harmful-site blocking.
  • While they are adults under the Child Welfare Act, they remain protected under youth-protection rules.

Movie Ratings

  • "Restricted" (cheongsonyon gwallam bulga) movies require viewers to be at least 18 (international age).
  • This follows the standard set by the Youth Protection Act.

Buying Tobacco and Alcohol

  • You must be 19 (international age) or older to purchase these products.
  • Both laws prohibit sales to anyone below this age.

Common Points of Confusion

Important Reminders

  • An 18-year-old is an adult under the Child Welfare Act but still a "cheongsonyon" (youth) under the Youth Protection Act.
  • Legal responsibilities and rights may vary depending on which law applies in a given situation.
  • When using various services, always check which legal standard they follow.

Conclusion

Korea's age criteria for youth and children are set differently depending on the purpose of each law:

  • Cheongsonyon Bohobeop (Youth Protection Act): Under 19 (international age) -- focused on protection from harmful environments
  • Adong Bokjibeop (Child Welfare Act): Under 18 (international age) -- focused on welfare and healthy development

Age 18, in particular, sits right on the boundary between the two laws, so different standards may apply depending on the situation. Whenever you use a service in Korea, it is a good idea to check in advance which legal standard that service follows.

Understanding these differences in age criteria helps you grasp minor-protection policies and related regulations more accurately and reduces confusion in everyday life.
