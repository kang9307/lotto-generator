How Far Can You Trust an Unproven Treatment

Registration, phase, control group, blinding, primary endpoint, replication - the six boxes to check when someone says a treatment reverses a disease, and why stories of improvement are not evidence

Aug 17, 2026 Health & Wellness
Medical disclaimer: This article is for information only and does not replace medical diagnosis or treatment. Decisions about treatment should be made in consultation with the clinician caring for the patient. Nothing here recommends or rejects any specific procedure, and nothing here should be used as a reason to stop a current treatment or start a new one.

When Someone Says a Treatment Reverses the Disease

If you are close to someone who is ill, a link eventually arrives. A short clip of an operation, a caregiver saying their father came back, and one word doing an enormous amount of work: reversed. The next day a piece arrives from the other direction saying the whole thing is dangerous. Both are confident. Neither tells you what either conclusion rests on.

This article does not urge you toward any procedure or away from one. That judgment depends on the stage of the disease, the other conditions in play, the medicines already in use and whether the body can tolerate an operation, and the only people positioned to weigh those are the clinicians treating the patient. What this article offers is the part you can do from where you are standing: work out where the claim currently sits.

The worked example is the Alzheimer's surgery dispute that ran through the journals in 2026. Not because it is unique, but because it shows all six boxes empty at once. The same structure repeats in oncology, rare disease, chronic pain and autoimmune conditions.

The Dispute Itself - Only What Is Documented

In 2026 Nature published a news feature headlined as a controversial Alzheimer's surgery said to reverse symptoms. The procedure is deep cervical lymphaticovenous anastomosis (dcLVA): fine lymphatic vessels deep in the neck are joined to nearby veins, creating a route for fluid and waste proteins leaving the brain to drain more readily into the bloodstream.

None of that is fanciful on its face. Waste clearance pathways from the brain have been a genuine field of study for over a decade, and impaired drainage as a contributor to protein accumulation is a hypothesis that gets serious discussion. There is a place for the idea to stand. Having a place to stand and working in humans are different sentences.

What the public record establishes:

  • The first operation was performed in September 2020; the first report appeared in 2022 in a Chinese-language journal, from a microsurgeon.
  • Footage shown at surgical meetings carried it internationally, and it was reported that thousands of operations followed in China.
  • A 2026 review in Frontiers in Aging summarised the clinical evidence as small-sample, single-centre, uncontrolled observational studies without randomization, blinding or control groups. Sample sizes ran from 1 to 139, mostly under 50.
  • Follow-up was predominantly one to six months, with the longest reported at nine months. Outcomes leaned on cognitive scales such as MMSE and MoCA plus caregiver report.
  • That review states plainly that no study has confirmed improvement in daily functioning (ADL/IADL).
  • A commentary in Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience dated 26 November 2025 counted five clinical studies, four of which enrolled seven patients or fewer and one 26, with the longest follow-up at 180 days.
  • On 8 July 2025 China's National Health Commission issued a notice prohibiting clinical application of the procedure for treating Alzheimer's disease.
Two things have to be held at once here. One: on the evidence available, nobody can say this operation changes the course of the disease. Two: the hypothesis has not been disproved either. Unverified and false are different states, and collapsing them sends you wrong in both directions.

Where to Find the Registration Number

A registration number is a short alphanumeric string. Entries on the United States registry begin with NCT, the Chinese registry uses ChiCTR, and Korea's Clinical Research Information Service uses KCT. Paste the number into the registry search and you get study type, allocation, masking, target enrolment, primary endpoint and current status as submitted. It is a plan filed by investigators rather than a vetted document, but a plan on the public record is what makes later results checkable.

Open a few and it becomes obvious that being registered says nothing about quality. Among the entries on this topic there is an observational study planning eight participants, and there is also a multicentre randomized study targeting 186 people whose masking field reads none. The primary endpoint of the latter is the proportion of participants whose cognitive scale improves by four points or more at 48 weeks, surgery versus medication, and its status at registration was not yet recruiting. That much reading is enough to establish that no conclusion can exist yet.

What the Phase Tells You, and What It Does Not

Phases come from drug development. The scale below follows the definitions the US FDA publishes. Procedures and surgery borrow the labels loosely, so a registry entry marked phase 3 for an operation should not be read with the weight of a drug phase 3. The underlying idea of staged evidence still holds.

StageRough sizeQuestion it answersWhat a result at this stage supports

Hold that table and one common misreading resolves itself. In clinical trials does not mean the benefit is established; it means the benefit is not yet established, which is why a trial is running. News of a phase 1 start regularly travels as a breakthrough, and phase 1 does not even ask the efficacy question.

Why Stories of People Who Got Better Are Not Evidence

What moves people is never the paper. It is the person. And the story is not a lie. That individual may genuinely have improved. What does not follow from the story is that the procedure is why, because four effects overlap.

Natural course and fluctuation

Chronic disease does not decline in a straight line. It oscillates. A passing infection clouds things for days; a week of decent sleep sharpens them. Depending on which point you take as your baseline, a stretch of improvement appears with no treatment at all.

Regression to the mean

People go looking for a new treatment when things are at their worst. Statistically, the measurement that follows an extreme one tends to be less extreme, drifting back toward the average, whether or not anything is done. If treatment began near a trough, some of the improvement that follows was already scheduled.

Placebo response and expectation

Expectation genuinely changes how symptoms are reported, and the more dramatic the intervention, the larger that shift. This is not speculation; it has been tested directly.

  • A 2002 NEJM trial randomly assigned 180 participants with knee osteoarthritis to arthroscopic debridement, arthroscopic lavage, or placebo surgery in which skin incisions were made and the arthroscope was never inserted. Outcomes after real surgery were no better than after the placebo procedure.
  • Two blinded vertebroplasty trials published in NEJM in 2009 compared the procedure with a sham and found no advantage at any time point. Both groups nonetheless improved significantly in pain and disability.

That last sentence is the point. Without control groups, those trials would have been published as a marked reduction in pain after the procedure. Because placebo response grows with invasiveness, surgery and procedures need controls more than pills do, not less. If an improvement bought at the price of infection and nerve injury could have come from the incision alone, that is something worth establishing.

Observer bias

Much of a cognitive score is assigned by a person. Caregiver questionnaires especially. A family that badly wants improvement, filling in items after committing to an operation, will record the same condition more favourably. Not from dishonesty. Blinding exists to solve that with design rather than with virtue.

Why Alzheimer's Disease Is Especially Exposed

The same traps are deeper in some conditions than others. Cognitive decline is where four conditions align at their worst.

  • Day-to-day variation is large. Someone clouds over in the afternoon and is sharp the next morning. Choose a few observation points and you can construct a story running in either direction.
  • The measurement is subjective. MMSE, MoCA, ADAS-Cog and CDR-SB are useful instruments, but none of them is a machine reading a value the way a blood pressure cuff does. Practice effects also nudge scores upward on repeat testing.
  • The rater is usually family. Daily function is reported by the person living with the patient, and that report moves with the caregiver's own state and hopes.
  • The situation is genuinely urgent. The trajectory is predictably bad and no treatment reliably reverses it, so waiting has a real cost. That is exactly why unverified options exert such pull.

Please do not read the last item as a lecture. Desperation is not a defect of reasoning. The situation really is desperate. But that does not make verification less necessary; it makes it more so. The greater the reason to accept risk, the clearer it should be what the risk is being accepted for.

Where the evidence is far more solid is on the risk-reduction side. Modifiable risk factors and lifestyle management are set out in Complete Guide to Dementia Prevention, so they are not repeated here.

How to Read the Word Reverse - What Was Measured, What Improved

Reverse is not a clinical term. When you meet it, break the sentence into three pieces and interrogate each.

  1. What was measured. A cognitive test score, a brain imaging metric, a fluid biomarker, or a questionnaire completed by a caregiver. Each carries a different meaning.
  2. How much it improved. Statistically significant and noticeable to the patient are not the same thing. Every scale has its own discussion of the minimum clinically important difference, and a change below that threshold exists only in the table.
  3. Compared with what. The patient's own pre-operative self, or an untreated group. If it is the former, natural course and regression to the mean are baked in.
A biomarker that moved is not a promise of clinical benefit. Results where markers improved while patients did not have appeared repeatedly in this field. For a single case followed all the way up the ladder of evidence, see Semaglutide and Dementia Risk - How Far Does the Evidence Actually Go. For reading test numbers themselves, Reading At-Home Test Results is the companion piece.

A claim of reversal that fails those three questions usually translates as: compared with his own pre-operative self, the score his family assigned went up over a few weeks. That sentence is not false. It simply cannot tell you whether the course of the disease changed.

What Regulatory Approval Means and Where It Stops

Approved and not approved are both powerful signals, and both are routinely over-read.

  • Approval is a conditional judgment. Under a defined use in a defined population, benefit was judged to exceed risk. That is not the same as working for everyone.
  • Its scope ends at the population studied. Nothing in it guarantees the same result at a different disease stage, a different age band, or alongside different medicines.
  • Absence of approval is not a verdict of no effect. The evidence may simply not have been submitted. What remains true is that nothing definitive can be claimed in that state.
  • Drugs and procedures travel different routes. A new surgical technique does not go through drug licensing.

In Korea there is a separate gate for medical procedures. New health technology assessment, introduced in 2007, has the National Evidence-based healthcare Collaborating Agency evaluate the safety and clinical effectiveness of a new technique, with the outcome fixed by notification from the Ministry of Health and Welfare. Passing that gate is what allows use in practice. Techniques with adequate safety but thin efficacy data, where early adoption is judged necessary, can be designated limited medical technologies and performed for a set period only at institutions named by the Minister, while data is collected.

Knowing the structure has a practical use: when a procedure is proposed, you can ask which box it sits in. Is it an existing technology, one that has passed assessment, a limited medical technology, part of a clinical trial, or none of the above. If the answer is the fifth, that itself is important information. Readers outside Korea have equivalents to ask about, since most jurisdictions regulate devices, drugs and procedures through separate channels.

One more caution. A regulatory decision in the country where a procedure originated does not automatically apply elsewhere. A prohibition in one jurisdiction may not even be under review in another, which is how cross-border treatment travel arises, and which inflates every item in the next section.

Cost and Risk - What an Unproven Procedure Actually Demands

An intervention with unproven benefit still carries entirely certain costs. No company or price is named here. The categories of cost matter far more.

Financial

  • Unverified interventions are typically outside insurance coverage, meaning the full amount is out of pocket.
  • Beyond the quoted procedure fee there are often separate charges for anaesthesia, admission, imaging and pre-operative assessment.
  • If it is done abroad, flights, accommodation, interpreting and an accompanying carer are added.
  • Who pays when a complication needs treating usually falls to the patient unless a contract says otherwise.
  • Travel and indemnity policies sometimes exclude unproven interventions and their complications.

Physical

The hazards of deep neck surgery are itemised in the literature. The review and commentary cited above list vascular injury to the internal jugular vein or cervical arteries, palsy of cranial nerves IX to XII, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, infection, lymphatic leakage, thrombosis or occlusion of the anastomosis, and venous thromboembolism as perioperative risks, with anastomotic occlusion and drainage imbalance flagged for the longer term. In older patients the risks of pneumonia, delirium and thrombosis rise alongside.

Opportunity

  • Time and resources shift away from established management and support services.
  • Having had the procedure can become an exclusion criterion for other trials. A study you would want to join later may no longer be open to you.
  • The exhaustion a family absorbs when large hopes come to nothing is a real cost too.

None of this is listed to frighten anyone. It is information the decision-maker is entitled to. Nor is choosing to accept risk wrong in itself. Accepting it without knowing the likelihood of benefit and accepting it knowingly are different acts.

If You Are Considering a Clinical Trial

Among the ways to reach an unproven treatment, a clinical trial is the one with structure around it. It guarantees no benefit, but the plan is public, an ethics committee has reviewed it, and participant rights are written down. Korean studies are searchable through the Clinical Research Information Service run by the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency; international studies through ClinicalTrials.gov.

Participant rights follow clear principles: the right to be fully informed and to decide without pressure, the right to consult family or another physician, the right to withdraw at any time even mid-study, the right to face no disadvantage for withdrawing, and the right to keep a copy of the consent document. Participants whose cognition limits their capacity to decide are entitled to additional protection, which means stricter ethics review and a separate consent process involving a legally authorised representative.

Two things must not be confused. Taking part in a clinical trial and paying to receive an intervention that happens to be under investigation are different arrangements. The second is care, not research, and the registry's protections do not apply to it. What separates them is whether there is a consent document and a registration number.

Closing - Where the Decision Actually Happens

Reduced to three sentences:

  1. Put the claim in the six boxes. Registration, phase, control group, blinding, primary endpoint, replication. The more boxes are empty, the more the claim is a hypothesis rather than a conclusion.
  2. Anecdotes are sincere and are not evidence. Natural course, regression to the mean, expectation and observer bias overlap, and the more invasive the intervention, the heavier that overlap.
  3. Unverified does not mean false. The purpose is knowing which sentence you are entitled to say today, not closing a door.

And there is one thing this article deliberately does not do. It does not decide. Whether to have a treatment, decline it, or apply to a trial has to be worked out with someone who knows the disease stage, the comorbidities, the current medicines and whether the body can withstand an operation, and that conversation belongs in a consulting room. What this article can hand you is the list of questions to carry in with you.

Those questions are six. Is it registered. What stage is it at. Was there a comparison group. Were the assessors blind to allocation. What was fixed in advance as the thing to measure. Has anyone else reproduced it.
