The Complete Moving Checklist: What to Do, Costs, and Paperwork by Timeline

From four weeks out through moving day and after - choosing a mover, packing, address changes, and cost-saving tips, all in one practical guide

Jul 6, 2026 Lifestyle

Introduction: A Timeline Is Half the Battle

Signing a lease or closing on a home isn't the finish line - it's the starting gun. You still have to pick a mover, sort through your belongings, handle paperwork like address changes and utility transfers, and get the sequence right on moving day itself. When everything lands at once, something inevitably slips through the cracks. This guide, current as of July 2026, breaks moving preparation into a timeline from four weeks out through moving day and after, and walks through how to choose a mover, handle the paperwork, and cut costs along the way.

One caveat up front: exact procedures and costs vary by location, home type, and the size of your move, and address-registration rules differ from country to country. This article covers the general flow and is not a substitute for your local authority's official guidance or legal advice. Always cross-check the latest rules for where you live.

1. The Moving Timeline: A Checklist at a Glance

Moving prep feels overwhelming as one big blob of tasks, but breaking it into phases makes everything clearer. Use the timeline below as a baseline and shift it earlier or later to fit your own schedule.

1.1 Four Weeks Out: Nail Down the Big Picture

This is when you lock in your moving date and rough out a budget. Getting quotes from at least three moving companies is standard practice, and moving on a weekday during the off-peak season tends to be cheaper. At the same time, start broadly sorting what you're keeping versus getting rid of. If you have kids, start the school-transfer paperwork; if you have a pet, begin carrier-crate training early.

1.2 Two Weeks Out: Lock In Quotes and Start Packing

Confirm your mover and get a written contract in place. Start packing non-essentials first - off-season items, books, odds and ends you won't need before the move - to cut down on last-minute chaos. Sketch out a rough furniture and appliance layout for the new place, and if you need services like internet installation scheduled, book them now.

1.3 One Week Out: Focus on Paperwork

Utility hookups - gas in particular - often can't be disconnected and reconnected on the same day, so book at least a week ahead. This is also the time to prepare your change-of-address filing, set up mail forwarding, and knock out address updates for banks, insurance, and subscriptions in one batch. Defrost the refrigerator ahead of time, and as you pack the last of your things, set aside cleaning supplies and trash bags you'll need on moving day itself.

1.4 Moving Day: Sequence and Checkpoints

Keep ID, contracts, medications, and valuables with you, separate from the moving truck. Before you leave the old place, do one last check for leaks, locked windows, and the gas valve, and confirm the mover's crew count and inventory list. At the new place, check furniture and appliances for damage on the spot - it's the easiest way to head off disputes later.

1.5 After the Move: The Wrap-Up List

Finish your change-of-address filing within the required window (this varies by country and jurisdiction), and settle the final utility bills at your old address. Double-check that your address actually updated with banks, card issuers, and subscriptions, and take a moment to introduce yourself to the building manager or new neighbors - it makes settling in noticeably easier.

2. Choosing a Mover: Full-Service vs. Partial vs. Self-Pack

2.1 What Separates the Three, and What They Cost

With a self-pack move, you pack and organize everything yourself and the company only handles transport - it's the cheapest option, but demands the most time and labor from you. A partial-service move splits the work: the company packs large items like the refrigerator, washer, and TV, while you handle smaller odds and ends - a reasonable middle ground. A full-service move has the company handle everything from packing to arranging furniture in the new place to final cleanup - fastest and most convenient, but the most expensive. Pricing scales heavily with volume, distance, floor level, and elevator access, so treat any specific figure as a rough guide rather than a quote.

2.2 Comparing Quotes and What to Check in the Contract

Getting quotes from at least three companies is standard, and an in-home estimate is more accurate than a phone quote since it's based on your actual belongings. Keep the written estimate - in practice, it carries the same weight as the contract itself. Confirm upfront whether the quote includes items like a ladder truck for high floors, AC reinstallation, packing materials, and any surcharge tied to elevator access or parking, since these are the most common sources of surprise charges on moving day. Get cancellation and change-of-plan fee terms in writing before you sign.

2.3 Avoiding Scams and Hidden Fees

Treat a quote that's dramatically lower than every other bid with suspicion rather than relief. Avoid any company that wants to skip a written contract, or that asks you to sign paperwork with blank spaces where the price, date, or other key details should be. Be cautious of large upfront cash deposits - use a payment method that leaves a paper trail where possible, and check the company's reviews and business registration before booking.

3. Sorting Your Belongings and Decluttering

Since moving cost scales with volume, cutting down what you own is the single most reliable way to save money. Anything you haven't used in the last 90 days and don't expect to use in the next 90 is a reasonable candidate to let go of. Donating, selling secondhand, or giving items away not only lightens your load but can put a little cash back in your pocket. If you have the time, start sorting low-priority items - off-season gear, books, odds and ends - two to three weeks out to avoid a last-minute scramble. Tell your mover about anything you're getting rid of before the quote so it isn't factored into the estimate.

4. Handling Address Changes and Paperwork

4.1 Change of Address and Mail Forwarding

In the United States, submitting a change of address with USPS - either online or at your local post office - is the standard first step, and it's worth pairing with updates to your driver's license, vehicle registration, and voter registration. Mail forwarding through USPS typically lasts about a year, but it's not a substitute for updating your address directly with every bank, subscription, and service you use - forwarding is a safety net, not a permanent fix.

4.2 Utilities, Internet, and Subscription Addresses

Gas service in particular often can't be shut off and turned back on the same day, so schedule it at least a week ahead; electricity, water, and internet transfers should be scheduled early too, so you're not left without service. Bank, insurance, and credit card address updates are easy to forget, so it helps to track them on a dedicated checklist, and don't forget to update the billing and shipping address on your online accounts and subscriptions as well.

4.3 Address Rules Vary by Country - What to Keep in Mind

Address-registration systems differ meaningfully from country to country, so if you're moving internationally, always confirm the local procedure separately. In South Korea, for example, residents are expected to file a change-of-residence report (전입신고) within 14 days of moving, and a postal forwarding service can be requested at the same time. In Japan, you typically file a moving-out notice with your old municipal office and a moving-in notice with the new one, each within a set window, and postal forwarding through Japan Post is a separate application that can take several business days to process - so it pays to apply early. In China, updating residence registration is generally handled through the local police station or an authorized community service office, and utility accounts may stay in the landlord's name for renters in some cases, which is worth confirming before you sign a lease. Because the details vary this much, treat this section as general orientation and confirm the current official procedure for your specific country and municipality.

5. Cost-Saving Tips and Common Mistakes

5.1 Ways to Cut Costs

Moving on a weekday during the off-peak season, rather than during peak spring or back-to-school rushes, tends to bring the price down. Simply comparing quotes from at least three companies often reveals a meaningful spread even for the same job. Reducing what you own to shrink the total volume, and choosing a partial-service move over full-service when it fits your situation, are both practical ways to lower the bill.

5.2 Mistakes People Commonly Make

Letting a mover sense that you're in a time crunch can invite extra charges, so negotiating with some room to spare helps. Failing to confirm whether a quote includes a ladder truck, AC reinstallation, or packing materials is one of the most common ways people get hit with surprise fees on moving day. Booking internet or gas service too late can leave you without it for days, and forgetting to set up mail forwarding can mean losing track of important mail - both belong on your checklist.

6. Moving Tips for Pets and Appliances

6.1 Moving With a Pet

Moving day is loud and full of strangers coming and going, which makes it a genuinely stressful stretch for a pet. Where possible, keep them in a carrier or another safe, enclosed space, and bring along a familiar blanket or toy - the familiar scent helps them settle. Once you're in the new place, set up food, water, litter, and bedding in a layout similar to the old home to shorten the adjustment period, and update the address on any ID tag or microchip registration.

6.2 Moving Refrigerators, Washers, and Other Large Appliances

Defrost and unplug the refrigerator at least 24 hours before the move, and keep it upright during transport rather than laying it on its side, to protect the compressor. After reinstalling it, wait the period specified in the manufacturer's manual before plugging it back in. For washing machines, drain any remaining water completely and confirm the shipping (transit) bolts are in place before the move; at the new home, check that the floor is level before installing it, and run the first cycle empty as a test. Relocating an air conditioner's outdoor unit usually requires a separate technician visit and additional cost, so confirm whether that's included when you get your quote.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. When should I start preparing for a move?

A reasonable rule of thumb is to start big-picture planning around four weeks out and shift into serious packing around the two-week mark. If you have a lot to move, or a school transfer or job relocation overlapping, start six to eight weeks out instead.

Q2. Should I choose full-service, partial, or self-pack?

Full-service is most convenient if you're short on time or have a lot to move. Partial-service is a reasonable middle ground for saving money while sharing some labor. Self-pack is worth considering for a small, light move.

Q3. Do I really need to update my address right away?

Yes - delaying it can mean missed mail and official notices, and some jurisdictions apply penalties for late registration. Rules vary by country, so check your local requirements.

Q4. What's the most reliable way to cut moving costs?

Moving on a weekday in the off-peak season, comparing at least three quotes, and decluttering beforehand to reduce volume are generally considered the most reliable ways to lower the total cost.

Q5. What should I keep with me instead of packing on the truck?

ID, contracts, medications, valuables, and a change of clothes are safest carried with you directly, separate from the moving truck.

Conclusion: Three Things to Remember

Moving prep comes down to three things: a timeline checklist, choosing the right mover, and staying on top of paperwork. Work through the four-week, two-week, one-week, moving-day, and after-the-move phases in order, get written quotes from at least three movers before signing anything, and split address changes, mail forwarding, and utility settlements cleanly between before and after the move so nothing slips through.

Exact procedures and costs vary by region, country, and how much you're moving, so treat this guide as a general roadmap and confirm the latest official guidance for where you live, along with your specific mover's terms.
