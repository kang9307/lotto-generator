Querying Korean law in everyday words instead of jargon - and its promise and limits

Introduction: Why Is the Law So Hard to Read

Suppose your lease is ending but your landlord will not return your deposit. Search online and you are buried in phrases like "Housing Lease Protection Act Article 3-2," "right to preferential reimbursement," and "perfection requirements." All you wanted to know was "how do I protect my deposit," yet to find the answer you must first learn the language of law. The law applies to everyone, but the ability to read it is never distributed equally.

Reaching No. 8 on GeekNews (news.hada.io) on June 13, 2026, MiniLex takes aim at exactly this gap. Per its description, MiniLex is a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) demo that helps citizens search Korean law in everyday language instead of legal jargon. Ask plainly - "what do I do if I cannot get my deposit back?" - and it retrieves the relevant statutory provisions and composes an answer grounded in them, in plain words. It is a small demo limited to seven laws, but the question it raises is not small: the democratization of access to legal information.

This article does more than introduce one tool. We will work through the problem it tackles (legal access and the jargon barrier), the technology beneath it (what RAG actually is), the meaning of plain-language legal search, the implications of a seven-law scope, the promise and the hallucination risk of legal AI, and its relationship to Korea's existing official services. Unverified implementation claims are marked "per its description."

1. The Problem MiniLex Tackles - The Wall of Legal Access

Access to legal information has three layered walls. MiniLex aims to lower the first of them in particular.

  • The jargon wall: Terms like "perfection requirement," "bona fide acquisition," or "acceleration clause" are far from everyday speech. For someone who does not even know what to search for, the search box itself is a closed door.
  • The structure wall: Korean law is layered into statutes, enforcement decrees, enforcement rules, and notices, with general laws and special laws crossing over. Reading a single provision rarely gives you the whole picture.
  • The cost wall: A lawyer's consultation costs money, and it is hard to call a professional for every minor everyday issue. The "quick check" demand in between is mostly left unmet.

MiniLex's premise is simple. People may not know the exact statute name "Housing Lease Protection Act," but they can describe their situation: "I did not get my deposit back." So what if the system takes that everyday language and connects it to the right statute? That idea, meeting natural-language interfaces and RAG, became the small demo that is MiniLex.

The law applies to everyone, but the ability to read it is not given to everyone. The gap MiniLex tries to narrow is exactly this.

2. What Is RAG - Generation Backed by Retrieval

At the heart of MiniLex is RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation). The name is long but the structure is clear. RAG splits into two steps.

  1. Retrieval: It takes the user's question and finds semantically close documents in a database. In MiniLex these documents are statutory provisions. The question and the provisions are each turned into embeddings (numeric vectors carrying meaning), and the closest provisions are selected in vector space.
  2. Generation: The retrieved provision text is handed to the LLM as grounding, with the instruction "answer the question based on these provisions." The model composes its answer tied to the real statutory text in front of it, not its vague memory.

Why does this matter? Ask a plain LLM about the law and it may invent answers from its blurry training-time memory, plausibly fabricating provisions or precedents that do not exist. RAG reduces such hallucination by grounding the answer in real source text, and at the same time lets the tool show "which provision did this answer come from." Thomson Reuters' legaltech primer likewise explains that RAG "draws directly from retrieved text to produce more detailed and accurate answers."

This idea of retrieving external knowledge to inject into an LLM call is not unique to law. The same family of architecture, applied to conversation context and user facts, is explored more deeply in our analysis of Supermemory - The Memory and Context Engine for AI.

3. How MiniLex Works

Based on its description, MiniLex's processing flow simplifies to roughly this:

Citizen's everyday-language question
   "I am not getting my deposit back"
        |
        v  [1] Question embedding
   Convert the question to a meaning vector
        |
        v  [2] Retrieval
   Pick the closest provisions among 7 laws
   e.g., Housing Lease Protection Act provisions
        |
        v  [3] Generation
   LLM composes a plain-language answer grounded in them
        |
        v  [4] Source display
   Answer + the cited statutory provisions shown together

The key is the pairing of the last two steps: explain in plain words, but show the statutory provisions that grounded the answer so the user can check the source directly. This "show your sources" property is precisely why RAG can earn more trust than a plain chatbot in the legal domain. Instead of trusting the answer alone, the user can click the cited provision and verify it.

4. The Seven-Law Scope - Why Start Small

MiniLex covers not all of Korean law but only seven laws as a demo. This narrow scope looks like a limit but reads as a deliberate design choice.

  • Quality control: A narrower scope means fewer provisions to search, making retrieval errors (pulling the wrong provision) and hallucination easier to control. Validating accuracy within seven laws before widening is the safer path.
  • Everyday relevance: The laws a demo covers are likely close to citizens' daily lives - leases, labor, consumers. The areas too minor to call a lawyer for, yet worth knowing to protect your rights, are exactly where RAG legal search adds the most value.
  • Verifiability: With a bounded target, it is easy for humans to check whether the demo's answers match the actual provisions. In legal AI, evaluation matters as much as development.

Academic legal-RAG research heads the same way. The KBL benchmark for Korean legal understanding, the LRAGE tool for evaluating multilingual legal RAG, and the LexRAG benchmark for multi-turn legal consultation all grow alongside infrastructure that measures "how accurately do you retrieve and answer." A small demo like MiniLex is the citizen-friendly entrance to this larger research current.

5. The Meaning of Plain-Language Legal Search - Democratizing Law

The biggest theme MiniLex raises is not technical but social. Being able to search law in everyday language means lowering the entry barrier to legal information, and that is one piece of the larger current called "legal democratization."

Traditionally, legal information concentrated among the few who could read and interpret it, because you had to know the exact terms even to begin a search. A natural-language interface moves that starting line. Now even someone who does not know "what to ask" can begin a search just by describing "their situation." This resolves the very first stage of the information gap - the stage of "not knowing the search term."

Caution is needed, of course. Lowering the barrier and planting false confidence are a paper's thickness apart. Given an easy, instant answer, users readily mistake it for a "settled conclusion." So the more a tool democratizes law, the more clearly it must mark the line that "this is reference, not a final answer." Accessibility and honest expectation-setting must travel together for democratization to be real.

6. Hallucination in Legal AI - The Most Dangerous Lie

The reason RAG is attractive in the legal domain is the same reason it must be handled with the most care: accuracy ties directly to rights and obligations. A plausible but wrong answer - a hallucination - is an inconvenience elsewhere but a loss in law.

A frequently cited study is relevant here. When Stanford's RegLab and HAI researchers evaluated commercial AI legal research tools from LexisNexis and Thomson Reuters, they found that despite using RAG, these tools hallucinated between 17 and 33 percent of the time. The industry advertised that RAG "reduces hallucination to nearly zero," but the empirical study showed that claim was overstated.

The lesson of this finding is not "RAG is useless" but the opposite. RAG clearly reduces hallucination yet does not eliminate it. So a legal RAG tool must have two things.

  • Verifiability: The user must be able to click the cited provision and check it immediately. The principle that a lawyer should see which documents were retrieved and how the model used them applies just as much to citizen-facing tools.
  • Clear disclosure of limits: Users must be made aware of the two failure modes - retrieval gaps (failing to find the relevant provision) and faulty synthesis (plausibly weaving in the wrong provision) - so "for reference, not advice" must be stated inside the tool.
To stress again: The answer of a RAG-based legal tool is not a conclusion in itself but a "starting point for checking." The same provision applies differently depending on the facts and on case law and special laws. Always go through the source text and a professional before exercising any right.

7. Comparison With Official Services - Complement, Not Competitor

Korea already has excellent official legal information services. To understand a RAG demo like MiniLex properly, we must place it in relation to them.

Service How it works Strength Drawback
National Law Information Center (law.go.kr) Unified search of current and historical statutes, cases, admin rules Most authoritative statutory text, comprehensiveness You need to know a law name or keyword
Easy-to-Find Everyday Legal Info (easylaw.go.kr) Topic-based explanations under the Ministry of Government Legislation Plain, expert-reviewed explanations You must follow fixed topic categories
MiniLex-style RAG demo Everyday-language question to provision retrieval and generation Natural-language input, low entry barrier Hallucination risk, narrow scope, unofficial and for reference

The key point is that these need not be seen as competitors. The official services provide authoritative text and reviewed explanations - a foundation of trust a RAG tool can never replace. A RAG demo, in turn, acts as an entrance that takes the vague questions arriving in natural language and guides users to that authoritative text. The ideal picture is one where RAG treats the official text as both its retrieval target and its source, linking each answer to the corresponding provision on law.go.kr. That is a complementary structure combining natural-language access with official authority.

8. Use by Citizens and in Practice

Who, realistically, finds such a tool useful, and how? Let us split the use cases concretely.

  • A citizen's first check: Before calling a lawyer, quickly identify "roughly which law relates to my situation." It sets the direction of the next action (checking the source, booking a consultation).
  • Non-legal practitioners: HR, admin, or startup staff checking the basic provisions of labor and contracts in everyday language. Important decisions, however, must still go through professional review.
  • Legal education: A learning aid for students or the public to grasp the structure of law. By asking questions and receiving relevant provisions, they build legal literacy.

The common thread is that all are uses as a starting point. A RAG legal tool is less a destination than a compass that sets the first direction out of a fog. Its value is especially large on topics close to daily life, like a tenant's rights; the specifics of those rights are organized separately in our Tenant Rights and Lease Protection Guide.

9. The Difficulty of Korean Specialization - Language and System

RAG over Korean law is not a matter of simply porting an English-language tool. Several Korea-specific difficulties exist.

  • Korean legal language: Sino-Korean legal terms, long single-sentence provisions, and legislative techniques like incorporation and deeming challenge embedding and retrieval accuracy. A model trained and validated on Korean legal corpora is needed.
  • Frequent amendments: Laws change often. If the searched data fails to reflect the latest current law, there is a risk of answering on the basis of repealed old provisions. The data-refresh cycle is at the core of trust.
  • The weight of cases and decrees: Korean law is statutory, but actual application is often governed by case law, enforcement decrees, and notices. A demo searching only the main statute may give answers that are "right on the law but different in reality."

These difficulties re-explain why a demo like MiniLex starts from a narrow scope. Korean legal AI is, before it is a technical problem, a problem of how to capture data freshness and institutional context.

10. Responsibility and Ethics - Who Answers for the Answer

The last wall legal AI must clear is not technology but responsibility. If a RAG tool gives a wrong answer and a user suffers a loss, who is responsible? There is, as yet, no clear social consensus on this question.

So today's citizen-facing legal RAG demos consistently take the same stance. They limit themselves to providing information, make clear they are not legal advice, and return final judgment to the user and the professional. This is not a mere disclaimer but an honest admission that, at the current state of the art, hallucination cannot be reduced to zero. MiniLex's posture of staying a small demo while stressing "for reference" can be read, rather, as an expression of the responsibility legal AI ought to carry.

11. Conclusion + Three Things for Users

MiniLex is less a single product than a small milestone showing, at a citizen's eye level, the 2026 current of "searching law in plain language." Of the walls of legal access - jargon, structure, cost - it lowers the first with the technology of RAG, and it tries to secure verifiability by grounding answers in statutory text. Its narrow seven-law scope, its honest caution about hallucination, and its complementary relationship with official services together show how far this current has come and what remains to be solved.

For users who will meet such tools, three closing points.

  1. Look at the source, not the answer. The real value of a RAG legal tool is not the plain-language answer but the statutory provision it cites. Once you have read the answer, always click the cited provision and check it yourself.
  2. Use it as reference, not as a conclusion. As the 17-33 percent hallucination rate shows, no legal AI yet replaces a lawyer. Go through the law.go.kr source and a professional before exercising any right.
  3. Run it alongside official services. Vague natural-language starts with RAG; precise confirmation with the National Law Information Center and Easy-to-Find Everyday Legal Info. Using both entrances together is the safest way.
