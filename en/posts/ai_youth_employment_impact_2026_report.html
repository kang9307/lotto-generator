Executive Summary

This report uses only facts that could be confirmed against official statistics and research as of September 29, 2026 to describe how AI is affecting the youth job market. Figures from the original January 2026 version that had no traceable source, such as replacement-risk percentages, counts of new jobs, and salaries by occupation, have been removed. The key points are:

  • Weak youth employment shows up in the statistics: In South Korea, the August 2026 employment rate for ages 15-29 was 44.1% (down 1.0 percentage point from a year earlier, the 28th straight monthly decline) and the unemployment rate was 5.4% (up 0.5 point), according to the national statistics office.
  • AI's share is not settled: A Stanford study reports employment of 22-25 year-olds in AI-exposed jobs about 19% lower, but says this is not a causal estimate, and a New York Fed analysis notes that the rise in youth unemployment began before AI spread.
  • The signal appears at the hiring stage: In US data, the entry-level gap in AI-exposed jobs showed up as fewer new hires rather than more layoffs, and no comparable gap appeared for experienced workers.
  • Projections are projections: The World Economic Forum's January 2025 report expected 170 million jobs created, 92 million displaced, and a net gain of 78 million by 2030. That was a forecast at the time, not a settled outcome.
  • Policy is at the announcement stage: Government youth job measures keep coming, but within the scope checked this time, no evaluation of their effect on youth employment in the AI era was found.

1. Introduction: Research Background and Objectives

Since the emergence of ChatGPT in 2023, generative AI has developed quickly and affected industries across the board. More recently, AI agents are said to be taking on complex work beyond simple repetitive tasks.

These changes can affect the labor market in two directions. They can raise productivity and create new industries, but they can also displace existing jobs and dampen hiring. Many observers expect young people entering the labor market to feel it first. The definition of "youth" differs by statistic. In the Korean statistics office's data, youth means ages 15-29, and this article uses that range unless stated otherwise.

This report has the following objectives:

  • Describe the impact of AI on the youth job market using statistics and research that could be confirmed as of September 2026
  • Give a balanced view of the employment weakness and the new opportunities facing young people
  • Separate what the research says from what it does not say, and describe policy responses only as far as they were confirmed
  • Offer response strategies that young people can pick from according to their own situation

2. AI Technology Status and Labor Market Impact in 2026

2.1 Directions of Generative AI Development

AI technology is described as developing in the following directions. These items describe general trends; adoption-rate figures could not be confirmed this time.

  • Spread of Multimodal AI: AI models that handle text, images, voice, and video together are used in content creation, customer service, and education.
  • Introduction of AI Agents: Agents that carry out complex tasks in multiple steps, beyond simple Q&A, have begun to enter workplaces.
  • Industry-Specific AI: AI solutions tailored to finance, healthcare, legal, and manufacturing are appearing.
  • Lower Barriers to Building: No-code and low-code platforms let people build AI applications without coding.

2.3 The World Economic Forum's Job Outlook

The World Economic Forum's "Future of Jobs Report 2025," published in January 2025, projected job changes through 2030 as shifts including AI and automation continue.

  • Scale: 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net gain of 78 million, with roughly 22% of jobs affected.
  • Declining Occupations: Postal service clerks, administrative assistants, and cashiers were among the fastest-declining roles.
  • Growing Occupations: Technology, data, and AI roles were expected to grow fastest, with core economy roles such as care and education also growing.
  • Skills Change: About 40% of skills required on the job were expected to change, and 63% of employers named the skills gap as a key barrier.

This is a forecast as of January 2025. The earlier version of this article cited a "Future of Jobs Report 2026" and per-occupation changes (data entry clerks -47%, prompt engineers +124%, and so on). Those did not match the 2025 report that was checked and have been removed.

3. Negative Impact on Youth Employment

3.1 Rising Entry Barriers: Weaker New Hiring

Noteworthy Trend: In US data, the employment gap for 22-25 year-olds in AI-exposed jobs appeared as fewer new hires rather than more layoffs, and the same gap was not seen among experienced workers. The authors ask that it be read as an early indicator, not a causal estimate.

Youth employment in Korea remains weak, but the cause cannot be reduced to AI alone. In its youth job measures reported in July 2026, the government also said it would prepare tailored measures for weak sectors such as manufacturing and construction. The earlier claim in this article that "large-company new graduate hiring fell an average of 18%" could not be traced to a source and has been removed.

The reasons most often offered for companies preferring experienced hires over new graduates are listed below. They are observations, not something this article verified.

  • Immediate Productivity Requirements: Preference for people who can work with AI tools and deliver results at once
  • Training Cost Reduction: Less need to train new hires for tasks AI now handles
  • Uncertainty Response: Preference for proven talent in a fast-changing environment
  • Team Size Reduction: Smaller teams justified by AI adoption

3.2 Youth Jobs Exposed to AI

"AI exposure" in the research means how much the tasks in a job overlap with what AI can do. It is not the probability that the job disappears. Among entry points commonly used by young workers, tasks heavy on standardized information handling are often mentioned, as below. The per-occupation "replacement risk (%)" attached in the earlier version could not be traced to a source and has been removed.

  • Administrative Assistants: Document preparation, data organization, schedule management
  • Customer Service Representatives: Basic customer support and inquiry handling
  • Content Production Assistants: Basic design, video editing, copywriting
  • Basic Translation/Interpretation: General document translation, basic interpretation
  • Market Research Assistants: Data collection and basic analysis

3.3 Job Quality: Prolonged Joblessness and Underemployment

The official data checked this time show that young people's employment has worsened. They do not say whether AI is the reason.

  • Fewer Employed: In August 2026, 3.428 million young Koreans were employed, 143,000 fewer than a year earlier.
  • Prolonged Joblessness: In the statistics office's May 2026 youth supplementary survey, 241,000 people (19.4%) had been jobless for three years or more after leaving school, the highest since the item began in 2007.
  • Underemployment: In the US, 42.0% of recent college graduates worked in jobs that did not require a degree in June 2026 (NY Fed).

The earlier figures of "starting salaries down 3.2% in real terms" and "contract/intern share of entry-level hiring up from 34% to 47%" could not be traced to a source and have been removed.

4. Positive Impact on Youth Employment

4.1 New AI-Related Jobs

Areas of Opportunity: The World Economic Forum's January 2025 outlook named technology, data, and AI as the fastest-growing fields. That young people have an edge in learning new technology is closer to an expectation, and no statistic confirming it was found.

Occupations newly mentioned alongside AI include the following. Average salaries and hiring volumes by occupation could not be traced to a source and have been removed this time.

  • Prompt Engineer: Designing inputs to get the desired results from AI models
  • AI Trainer: Data labeling and quality management for model training
  • AI Ethics Consultant: Reviewing AI systems for bias and setting ethical guidelines
  • AI-Human Collaboration Designer: Designing how AI and people work together in an organization
  • AI Product Manager: Planning and managing AI-based products and services

4.2 Productivity Gains: Research Results Are Mixed

The claim that skilled AI use speeds up work gives different results depending on conditions. The earlier version's "2.8 times the output for proficient AI users" and "promotion timelines shortened by 1.4 years" could not be traced to a source and have been removed and replaced with studies that were confirmed.

  • GitHub controlled experiment (2022): In a task of writing a JavaScript HTTP server, 95 developers in the Copilot group were 55% faster. This was a single task.
  • METR randomized trial (July 2025): With 16 experienced open-source developers and 246 tasks, tasks where AI was allowed took 19% longer, though participants felt they were 20% faster. In a February 24, 2026 follow-up, METR said the 2025 sample may be biased by a selection effect from participants who did not want to work without AI, that developers in early 2026 were likely faster than the 2025 estimate but the evidence for the size is weak, and that it is changing the experiment design.
  • Anthropic learning experiment (January 2026): In an experiment where mostly junior developers (52 people) learned an unfamiliar Python library, the AI group scored 17% lower on a comprehension quiz and the time saved was not significant.
  • Focus on High-Value Work: Whether time saved by automation is reinvested in learning and strategic work appears to decide the outcome. This is a viewpoint, not a measurement.

These studies are covered in more detail in the AI amplification article.

4.3 Entrepreneurship and Freelance Markets

Some observers say AI can lower barriers to youth entrepreneurship and freelancing. No statistics measuring the results were confirmed this time, so this is described only as a possibility.

  • Easier Solo Entrepreneurship: Small teams can run services at a scale that used to require more people.
  • Wider Freelance Scope: AI assistants widen the range of work one person can handle.
  • Global Market Access: AI translation and localization tools may lower barriers to entering overseas markets.
  • Lower Startup Costs: AI-based services may reduce initial labor costs.

4.4 Strengths of a Generation Exposed to AI Early

The following strengths of young people are often mentioned. No statistics verifying generational traits were confirmed this time, so read them as observations.

  • Technology Adaptability: Quickly learning new AI tools and platforms
  • Digital Literacy: An intuitive sense of AI's possibilities and limits
  • Creative Utilization: Openness to creating new value with AI as a tool
  • Less Resistance to Collaboration: A tendency to accept working with AI naturally

5. Domestic and International Policy Response Status

5.1 Korean Government Youth Employment Policies

The government moves confirmed through news reports this time are listed below. Both are as reported, and the confirmed contents should be checked again against official government announcements.

  • Youth job recovery plan (reported July 2026): The Ministry of Finance and Economy and the Ministry of Employment and Labor were reported to be finalizing and announcing it during the third quarter. It aims to train at least 200,000 specialists in advanced industries such as AI and semiconductors by 2030, identify at least 200,000 quality jobs, and prepare tailored measures for weak sectors such as manufacturing and construction. Whether the final plan has been announced was not confirmed this time.
  • Youth support budget: In the 2027 budget proposal reported as announced on August 28, 2026, comprehensive investment by youth life stage rises 53.5%, from 28.2 trillion won to 43.3 trillion won. This is the government's proposal before National Assembly review.

The earlier version's "100,000 AI trainees a year with a 2026 budget of 420 billion won" and "up to 100 million won for youth AI startups" could not be traced to a source and have been removed.

The following limits of the policies have been pointed out. They were not verified this time and reflect the assessment at the time of first publication.

  • Persistent mismatch between education and actual industry demands
  • Lack of direct countermeasures for AI-driven job losses
  • Access gaps in AI education for youth outside major metropolitan areas

6. Response Strategies for Young People

6.1 Essential Skills Development Roadmap

Here are the core competencies worth building in the AI era, divided into three phases. The durations are examples and should be adjusted to your situation.

Phase 1: AI Literacy Fundamentals (about 3-6 months, as an example)

  • Understanding basic AI concepts and principles
  • Proficiency with major AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney, etc.)
  • Critical understanding of AI possibilities and limitations

Phase 2: Professional AI Application (about 6-12 months, as an example)

  • Advanced AI tool usage in your field of study or interest
  • Work automation and efficiency improvement using AI
  • Ability to evaluate and improve AI output quality

Phase 3: AI Collaboration Expert (12+ months, as an example)

  • Integrated AI tool use in complex projects
  • Designing human-AI collaboration processes
  • Proposing and executing AI-based innovation ideas

6.2 Fields Worth Watching

The World Economic Forum's January 2025 outlook expected technology, data, and AI to grow fastest. The list below is a set of candidates in that direction; per-field projection figures were not confirmed.

Candidate Fields

  • AI/Data: ML engineers, data scientists, AI product managers
  • Cybersecurity: AI-based threat detection, security architects
  • Digital Healthcare: AI diagnostic assistance, health tech development
  • Sustainability/ESG: AI-based carbon management, environmental data analysis
  • Creative Tech: Content production using generative AI

6.3 AI Skill Enhancement Strategies

Specific approaches to building practical AI skills:

  • Online Learning Platforms: Take AI-related courses on Coursera, edX, Naver Boostcourse, and similar sites
  • Project-Based Learning: Build practical experience through personal projects or open-source contributions
  • AI Community Participation: Join AI study groups, hackathons, and meetups
  • Portfolio Building: Document AI-assisted results on GitHub, blogs, and similar sites
  • Certification: Obtain AWS, Google, or Microsoft AI certifications or data analysis credentials

7. Conclusion and Recommendations

As of September 2026, it is confirmed that youth employment indicators have worsened. But research differs on how much of that AI accounts for, and none has settled the question either way. The conclusion of this check is that it is hard to call the situation simply a crisis or simply an opportunity.

Key Implications

  • There are signs that entry-level employment in AI-exposed jobs is relatively weak, but they are not causal estimates.
  • Remote work, the business cycle, and sector factors are also cited for weak youth employment.
  • No confirmed statistic showing how much AI skills widen the employment gap was found this time.
  • Studies of the effect of using AI give mixed results depending on the task and the user.

Policy Recommendations

  • Education System Reform: Reflect AI literacy in university curricula and strengthen industry-academia linkages
  • Transition Support Enhancement: Reskilling and transition support for workers whose jobs are changing because of AI
  • Expanded AI Access: Improve AI education accessibility for youth outside the capital region and from low-income backgrounds
  • New Employment Form Support: Build social safety nets for AI-based freelancers and solo entrepreneurs

Advice for Young People

  • View AI as a Tool, Not a Threat: Approach it as a way to raise your value, but be able to verify its output yourself.
  • Make Continuous Learning a Habit: Learn steadily to keep pace with technological change.
  • Strengthen Uniquely Human Competencies: Build creativity, empathy, and ethical judgment, which AI does not easily replace.
  • Actively Pursue Practical Experience: Build experience with AI through internships, projects, and entrepreneurship.
  • Expand Networking: Gain information and opportunities by connecting with experts and peers in your field.

The less settled the direction of change, the clearer what an individual can do: read statistics accurately, avoid being swayed by inflated figures, and try using AI in your own field while checking the results. As in the earlier version, cooperation among government, companies, educational institutions, and young people themselves matters.

## 1. What to check before quoting a figure

| Check | Why | Example confirmed in this article |
|---|---|---|
| The age range of "youth" | Ranges differ by statistic | Statistics Korea uses 15-29, US BLS uses 16-24, NY Fed recent graduates are 22-27 |
| Reference month and seasonality | Monthly values swing widely | Read Korean data against the same month a year earlier. US July is when students crowd into summer job searches |
| Does the study claim causation? | A descriptive pattern is not a causal estimate | The Stanford study says it is not causal, and the NY Fed's 64% is a back-of-the-envelope calculation |
| Forecast or result? | A forecast is not a settled outcome | The World Economic Forum's 2030 figures are a forecast as of January 2025 |
| Can the source be opened? | Do not quote "replacement risk %" without a source | This article removed figures whose original sources could not be found |
