What Was Released - The Announcement and the Repository

On October 6, 2026 OpenAI published a research post titled Sharing AI progress in mathematics. It said OpenAI is releasing a broad range of new mathematical results produced by an internal frontier model. The results sit in the GitHub repository openai/math as manuscripts. The only numbers written in the body of the announcement are 10 reasoning summaries and an average compute of roughly three hours. The figures 722, 372 and approximately 4,000 live in the repository README and overview document.

The first thing to separate when reading this material is the unit of each number. Whether a figure counts manuscripts, families or problems posed to the model turns a phrase like several hundred papers into entirely different claims. The announcement describes the release method as follows.

  • Consultation. OpenAI said it consulted the independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence at the Institute for Advanced Study to develop best practices, and drew on its advice and public recommendations in deciding how to release the results.
  • Hosting. The results are published in a GitHub repository with protocols for paper revisions and citations. OpenAI said it continues to explore community-hosted alternatives that meet the committee's guidelines.
  • Formal proofs. OpenAI said it is sharing Lean formalizations of many of the proofs and will update the repository as it obtains more. The announcement describes Lean as a programming language that allows mathematical proofs to be checked by a computer.
  • Transparency material. The repository is to include 10 summaries of the model's reasoning, estimates of compute spent in terms of Pro usage on ChatGPT, and statistics on the number of attempted problems.
  • Support plans. OpenAI said it will fund workshops, conferences and special programs around understanding major results produced by AI, and will share more in the near future.
  • The model. OpenAI said it is working to release the model that produced these results responsibly. The announcement gives no model name, size or release date.

The README restates this from the repository side. The collection consists of mathematical manuscripts and supporting proof artifacts produced by an internal OpenAI model. It explains that OpenAI evaluates its models on open research problems as part of model development and expanded these evaluations after performance on its existing mathematical evaluations saturated. A further sentence says some outputs build upon earlier results produced by the models.

722 and 372 - One Number Counts Manuscripts, the Other Counts Families

The README states that the current catalogue contains 722 manuscripts organized into 372 families. A family groups related papers. By the README's definition a single family may include a principal result, companion arguments, consequences or alternative proofs, and each family is classified by mathematical discipline. The cover of the overview document carries the line 372 result families in 722 manuscripts and the date October 6, 2026.

The 722 also matches the folder count. The preprints folder holds 722 subfolders, one per manuscript (calculated from the GitHub directory listing). The README says each manuscript folder contains PDFs, source files, and manuscript-specific citation and build instructions.

The word result points to different layers in different documents. The announcement says new mathematical results, the README says results at different stages of verification and each result used three hours, and the catalogue refers to other families by number, as in result 032. When carrying the figures over, naming the layer as 722 manuscripts and 372 families is the safer wording. The table below lists every number in this material with its unit and denominator.

About 4,000 Problems and Three Hours - What the Denominator and the Average Cover

The README says that over the course of the evaluation the model was posed approximately 4,000 problems, and that aggregating the output into result families and manuscripts and requiring an appropriate level of significance led to the catalogue. The 4,000 counts problems posed, while 372 and 722 count outputs that passed aggregation and a significance requirement. The README does not say whether one problem leads to one family, whether one problem can yield several families, or what standard decided significance. A quotient such as 372 divided by 4,000 is therefore not a success rate, and no success rate can be computed from this material alone. The announcement says statistics on the number of attempted problems are published in the repository.

The three-hour figure appears in nearly the same form in the announcement and the README. The announcement says the average result used the equivalent compute of roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking, and the README says each result used on average three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute. Four conditions apply when reading it.

  • It is an average. Nothing is said about minimum, maximum or distribution. Some results may have used far less and some far more.
  • It expresses compute in terms of Pro thinking time. No conversion to elapsed time, GPU time or subscription cost appears in the source, and none is made here.
  • It covers the vast majority. The README says most results came from the same procedure with an unreleased internal model, and it names exceptions to that procedure (section 6).
  • The layer behind the word result may differ by sentence. This is taken up in the second-to-last section.

Reasoning summaries are published for only 10 families. The README calls them abridged summaries. Ten out of 372 is about 2.7% (calculated), and they are not a reasoning record for all 722 manuscripts. All 10 families carry a Lean document link (calculated).

The mean is 722 divided by 372, about 1.94 manuscripts (calculated), but the most common value is one. Reading the two numbers goes wrong in both directions. Taking 722 as the number of independent results hides that a manuscript placed second or later in a family may, under the README's classification, be a companion argument, a consequence or an alternative proof. Looking only at 372 hides the breadth of large families such as 034, whose 14 manuscripts carry different titles. How many of the 350 manuscripts beyond the first in each family (722 minus 372, calculated) play each role is a separate question, and the README gives no distribution of roles.

The meaning of a Lean link also needs a boundary. The link at the end of a family summary marks that a formalization document exists in the repository. Whether the formalization covers every theorem of every manuscript in the family has to be read separately from the documents in the lean folder and the formalization catalogue (formalization.yaml), which the README says describes the formal proofs, their associated papers and verification configurations. What formalization in general does and does not check is covered in Formalization Is Not Proof - What 13 Million Lines on Fermat's Last Theorem Guarantee.

The README in the lean folder says the library is organized as a single large library and recommends compiling only small portions at a time. Its technical note says compiling the whole library may fail if the Linux setting vm.max_map_count is too low, and one workaround is to build Lean with the CMake option -DMMAP=OFF. The Comparator README, which gives additional checking instructions, describes installing comparator, landrun and lean4export onto the PATH and then running from the lean folder with a challenge JSON file.

Reading the Classification Through Cases - The Irrationality Exponent of π, the Mahler Conjectures, the Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis and a Counterexample Family

The catalogue gives each family a one-paragraph summary and lists the manuscript titles and abstracts beneath it. The four families below are translations of those summaries. The statements are claims as the catalogue writes them, not a ruling on whether they are correct.

  • Family 017 - The irrationality exponent of π is 2. The catalogue says it proves that the irrationality exponent of π is exactly 2: for every ε > 0 and all sufficiently large denominators q, every rational p/q satisfies |π − p/q| ≥ q−2−ε. It says the convergence of the Flint–Hills series, with angles in radians, is proved as well. There is one manuscript, a Lean link exists, and the family is among the 10 with a reasoning summary.
  • Family 087 - The Mahler conjectures. The catalogue says it resolves the symmetric and nonsymmetric geometric Mahler conjectures in every dimension, with Hanner polytopes and simplices as the respective volume-product minimizers and all equality cases classified. The corresponding sharp functional Mahler inequalities, and the statement that for n ≥ 2 every symmetric polar product K×K° in dimension 2n has Gromov width 4, belong to the same family. It has three manuscripts, a Lean link and a published reasoning summary.
  • Family 003 - The quasi-Riemann hypothesis. The catalogue says it proves that every Dirichlet L-function, including ζ(s), is zero-free in Re s > 7/8, and that the same half-plane is zero-free for every finite-order Hecke L-function over ℚ(√−3). A companion is said to give a different proof of the zero-free half-plane Re s > 11/12. The three manuscripts are the 7/8 manuscript, one titled alternate 11/12 proof, and one titled Uniform exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros. The README names work on a zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function as an exception to the fixed procedure and says the Re(s) > 11/12 writeup was human edited for readability. The README does not give the family number of that work, so linking it to this family is an interpretation based on matching content.
  • Family 197 - A counterexample family. The catalogue says it constructs a finitely presented torsion-free nonsofic group whose group algebra over 𝔽2 is not directly finite, and presents this as disproving Kaplansky's conjecture even without torsion. Refutations of Gottschalk's surjunctivity conjecture and of the unrestricted Determinant Conjecture sit in the same family. It has four manuscripts, a Lean link and a published reasoning summary.

Measured against the README's definition of a family, the four cases have different shapes. Family 017 is one manuscript that is the family. Family 087 holds three manuscripts covering the symmetric case, the general convex-body case and the width of polar products. Family 003 holds a 7/8 manuscript, an alternative proof for the 11/12 region and a manuscript on Landau–Siegel zeros. Family 197 holds four manuscripts: the torsion-free group algebra, a characteristic-two counterexample, an odd-characteristic counterexample and a determinant-conjecture counterexample. The catalogue does not attach a role label such as principal result or alternative proof to each manuscript, and the role is read from the family summary sentence and the manuscript title. The summaries of these four families state what is achieved and do not classify the relation to results already known in the literature, such as whether a result improves an earlier one or gives a new proof. The only statement about novelty is OpenAI's own phrase new mathematical results in the announcement, so any individual judgment has to come from comparing the manuscript text with the literature of the field.

For a reader who wants to examine the figures and claims, the following order fits.

  • Read the README sections How the results were produced and Versions and citations first, to take in the procedure, the exceptions and the version rules.
  • In the overview document, read the family summaries for the field of interest and note the family number.
  • In the manuscript map CONTENTS.md, see the manuscript list for that family and whether it has a Lean link.
  • Open the PDF and the manuscript-specific citation and build notes in the manuscript folder. Titles and abstracts show whether another manuscript in the same family is the principal result or a companion argument.
  • If a Lean link exists, read the family's document in the lean folder and the formalization catalogue to see which manuscripts it ties to and what verification configuration it has.
  • To check a formal proof directly, follow the tool requirements in the Comparator instructions and the compilation advice in the lean folder README.

Standards for Citing and Judging This Material

Figures and claims need their unit and attribution when they are repeated. The places where this material is most easily misquoted are listed below.

  • Numbers with units. Write 722 manuscripts and 372 families. Phrases such as 722 results or 722 proofs are not wording the README uses.
  • Verification as stages. Instead of AI proved 722 papers, write that OpenAI released 722 manuscripts and said the verification stages differ. Families with a Lean link number 235 (calculated).
  • Three hours as an analogy. It expresses average compute in terms of Pro thinking time. It is neither elapsed time nor cost.
  • 4,000 as a denominator of problems posed. The mapping that would make it the denominator of a success rate is not given.
  • Individual theorems with coordinates. Give the family number, the manuscript folder name, the repository version and whether a Lean link exists. The repository says corrections are recorded as new versions.
  • Exceptions alongside the claim. The Riemann zeta zero-free region and the Hodge Conjecture for CM abelian varieties are named by the README as exceptions to the fixed procedure. Cite them with the note that the procedure differed.
  • The location may change. OpenAI said it is exploring community-hosted alternatives.

What the mathematical community needs to settle when solving and understanding come apart is discussed in What the Mathematical Community Must Re-decide When Solving and Understanding Diverge. What disappears from outputs that arrive as conclusions only is covered in The Answer Is Correct but Unusable - What Vanishes When Only the Conclusion Arrives. How variation experiments separate the points where a score fails to prove ability is covered in Telling Reasoning From Recall - Four Variation Experiments for Judging AI Math Performance.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • Whether result in the average-compute sentence means a family or a manuscript. The announcement and the README both use only the word result in that sentence without defining it. All that is established is that the catalogue attaches result numbers to families.
  • The mapping between the approximately 4,000 problems and the 372 families and 722 manuscripts, and the standard used to judge an appropriate level of significance.
  • Whether the compute estimates and the attempted-problem statistics that the announcement says are in the repository exist as a separate file. Apart from README sentences, no such item appears in the listing.
  • The contents of the per-family documents in the lean folder and of formalization.yaml, that is, which part of each manuscript a formalization covers.
  • Per-manuscript human review history and peer review status. The README names human editing at one place only, the Re(s) > 11/12 writeup, and reactions from outside mathematicians appear in neither the announcement, the README nor the catalogue.
  • The text of the manuscript PDFs, the contents of the reasoning summary PDFs and the terms of the LICENSE.
  • The model's name, size and release date. The announcement says only that a responsible release is being prepared.
  • The calculated values in the body, such as 235, are based on CONTENTS.md and the overview document as of October 7, 2026. The repository is revised, so they may change afterward.
