Korea Vehicle Tax: How That Number Is Calculated

Displacement builds the base, vehicle age cuts it, prepayment cuts it again, and selling the car splits it by the day

Aug 16, 2026 Finance & Economy

This article is for general information only. Actual tax amounts and eligibility vary with individual circumstances. For final confirmation, follow the guidance of your competent local government and Wetax. This covers the Korean system only; figures here do not transfer to any other country.

1. June and December: where the number comes from

The notice shows only the result. One figure lands in the mailbox, and nothing on it explains why that figure and not another. So when a colleague with a similar-looking car mentions paying noticeably less, suspicion follows immediately.

The answer is usually one of two things. A different engine displacement, or an older car. Korean vehicle tax runs on remarkably few variables, and once you know them the notice becomes predictable. Purchase price, fuel economy and mileage are not among them.

What follows is where the calculation starts, where it gets cut, how and when it is paid, and what happens if it is not. Rates and articles were confirmed as of 16 August 2026, and figures tied to a specific tax year are labelled with that year. Buying and selling is covered separately in the used car buying guide, and periodic inspection in the vehicle inspection guide.

2. It is a local tax, and it attaches to ownership

Many people assume this is a national tax. It is not. Vehicle tax in Korea is a local tax, assessed and collected by the local government where the vehicle is registered. That is why it is handled through the Wetax family of portals rather than the national tax service, and why enquiries go to the tax division of the relevant city, county or district office.

The more consequential point is what it attaches to. Ownership tax attaches to owning, not to driving. A car driven 100 km in a year and one driven 30,000 km are taxed identically if displacement and age match.

That distinction produces a few practical consequences.

  • A parked car is still taxed — not driving it does not stop the assessment
  • If you are disposing of a car, the burden stops only when deregistration or transfer of registration actually completes
  • The taxpayer is the owner on the registration record — who actually drives it is not the test
  • Regular assessments go to whoever owns the vehicle on 1 June and 1 December

Deciding to sell and having the title move are two different events. That distinction is essentially the whole of the pro-rata discussion later on.

3. The base: displacement times a per-cc rate

The starting point is simple. Article 127(1) of the Local Tax Act sets the annual amount for a passenger vehicle as engine displacement multiplied by a per-cc rate, stepped up as displacement bands rise.

Real numbers make it concrete. These assume a private-use car aged two years or less, so no age relief yet, with local education tax of 30 percent added.

  • 999cc — 999 × 80 = 79,920 won, education tax 23,976 won, total 103,896 won
  • 1,598cc — 1,598 × 140 = 223,720 won, education tax 67,116 won, total 290,836 won
  • 1,999cc — 1,999 × 200 = 399,800 won, education tax 119,940 won, total 519,740 won
  • 2,497cc — 2,497 × 200 = 499,400 won, education tax 149,820 won, total 649,220 won

What stands out is the cliff around 1,600cc. One additional cc moves the rate from 140 won to 200 won: 1,600 × 140 = 224,000 won, while 1,601 × 200 = 320,200 won, a gap of more than 96,000 won in vehicle tax alone. That is why so many Korean compact models sit at 1,598cc or 1,599cc, right up against the line.

Your arithmetic may miss the notice by a few hundred won because of rounding and per-period truncation. The amount printed on the notice is what is owed. Treat the calculation as a way to anticipate the range and to notice when something looks wrong.

Commercial and private use carry different rates

Two vehicles of identical displacement can differ almost tenfold, and the reason is the commercial-use column. For a 2,000cc car, private use runs at 200 won per cc while commercial use runs at 19 won.

Commercial use means registered for passenger or freight transport business — taxis, rental fleets, business cargo vehicles. A car used for commuting and weekend trips is private use even if it is registered to a business. The test is the use recorded on the vehicle registration certificate, not whether the owner is an individual or a company, so the certificate settles the question.

Local education tax adds 30 percent

One more line inflates the notice. Article 151 of the Local Tax Act sets local education tax at 30 percent of the vehicle tax payable. On a private passenger car, education tax appears alongside vehicle tax on the same notice, and the sum of the two is what actually gets paid.

  • It is a separate tax item but arrives on the same notice
  • The base is the vehicle tax after relief — age relief shrinks the education tax too
  • A prepayment deduction that reduces vehicle tax moves the education tax the same way

4. Age relief: 5 percent a year from year three

Older cars are taxed less, and this is not municipal discretion — it is a formula in the statute. Article 127(2) of the Local Tax Act sets each half-year amount for a private-use passenger vehicle as follows.

Half-year amount = A/2 − (A/2 × 5/100)(n − 2)
A is the annual amount and n is the vehicle age, treated as 2 where lower and as 12 where higher.

The formula looks awkward but the result is plain. Relief begins in the third year, grows 5 percentage points a year, and stops at 50 percent in year twelve. Beyond that, more years change nothing.

  • Years 1 to 2 — no relief, full annual amount
  • Year 3 — 5 percent relief
  • Year 5 — 15 percent relief
  • Year 10 — 40 percent relief
  • Year 12 onward — fixed at 50 percent relief

Take the 2,000cc private car. Its 399,800 won drops 15 percent at age five to 339,830 won, with education tax of 101,949 won on top for a total of 441,779 won — roughly 78,000 won below the 519,740 won it carried when new.

Two cautions. First, this relief applies only to private-use passenger vehicles; flat-rate categories such as buses and cargo vehicles are outside it. Second, age counts from first registration, not from your purchase. Buy a three-year-old car today and you have owned it for a day, but its age is three, so relief has already begun.

5. Vehicles with no displacement: EVs and hydrogen cars

Since the calculation multiplies displacement, it collapses for vehicles that have none. Article 127(1) of the Local Tax Act groups these as other passenger vehicles and assigns a flat amount.

  • Private use — 100,000 won a year, plus 30,000 won education tax, for 130,000 won
  • Commercial use — 20,000 won a year, which is where electric taxis land

The amount is unrelated to size, price or output. A compact EV and a large premium electric SUV pay the same. Against roughly 520,000 won a year for a 2,000cc combustion car, the gap is wide, and it is a recurring reason the displacement base gets questioned.

Hybrids are different. They have an engine with a stated displacement, so they are calculated on the ordinary displacement basis. Using less fuel does not enter the vehicle tax calculation. Running costs for hybrids are covered in the hybrid fuel economy guide.

7. Due dates, payment channels and prepayment

Regular periods fall in June and December

Article 128(1) of the Local Tax Act splits the year into two.

  • First period (ownership 1 January to 30 June) — payable 16 to 30 June
  • Second period (ownership 1 July to 31 December) — payable 16 to 31 December

Where the annual amount is 100,000 won or less, the whole year may be collected with the first period assessment. That is why owners of light cars and small EVs often see a notice only in June. A deduction still applies to the portion attributable to the second period.

Where it can be paid

Several channels are open.

  • Wetax (wetax.go.kr) and the Smart Wetax app — the nationwide route
  • Seoul ETAX (etax.seoul.go.kr) and the STAX app — for vehicles registered in Seoul
  • ARS telephone payment, virtual account transfer, and GIRO (giro.or.kr)
  • Bank CD and ATM machines, bank counters, or the local government office in person
  • Notice payment menus in apps such as Kakao, Naver and Payco

Setting up a direct debit removes the missed-deadline failure mode entirely. Where the final day falls on a Saturday or public holiday it moves to the next business day, and local governments sometimes announce an extension. The date on the notice is the one that governs.

Prepayment: pay early, pay less

Prepayment means settling the full year up front and receiving a deduction for doing so. Article 128(3) of the Local Tax Act allows filing and payment between the 16th and the end of January, March, June or September, with the deduction calculated at a rate set by Presidential Decree within 10 percent of the annual amount.

That it is built as interest matters. The longer the prepaid stretch, the larger the deduction, so January is the largest and it shrinks from there. And because the rate sits in a decree, the discount changes every year. If you remember it being 10 percent, today's figure is a long way from that.

These are the 2026 tax year figures published by Sejong City on 29 December 2025 in its notice on the vehicle tax prepayment scheme, confirmed on 16 August 2026. They do not carry over to other years.

  • The January deadline is nominally 31 January, but where that falls on a holiday some local governments extend into early February
  • Vehicles that prepaid the previous year often receive a payment slip without a fresh application
  • Selling or scrapping after prepaying triggers a settlement of the remaining months
  • Applying without paying within the window means prepayment does not take effect and the regular schedule resumes
  • Confirm the current year rate with the local notice or Wetax immediately before filing

8. What happens if it goes unpaid

Missing the deadline brings trouble on two fronts: money, and losing the use of the car.

Money first. Article 55 of the Framework Act on Local Taxes imposes a late payment penalty of 3 percent of the unpaid amount. Where the arrears are 450,000 won or more, a further 0.66 percent accrues for each month that passes, and this monthly component does not run beyond 60 months.

  • The 3 percent is a fixed component that attaches even one day late
  • The 0.66 percent accrues monthly only where arrears reach 450,000 won
  • The monthly component caps at 60 months and stops growing after that

Then the car. Article 131 of the Local Tax Act is titled seizure of vehicle registration plates. The head of the city, county or district may decline to issue a vehicle registration certificate or request seizure of the registration plates from an owner in arrears. Article 70 of the Local Tax Collection Act likewise allows the head of a local government to seize the plates of a vehicle in arrears.

In practice a first instance of arrears brings seizure after notice, while vehicles with two or more instances are seized without prior notice. A car without plates cannot be driven. Article 131(2) does provide an exception: where seizure would make it difficult for the taxpayer to maintain a livelihood, the plates may be returned or the seizure temporarily lifted. Anyone who believes this applies should raise it with the local government and follow the procedure.

Accumulated arrears can escalate to attachment of deposits or wages. If payment is difficult, the better move is to contact the local tax office about instalments or a deferral rather than let it sit. The counter is open, and the penalty keeps accruing during the silence.

9. When ownership changes: sales and pro-rata calculation

The most common misunderstanding in a sale is assuming the tax transfers the day the contract is signed.

Article 128 of the Local Tax Act provides that where a vehicle is transferred or deregistered, the tax may be calculated on a daily pro-rata basis by reference to the registration date. In practice, for a used car sale the transferor and the transferee are each assessed a pro-rata amount from the date of transfer — the transferor through an occasional assessment, the transferee through either an occasional or a regular one.

Everything turns on the registration date.

  • Even after handing over the car and receiving payment, until transfer of registration completes the taxpayer for that period is still the previous owner
  • So the seller should verify completion directly on the vehicle registration record
  • The buyer bears only the period from the registration date — not the whole June or December notice
  • Scrapping and export deregistration follow the same logic, covering the period to the deregistration date
  • Where the year was already prepaid, the remaining months become a settlement item
  • Newly registered vehicles are likewise billed pro-rata from registration to the end of that period

Sellers usually lose out when the title transfer drags. Hand a car to a dealer and, if the registration stays in your name until it is resold, the tax for that stretch comes to you. Fix a transfer deadline in the contract and check it yourself once the date passes. The wider sale process is in the used car buying guide, and total cost of ownership in buying versus leasing.

10. What could not be confirmed

These are the points left without a stated figure. A number without a foundation is worse than a gap.

  • Whether and when the tax base changes — a shift from displacement to vehicle value or carbon emissions is discussed, but no enacted amendment or decree setting a start date or rates was found. As of 16 August 2026, Article 127 of the Local Tax Act still runs on displacement
  • Variation in deadlines between local governments — several published January 2026 prepayment deadlines falling in early February, on differing dates. Whether that reflects a single nationwide rule or individual notices is unresolved
  • Conditions and limits of specific exemptions — reliefs for persons with disabilities, veterans, multi-child households and others sit in the Restriction of Special Local Taxation Act, with finely divided conditions. They are not covered here
  • Rounding and truncation on the notice — the exact handling behind small gaps between calculation and billed amount could not be confirmed

These belong to the local government or Wetax guidance. The body that determines tax amounts and exemption eligibility in any individual case is the competent local government, and this article does not stand in for that determination.

11. Frequently asked questions

Why is vehicle tax billed twice, in June and December?

The year is split into halves and each is assessed separately. Article 128(1) of the Local Tax Act sets 16 to 30 June and 16 to 31 December. Where the annual amount is 100,000 won or less the full year may be collected with the first period, so some owners see only a June notice.

Why is the tax on an EV so low?

The calculation multiplies displacement and there is none to multiply. Article 127(1) treats such vehicles as other passenger vehicles at a flat 100,000 won a year private and 20,000 won commercial. Price and output are not inputs.

When is prepayment most advantageous?

January, since the deduction grows with the months prepaid. For 2026 the published figures were roughly 4.57 percent in January, 3.76 in March, 2.52 in June and 1.25 in September. The rate follows a decree and changes annually, so confirm the current year before filing.

Does an older car keep getting cheaper?

It falls 5 percentage points a year from year three, reaches 50 percent at year twelve, and stays there. The formula is in Article 127(2), and it applies to private-use passenger vehicles. Age runs from first registration, not from purchase.

If I sell mid-year, who pays?

Each side pays for the months owned. The test is the date transfer of registration completed, not the contract date. If the change is delayed the tax for that stretch stays with the previous owner, so confirming completion on the registration record is the safe step.

12. In closing: three things make the notice readable

Vehicle tax looks intricate but runs on few variables. Displacement builds the base, age cuts it, prepayment cuts it once more. Add the 30 percent education tax and the notice is broadly predictable before it arrives.

The practical checklist is equally short. Whether to prepay in January or stay on the regular schedule, whether the June and December deadlines get missed, and whether transfer of registration actually completed when the car was sold. The last item causes the most trouble.

Readers outside Korea should treat none of the above as portable. Other jurisdictions levy their own annual charges on vehicle ownership under different names and different bases — Japan assesses a prefectural Category-Based Vehicle Tax on the owner as at 1 April, and China levies a vehicle and vessel tax collected locally — and their rates, deadlines and reliefs are set entirely by their own law.
