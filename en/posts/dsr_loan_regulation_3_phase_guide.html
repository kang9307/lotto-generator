🎯 What Is Stress DSR?

Stress DSR is a system that adds a certain level of additional interest rate (called the "stress rate") when calculating DSR for borrowers with variable-rate loans. This accounts for the possibility that their repayment burden may increase due to rising interest rates, thereby adjusting the loan limit accordingly. It does not affect the actual loan interest rate.

+1.5%

  • Acts as an "automatic brake" during rate-cut cycles

🧮 How Stress DSR Is Calculated

📐 Stress DSR Calculation Formula

Stress DSR = DSR calculated at (current rate + stress rate)

  • Stress Rate: Currently 1.5% under Phase 3 (0.75% for provincial mortgage loans)
  • Application Method: Stress rate is added only to variable-rate loans
  • Purpose: Proactively prevent the risk of increased repayment burden when rates rise

💡 Stress DSR Calculation Example

Example: Annual income 60,000,000 KRW, applying for 400,000,000 KRW mortgage at 3% variable rate
• Standard DSR: Calculated at 3% current rate → Monthly payment: 1,690,000 KRW
• Stress DSR: Calculated at 3% + 1.5% = 4.5% → Monthly payment: 2,030,000 KRW
• Stress DSR = (2,030,000 KRW x 12 months / 60,000,000 KRW) x 100 = 40.6%
• Result: Exceeds 40% DSR limit, so loan amount adjustment is required

🏠 Impact on the Real Estate Market

1️⃣ Stricter Loan Approval Standards

  • Reduced loan-to-income limits: Average 20-30% decrease compared to before
  • Harder for multi-borrowers: Disadvantageous for those with personal and credit card loans
  • High earners also affected: The 40% cap applies regardless of income level

2️⃣ Market Changes

  • Reduced purchasing power: Lower effective home purchase price range
  • Jeonse (key-money deposit) market activation: Increased preference for jeonse over purchases
  • Loan product diversification: Financial institutions developing new products
