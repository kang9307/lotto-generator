Overview: What Are Korea's Four Major Social Insurances?

Korea operates a mandatory social safety net known as the Four Major Social Insurances (4대사회보험). These are:

  • National Pension (국민연금) — retirement, disability, and survivor income
  • National Health Insurance (건강보험) — medical cost coverage
  • Employment Insurance (고용보험) — unemployment benefits and job training
  • Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance (산재보험) — work-related injury and disease coverage

Any workplace with one or more employees is required to enroll all workers in all four insurances. Foreign employees working in Korea are also generally subject to the same obligations, with some exceptions based on visa type and bilateral agreements.

1. National Pension (국민연금)

1.1 Who Must Enroll

All Korean residents aged 18–59 must enroll in the National Pension. Workplace subscribers include all employees and employers in businesses with one or more employees. Self-employed individuals enroll as local subscribers. Foreign nationals working in Korea are generally required to enroll, with exceptions for certain nationalities (where reciprocal exemption agreements exist) and specific visa categories (e.g., cultural arts, industrial trainee, general training).

1.2 2026 Premium Rate — Pension Reform

Effective 2026, the National Pension contribution rate increased from 9% to 9.5% as part of a pension reform package (parametric reform). The rate will rise by 0.5 percentage points each year until it reaches 13% in 2033.

  • Employee contribution: 4.75%
  • Employer contribution: 4.75%
  • Standard monthly income ceiling: KRW 6.37 million (approx. USD 4,650, adjusted annually)
  • Standard monthly income floor: KRW 400,000

1.3 Benefits

  • Old-age pension: Monthly payments from age 63 (gradually rising to 65) after a minimum 10-year contribution period — paid for life
  • Disability pension: For qualifying disabilities that arise while insured
  • Survivor pension: Paid to spouse, children, or dependents upon the death of an insured person
  • Lump-sum refund: If eligibility requirements are not met, accumulated contributions are returned with interest

2. National Health Insurance (건강보험)

2.1 Who Must Enroll

All residents in Korea — including foreign nationals — are required to enroll in the National Health Insurance (NHI). Employees in workplaces with one or more workers are automatically enrolled as workplace subscribers. Foreign employees must be reported by their employer within 14 days of hiring.

2.2 2026 Premium Rates

The 2026 workplace subscriber rate is 7.19% of standard monthly remuneration (up from 7.09% in 2025).

  • Employee: 3.595%
  • Employer: 3.595%
  • Long-term Care Insurance (노인장기요양보험): 13.14% of the Health Insurance premium, split 50/50

What is Long-term Care Insurance?

Long-term Care Insurance provides in-home or facility-based care services for elderly individuals with dementia, stroke, or other conditions that impair daily living. It is collected alongside Health Insurance premiums by the NHIS and is proportional to your health insurance premium amount.

2.3 Benefits

  • Medical care benefits: Reduced co-payment at hospitals, clinics, and pharmacies
  • Health checkups: Free regular checkups for subscribers and dependents
  • Pregnancy/birth support: Medical expenses covered via the National Happy Card program
  • Long-term care benefits: Home-based or facility services after eligibility assessment

3. Employment Insurance (고용보험)

3.1 Who Must Enroll

Employment Insurance is mandatory for employees in workplaces with one or more workers. From 2026, coverage is progressively expanding to platform workers, freelancers, and short-hour workers. Foreign nationals with E-9 (non-professional employment) or H-2 (visit employment) visas are enrolled as insured persons, but must separately apply for unemployment benefit coverage.

3.3 Benefits

  • Job-seeker allowance (unemployment benefits): Up to 270 days at 60% of prior average wage; requires at least 180 insured days in the prior 18 months and involuntary separation
  • Early re-employment bonus: A portion of remaining UI payments if rehired quickly
  • Parental leave benefits: A percentage of usual salary paid during parental leave
  • Vocational training subsidies: Training costs covered for both employers and workers
  • Employment retention subsidies: Payroll support for employers maintaining headcount during downturns

4. Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance (산재보험)

4.1 Who Must Enroll

All businesses using one or more workers under the Labor Standards Act are mandatorily covered. The employer pays 100% of the premium — employees pay nothing. Foreign workers are subject to the same coverage. Coverage includes work-related accidents, occupational diseases, and commuting accidents (since 2018).

4.2 2026 Premium Rates

The 2026 average Industrial Accident Insurance rate is 1.47% of the total payroll — unchanged from 2025 (Ministry of Employment and Labor Notice).

  • Employer pays 100% (no employee contribution)
  • Rates vary across 28 business categories based on industry risk statistics
  • Commuting accident rate: 0.06 per mille (all industries)
  • Wage claim guarantee levy: 0.06 per mille (businesses with 20+ employees)

4.3 Benefits

  • Medical care benefits: Full treatment cost for work-related injuries or diseases (after approval)
  • Temporary disability benefits: 70% of average wage for days unable to work during treatment
  • Disability benefits: Annuity or lump-sum payment based on disability grade after treatment
  • Survivor benefits: Annuity of 52–67% of average wage, or lump-sum, for families of deceased workers
  • Nursing care benefits: When nursing care is required after treatment
  • Vocational rehabilitation: Return-to-work support and retraining for injured workers

5. Enrollment and Reporting Procedures

5.1 Enrollment (Acquisition) and Termination (Loss) Reporting

Employers must file enrollment (acquisition) and termination (loss) reports within 14 days of hire or separation. All four insurances can be reported together through the Four Major Social Insurance Information Integration Center (www.4insure.or.kr) or the Health Insurance EDI system. Late filings may incur penalties.

5.2 Annual Premium Reconciliation

Each March, employers submit a report of prior-year total remuneration to reconcile Health Insurance and National Pension premiums. Salary changes during the year should be reported separately via a remuneration change notification.

5.3 Notes for Foreign Workers

Check the applicable visa type before enrolling a foreign employee. Key variations:

  • National Pension: Exemptions apply for nationals of countries without bilateral totalization agreements, or holders of specific visa types
  • Health Insurance: Mandatory for nearly all foreign employees; report within 14 days
  • Employment Insurance: Coverage scope depends on visa type; E-9 and H-2 holders require a separate application for UI benefit coverage
  • Industrial Accident: Mandatory for all workers regardless of nationality

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. If my salary is very low, do I still have to pay into the four insurances?

Yes. Any employee at a workplace with one or more workers is subject to mandatory enrollment regardless of wage level. For National Pension, a minimum standard monthly income floor of KRW 400,000 applies, so contributions are calculated on at least that amount.

Q2. Are part-time or daily workers also covered?

Daily workers are automatically covered by Industrial Accident Insurance. Employment Insurance applies if the worker is employed for 1 month or more and works at least 60 hours/month. Health Insurance and National Pension apply for employment of 1 month or more; otherwise, the worker may fall under local subscriber status.

Q3. How do I apply for unemployment benefits?

File a job-seeker registration at your local Employment Welfare Plus Center (고용복지플러스센터) or online through WorkNet (www.work.go.kr). You must have been involuntarily separated, have at least 180 insured days in the prior 18 months, and actively seek work. Voluntary resignation generally disqualifies you unless you meet specific exceptions (contract expiration, involuntary relocation, etc.).

Q4. Where can I calculate my exact four-insurance premiums?

Use the free calculators at the Four Major Social Insurance Information Integration Center (www.4insure.or.kr) or NodongOK (www.nodong.kr) for a quick estimate.

Q5. Does filing an industrial accident claim raise the company's premium rate?

Yes, potentially. Businesses with 30 or more employees are subject to an experience rating system where their actual accident history adjusts the premium rate by up to ±50%. Smaller businesses are not individually rated.
