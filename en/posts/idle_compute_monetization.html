The Bill the Word Idle Leaves Out - Costing Spare Compute Before You Rent It Out

Power, cooling, wear, contract terms, the security boundary and the filing duty, gathered into one sum that measures total cost rather than net gain

2026-09-10 Tech & IT

It is true that the machine sitting at home spends most of the day doing nothing at all. It is equally natural that someone would propose paying for those hours. The arithmetic looks simple from a distance. The machine is on anyway, so whatever comes in is what stays.

There are line items that fall out of that arithmetic entirely. They are not small items either. In some households their sum is larger than the money coming in.

What follows is not an introduction to that kind of service. It is an attempt to lay out, as line items, what the phrase 「spare resources」 is quietly covering up. There is one axis running through the whole thing: count total cost, not net gain.

This is general information rather than legal or tax advice. Electricity rates and tax rules are quoted as of the time of writing and some of the components are adjusted periodically, so an actual decision should rest on the published figures at the time of billing and on guidance from the relevant authority. No particular operator or platform is named here, and no earnings figure is offered. What can be offered is the frame of the calculation. The Korean rules described below apply to Korean residents; under a different jurisdiction the requirements will be different, and the local equivalents are what should be read.

The trigger, and the limits of what the source actually said

In September 2026 a technology magazine piece on gathering spare compute from personal machines and pointing it at artificial intelligence inference made its way into Korean forums. Because scope tends to widen every time a piece passes through a summary, it is worth narrowing back to what the article itself put on the page.

Here is what it states.

  • The target is inference. The piece says in its own words that training new frontier models is likely to remain the province of the very large operators
  • The models that fit are smaller open models fine-tuned for particular business tasks. They do not come anywhere near the resources demanded by state-of-the-art frontier models
  • One operator claims a latency of 100 milliseconds or less on its platform. That is the operator's own claim, and the measurement conditions behind it are not printed in the article
  • From another operator comes the remark that losing 100 nodes out of a network of 250,000 would not matter. It is an illustrative statement about the property of the structure, not a description of an actual deployment
  • For hardware it names the small racks that hobbyists keep in basements and garages, gaming PCs, and laptops that do not get much use
  • No payment amount and no payout structure appear in the article. It goes as far as saying that spare compute can be put to use and passive income earned in the process
  • It cites a volunteer-based distributed computing project that ran from 1999 to 2020 as the precedent
  • Security gets a fair amount of space. Least privilege, an isolated workload, authenticated and encrypted communication, segmented work with only the minimum information exposed, open-sourced scheduling software, and the host's ability to pause the node, revoke access and remove the software at any time

What the article does not cover matters just as much. Electricity cost, hardware wear, the host's own tax obligations and participant uptime are simply not in it. The word taxes appears once, in the context of large datacenters returning little to the communities that host them, and that sentence has nothing to do with a participant's filing duty.

Put plainly, what the article contains is a description of the structure and a set of claims from the operator side. What it does not contain is the participant's own profit-and-loss sum. Building that sum is the work below.

What the word idle hides

The phrase 「the machine is on anyway」 rests on an assumption that power consumption has two states, on and off. Real machines do not behave that way.

Modern processors and graphics cards change clock and voltage according to load. With nothing to do they drop into a low-power state; when work arrives they climb. Which means the difference in power draw between idle and load turns directly into a difference on the bill. What is being sold is not 「the hours it spends doing nothing」. It is the act of converting hours spent in a low-power state into hours spent at high load.

StateWhat it is doingPowerHeat and noiseWear
OffNothing at allNear zeroNoneNone
Idle (powered on)Waiting. Clock and voltage are downLowLowMostly fans and storage
Under loadProcessing somebody else's workHighHighAcross the components

The expression 「selling idle resources」 sounds like an offer to sell the second row. What actually happens is that the second row turns into the third. The number that belongs in the sum is not the absolute value of the third row but the difference between the two rows.

That difference varies a great deal from machine to machine, which is why a figure somebody else calculated cannot simply be adopted. The only way is to measure your own. Put a power meter between the wall socket and the machine, run an hour idle and an hour under load, and two numbers come out. The rated wattage on the spec sheet and the capacity of the power supply are neither of them that number. The first is usually a ceiling, and the second is not a consumption figure at all but a supply capability.

Where the same kilowatt-hour becomes two different numbers

This is where the Korean residential tariff structure enters decisively, and the section is Korea-specific from here on. The reason it matters is that knowing how many extra kilowatt-hours were used in a month is not, on its own, enough to produce a charge.

The residential low-voltage schedule published by Korea Electric Power Corporation is a three-tier progressive structure. The figures below are those effective from 9 November 2023.

Consumption above 1,000 kWh in summer and winter is charged at 736.2 won per kWh. High-voltage residential supply uses the same tier structure at lower rates. And the table is not the whole bill. A climate-environment charge and a fuel-cost adjustment are applied per kWh on top of it, with taxes and levies above those again. The unit prices of those two are adjusted periodically, so the figures published at the time of billing are the ones to read off the schedule.

What has to be read out of this table is not the tier rates themselves but where the added consumption lands. Under a progressive tariff, newly added usage sits not in the lowest tier but in the highest tier the household has already reached. A household already inside tier three that adds 72 kWh in a month has all 72 of those kilowatt-hours priced at 307.3 won; a household that finishes inside tier one pays 120.0 won for exactly the same 72 kWh. The same electricity differs by more than a factor of two and a half.

There is one more step. If the added consumption pushes the household across a tier boundary, it is not only the amount above the line that becomes expensive. The fixed monthly charge moves with it. The 910 won of tier one becomes 7,300 won in tier three, so in the month the boundary is crossed a further 6,390 won attaches on that basis alone.

In other words, which tier your household currently sits in decides the outcome before the hourly rate ever gets a chance to. Run the identical workload under identical conditions and a summer in a four-person home and a spring in a one-person flat produce different sums. The procedure for working an electricity bill has already been set out in the distance from rated wattage to your bill and in the progressive tariff and summer electricity, so it is not repeated here.

Most of the rows in this table occur whether or not any payment arrives. In a month when little work comes in and the payout is small, the power, the depreciation and the exposure are all still there. Revenue varies and cost stays close to fixed, and that asymmetry is a property of the structure rather than an accident of a bad month.

Heat and noise, and the condition that the heat is handled indoors

The decisive difference between a datacenter and a home is where the heat is dealt with. Nearly all the electricity a machine consumes comes back out as heat. In a datacenter that heat is pushed out of the building by dedicated plant, and the cost of doing so sits in the operator's cost base. At home it comes out into a room with a person in it.

In summer this gets counted twice. If the machine draws an extra 100 W, an extra 100 W of heat enters the room, and the cooling unit spends further electricity pushing it back out. If that unit has a coefficient of performance of 3, the additional cooling power is roughly a third of the load. So the summer increment is not the machine's extra draw but something in the region of 1.3 times that increment, and it lands, of all places, in the summer tier three. Summer is both the season of heavy usage and the season of high marginal rates.

In winter the direction reverses and the machine's heat substitutes for part of the heating. Whether that is a gain depends entirely on what the heating was running on. If it was electric, the substitution is real; if it was piped gas, what has happened is a switch to a heat source with a different price per unit of energy. And in spring and autumn, when no heating is called for, the item simply vanishes from the sum.

Noise is the first thing to drop out, because it does not convert into money. But a machine whose fans run at high speed around the clock, sitting in a bedroom or a studio flat, is a real cost. Moving it to another room takes cabling; soundproofing takes money. Not being expressible in currency and not being a cost are two different statements. This is the item most likely to become decisive in a small flat.

Wear and life - remaining lifespan is an asset you spend from now on

Hardware wears by use, not by calendar. The belief that a machine already owned carries no depreciation is the most common error in this particular sum. The money already spent cannot be recovered, but the remaining lifespan is an asset that gets spent from now on.

Setting up hourly depreciation is straightforward. Take the purchase price less the expected residual value and divide by the expected remaining service hours. Continuous operation changes the rate at which those hours are consumed. Take a machine used two hours a day and run it twenty hours a day, and one calendar year now delivers what used to take ten. Framed that way, depreciation stops being an accident and becomes a scheduled expense.

Different components wear in different ways.

  • Fans and pumps are rotating parts. Bearing life scales with running hours
  • Thermal interface material dries out as it cycles through heating and cooling
  • A power supply contains electrolytic capacitors that are sensitive to temperature
  • Storage, solid-state drives in particular, has a ceiling on total bytes written, and that figure is on the datasheet
  • Graphics memory and the card's voltage regulators run hottest of anything when load is sustained

No numbers are printed here because the spread between components is far too wide for a generic figure to help. The life figures for the parts you own are in the manufacturer's own documentation. What gets in the way when hardware past its original purpose is put back to work is set out separately in the rest of the bill on cheap hardware.

Warranty is a line item too. Whether continuous paid operation falls inside the warranty conditions of a consumer product differs from product to product. That is something to establish from the warranty document and the manufacturer's warranty terms.

Terms - what the line, the electricity and the tenancy each decide

These are three separate contracts and each has to be checked on its own. One of them being fine says nothing about the other two.

The broadband line. Whether a consumer plan's terms of service restrict commercial use or resale differs by provider and by plan. The contract and the terms themselves are where that gets settled. Three things are worth locating: whether a clause on commercial use exists, what conduct it points at if it does, and what the consequence of breach is. Alongside them, look at the fair-use policy and the upload bandwidth. Consumer lines are commonly engineered with a narrower upstream than downstream, and the direction being sold in this structure is chiefly the upstream.

The electricity contract. Residential supply is a contract class applied to residential customers. Whether doing paid work on a machine inside a home affects that classification is not something the tariff schedule settles on its own. It is a question to put to the utility.

The tenancy. A lease and a building's management rules may carry clauses on noise, electricity use and the installation of equipment. A tenant reads the lease; a resident of a managed building reads the rules.

If any one of the three is breached, a loss can arrive in the form of termination or a penalty regardless of how much was earned. These are not large line items in expectation, but they have the property that if they occur they can exceed the sum of everything else, which argues for putting them at the front of the calculation rather than the back.

Admitting an untrusted executor inside your own network

This is the core of the structure.

Running somebody else's work on your machine means, technically, that code you do not control is executing inside your network perimeter. Home firewalls and routers are generally built to stop things coming in from outside. In this structure the executor starts out on the inside. A design that locks the door does nothing at all about a guest who is already in the room.

Counting what is on the inside gives a sense of the scale. The router's administration page, the storage box and the files on it, the printer, cameras, a smart-home hub, shared folders on other PCs, and the phones attached to the same wireless network. Home devices are largely configured on the assumption that anything on the same network can be trusted. Admin pages that open without authentication and devices still carrying their factory password are common. That assumption was resting on the premise that no untrusted executor would ever be on the network.

And one more thing. Outbound traffic leaves through a line in your name and out of your public address. The first-instance record of what happened at that address attaches to you. However thoroughly the operator segments workloads and applies least privilege, that is control the operator exercises over its own system, and it does not change whose name is on the line.

The controls the source article lists — least privilege, an isolated workload, authentication and encryption, minimum information exposure, an open scheduler, and the ability to pause, revoke and delete at any time — are meaningful. But they are controls provided by the operator. The boundary that has to be built on your side is a separate piece of work, and the responsibility for building it rests with you.

There is a separate list of things no layer here touches.

  • That heat is coming out
  • That parts are wearing

The principle compresses into one line. Isolation is not a substitute for trust; it is a device for narrowing the range of what has to be trusted. How far it has to be narrowed depends on what is attached to that machine and what else is on that network. The concrete procedure for splitting a home network is in home router security, and the idea of a trust boundary itself is set out in why the client cannot be trusted.

The other direction - when you are the buyer, where does your data sit

Look at the same structure from the other side and the boundary flips. If you become the one renting somebody else's idle machine cheaply to run your own work, then this time your data is loaded into the memory of a machine you know nothing about.

Listing what actually goes across makes the judgement easier. Input data, prompts, model weights, outputs, and job metadata. If any one of those contains something that must not leave, a price difference stops being a basis for a decision.

Three states have to be separated.

  • In transit — is it encrypted. This is largely a solved problem, and the source article names authenticated and encrypted communication
  • At rest — do job files remain on that machine's disk, are they deleted afterwards, and what tells you that they were
  • In use — during computation the data sits in memory in the clear. In an environment where the machine's owner holds administrative rights on that machine, there is no general way to prevent that. Hardware-level confidential computing aims squarely at this problem, but using it requires both the hardware and the software stack to support it

The third is the heart of this direction. If the seller's problem was 「someone else's code is running on my machine」, the buyer's problem of the same magnitude is 「my data is sitting in someone else's memory」. The criteria for deciding what may go into external computation at all are set out in judging what company data may go into an external model.

Which workloads fit this structure

The article's restriction of scope to inference is not modesty. There is a structural reason for it.

Training requires the nodes to talk to one another. Gradients are exchanged and state is brought into line. That traffic is sensitive to bandwidth and to latency, and one slow node ties the whole run to its pace. Machines connected over consumer broadband and scattered geographically contradict that condition head on. That property is exactly why the article leaves frontier-model training with the large datacenters.

Inference is generally the case where each request stands alone. The nodes have no need to talk. When one node disappears, that one request is simply reissued to another. The remark about losing 100 out of 250,000 points precisely at this property.

AxisThe side that fitsThe side that does not
Inter-node communicationNone. Requests are independent of each otherSynchronisation is required
Latency requirementSeconds are acceptableA millisecond ceiling applies
MemoryFits on one card or one machineNeeds several cards stitched together
StateNothing persists between requestsA long session runs on
Data sensitivityData that may be exposedData that must not leave
Interruption toleranceA node dropping out is absorbed by a retryInterruption is failure

The 100 milliseconds or less that one operator advertises should be read inside this table too. It is a target the platform has set for itself, and whether any individual node actually lands inside it depends on where that machine sits, how wide the line's upstream is, and what else is loading that machine at the moment. As noted above, the direction being sold here is chiefly the upstream, and consumer plans commonly engineer that direction narrow. The tighter the latency condition on a workload, the more whether your line meets that condition is settled before the question of profit ever comes up.

What kind of income is the payment

From here the discussion follows Korean rules and applies to Korean residents. Elsewhere the categories and thresholds will be different, and the local tax authority's guidance is what should be read.

What is received is income. The National Tax Service describes comprehensive income tax as a structure that aggregates interest, dividend, business, employment, pension and other income, and the final return is filed between 1 May and 31 May each year.

The live question is whether the payment is business income or other income. The usual axis is whether the activity is continuous and repeated. Trying it once and stopping is a different thing from adding equipment and running it around the clock. Which side an individual case falls on is for the tax authority's advisory service and a qualified tax professional to determine.

Other income comes with one option attached. Where other income has been withheld at source and the annual total of such income amounts stays at or below 3 million won, the taxpayer may choose between aggregating it into comprehensive income and letting the withholding settle the liability. That choice, though, presupposes that withholding actually occurred. Receiving payment from an operator abroad with no withholding puts the case on a different footing.

There is one more wrinkle. Some arrangements pay in tokens or points rather than cash. In that case what is measured, when, and at what value becomes an additional question. That too belongs to the domain of tax judgement.

From the point of view of the calculation only one thing matters. It is the after-tax figure, not the gross receipt, that goes into the net sum. The arithmetic only lines up if it is built on the premise that the filing duty is met. The overall shape of Korean comprehensive income tax is covered separately in the guide to filing comprehensive income tax.

A frame you can put your own numbers into

No amount is offered here because none can be. The machines differ, the tariff tiers differ and the tax rates differ. What can be offered is a frame, and the values come out of each reader's own socket, meter reading and income classification.

Working per hour

net per hour
  =  gross received per hour
  −  income tax and local income tax component
  −  (delta_W / 1000) x tier_rate
  −  in summer, (delta_W / 1000) x tier_rate / COP
  −  depreciation per hour
  −  consumables and maintenance per hour

  delta_W        = wattage under load − wattage at idle. Measured at the wall socket
  tier_rate      = the won per kWh of the tier the added usage lands in
  COP            = coefficient of performance of the cooling unit, from its spec sheet
  depreciation   = (purchase price − expected residual) / expected remaining hours

Where each term comes from is worth attaching.

  • Gross per hour — the figure the operator offers. Look at whether it is fixed or proportional to throughput, and at the payout interval and any minimum payout
  • delta_W — has to be a measurement. Neither the rated wattage on the spec sheet nor the power supply capacity is this value
  • tier_rate — decided by which tier this month's consumption already sits in. It changes month to month
  • COP — on the cooling unit's spec sheet. This term only attaches in summer
  • Depreciation per hour — can be built up component by component. For storage, a bytes-written basis fits better
  • Tax rate — depends on the income classification and on the year's other income in total

What flips when the tier changes

Putting two different tier rates into the same frame exposes the property of the structure. Outside the summer season tier one is 120.0 won per kWh and tier three is 307.3 won. With a delta_W of 200 W running twelve hours a day, the added monthly consumption is 72 kWh. On the energy charge alone that is roughly 8,600 won in tier one and roughly 22,100 won in tier three. The other charges and taxes sit on top, and if a boundary was crossed the difference in fixed charge attaches as well.

The gross received is identical in both cases. Only the cost more than doubles. Which is why the same offer leaves something behind in one household and nothing in another. Depreciation, cooling and tax then stack on top of that.

One further property deserves a note. Increase the running hours and the gross generally rises in a straight line, but the energy charge steps up the moment a tier boundary is crossed. Which means there exists a region where running the machine more makes the net per hour worse. That is precisely where the intuition of 「more hours, more profit」 breaks. It also means this is not a calculation that gets done once. It is the sort that has to be redone every metering cycle.

Five lines to read before selling anything

  1. Measure delta_W at the wall socket. One hour idle, one hour under load. Without this number everything else is guesswork
  2. Look at which tier this month's consumption sits in and pick up the rate of the tier the added usage will land in
  3. Find the relevant clauses in the broadband terms of service and in the tenancy. If there are none, then you have established that there are none
  4. Decide how that machine will be separated from the rest of the network. If there is no workable way to separate it, that in itself is the answer
  5. Settle in advance which income category the payment falls under, and compute the after-tax figure on that basis

If a number survives all five lines, then it is worth doing. If one of the items cannot be cleared, that item is larger than the payment. It is normal for this calculation to come out differently for different people. Different answers to the same offer are the exact property of this structure, and that is why importing somebody else's conclusion is the most dangerous method available for this particular judgement. What newly appears when one person runs a single machine to keep an agent resident on it is covered separately in running one server as an individual.

What could not be confirmed

  • The current unit prices of the climate-environment charge and the fuel-cost adjustment — these could not be cross-checked in the body of the tariff schedule. They are adjusted periodically, so the figures published at billing time are the ones to use
  • Whether paid activity affects the residential electricity contract class — the tariff schedule carries no criterion for that. It is a question for the utility
  • Commercial-use restrictions in consumer broadband terms — these differ by provider and by plan and cannot be generalised. The principle of not naming any particular operator applies here as well
  • Clauses on continuous operation in consumer hardware warranty terms — these differ by manufacturer
  • The measurement and aggregation conditions behind the 100 milliseconds and the 250,000 nodes quoted in the source — the article does not state them
  • What participants actually receive and how payouts are structured — absent from the source. It is not supplied here either
  • The detailed criteria dividing business income from other income — determination in an individual case belongs to the tax authority and a qualified tax professional
  • Real-world duty cycle and churn among participating nodes — the source does not cover it
