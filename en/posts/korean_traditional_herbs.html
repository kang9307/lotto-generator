10 Amazing Benefits and Uses of Traditional Korean Herbs

10 Amazing Benefits of Korean Traditional Herbs

2025-06-02 Health

Traditional Korean herbs have been used for thousands of years to promote health and prevent disease. These herbs hold an important place in Korean traditional medicine (known as hanbang), and as modern scientific research continues to validate their benefits, they are gaining attention around the world. This article takes a close look at 10 traditional Korean herbs, their benefits, and how they are used.

The History and Significance of Traditional Korean Herbs

Traditional Korean herbal medicine began to take shape during the Three Kingdoms period and was systematically compiled during the Joseon Dynasty in the famous medical text Donguibogam. The Korean Peninsula's distinct climate and geography provide an ideal environment for a wide variety of medicinal plants, which helped Korea develop its own unique style of herbal medicine.

Korean medicine (hanbang) sees the human body as an organic whole and approaches disease not simply as a matter of symptom removal but as restoring fundamental balance. Based on this philosophy, traditional herbs focus on supporting overall well-being rather than targeting isolated symptoms.

10 Traditional Korean Herbs: Benefits and Features

1. Insam (Ginseng)

Insam (ginseng) is the herb most closely associated with Korea and is recognized worldwide. Its primary active compounds, ginsenosides, offer a variety of health benefits.

Key Benefits

  • Immune support: Enhances immune function by activating T cells and natural killer (NK) cells
  • Fatigue recovery: Improves energy metabolism and stamina
  • Cognitive function: Supports memory and concentration with neuroprotective effects
  • Blood sugar regulation: Improves insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism
  • Antioxidant activity: Protects cells from reactive oxygen species

How to Use

  • Fresh ginseng (susam): Used in decoctions, porridge, and teas
  • Red ginseng (hongsam): Consumed as concentrated extracts, powders, and preserved confections
  • Recommended daily intake: 3-5 g (consultation with a doctor or Korean medicine practitioner is recommended)

2. Hwanggi (Astragalus, Milk Vetch Root)

Hwanggi (astragalus) is a core herb in Korean medicine for replenishing qi (vital energy). It is excellent for immune support and boosting energy.

Key Benefits

  • Immune strengthening: Stimulates white blood cell production and activates immune cells
  • Energy boosting: Helps with fatigue recovery and physical endurance
  • Cardiovascular health: Helps regulate blood pressure and improve vascular function
  • Diuretic action: Relieves swelling and supports kidney function
  • Anti-inflammatory: Reduces chronic inflammation and supports joint health

How to Use

  • Used in medicinal teas, decoctions, and as a soup ingredient
  • Often combined with other herbs in traditional prescriptions
  • Dosage should be adjusted based on constitution and symptoms

3. Danggui (Angelica, "Female Ginseng")

Danggui (angelica) is sometimes called "female ginseng" and is especially valued for its benefits to women's health.

Key Benefits

  • Improves blood circulation: Helps prevent and improve anemia
  • Hormonal balance in women: Eases menstrual cramps and menopausal symptoms
  • Anti-inflammatory: Helps with arthritis and inflammatory conditions
  • Liver protection: Supports detoxification and helps prevent liver damage
  • Immune regulation: Helps maintain immune balance

How to Use

  • Used in decoctions, teas, and herbal liquors (danggui-ju)
  • Commonly used for postpartum recovery and anemia
  • Recommended around menstruation and after childbirth

4. Gugija (Goji Berry, Wolfberry)

Gugija (goji berries) are small, sweet red fruits famous for their eye health and anti-aging benefits.

Key Benefits

  • Eye health: Protects the retina through antioxidants
  • Liver function: Protects liver cells and supports detoxification
  • Anti-aging: Scavenges free radicals and prevents cellular damage
  • Immune boosting: Enhances immune system function
  • Blood sugar regulation: Supports glucose management in people with diabetes

How to Use

  • Eat fresh, or dry and steep into tea
  • Used in liquors (gugija-ju), porridge, and medicinal rice dishes
  • Combined with other herbs in decoctions

5. Omija (Schisandra Berry)

Omija is a unique berry that combines five flavors — sweet, sour, bitter, salty, and pungent — and offers a wide range of benefits.

Key Benefits

  • Liver protection: Prevents liver cell damage and supports detoxification
  • Respiratory health: Eases coughs and improves lung function
  • Stress relief: Acts as an adaptogen, improving stress resilience
  • Cognitive function: Improves memory and concentration
  • Antioxidant: Neutralizes free radicals and slows cellular aging

How to Use

  • Made into tea, liquor (omija-ju), fruit punch, and syrup (omija-cheong)
  • Used in combination prescriptions with other herbs
  • Dried for long-term storage, making it available year-round

6. Sansuyu (Cornus Fruit)

Sansuyu is a red berry known to support kidney and liver health in particular.

Key Benefits

  • Kidney support: Promotes urinary tract health and protects the kidneys
  • Liver protection: Supports liver cell regeneration and detoxification
  • Blood pressure regulation: Helps manage hypertension and supports vascular health
  • Immune enhancement: Anti-inflammatory action and immune support
  • Blood sugar regulation: Helps ease symptoms of diabetes

How to Use

  • Used in teas, liquors, and syrups (sansuyu-cheong)
  • Combined with other herbs in decoctions
  • Dried and ground into powder for use in food

7. Cheongung (Cnidium, Szechuan Lovage Root)

Cheongung is mainly used to improve blood circulation and relieve pain.

Key Benefits

  • Blood circulation: Disperses stagnant blood (eohyeol) and promotes blood flow
  • Headache relief: Eases migraines and tension headaches
  • Reduces inflammation: Helps with arthritis and rheumatic symptoms
  • Women's health: Relieves menstrual pain and regulates menstruation
  • Calming effect: Reduces stress and improves sleep quality

How to Use

  • Used in decoctions, pills, and teas
  • Combined with other circulation-boosting herbs
  • Especially effective for headaches and menstrual pain

8. Baekchul (White Atractylodes Rhizome)

Baekchul is especially effective for improving digestive function and strengthening the spleen.

Key Benefits

  • Digestive support: Improves gastrointestinal function and digestion
  • Spleen strengthening: Enhances nutrient absorption
  • Immune enhancement: Activates immune cells and strengthens defenses
  • Diuretic action: Relieves swelling and balances body fluids
  • Fatigue recovery: Improves energy metabolism and endurance

How to Use

  • Used in decoctions, teas, and pills
  • Especially effective for indigestion and diarrhea
  • Recommended for people with a "damp" constitution or edema

9. Hwangnyeon (Coptis Rhizome, Golden Thread)

Hwangnyeon has strong antimicrobial properties and is known for cooling heat in the body.

Key Benefits

  • Antimicrobial: Inhibits a wide range of bacteria and viruses
  • Fever reduction: Reduces internal heat and inflammation
  • Digestive protection: Eases gastrointestinal conditions and diarrhea
  • Liver protection: Improves liver function and removes toxins
  • Antioxidant: Scavenges free radicals and protects cells

How to Use

  • Used in decoctions, pills, and topical preparations
  • Its strong bitter taste means it is usually combined with other herbs
  • Effective for febrile and infectious conditions

10. Gamcho (Licorice Root)

Gamcho (licorice root) is called the "mother of medicines" for its wide range of effects. It also helps harmonize the actions of other herbs in a formula.

Key Benefits

  • Anti-inflammatory: Reduces inflammation through cortisol-like effects
  • Gastrointestinal protection: Helps prevent and treat gastric ulcers
  • Respiratory health: Eases coughs and helps clear phlegm
  • Detoxification: Removes toxins and protects the liver
  • Harmonizing agent: Enhances the effects of other herbs and reduces side effects

How to Use

  • Included in nearly every kind of Korean herbal preparation — teas, decoctions, and pills
  • Used alone, but most often combined with other herbs
  • Excessive intake can raise blood pressure, so use with caution

Modern Applications of Traditional Korean Herbs

Development as Health Functional Foods

As science validates the benefits of traditional herbs, many are now developed into health functional foods. Ginseng, red ginseng, and schisandra are processed into extracts, powders, capsules, and other forms for everyday wellness.

Use in Cosmetics

The antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-aging properties of Korean traditional herbs have attracted the cosmetics industry. Products featuring extracts of ginseng, angelica, and licorice root support skin health and beauty.

Use in the Food Industry

Traditional herbs are also widely used in health drinks, teas, liquors, and seasonings. Fermented beverages and medicinal teas made with these herbs are especially popular as functional health drinks.

Precautions When Using Traditional Korean Herbs

Consider Individual Constitution and Health Conditions

Korean medicine tailors herbal use to an individual's constitution and current condition. Using herbs that don't suit your constitution or situation can cause adverse effects, so it's best to consult a qualified practitioner.

Drug Interactions

Some herbs can interact with modern medications. For example, licorice root may interact with blood pressure medications, and ginseng may interact with anticoagulants. If you are taking medications, always consult your doctor or Korean medicine practitioner.

Check Quality and Safety

For safe and effective use, buy from trusted sources. Choose organic herbs free from pesticides or heavy metal contamination and, whenever possible, use certified quality products.

Conclusion

Traditional Korean herbs are a natural gift drawn from thousands of years of experience and wisdom. Modern science continues to validate their benefits, and they are gaining global attention. Yet their true value lies not in quick symptom relief but in a holistic approach that restores balance and harmony in the body.

Used thoughtfully alongside healthy lifestyle habits, Korean traditional herbs can be a powerful ally in promoting health and preventing illness. Because herbs are still medicines, however, it's essential to seek expert advice and choose the ones that suit your constitution and condition.

We hope the wisdom of Korea's precious herbal heritage continues to contribute to healthier lives around the world.
