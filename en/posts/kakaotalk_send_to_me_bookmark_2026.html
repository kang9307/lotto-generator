Turn KakaoTalk's Note to Self Into a Bookmark System - A Link and Memo Life Hack 2026

The fastest personal drawer you already have installed

Jun 8, 2026 Lifestyle

Introduction: You Save the Links, but You Never Find Them Again

You stumble on a YouTube video you really want to watch later. You spot a great article on your morning commute. A friend sends you a link to a restaurant. And then, when you finally have time, where did all those links go? Browser bookmarks are buried too deep to ever reopen, the note app you installed sits untouched, and screenshots vanish somewhere in your camera roll. Saving something and never finding it again is one of the quiet, everyday frustrations of digital life.

But if you live in Korea, there is one app you open dozens of times a day: KakaoTalk. And inside it there is a quiet little space called the Note to Self chat. This article is about a life hack: using that "send to me" (Note to Self) feature as a lightweight bookmark system. We will look at how to use it well, how far it goes, what to use to cover its gaps, and how it compares with dedicated tools like Notion and Pocket. The short version: it is not a perfect organizer, but as the lowest-friction parking spot you will ever have, it is almost unbeatable.

1. What Is the Note to Self Chat?

The Note to Self chat is exactly what it sounds like: a chat room where you talk to yourself. Around 2020 KakaoTalk split this into a proper menu item, reachable directly from your own profile at the top of the Friends list. Every message, photo, video, file and link you send here piles up in a private space that only you can see.

The key point is that this room is completely invisible to anyone else. There are no read receipts, no pressure to reply - it is simply a notepad and a storage box you throw things into for yourself. The action people casually call "sending to me" - tapping the share button in another app, choosing KakaoTalk, then picking the Note to Self chat - is just the act of sending content into this room.

The best tool is not the one you have to learn, but the one already in your hand. Note to Self is powerful not because it has flashy features, but because you already open it every single day.

2. Why It Works So Well as a Bookmark - Three Reasons

There are countless dedicated bookmark apps, so why bother with a messenger? It comes down to three things.

2.1 Access anywhere - real-time desktop and mobile sync

KakaoTalk syncs your phone and PC perfectly in real time. The link you toss in from your phone on the subway opens instantly in the desktop app once you sit down at your desk. No separate login, no sync setup. The fact that the "device you saved from" and the "device you read on" can be different without you thinking about it is a bigger convenience than it sounds.

2.2 Immediacy - almost no friction

Almost every app and web page has a share button, and on those menus KakaoTalk is nearly always near the top. Two or three taps send anything to your Note to Self. Compared with opening a note app, choosing a title and pasting, the friction is dramatically lower. A saving tool you skip when you feel lazy is useless; KakaoTalk is already open, so there is no room to feel lazy.

2.3 Automatic previews - links turn into cards

When you send a link to yourself, KakaoTalk automatically pulls in the page's title and thumbnail and shows it as a card. Instead of a bare URL, you get an "oh, that was that video" recognition at a glance. That means you can scroll and skim what you saved very quickly later. This automatic preview effectively does the job of a bookmark thumbnail.

3. Use Case 1: Saving Links - Your Own Digital Scrapbook

The most basic use is collecting links. An interesting article, a video to rewatch, a product page you want to buy, a half-read blog post - whatever it is, share button to KakaoTalk to Note to Self, and you are done.

One small habit makes it far stronger. Instead of dropping the link alone, add a one-line memo with it. For example, after a video link, write "recipe to try this weekend"; later, when you search "recipe", it surfaces immediately. The link card's title alone does not always match a search, so plant the keywords you will later recall yourself.

The links you gather can be viewed together under Talk Drawer to Links from the chat menu, so it functions like a personal scrapbook tailored to you.

4. Use Case 2: Memos and Quick Capture - Replacing Your Note App

The second use is the pure notepad. A sudden idea, an account number to jot down, a parcel tracking number, a Wi-Fi password, a parking spot - instead of opening your phone's note app, send a single line to your Note to Self and be done.

Where it really shines is moving text between devices. When you want to move a long block of text copied on your phone to your PC, you do not need email or a cloud detour - one "send to me" lets you paste it straight into the desktop app. The reverse works just the same. It is, in effect, the fastest personal clipboard bridge you have.

  • Quick capture: a great queue to dump "things to sort out later" into.
  • Checklists: send shopping items one line at a time and mentally cross off what is done.
  • Quotes and lines: a personal collection for sentences you liked in books or articles.

5. Use Case 3: Photos and Files - Like a Personal Cloud

Note to Self is not just for text. You can send photos and videos as well as document files like docx, pdf and ppt, so it works like a lightweight personal cloud. Drop in photos of your ID or certificates, frequently used template files, or presentation decks, and you can pull them out with just your phone when you are out and in a hurry.

There is one important limit here, though. Media uploaded to ordinary KakaoTalk chats can expire after a certain period and become undownloadable. The Note to Self chat is no exception while you are on the free tier - re-downloading old photos and files is not guaranteed. If long-term storage is your goal, it is safer to also use a paid backup service (see section 6) or a separate cloud. The rule is simple: temporary storage is fine, permanent storage needs care.

6. Use Case 4: Schedules - Straight Into Talk Calendar

A surprisingly little-known feature is scheduling. Try typing a message that contains a date or time into your Note to Self. Write something like "dentist appointment tomorrow at 3 pm" or "project deadline on November 25", and that date-and-time portion automatically becomes link-like and tappable.

Tapping it opens a window to register the event in Talk Calendar, so the act of leaving a memo flows directly into adding a schedule. A natural pattern emerges: dump your to-dos to yourself as messages first, then promote only the ones with a date into Talk Calendar. Note that this auto-recognition can behave differently depending on app version and input format, so if it does not trigger, just add it manually in Talk Calendar.

7. Limit 1: No Organization or Tags

Now let us be honest about the weaknesses. The biggest limit of Note to Self is that it has no structural organization. Because it is fundamentally a chat room that flows in time order, everything you send simply stacks up in one long line - you cannot group items into folders or sort them with tags.

  • No folders: you cannot split items by topic like "work", "hobby", "shopping".
  • No tags: there is no labeling that lets you gather items together later.
  • Edit constraints: a sent message is hard to edit, and tidying up means deleting items one by one.

So the more you save, the more a paradox appears: it is easy to pile things up but increasingly hard to find them again. It matters to accept this limit. Note to Self is not an organizer - it is the parking spot with the widest entrance.

8. Workaround 1: Search - In the End, Keywords Are the Answer

If there are no folders and no tags, what fills that gap is search. Tap the search (magnifier) icon at the top of the chat, type a keyword, and it finds the past messages that contain that word.

This is exactly why the "one-line memo habit" from section 7 is decisive. If you add the keyword you will later search for when you send a link or photo, you get an effect much like tagging. For instance, attach your own marker like "#work" to every work-related item, and you can pull them all up at once by searching "#work". There is no official tag feature, but you can mimic it with "manual tags" - deliberately inserting agreed-upon words.

9. Workaround 2: Talk Drawer - View by Type

The second workaround is the Talk Drawer. Tap the menu (three-line icon) at the top right of the chat and open the Talk Drawer, and it shows the photos, videos, files and links exchanged in that room sorted by type at a glance. It is handy when, within a conversation that flows in time order, you want to see "only the links I sent" or "only the files I saved".

For reference, KakaoTalk also places a separate Drawer menu under the More (...) tab so you can view your KakaoTalk data on the web as well. It is not as precise as folders and tags, but as an "automatic grouping by type" it fills the organization gap to some degree.

10. Workaround 3: Talk Calendar and Paid Backup

The third is the Talk Calendar mentioned earlier, plus paid backup for long-term storage. Talk Calendar acts as a bridge that recognizes dates in your Note to Self and promotes them into events, while reminders and recurrence for the events themselves are handled on the calendar side.

If long-term storage worries you, consider a paid backup service. Kakao runs a subscription service that backs up chat contents and media to the cloud; as of August 2025 the former "Talk Drawer Plus" was reorganized into "Talk Cloud" with a renamed and revised pricing plan. Since old media retention is not guaranteed on the free tier, if you truly need to keep photos and files for a long time, it is safer to run such a paid backup or a separate cloud in parallel.

11. Privacy and Security - What to Put In and What to Keep Out

When a storage spot is this convenient, it is easy to absentmindedly toss in sensitive information too. This is worth pausing on. The Note to Self chat is indeed a private space invisible to others, but that does not make it an encrypted vault. If you lose your phone, or someone gets hold of it while it is unlocked, that person can open KakaoTalk and read your Note to Self contents exactly as they are.

So I recommend the habit of either not putting the following in at all, or deleting them the moment you are done.

  • Financial and authentication data: passwords, card numbers, one-time codes and digital certificates are safer left out, even temporarily. If you must move one, delete it right after use.
  • ID and sensitive photos: keeping photos of your ID or passport permanently in Note to Self raises the risk of misuse if the phone is lost. The rule is to upload only when needed and delete immediately.

By contrast, what you can drop in freely is low-stakes information whose leak would cause little harm - links, public articles, idea memos, shopping lists. In short, remember the line that Note to Self is a convenience-oriented temporary store, not a secret vault, and you can keep both convenience and safety. Turning on device lock (biometrics) together with KakaoTalk's own app lock adds one more layer of first-line defense.

12. Comparing Alternatives - When to Switch to Notion or Pocket

Note to Self is not a cure-all, so it helps to set criteria for when to move to a dedicated tool.

Tool Strength Best when
KakaoTalk Note to Self Immediacy, real-time sync, nothing to install Quick parking, moving text between devices
Notion Folders, tags and databases for structured organizing You have many links and want them structured by topic
Pocket-style read-it-later Read later, offline, clean reading view You want to collect articles and read them calmly
Browser bookmarks Folder sorting, device sync, free Pinning sites you visit often, mostly on a PC

A rough rule: if your saved items number in the dozens and are temporary - things you just need not to forget - KakaoTalk is enough. But once they grow into the hundreds and you organize and revisit them often, an organizer like Notion is better, and if reading articles is the goal, a read-it-later tool wins. For how to use Notion seriously, our separate piece, the complete guide to using Notion AI at work, goes into detail. You do not have to pick only one: the most practical combination is to split roles - KakaoTalk as the temporary entrance, a dedicated tool for permanent storage.

13. Practical Tips - Use It This Way and You Won't Lose Things

Finally, here are the tips that worked best in practice when using Note to Self as a bookmark.

  1. Always add a keyword line to links. Card titles alone search poorly. Write the word you will later recall yourself.
  2. Pick your own markers. Prefix items with agreed tokens like "#work", "#buy", "#recipe" and they search at once like manual tags.
  3. Clear it out regularly. Delete handled items to keep the chat light, which keeps search fast. Manage it like an inbox you empty.
  4. Send important files to Talk Cloud or a separate cloud. Note to Self is a temporary box. Move files you must keep into a backup.
  5. Write dates in natural language to promote them. Type something like "tomorrow at 3 pm" and event registration happens in one step.
  6. Move long text between devices via KakaoTalk. Phone-to-PC copy and paste is faster than email or cloud.

14. Conclusion + Three Things to Try Today

KakaoTalk's Note to Self is not a flashy organizer. There are no folders, no tags. But as a parking spot that you already open every day, that links your PC and phone in real time, and that takes anything in two or three taps, it is almost unbeatable. The key is to accept its limits, fill the gaps with search, the Talk Drawer and keyword memos, and leave permanent storage to dedicated tools.

Let us close with three things you can try today.

  1. Send one page you have open right now to your Note to Self, and add a keyword line after it. You will experience the fastest bookmark workflow in under a minute.
  2. Decide on one personal marker. Agreeing on just "#work" lets you pull up all work-related items at once.
  3. Type "tomorrow at 3 pm" once. Watching how it links into Talk Calendar ties your memos and scheduling into a single flow.
