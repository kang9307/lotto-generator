This post organizes the values in Anthropic’s October 7, 2026 announcement and the Claude Platform documentation into pricing, specifications, benchmarks and policy. Any value marked as a calculation is arithmetic that does not appear in the source text.

Overview: where Anthropic places Haiku 5.5

Anthropic published “Introducing Claude Haiku 5.5” on October 7, 2026 and describes the model as the cheapest, fastest and most capable small model it has released. That wording is Anthropic’s own characterization, not an independent ranking. The model ID is claude-haiku-5-5.

The Claude Platform model page presents the model for high-volume, low-latency work such as classification, extraction and routing, and lists subagent tasks alongside them. The announcement frames the use as high-volume, cost-sensitive work and gives summaries, compactions, database queries and classification requests as examples. It adds that the model pairs well with Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 as a subagent on coding work, and that because it is Anthropic’s fastest model to date it suits speed-sensitive tasks such as live customer support and browser use. The release of the larger model is covered in the Claude Opus 5.5 release summary, and a comparable exercise in reading a price table for another vendor’s model is in the GPT-6.1 Sol pricing summary.

  • Date and ID. The announcement is dated October 7, 2026, and the model ID is claude-haiku-5-5.
  • Intended work. Quick, repetitive workloads such as summaries, compactions, database queries and classification requests are the examples given.
  • Subagent role. The announcement says the model pairs well with Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 as a subagent on coding work.
  • Speed qualifier. Footnote 1 says it is the fastest model to date at each model’s standard speed, although it runs less quickly than the Opus models in Fast Mode.

The price table: reading under and over 100K tokens

The announcement’s price table is quoted per one million tokens. Only the Haiku 5.5 column is split, under the heading “prompts up to / over 100k”, with the two tiers joined by a slash in each cell. The table below unfolds that into two columns. The Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 5.5 columns have no tier split in the original table, so each shows a single value.

Per 1M tokens Haiku 5.5 (up to 100K) Haiku 5.5 (over 100K) Haiku 4.5 Sonnet 5.5
Cache reads $0.01 $0.05 $0.10 $0.10
Cache writes $0.125 $0.625 $1.25 $2.50
Input tokens $0.10 $0.50 $1.00 $2.00
Output tokens $0.50 $2.50 $5.00 $10.00

The announcement says Haiku 5.5 is especially good value for tasks with prompts up to 100,000 tokens, which made up around 90% of requests to the previous Haiku model. The pricing section of the model documentation restates the same numbers by category (input, output, cache write and cache read), and its input and output headline rates carry the word “From”.

  • Tier multiple (calculation). In all four rows the rate for prompts over 100K tokens is five times the rate for prompts up to 100K: $0.10 against $0.50, $0.50 against $2.50, $0.01 against $0.05, and $0.125 against $0.625.
  • Versus Haiku 4.5 (calculation). In the up-to-100K tier all four rows are one tenth of the Haiku 4.5 value, and in the over-100K tier all four rows are one half. That matches the 90% and 50% reductions in footnote 2.
  • Versus Sonnet 5.5 (calculation). In the up-to-100K tier, Haiku 5.5 input, output and cache writes cost one twentieth of the Sonnet 5.5 rate, and cache reads cost one tenth.

What the 75%, 90%, 50% and 20% figures each measure

The announcement uses four percentages with different bases. The table below reproduces each sentence as written and attaches its scope.

Figure Sentence in the source Scope and basis
About 75% on average On average, it now costs around 75% less to run. Average cost to run compared with Haiku 4.5; footnote 2 gives the basis of the calculation
90% lower Claude Haiku 5.5 is priced 90% lower than Claude Haiku 4.5 for requests up to 100,000 tokens Price for requests up to 100,000 tokens
50% lower and 50% lower for requests over 100,000 tokens. Price for requests over 100,000 tokens
90% of requests On Haiku 4.5, 90% of requests fell into the former category. Share of Haiku 4.5 requests that were up to 100,000 tokens; the unit in the sentence is requests
Around 20% this reduces the cost of Sonnet 5.5 on most agentic tasks by around 20%. Effect of cutting Sonnet 5.5 cache reads from $0.20 to $0.10; applies to most agentic tasks

Footnote 2 says the 75% calculation also accounts for the change in how many tokens it takes to complete a given piece of work between Haiku 4.5 and Haiku 5.5. Haiku 5.5 has an updated tokenizer, similar to those of Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, which means it uses slightly more tokens per task. The full sentence reads: “This calculation also accounts for changes between Haiku 4.5 and Haiku 5.5 in how many tokens are used to complete a given piece of work.”

  • Size of the token increase. The model documentation and the migration guide say the same text produces approximately 30% more tokens than on Haiku 4.5, and that the exact increase depends on the content.
  • Effective rate for the same text (calculation). With 30% more tokens, the effective rate in the up-to-100K tier is 0.10 times 1.3, or 13% of the Haiku 4.5 rate. For identical text that is 87% lower.
  • Request-weighted average (calculation). If 90% of requests pay 10% of the old rate and 10% pay 50%, the average is about 14%, or 86% lower. Multiplying by the 1.3 token factor gives about 18%, or 82% lower. The announcement’s 75% is smaller than both.
  • What the announcement does not state. The weighting behind the 75%, the split between input and output, and the typical token size per request are not given, so the calculation above cannot reproduce the 75%.

Accuracy and cost per attempt at each effort level

Anthropic calls Haiku 5.5 its first Haiku-class model to come with an adjustable effort setting, and says that, as with its other models, users can decide whether to optimize for cost or intelligence. The announcement charts three benchmarks at each effort setting: OSWorld 2.1 (offline subset), GDPval-AA v2.1 and Humanity’s Last Exam without tools. The table below reproduces the OSWorld chart. The figure in parentheses is the cost per attempt in US dollars.

Effort Haiku 5.5 Sonnet 5.5 GPT-6 Luna
Low 42.0% ($0.07) 57.9% ($0.68) 19.2% ($0.04)
Med 53.3% ($0.13) 66.0% ($0.93) 37.5% ($0.13)
High 61.3% ($0.18) 73.2% ($1.38) 42.3% ($0.14)
Xhigh 67.6% ($0.28) 81.1% ($2.22) 44.8% ($0.17)
Max 72.4% ($0.61) 83.9% ($5.73) 48.9% ($0.20)
Haiku 4.5 Plotted at Max only: 15.7% ($1.45)
  • GDPval-AA v2.1 (cost per task rather than per attempt). Haiku 5.5 scores 1125 ($0.01) at Low, 1277 ($0.03) at Med, 1420 ($0.09) at High, 1513 ($0.27) at Xhigh and 1620 ($0.87) at Max. Haiku 4.5 scores 735 ($0.24), Sonnet 5.5 at Max scores 1840 ($6.78), and GPT-6 Luna at Max scores 1437 ($0.09). The Elo values follow the chart’s labels.
  • Humanity’s Last Exam (no tools). Haiku 5.5 rises from 30.9% ($0.003) at Low to 45.9% ($0.21) at Max. Haiku 4.5 scores 10.2% ($0.09), and Sonnet 5.5 at Max scores 56.9% ($0.89).
  • Comparisons (calculation). On OSWorld, Haiku 5.5 at Max (72.4%, $0.61) has higher accuracy and a lower cost per attempt than Sonnet 5.5 at Low (57.9%, $0.68). Haiku 5.5 at Med (53.3%, $0.13) has higher accuracy and a lower cost than GPT-6 Luna at Max (48.9%, $0.20).
  • Direction of cost at Max (calculation). In the charts, Haiku 5.5 at Max costs less per attempt than Haiku 4.5 on OSWorld, falling from $1.45 to $0.61, but costs more on GDPval-AA, rising from $0.24 to $0.87, and on Humanity’s Last Exam, rising from $0.09 to $0.21. The lower price list therefore does not mean a lower cost per attempt at every setting.

Anthropic says this chart shows an important difference between Haiku 5.5 and its larger models. Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 remain better choices for complex agentic coding tasks like those measured by Terminal-Bench 4.0, while Haiku 5.5 is best suited to more narrowly scoped tasks, such as compaction, summarization or subagent work, that might otherwise have been cost-prohibitive with previous versions of Claude. All the figures above are Anthropic’s own published results. The chart captions in the announcement describe the benchmarks this way: OSWorld 2.1 measures how well agents can operate a real computer to finish long, multi-step tasks; Terminal-Bench 4.0 measures how well a model can complete complex, multi-step professional tasks within a command-line interface; and Humanity’s Last Exam is a test of expert-level academic knowledge and reasoning. GDPval-AA is an evaluation by Artificial Analysis and covers real-world professional work across 44 occupations.

Six early-customer statements

The announcement says that in early testing its customers reported results consistent with the performance and cost improvements above, and it prints six quotations. The points below summarize each one. Every statement is the named company’s own, published by Anthropic.

  • Asana (Aaron Vinh, Staff Software Engineer). On the evaluation suite for its AI agent product AI Teammates, compared with the model it uses today, Asana reported over a 30% reduction in latency for task completions and up to 2.5x faster inference per agent turn. Its summary: “It’s a noticeably snappier experience.”
  • HubSpot (Ze’ev Klapow, Distinguished Software Engineer). On its simulated-portal evaluation of CRM tasks, HubSpot reported the best score it has seen on that suite, 92.8% averaged over three runs. Separately, on a CRM audit task that asks models to identify stale but ambiguous records, it said Haiku 5.5 was fastest among all models tested, with the highest hit rate and the lowest false positive rate. HubSpot says it mostly tests the smaller, more efficient models.
  • AlphaSense (Daniel Campos, Distinguished Engineer). For Ask in Document, which makes about 8M calls a week in production, AlphaSense ran 400 queries and reported a statistically significant improvement over Haiku 4.5, 0.84 against 0.76.
  • Box (Yashodha Bhavnani, VP of AI Products). In early testing, Box said Haiku 5.5 scored 11 points higher than Haiku 4.5 at about half the latency. Box said it would put the model to use on “analytical work that runs at scale, from cost reports to financial summaries and weekly recurring reviews.”
  • Rogo (Alex Wang, Applied AI). Rogo said the model fits short, high-volume work such as quick lookups, subagents and summaries. Its example is a Haiku 5.5 subagent pulling the segment revenue line from a 10-K while a bigger model builds the deck. Rogo’s assessment: “It’s accurate enough that we’d trust it there, and fast and cheap enough that we can run it a lot.”
  • Cognition (Walden Yan, Co-Founder and CPO). Cognition said Haiku 5.5 joins the sidekick lineup in Devin Fusion, and that with Haiku 5.5 as the sidekick Fusion holds a FrontierCode score of 66.2 while cutting cost and latency. That 66.2 belongs to a Fusion configuration with Opus 5.5 as the lead, so it cannot be compared directly with the 46.4% for Haiku 5.5 alone in the table above (interpretation). Cognition adds that it can be tried today in the Devin CLI with Opus 5.5 as the lead.

Sonnet 5.5 cache-read cut and the Max and Team API credits

On the same day Anthropic announced two more changes to the value of its model range. First, starting that day, the price of cache reads on Claude Sonnet 5.5 fell by 50%, from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens. Because cache reads make up a large share of token consumption, Anthropic says this reduces the cost of Sonnet 5.5 on most agentic tasks by around 20%. Second, within the week, a monthly API credit for use on the Claude Platform rolls out to all Max and Team subscribers. The amounts come from the announcement and the Help Center article, and the table below follows the Help Center.

Plan Monthly credit Note
Max 5x $100 Same as the announcement
Max 20x $200 Same as the announcement
Team (Standard seats) $20 per seat Credits for all seats are pooled into one monthly balance
Team (Premium seats) $100 per seat The pooled balance is capped at $500
Team example Three Standard seats and two Premium seats: $260 Example from the Help Center; calculated from the seats on the plan when credits are granted
  • Eligibility. Max and Team plans, including discounted Team plans, are eligible, and the article says Free, Pro and Enterprise plans are not. A new subscriber can claim after seven days on an eligible plan.
  • Claiming. The credit is claimed by linking one Claude Console organization to the plan. Only one organization can be linked, and the subscriber cannot change it without contacting support.
  • What it covers. The announcement says the credits can be used on any model. The article names the Claude API (Messages and Message Batches), the Console Playground, Claude Managed Agents and the Claude Agent SDK.
  • What it does not cover. It does not apply to interactive Claude Code in the terminal, IDE, desktop or web, to extra usage in Claude products, or to Claude on Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI or Microsoft Foundry.
  • Renewal. Credits refresh each billing cycle, and unused credits expire at the end of the cycle. They are spent before purchased credits and are separate from the plan’s usage limits.

When the balance runs out, usage continues only if the organization has purchased credits or auto-reload, and otherwise API requests stop until the next monthly credits arrive. The article states that usage is never charged to the Claude plan. Upgrading from Max 5x to Max 20x grants prorated credits right away, while canceling or moving to an ineligible plan stops new credits but leaves credits already received usable until they expire. Moving from Max to Team ends the Max link, and a Team owner can claim the team’s credits once the Team plan has been active for seven days.

Changes listed in the migration guide

The Claude Platform migration guide for Haiku 5.5 lists, in order, the items that affect code moving from Haiku 4.5. The eight below are the ones that touch the request format and billing directly.

  • Model ID. Replace claude-haiku-4-5-20251001 or claude-haiku-4-5 with claude-haiku-5-5. On Bedrock the ID changes from anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5 to anthropic.claude-haiku-5-5, and on Google Cloud from claude-haiku-4-5@20251001 to claude-haiku-5-5.
  • Recount tokens. The same text becomes about 30% more tokens, so the guide tells readers to recompute max_tokens limits and cost estimates.
  • Thinking configuration. Sending {"type": "enabled", "budget_tokens": N} returns a 400 error. Use {"type": "adaptive"} with output_config.effort. Adaptive thinking is on by default.
  • Thinking block display. By default a thinking block returns an empty thinking field and only a signature. To receive summarized thinking, set display to summarized.
  • Sampling parameters. Remove temperature, top_p and top_k. If temperature is sent it must be 1 and top_p must be 0.99, and any other value, and any top_k, returns a 400 error.
  • Prefill. A request that ends with an assistant turn for the model to continue returns a 400 error even with thinking off. End the messages with a user turn.
  • Computer use tool. On the Claude API and Google Cloud only computer_toolset_20260801 is supported, and declaring computer_20250124 returns a 400 error. The browser use tool browser_toolset_20260801 is also supported, which Haiku 4.5 does not offer.
  • Refusals and capacity. Safety classifiers can decline a request, so stop_reason refusal must be handled, and there is no server-side fallback. Priority Tier is not supported on Haiku 5.5. Thinking blocks are valid only in the account that produced them, and a change to earlier turns returns a 400 error.

The guide covers migrating Messages API code, and it says that anyone using Claude Managed Agents needs no change beyond updating the model name. It says that running /claude-api migrate in Claude Code invokes the bundled Claude API skill, which swaps the model ID, applies the breaking parameter changes it can, and produces a checklist of items to verify manually. A forced tool_choice (any, or a named tool) is accepted, but the response then starts with the tool call and has no thinking block. To let the model think before calling a tool, the guide says to keep tool_choice on auto and say in the prompt when to use the tool. The announcement adds that the Claude Python and TypeScript SDKs are updated to support computer use and browser use in beta, and that the model is available on all platforms, naming Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.

For replacing prefill, the guide suggests structured outputs or tools with enum fields for output format, a request for a direct answer in the system prompt in place of a preamble, and a continuation instruction moved into the user message. On earlier models, it says Claude Haiku 3.5 is retired on the Claude API and Amazon Bedrock and Claude Haiku 3 is retired on the Claude API, and that requests to a retired model fail.

Safety evaluation and safeguards

The safety section of the announcement has two parts, alignment and safeguards, and refers to the system card for the evaluation process and detailed results.

  • Alignment. Anthropic reports major improvements across almost all of its alignment evaluations relative to Haiku 4.5, with far fewer instances of misaligned behavior and a lower willingness to cooperate with misuse.
  • Cybersecurity safeguards. They are described as more restrictive than Haiku 4.5’s but somewhat less restrictive than those applied to other recent models. They permit a wider range of defensive tasks than the safeguards for Sonnet 5.5, but still block penetration testing and other techniques more likely to be used by attackers. The announcement’s own wording is that the safeguards “permit a wider range of defensive tasks than our safeguards for Sonnet 5.5.”
  • Biology safeguards. They are the same as for Sonnet 5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5. They allow research biology questions but restrict requests that Anthropic judges likely to cause harm.

The announcement says that organizations working on wider-ranging biology and cyber activities can apply to the Life Sciences Verification Program and the Cyber Verification Program.

What could not be confirmed

  • The evaluation settings in the system card. The system card, which the announcement points to for how evaluations are run, is a PDF whose body was not read, so the effort level and number of attempts behind the performance table are inferred only from matches with the chart values.
  • The weighting behind the 75% figure. Neither the announcement nor its footnotes give per-request token sizes or the input and output split, so the arithmetic could not reproduce it.
  • Official specifications and pricing for GPT-6 Luna. Only the comparison values in the announcement tables were carried over, and no material from that vendor was used.
  • Actual billing rates and regional availability dates on Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. The pricing pages of those platforms were not opened.
