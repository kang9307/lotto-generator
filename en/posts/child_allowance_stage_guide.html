Korea Child Allowance and Cash Benefits by Stage

From the first 60 days to primary school - what to file and when, as of August 2026

2026-08-09 Life & Practical

Read this first - the reference date and the limits of this page

This page describes the position as of August 2026 and does not replace the eligibility review carried out by a community center or by Bokjiro. These are programs for residents of the Republic of Korea. The age ceilings and amounts for the child allowance and the related cash benefits have changed every year, and 2026 already brought one large change in April. Whether a particular household qualifies, and how much it actually receives, cannot be settled by this page. That is decided by the eup, myeon or dong community center and by the Bokjiro review. Before filing, check the current guidance on Bokjiro (bokjiro.go.kr) and Gov24 (gov.kr) directly.

What has to be finished within 60 days of birth registration

When a child is born in Korea, money does not arrive through a single channel. The program names sound alike, which invites people to treat them as one thing, but they rest on different legal bases, cover different ages, and are reviewed separately even when the filing counter is shared. That is how a family ends up filing one application and assuming the rest were covered.

The number to fix in mind first is 60 days. Both the child allowance and the parental benefit are paid retroactively from the month of birth if the application is filed within 60 days counting the birth date itself. File after that line and payment runs from the month of application. The months in between are gone.

  • The 60 days are counted including the date of birth, not from the day the birth was registered
  • The parental benefit for age 0 is one million won a month, so the amount that cannot be recovered grows quickly with each month of delay
  • The safest route is the happy birth one-stop service at the community center on the day of birth registration. The child allowance, the parental benefit and the first meeting voucher can all be filed at one counter
  • Online, the channels are Bokjiro and Gov24. Note that online filing for the child allowance is available only when the applicant is a parent with parental authority; grandparents, institutions and other guardians have to file in person
  • The first meeting voucher does not follow the 60-day rule. It carries its own spending deadline, covered further down
Most of the missed-deadline cases come down to one assumption: that registering the birth automatically switches the benefits on. Birth registration and benefit applications are separate procedures. The one-stop service handles both at the same desk; it does not mean the allowance attaches itself to the registration.

The first thing to notice is that none of the five rows carries an income test. The child allowance became universal in January 2019, with no income or asset screening, and the parental benefit and the first meeting voucher set no income or asset threshold either. Deciding in advance that a household earns too much to bother applying is the most common self-inflicted loss in this area.

The second thing is that the vertical axis is time. The first meeting voucher lands once at birth, the parental benefit runs to age two, the home care allowance runs from two until school entry, and the child allowance cuts across all of it until age nine. Only the child allowance genuinely overlaps; the others mostly hand over the baton.

Laid out in the order the child grows, the same information makes the required action obvious.

The two cells people skip are the month the child turns 24 months and moving house. Twenty-four months is where the program swaps out entirely and a choice has to be made, and since the 2026 reform a move has become an event that actually changes the amount. It used to be 100,000 won everywhere, so relocation had no effect. That is no longer true.

Child allowance - April 2026 changed the age and the amount at once

A large share of what surfaces in search still says under 8 years, 100,000 won a month. Two things changed simultaneously in April 2026, so that sentence is no longer the current standard.

First, the age ceiling moved from under 8 to under 9. This was not a one-off adjustment; it is designed as a staircase.

  • 2026: under 9 years
  • 2027: under 10 years
  • 2028: under 11 years
  • 2029: under 12 years
  • 2030: under 13 years

The age expansion came with retroactive processing. Roughly 430,000 children born between January 2017 and March 2018, whose payments had stopped on passing age 8 and who fell back inside the under-9 line, received the unpaid January to March 2026 amounts retroactively. In that situation the payment was arranged by official action without a new application from the guardian. Where the payment details could not be verified, the arrears follow once the details are confirmed, so if a child appears eligible but nothing has arrived, the first step is to ask the community center whether the account details are on file.

Second, the amount now depends on where the family lives. The basis applied from the May 2026 payment is as follows.

Region categoryPaid in cashPaid as a local gift certificate

A few cautions when reading that table.

  • The headline maximum of 130,000 won appears only when the family lives in a special population-decline area and takes the local gift certificate. Two conditions have to hold at once
  • The 10,000 won certificate top-up requires the local government to enact or amend an ordinance. It does not switch on automatically just because an area is designated, so it has to be checked with the city or county office
  • Which places count as population-decline areas, and which of those are preferential or special, follows a separately designated list. It is not something that can be guessed from population size or general impression
  • The April 2026 payment was a transition month and worked differently from the May-onward basis. Judging the current amount from an April bank statement will mislead
  • Payment lands on the 25th of each month, moving to the preceding business day when the 25th falls on a weekend or public holiday
  • Eligibility requires Korean nationality and a normally issued resident registration number

Parental benefit - sending the child to a center changes what hits the account

The parental benefit goes to households raising a child aged 0 to 23 months. The age 0 band (0-11 months) is one million won a month and the age 1 band (12-23 months) is 500,000 won. That much is simple. The place where real deposits diverge from expectations is elsewhere.

The parental benefit is not purely a cash transfer. It arrives in one of two forms.

  • Cared for at home - the full amount arrives as cash
  • Using a childcare center or the full-day child care service - support is delivered first as a voucher (childcare fee or full-day care), and only where that voucher is worth less than the parental benefit does the difference arrive as cash

Put plainly, enrolling in a childcare center does not remove entitlement to the parental benefit. Entitlement stays; part of the money simply flows to the center first and only the remainder reaches the account. That is why the deposit appears to shrink suddenly in the month enrollment starts. Support did not fall; its form changed.

The age 1 band produces a different outcome again. The parental benefit at age 1 is 500,000 won a month, while the childcare fee voucher rate can sit close to that figure or above it, so the cash difference may be much smaller than in the age 0 band or absent altogether. That is why families describe the monthly top-up as disappearing when the child turns one.

The exact cash difference depends on the childcare fee support rate for that year, and the rate is adjusted annually. That is why no fixed figure is written here. The difference currently applied is best confirmed through Bokjiro or the childcare center itself.

The practical rules for filing and payment move the same way as for the child allowance.

  • Filing channels are the community center in person, Bokjiro online, Gov24, and the happy birth one-stop service
  • Filing within 60 days counting the date of birth backdates payment to the month of birth; later filings start from the month of application
  • Payment lands on the 25th of each month
  • The rate switches automatically at the move from age 0 to age 1. What does need a change filing is a change in childcare center use
  • The parental benefit is paid independently of parental leave. The leave benefit calculation is set out separately in Korea parental leave benefit and the both-parents special rule

First meeting voucher - not cash, and it expires

The first meeting voucher is the lump-sum support paid once at birth. For children born on or after 1 January 2024 it is 2 million won for a first child and 3 million won for a second or later child. Because the amount rises from the second child, a family that remembers the figure from their first will get it wrong.

Mistakes here concern the form and the deadline rather than the amount.

  • It is not cash but voucher points loaded onto a Kookmin Haengbok card. Waiting for 2 million won to appear in a bank account is a misunderstanding
  • Eligibility covers a child whose birth has been registered and who has been normally issued a resident registration number
  • Children born on or after 1 January 2024 must apply and use the voucher within two years of the date of birth on the resident registration. For earlier births the window was one year
  • Once the deadline passes, any remaining balance expires. Households without large purchases really do reach the deadline with money left
  • Multiples are counted by birth order, so twins as a first delivery are calculated as a first and a second child
  • Some merchant categories are excluded. If a single large purchase is planned, confirm first that the merchant category is eligible

Applications go through a community center or public health center in person, Bokjiro or Gov24 online, or the happy birth one-stop service. Filing is open at any time, so a family that missed it at birth registration can still apply within the deadline.

The one choice that splits at 24 months

The month a child turns 24 months is where the support structure swaps out wholesale. The parental benefit ends and either the home care allowance or childcare fee support begins. The key point is that only one of the two can be taken.

  • Home care allowance - 100,000 won a month for a child cared for at home without using a childcare center, kindergarten or the full-day child care service. It covers 24 months up to 86 months, that is, until primary school entry
  • Childcare fee support - for a child attending a childcare center, the fee is supported as a voucher. In that case the home care allowance is not paid
  • Kindergarten tuition support - support for kindergarten attendance runs on a different track from childcare fees. Either way it is not combined with the home care allowance

The friction in practice comes from switching mid-course. A child cared for at home who starts attending a center requires a change of status filing from the home care allowance to childcare fee support, and the reverse when the center is left. Skipping that filing means support keeps flowing that should not, and it later becomes subject to recovery.

The 86-month line is set against primary school entry. School entry ends the home care allowance, but the child allowance continues. On the 2026 basis that means the child allowance runs to roughly the second year of primary school, and if the age expansion proceeds as announced the range widens further.

Child allowance and parental benefit stack - what combines and what does not

This is the most common question, and the answer is direct: the child allowance and the parental benefit are received together. They are separate programs and neither reduces the other. The first meeting voucher is separate as well.

The lines fall like this.

  • Combine - child allowance + parental benefit. Both land in the same month across the 0-23 month band
  • Combine - child allowance + home care allowance, in the home-care band after 24 months
  • Combine - child allowance + childcare fee support. Attending a center does not touch the child allowance
  • Combine - the first meeting voucher is paid independently of all of the above
  • Sequential, not simultaneous - parental benefit (0-23 months) and home care allowance (24 months and up). They meet at a boundary and hand over
  • One or the other - home care allowance versus childcare fee support (or kindergarten tuition support)

So a household caring for an infant at home receives, in a given month, one million won of parental benefit and the child allowance together. Families do file for the child allowance alone without realising the combination exists, and once 60 days pass that gap cannot be undone.

Local government birth grants are outside what this page can tell you

The four programs above are national schemes running the same way nationwide. Yet the money that actually reaches a household often does not stop there, because provinces, cities and counties run their own locally funded birth grants, congratulatory payments and baby-goods support.

  • Local support differs by region in name, amount and conditions alike. A multiple-fold gap with the next district over is not unusual
  • Residency requirements are common, typically a minimum number of months of resident registration in the area before the birth
  • Some places pay in several instalments rather than a lump sum, requiring continued residence at each payment point
  • Filing deadlines are sometimes shorter than those of the national programs
  • Verification belongs to the local service search on Bokjiro or the city, county or community center where you live. Copying an amount from a blog about another region will almost never match

Where to file, when payment lands, and what happens if you miss it

There are four channels, and which ones are open depends on the situation.

  1. Happy birth one-stop service - files the child allowance, parental benefit and first meeting voucher in one go during birth registration. This is the route that drops the fewest items
  2. Eup, myeon or dong community center in person - required where the applicant is not a parent, or where documents need to be verified
  3. Bokjiro (bokjiro.go.kr) - available on PC and the mobile app. Payment status is checked here too
  4. Gov24 (gov.kr) - the integrated online filing portal

Payment is set for the 25th of each month, moving to the preceding business day when the 25th falls on a weekend or public holiday. The first month after filing can take longer because of review and registration, sometimes arriving combined the following month, so an empty account on the 25th is not by itself a sign that something went wrong.

Where a deadline has already passed, the position is as follows.

  • Child allowance or parental benefit past 60 days - filing is still possible. There is simply no backdating, and payment runs from the month of application
  • First meeting voucher past its deadline - once the filing and spending window closes there is no way to revive it, and the balance expires
  • Newly eligible again through the age expansion - these cases can be handled by official action, so asking the community center about the status of the payment details is faster than waiting indefinitely
  • Changed bank account - without an account change filing the transfer fails. Moving house, a legal name change and a new account all require notification

What could not be confirmed

These are the items left without a fixed figure here, either because official guidance did not give a clear current value or because the value varies by locality and year.

  • The exact cash difference paid when a childcare center is used - the childcare fee support rate is adjusted annually, so Bokjiro or the center should be checked
  • The full list of merchant categories excluded from the first meeting voucher
  • The specific list of cities and counties classed as preferential versus special population-decline areas
  • Which local governments are actually running the 10,000 won gift certificate top-up, since it presupposes an enacted or amended ordinance
  • The separate home care allowance rates for children with disabilities or in rural and fishing areas
  • Whether a child registered as a foreign national qualifies for the child allowance - official guidance states Korean nationality and a resident registration number as requirements, but exceptions arising from dual nationality, overseas Korean status or other individual circumstances cannot be judged from this page. Anyone in that position should ask the community center directly
  • Amendments scheduled after the second half of 2026 - the age expansion plan is published, but actual implementation depends on that year legislation and budget

What counts as improper receipt

Because these programs carry no income screening, the threshold is low, but the structure puts the burden of keeping the facts reported on the recipient. Neglecting that turns into improper receipt.

  • Continuing to draw the home care allowance after a child starts at a childcare center - by far the most common pattern
  • Failing to report a child staying abroad for an extended period - rules suspend payment past certain durations
  • Failing to report a change in the caregiving arrangement or in who the guardian is
  • Applying by false or otherwise improper means

In these cases payment stops and amounts already received are recovered. Where intent is established, recovery is not the end of it. An honest failure to report is handled differently from knowingly leaving a false situation in place, so if a change surfaces late, telling the community center first is always the better position.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Up to what age is the child allowance paid?

From April 2026 it is under 9 years. That is one year above the under-8 ceiling that applied through 2025, and the plan does not stop there: under 10 in 2027, under 11 in 2028, under 12 in 2029 and under 13 in 2030, rising a year at a time. Those later steps are scheduled rather than settled, so implementation depends on the legislation and budget of each year. Whether your own child qualifies now is confirmed at Bokjiro or the community center.

Q. Can the child allowance and the parental benefit be received together?

Yes. They are separate programs and neither reduces the other, and the first meeting voucher is separate as well. By contrast the home care allowance and childcare fee support are an either-or choice, and the parental benefit and home care allowance sit at 0-23 months and 24 months and up respectively, meeting at a boundary rather than overlapping in the same month.

Q. What happens if the application is filed more than 60 days after birth?

The benefit is not lost; backdating is. Filing within 60 days backdates payment to the month of birth, while filing later starts it from the month of application. Since the age 0 parental benefit is one million won a month, even a difference of one or two months is not small in money terms. The same rule applies to both the child allowance and the parental benefit.

Q. Does moving house change the child allowance amount?

Since the 2026 reform it can. Rates were set differently for the capital region, areas outside it, and preferential and special population-decline areas. It is no longer a flat 100,000 won nationwide. Filing the move-in report settles the residence basis, but whether a fresh application is needed is best confirmed with the community center.

Q. Does the parental benefit stop when the child starts at a childcare center?

It does not stop. Entitlement stays and the form of payment changes. Support arrives first as a childcare fee voucher, and where the parental benefit exceeds the voucher, the difference arrives as cash. The smaller deposit reflects part of the money going to the center first, not a cut in support. Starting or ending center attendance does, however, require a change filing.

Summary - the order to work through by stage

Memorising these programs one by one guarantees that one gets dropped. Pinning them to the child timeline is far safer.

  1. The day you register the birth - file the child allowance, parental benefit and first meeting voucher together through the one-stop service. This single step protects the rest
  2. Within 60 days counting the date of birth - if the above was missed, finish inside this line. Past it, backdating disappears
  3. The day you decide on a childcare center - the form of the parental benefit changes. The shift in the deposit is normal, but the filing still has to be made
  4. The month the child turns 24 months - choose between the home care allowance and childcare fee support and file the change of status. Skipping it leads to recovery later
  5. The day you move - file the move-in report. From 2026 residence changes the child allowance amount
  6. Two years from receiving the first meeting voucher - put the deadline in the calendar. The balance expires

Of the six, the ones that cannot be undone are the second and the sixth. Sixty days and two years are hard deadlines with no remedy afterwards. The rest can be straightened out with a late filing; those two cannot.

The amounts, ages and deadlines on this page reflect August 2026 and may be amended afterwards. Do not treat anything here as settling your household eligibility or payment amount. Eligibility and the actual amount are decided by the review at the eup, myeon or dong community center and Bokjiro. Before filing, check the current guidance on Bokjiro (bokjiro.go.kr) and Gov24 (gov.kr), and confirm individual cases with the Health and Welfare Call Center on 129. Receiving support on the basis of a false report, or without notifying a change in circumstances, leads to suspension of payment and recovery of amounts paid.
