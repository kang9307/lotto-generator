Current rates: 2026 household tariff

What was corrected from the earlier version

The earlier version said household rates rose 4% on average in 2025 and quoted 709 won for usage above 500 kWh; neither matches the current tariff, so both were corrected. According to a 2026-09-21 news report, KEPCO kept the base charge, energy charge and climate-environment charge unchanged for Q4 (October-December) and held the fuel cost adjustment at +5 won per kWh; household rates were reported as frozen for 14 straight quarters.

Deductions and discounts

The essential-usage guarantee deduction mentioned earlier is described as the additional reduction scheme (formerly the essential-usage guarantee deduction) for welfare-discount households using 200 kWh or less a month. Welfare discounts exist for basic livelihood recipients, the next-lowest income class, multi-child and multi-family households, birth households and welfare facilities. Eligibility and amounts were not confirmed this time, so check with KEPCO ON or the KEPCO call center.

What you can do right away

  • Set temperature: keep it at 26°C or higher and lower it only for the first cool-down.
  • Filter: clean it once or twice a month.
  • Outdoor unit: keep it 40 cm or more from walls.
  • Running time: energy falls with every hour you cut.
  • Fan: point the louver upward and run a fan to circulate the air.

What was removed

The earlier table of 8-hour daily costs by set temperature, the 8-hours-a-day limit and the claim that 26-28°C cuts power 50% were removed because no source was found. The claim that on-off cycling beats continuous running was also dropped for lack of evidence.

Saving on home appliances

Appliances at home

  • Refrigerator: open the door less and use the temperature recommended in the manual.
  • Dryer: air-dry first and use the dryer only when needed.
  • TV: lower the brightness and cut power when not in use.
  • Lighting: switch off unneeded lights and replace bulbs with LEDs.
  • Power strip: cut rarely used devices with the strip switch.

Alternative cooling methods

Ways to use the air conditioner less

  • Fan with AC: circulates air so the room cools faster.
  • Shading: block sunlight outside the window before it enters.
  • Ventilation: open a cross-breeze in cooler hours.
  • Cooling items: use only as a supplement for how warm you feel.

The earlier table of power use, upfront cost and cooling effect by method was removed because no source was found. Savings are quoted only up to the Energy Agency figures above.

Improving daily habits

Know your usage and adjust

  • Check: look up your bill and usage in KEPCO ON to see this month's tier.
  • Meter date: tiers follow usage in the billing period, so confirm your meter-reading date.
  • Timing: run the AC in the hottest hours and use fans and ventilation otherwise.
  • Record: note usage each month to see whether you are near a boundary.

Staying below the tier limits

Converted to daily usage

For a 30-day billing period, 300 and 450 kWh in July-August are about 10 and 15 kWh a day. The regular 200 and 400 kWh are about 6.7 and 13.3 kWh a day. The earlier version used 13 kWh a day as a summer figure, but that was the regular-months limit.

Checklist

  • I confirmed whether this billing period is July-August or a regular month
  • I checked how far current usage is from the boundaries (200, 300, 400, 450 kWh)
  • I cleaned the AC filter and cleared space around the outdoor unit
  • I set the temperature to 26°C or higher
  • I checked in KEPCO ON whether I qualify for a welfare discount

Wrapping up

The tariff itself is unchanged; what shifts in July-August is the tier boundary. Review your habits against the tier boundaries and the Energy Agency figures.

# Summer Household Electricity Checklist (Korea, residential low-voltage)

As of: 2026-09-29
Sources: KEPCO residential low-voltage tariff, Korea Energy Agency air-conditioner material, news report on KEPCO's Q4 announcement (2026-09-21)

---

## 1. Tier table

| Tier | Regular months (Jan-Jun, Sep-Dec) | July-August | Base charge (won) | Energy charge (won/kWh) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Up to 200 kWh | Up to 300 kWh | 910 | 120.0 |
| Tier 2 | 201-400 kWh | 301-450 kWh | 1,600 | 214.6 |
| Tier 3 | Over 400 kWh | Over 450 kWh | 7,300 | 307.3 |

A bill also adds the climate-environment charge, fuel cost adjustment and taxes. The calculations below cover base charge and energy charge only.

## 2. Formula for your usage

- July-August: energy charge = min(usage,300)x120.0 + max(0,min(usage,450)-300)x214.6 + max(0,usage-450)x307.3
- Regular months: energy charge = min(usage,200)x120.0 + max(0,min(usage,400)-200)x214.6 + max(0,usage-400)x307.3
- Add the base charge of the tier you reach (910 / 1,600 / 7,300) once.

## 3. Boundary check

| Check | July-August | Regular months |
|---|---|---|
| Enter Tier 2 | Over 300 kWh | Over 200 kWh |
| Enter Tier 3 | Over 450 kWh | Over 400 kWh |
| Daily equivalent (30 days) | About 10 / 15 kWh | About 6.7 / 13.3 kWh |

## 4. Air conditioner check (Energy Agency material)

- [ ] Set temperature 26°C or higher (each 1°C lower costs 7% more power)
- [ ] Clean the filter 1-2 times a month (an unclean filter raises power use 3-5%)
- [ ] Keep the outdoor unit 40 cm or more from walls
- [ ] Run a fan to circulate air

## 5. When to check again

- KEPCO's next quarterly rate adjustment
- A change to the tariff or the summer tier rule
