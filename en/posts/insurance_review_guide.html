Why Now Is the Right Time to Review Your Insurance

Insurance is one of the few financial contracts designed to run for decades. Yet most people's life circumstances — family structure, income, health, mortgage, retirement horizon — change far faster than their policy portfolio. Coverage that was appropriate at age 30 often becomes mismatched, duplicated, or full of gaps by age 40 or 50.

According to data from the Korea Insurance Development Institute, the average Korean household holds more than five active insurance contracts. A significant share of those contracts overlap on the same benefit items. Reorganizing those overlaps — what is called insurance remodeling (보험 리모델링 in Korean) — can redirect wasted premiums toward genuine protection needs or increase household cash flow.

This guide covers the concept and core principles of insurance remodeling, how to audit your current portfolio, the key distinctions between protection and savings policies, a comparison of actual-loss insurance (Silsohn) across generations, a pre-cancellation checklist, and the red flags to watch when switching policies. No specific product is recommended. The goal is to give you the framework to make an informed decision yourself or to walk into a professional consultation prepared.

1. What Is Insurance Remodeling?

1.1 Definition

Insurance remodeling is the systematic process of reviewing all your active policies, eliminating duplicate or excess coverage, filling protection gaps, and improving premium efficiency relative to your current life situation. It is not synonymous with canceling and re-enrolling. In practice, indiscriminate cancellation is the single most common mistake people make when they decide to "reorganize" their insurance.

Think of it like home renovation rather than demolition. You assess the existing structure first, identify what is genuinely underperforming or redundant, and then selectively upgrade or remove — while preserving anything that would be costly or impossible to replace.

1.2 Six Core Principles

  • Clarify protection objectives first: Know which risks (death, critical illness, injury, long-term care) you are covering before evaluating individual policies.
  • Eliminate true duplicates: For actual-loss policies, duplicates do not double your payout — indemnity rules cap reimbursement at actual expenses. For lump-sum policies, duplicates do pay multiple times, but excess coverage still wastes premium money.
  • Keep total protection premiums below 10% of income: A common guideline is 7–10% of monthly income as the upper limit for protection insurance premiums.
  • Set adequate coverage terms: Disease and injury coverage ideally runs to age 80 or beyond; life (death) coverage should last at least until dependents become financially independent.
  • Treat cancellation as a last resort: Before canceling, explore premium deferral, reduced paid-up conversion, or policy loans to keep coverage active during financial strain.
  • Verify with a licensed professional: Final decisions should be reviewed by an FSS-registered insurance planner or independent financial advisor.

2. Auditing Your Portfolio - My Insurance Finder

2.1 The Tool

The Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) operates My Insurance Finder (fine.fss.or.kr), a consolidated portal that displays all your life and non-life insurance contracts in one place. Log in with a certified digital ID or a simplified social-login option to see contract status and unclaimed benefit amounts across every insurer simultaneously.

The Korea Life Insurance Association's My Insurance Full View (내보험 다보여) service goes a step further by displaying itemized benefit amounts per contract, making it the more useful tool for spotting true duplicate coverage.

2.2 Audit Steps

  1. Pull a complete contract list from My Insurance Finder.
  2. For each contract, retrieve the policy certificate or key terms to identify each covered benefit, its amount, and its duration.
  3. Build a table mapping every benefit category (death benefit, cancer lump-sum, hospitalization daily benefit, surgery benefit, actual-loss, etc.) across all contracts to identify overlaps and gaps.
  4. Calculate total monthly premiums and express them as a percentage of household income.
  5. Compare the coverage structure against your current life stage: number of dependents, outstanding mortgage, years to retirement, current health status.

3. Protection Insurance vs Savings Insurance

3.1 Protection Insurance

Protection insurance pays a benefit when a specified risk event occurs — death, illness, injury. Most protection products are pure-risk or term-based: premiums are relatively low for the coverage provided, but nothing is returned at maturity. Whole life, term life, critical illness, and actual-loss health policies fall in this category.

3.2 Savings Insurance

Savings insurance combines a risk component with a savings mechanism, returning principal plus interest at maturity. Annuity policies, endowment savings policies, and variable universal life fall here. The catch: administrative expenses are front-loaded into the first several years. Early termination before that cost is amortized results in receiving less than what you paid in — sometimes significantly less. Tax-deferral and compounding benefits typically only materialize after 10 or more years of continuous holding.

3.3 Prioritization Rule

The standard planning sequence is: ensure adequate protection coverage first (death, critical illness, actual-loss), then allocate surplus premiums to savings products. Savings insurance should always be benchmarked against alternative financial products — bank deposits, index ETFs — before committing, because early surrender penalties make it an inflexible vehicle for short- or medium-term goals.

4. Actual-Loss Insurance Generations - Know Before You Switch

Korea's actual-loss health insurance (Silsohn, 실손보험) reimburses actual out-of-pocket medical expenses rather than paying fixed lump sums. The specific benefits differ significantly by generation, making generation identification critical before any remodeling decision.

4th GenJul 2021–May 202620%30%Per-item benefit caps on non-covered categories.
5th GenMay 2026–present20%Non-critical: 50%Premiums reduced sharply; non-critical non-covered copay raised to 50%.

Core rule: First- and second-generation actual-loss policies carry substantially better terms than anything available today. Once canceled, re-enrollment at equivalent terms is almost never possible due to health underwriting. Switching to a fifth-generation policy saves on premiums but trades away the breadth of coverage that older generations provide.

5. Pre-Cancellation Checklist

5.1 Alternatives to Cancellation

Before canceling a policy due to premium strain or coverage overlap, consider these options in order:

  • Premium deferral (automatic policy loan): The insurer advances the premium against the surrender value, keeping the contract active without a cash outlay.
  • Reduced paid-up conversion: Premiums stop, and the policy continues with a proportionally reduced benefit funded by the current surrender value.
  • Policy loan: Borrow up to 70–80% of the surrender value at a relatively low rate to meet immediate cash needs while keeping coverage intact.
  • Rider cancellation: Cancel only the specific riders that are redundant while keeping the base contract and its more critical benefits.

5.2 Mandatory Checks Before Canceling

  • Confirm the surrender value relative to total premiums paid.
  • Verify whether you can qualify for comparable coverage under current health conditions.
  • Understand that a new policy restarts the waiting period (typically 90 days to one year for disease claims).
  • For renewable policies, calculate the future premium trajectory — rates rise with age, so a policy canceled at 45 may cost far more if re-enrolled at 50.

6. Switching Policies - Regulatory Red Flags in 2026

In May 2026, Korea's Financial Supervisory Service issued a consumer alert targeting aggressive and often unnecessary policy replacements driven by commission incentives at some general insurance agencies (GAs). The FSS simultaneously introduced comparative disclosure of replacement contract rates by insurer, sales channel, and product to increase market transparency.

6.1 Documents You Must Receive

  • Comparative Guide Certificate (비교안내증서): A legally required document placing the old and new policies side by side — benefits, premiums, surrender values. If an agent does not produce this, consider it a red flag.
  • Confirm new policy approval before canceling the existing contract to avoid a coverage gap.
  • Align the new policy's coverage start date with the old policy's cancellation date.

6.2 When Switching Is Genuinely Warranted

  • The new policy covers a risk that is entirely absent from your current portfolio and cannot be added as a rider.
  • A renewable policy's rising premiums have made a non-renewable equivalent significantly cheaper over the remaining coverage horizon.
  • A major life-stage change (e.g., dependents now fully independent) fundamentally alters the risk profile you need to cover.

7. Remodeling Workflow Summary

  1. Inventory: Use My Insurance Finder to list all active contracts.
  2. Benefit mapping: Tabulate every covered benefit across all contracts.
  3. Prioritize: Confirm protection coverage is sufficient before reviewing savings products.
  4. Identify actual-loss generation: Apply the "keep unless compelling reason" rule to 1st- and 2nd-generation policies.
  5. Explore alternatives: Deferral, reduced paid-up, rider cancellation before any full contract cancellation.
  6. Require Comparative Guide Certificate: Mandatory for any replacement transaction.
  7. Consult a professional: FSS-registered planner or independent financial advisor before finalizing.
  8. Schedule a review cycle: Reassess after major life events or every three to five years.

Conclusion: Optimize, Don't Just Cancel

The central insight of insurance remodeling is that cancellation is rarely the right first move. The goal is optimization: reduce what is genuinely wasteful, preserve what would be costly to replace, and fill real gaps. First-generation actual-loss policies and long-standing protection contracts often have irreplaceable terms. The FSS's My Insurance Finder gives you the data to see the full picture. A licensed professional helps you interpret it correctly. Start with the inventory, work through the checklist, and resist the pressure to act before you have the full comparison in front of you.
