This article is based on the GrapheneOS official forum thread that was opened on August 29, 2026 and ran until October 5 (including the follow-up posts by the official account), the GrapheneOS features documentation, and the Android NDK documentation. As of October 1, Pixel 11 support is a conditional position, “not added unless MTE support ships”, and a permanent cancellation has not been declared. The position may change later.

Context and Overview - How the GrapheneOS Position on the Pixel 11 Changed

On August 29, 2026, the official account of GrapheneOS, a security-focused open-source mobile operating system, opened a thread on its official discussion forum. It said that after a week of work on the Pixel 11 series the port was only partial and could not be completed. The thread is titled “Pixel 11 doesn't yet meet the GrapheneOS security standards and may be skipped”. A user comment on September 8 notes that the word “yet” was added to the title. The thread ran until October 5, and the news reached the Korean technology community GeekNews on October 6.

The core of the problem is the absence of ARM hardware memory tagging (Memory Tagging Extension, MTE) support. The current wording of the August 29 first post reads: the port cannot be completed because of lack of support for ARM hardware memory tagging in software, firmware and “potentially hardware”. The official account wrote that it appears Google cut an important security feature to save money. The same day, when a user said Google customer support had given a positive answer, the official account replied that customer support is not a useful source of information on this. It wrote that it is verifiable that MTE support was thoroughly purged from the firmware and software and does not show up as supported by the hardware, and asked why Google had not already corrected the claim if it were wrong.

The account later changed, however. On September 1 the official account posted what it called good news: the Pixel 11 still has at least bare minimum support for MTE at a hardware level, and the firmware itself had MTE support added back. On October 1 it set out a conditional position.

  • August 29 - first statement. After a week of work the port was partial, and it could not be completed because of lack of MTE support in software, firmware and potentially hardware; the account strongly recommended against buying the Pixel 11.
  • September 1-2 - minimal hardware support and partial firmware restoration. It was confirmed that Android 17 QPR2 Beta 4 had partly restored MTE support in the firmware. Tests with a non-stock kernel suggested that MTE works.
  • October 1 - conditional position. The account said it will not add support for the Pixel 11 series unless it ships MTE support.
  • October 3 - further explanation. It wrote that it will not take the risk of adding preliminary support and then having to drop support for the device in December 2026 if MTE does not ship.

Neither the claim that support has been definitively cancelled nor the August 29 description of the matter as “not yet determined” fits the current position. The current position is conditional: support is possible if MTE ships, and will not be added if it does not. The official account also writes that it is not clear whether MTE will ship.

The key is firmware support. According to the September 1 post by the official account, Android 17 QPR2 Beta 4 was released on August 28 as the first beta with Pixel 11 support, but the Pixel 11 builds were uploaded not on August 28 but only about ten hours before the post. In that build MTE support is still completely disabled in the OS, and the firmware unconditionally passes arm64.nomte to the kernel. The firmware itself, on the other hand, had MTE support added back. On September 2 the account reported that with a kernel replaced by one that ignores that kernel parameter MTE appears to work, with what appears to be similar overhead as before.

Why would that be? On September 1 the official account speculated that most of the hardware acceleration in the CPU cache had been removed to save money, that performance was ruined, and that MTE was therefore disabled in the firmware. On September 2 it stepped back: the basis for that speculation is leaks reported by news publications, and its own testing so far does not show a substantial performance reduction. It also said it has no explanation for why the initial Pixel 11 firmware had MTE completely compiled out. According to the account, Google has not answered its questions.

The issues boil down to three points.

  • Whether the firmware supports it. The hardware has MTE and the QPR2 beta firmware restored part of the support, but the October 1 post says it remains fully disabled for the OS, even as a developer option.
  • An unknown cause. The official account writes that it is unclear whether there are serious CPU errata or whether MTE simply performs very badly, and that Google has not explained. It also notes that the leaks do not match what its own testing shows.
  • The burden of the support period. The official account calculated that adding the Pixel 11 would push the date by which devices without MTE reach end of life from June 2028 to September 2033. It says the last device without MTE is the Pixel 7, and that the Pixel 7a will be the last supported device to go end-of-life without MTE.

The Technical Mechanism of ARM MTE and How GrapheneOS Applies It

The Android NDK documentation explains that memory safety bugs are errors in handling memory in native programming languages, that they are common code issues, and that they lead to security vulnerabilities as well as stability problems. MTE, introduced with Armv9, is a hardware extension that allows you to catch use-after-free and buffer-overflow bugs in native code. Starting from Android 13, select devices support MTE, and the document’s list of known supported devices runs from the Pixel 8 through the Pixel 10a. The document was last updated on July 8, 2026, before the Pixel 11 launched, so the Pixel 11’s absence from that list is not evidence of anything.

The same document describes two modes. Synchronous (SYNC) mode is optimized for debuggability: on a tag mismatch the processor terminates the process on the offending load or store instruction with SIGSEGV and full information about the access, and in this mode MTE also acts as a security mitigation. Asynchronous (ASYNC) mode is optimized for performance: the processor continues until the nearest kernel entry, where it terminates the process with SIGSEGV without recording the faulting address. For apps MTE is disabled by default and is turned on with android:memtagMode. A custom allocator that wants to use MTE must align tagged allocations to 16 bytes, because tags can only be assigned for 16-byte chunks, known as granules.

How far GrapheneOS applies MTE is spread across the official account’s posts and the features documentation. The official account says it uses MTE across the entire base OS including the kernel and every standard base OS process, and that it is only temporarily disabled for a few device-specific processes. The Pixel 8 launched with hardware MTE support in October 2023, and GrapheneOS integrated it into hardened_malloc and began using it across the OS later that month. Android and the Pixel OS never started using it by default, and Android 16 Advanced Protection Mode enables it for only a few processes.

  • Kernel. According to the features documentation, basic hardware memory tagging is used in the main kernel memory allocators (slab, page_alloc, non-executable vmalloc) to provide probabilistic detection of all use-after-free and inter-object overflows, along with deterministic detection of use-after-free until the memory is allocated again.
  • hardened_malloc. Hardware memory tagging is used for slab allocations (128k and below), providing probabilistic detection of all use-after-free and inter-object overflows along with deterministic detection of all small/linear overflows and of use-after-free until the memory has been reused once and gone through the quarantines twice.
  • Vanadium browser. The features documentation lists MTE enabled for the main allocator of GrapheneOS’s hardened browser.
  • Crash reporting. Memory corruption caught by hardware memory tagging is always reported to the user. Users can turn on a compatibility toggle if it looks like an app bug, but these protections cannot be disabled for the base OS.

The official account explained that, to avoid losing all of the benefits of memory tagging on a Pixel 11 without usable MTE, it would have to consider HWASan in the kernel and elsewhere, which has 2x CPU overhead. Unlike MTE, HWASan cannot be used for code the project does not build itself, meaning user-installed apps and userspace driver and HAL code, because AOSP removed that code for Pixels in Android 16.

The BFU and AFU Security Gap - Titan M3 Gains versus the Loss of MTE

The official account acknowledges that the Pixel 11 has security improvements. Its August 29 post lists the move to post-quantum secure verified boot (ML-DSA), the replacement of Samsung Shannon IMS with AOSP IMS, and Titan M3, which should significantly improve protection against data extraction in the Before First Unlock (BFU) state. It adds that cutting MTE ruined this, and it judged that even though Titan M3 raises BFU security, losing MTE craters security in the After First Unlock (AFU) state.

A follow-up comment on August 30 spells this out. If the Titan M3 does greatly improve security, the benefit will mainly be protection against brute force attacks in the BFU state, while devices without MTE will be much weaker against exploitation in the AFU state. The Titan M3 also benefits the security of hardware keystore keys, but it pointed out that most apps using the hardware keystore APIs do not opt into the StrongBox keystore, and many apps do not use these APIs at all. The team said it would much rather lose the StrongBox hardware keystore than lose MTE or even PAC, because protecting the OS from being exploited matters more than better protecting a subset of keys after it has been exploited.

  • Post-quantum verified boot (ML-DSA). The official account says it makes no practical difference at present. There is a slim chance of it mattering within the seven years of support, and it is a hedge against future quantum computers able to break RSA 4096. It also said verified boot does not overlap with what MTE defends against.
  • AOSP IMS. Replacing Samsung Shannon IMS is listed as an improvement.
  • Titan M3. Expected to significantly improve protection against data extraction in the BFU state, though the team does not regard it as one of the most important security features.
  • Improvement for the price. The official account assesses the Pixel 11 as much more expensive for an incremental CPU improvement, the same underpowered GPU and reduced RAM for the Pro base models, and says it only just caught up to the last generation of Qualcomm cellular radio.

Ecosystem Comparison - Apple MIE, Android AAPM, and Snapdragon

The official account criticizes the Pixel 11’s missing MTE by comparison with other platforms. Apple’s Memory Integrity Enforcement (MIE) is an always-enabled feature on the iPhone 17, which it describes as a high-quality implementation of MTE using the latest standard extensions. It uses MTE in the most secure mode in the kernel and a large portion of userspace, and the account says Apple did a very good job integrating it.

Apple’s MIE and Android 16+ AAPM do not use MTE for user-installed apps unless those explicitly opt in. GrapheneOS enables it for more apps automatically, has a toggle for users to opt in for every user-installed app, and has a per-app toggle to opt out for incompatible apps, which are uncommon. Neither iOS nor Android encourages app developers to opt into MTE and other more aggressive security features, and Apple’s documentation warns developers of performance and stability issues. GrapheneOS’s approach enables forcing the use of MTE in the standard allocators regardless.

The official account also presented a chipset comparison. It says the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 is ahead of the Pixel 11 in the following ways.

  • Single-threaded CPU performance. About 40% higher.
  • Multi-threaded CPU performance. About 80% higher.
  • GPU performance. Over 100% higher.
  • Cellular radio. Far better.
  • MTE. It finally has it. The account says the next generation is what will be in the first Motorola with GrapheneOS.

A follow-up comment on August 31 points to the Pixel 10a as an alternative. It is still being sold; its display and cameras are worse, but it is not a bad device, and its CPU is only a bit slower than the Pixel 10 or Pixel 11. It arguably has a better GPU than both, since it kept the previous SoC’s Mali GPU instead of the poorly received PowerVR GPU, it has the same cellular radio as the regular Pixel 9 and 10, and it has most of the seven years of support remaining, plus MTE.

Loss of AOSP Reference Device Status and the Motorola Partnership

The official account’s August 29 post explains that the Pixel 9a and earlier devices (including Nexus devices) were the Android Open Source Project reference devices. Pixel support was removed from AOSP with Android 16, so it is now harder to support Pixels than many other devices, and massive progress towards open-source firmware and driver libraries was discarded. A September 1 post says Google has been increasingly silent in response to its communications since dropping Pixel support from AOSP with Android 16 in June 2025.

The official account says GrapheneOS ships AOSP patches months earlier than the stock Pixel OS and Linux kernel patches many months earlier. It still relies on Google for firmware and most driver updates, and it would like to move to new kernel branches earlier. These things can be improved with its Motorola partnership, it says. What the official account has said about Motorola is as follows.

  • Chip in the first Motorola device. The August 29 post says the next generation of the Snapdragon is what will be in the first Motorola with GrapheneOS.
  • Timing. In an August 31 comment it corrected the record: it has quite clearly said the first Motorola flagship will be in 2027, and the earlier remark that it might be late 2026 came before it knew that the Motorola Signature (2026) would not provide MTE.
  • Effect of the partnership. Reliance on firmware and driver updates, and the timing of moves to new kernel branches, can be improved with the Motorola partnership, it says.

The conclusion this comparison suggests is already in the team’s statements. It says the Pixel 10 is cheaper with similar hardware and MTE, and it recommended the Pixel 8, 9 and 10. On October 3 the official account wrote again that it will not support a new device without MTE for around seven years.

The official account also pins some hope on the Pixel 11a. Since the Pixel 10a was really a 9th generation Pixel, it hopes the 11a will likewise be based on the 10th generation and include MTE. That is an expression of hope, and nothing about Google’s plans has been confirmed.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • Why MTE is missing from the shipped Pixel 11 firmware. The official account writes that it does not know whether there are serious CPU errata or whether it simply performs very badly, says Google has not answered, and says the leaks reported by the media do not match its own testing.
  • Whether MTE support will later ship on the Pixel 11 series, and GrapheneOS’s final support decision in that case. The October 1 position is conditional, and the account wrote that it is not clear whether MTE will be supported.
  • When the hardware wording of the August 29 first post was edited. The earlier version seen when this article was first prepared said “near certainly hardware”, while the current wording is “potentially hardware”. The time of the edit could not be confirmed.
  • The model name, price and exact release date of the first Motorola GrapheneOS device. The official account says only 2027.
  • The chipset and MTE status of the Pixel 11a. There is only the official account’s expression of hope. The Pixel 11 is absent from the NDK documentation’s list of MTE-capable devices, but its last-updated date of July 8 means that absence cannot serve as evidence.
