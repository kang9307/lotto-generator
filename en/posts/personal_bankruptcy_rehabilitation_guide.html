Introduction: Two Legal Exits from Overwhelming Debt

Korean law offers two formal debt-relief pathways for individuals who cannot repay their debts: personal rehabilitation (개인회생) and personal bankruptcy and discharge (개인파산·면책). Both are grounded in the Act on Rehabilitation and Bankruptcy of Debtors (채무자 회생 및 파산에 관한 법률) and aim to give debtors a fresh start — but they work quite differently.

The simplest distinction: personal rehabilitation is for people who have income but debts they cannot fully service; personal bankruptcy is for people who have neither income nor assets sufficient to repay their debts. This guide covers both: eligibility, step-by-step procedures, repayment terms, discharge, credit impact, and how to access free legal aid. Note that this article covers Korean domestic law, applicable to individuals residing in or under the jurisdiction of South Korea.

2. Personal Rehabilitation: Eligibility, Procedure, and Discharge

2.1 Who Can Apply

Under the easylaw.go.kr guidelines, all four of the following conditions must be met:

  • Individual debtor only: corporations and associations are excluded. Must be a wage earner or self-employed individual.
  • Continuing future income: salary, pension, or regular business revenue that is expected to continue.
  • Debt caps: unsecured debts under KRW 1 billion; secured debts under KRW 1.5 billion.
  • Insolvency or risk thereof: currently unable or imminently unable to service debts.

Debtors who have existing income but file for bankruptcy instead of rehabilitation may have the bankruptcy petition dismissed as an abuse of process.

2.2 Filing

Applications are submitted to the competent district court (or the Seoul Rehabilitation Court for Seoul cases). Key documents include the application form, a statement of circumstances, asset and liability schedules, an income/expense summary, a preliminary repayment plan, and income evidence (pay stubs, bank statements).

2.3 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Application: File at the court
  2. Comprehensive stay order: Court suspends enforcement actions and auctions
  3. Document review / rehabilitation supervisor interview: Court may request additional documents; a court-appointed supervisor may interview the debtor
  4. Commencement decision: Court formally commences personal rehabilitation proceedings
  5. Creditors' meeting: Creditors submit opinions on the repayment plan
  6. Repayment plan approval: Court confirms the repayment plan
  7. Repayment execution: Debtor remits monthly payments for 3 years
  8. Discharge: Upon completion, court discharges remaining debts

The entire process typically takes 12 to 18 months from filing to discharge.

2.4 Disposable Income and Repayment Amount

The monthly repayment amount equals disposable income: gross monthly income minus court-recognized living expenses for the debtor and dependents. The living expense baseline is derived from the median income standard set by the court. This amount is remitted monthly to the court's account for distribution.

2.5 Discharge Effect

After 3 years of faithful repayment, the court grants a discharge, extinguishing all remaining debts covered by the repayment plan. Certain debts are excluded from discharge: taxes, fines, child support, and debts arising from fraud.

3. Personal Bankruptcy: Eligibility, Procedure, and Discharge

3.1 Who Can Apply

  • Individual debtors only: no debt ceiling.
  • Insolvency: unable to repay debts from existing assets.
  • No income requirement: however, if income exists, rehabilitation should be explored first.
  • Re-application: 7 years must have elapsed since a prior discharge.

3.2 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Application: Simultaneous filing for bankruptcy and discharge at the court
  2. Debtor examination: Court reviews documents and interviews debtor
  3. Bankruptcy declaration: Court declares bankruptcy; bankruptcy trustee takes over asset management
  4. Asset investigation and liquidation: Trustee investigates and liquidates assets
  5. Distribution: Proceeds distributed proportionally to creditors
  6. Discharge hearing: Court deliberates on whether to grant discharge
  7. Discharge decision: Court grants or denies discharge
  8. Reinstatement: Automatic upon discharge confirmation (no separate procedure)

The process typically takes 6 months to 1 year from filing.

3.3 Occupational Restrictions During Bankruptcy

During the period between bankruptcy declaration and discharge/reinstatement, certain civil and professional rights are restricted. These include serving as a civil servant, attorney, physician, or in other licensed professions, and acting as a legal representative or guardian. All restrictions lift automatically upon discharge.

3.4 Grounds for Denying Discharge

  • Concealment, destruction, or unauthorized transfer of assets
  • False statements in the creditor list or asset schedule
  • Prior discharge within the past 7 years
  • Gambling, reckless spending, or other conduct that caused or worsened insolvency
  • Failure to cooperate with the court or bankruptcy trustee

4. Credit Impact and Recovery

4.1 Public Credit Record

Upon discharge, the court reports the fact to Korea Credit Information Services (KCIS / 한국신용정보원). This public record typically affects credit scoring for up to 5 years. During this period, new loans and credit card applications may face restrictions.

4.2 Early Deletion for Rehabilitation Debtors

Personal rehabilitation debtors who have made faithful repayments for 12 or more consecutive months may request early deletion of the public record through the court notification system. This can accelerate credit recovery meaningfully.

4.3 Credit Recovery Timeline

  • Immediately after discharge: Credit score near minimum
  • 6–12 months: Public record deletion begins; score starts to rise
  • 1–2 years: With diligent credit use (small cards, on-time payments), scores can reach 700–800 range
  • 3–5 years: Normal financial activities generally restored

The Credit Counseling and Recovery Service (ccrs.or.kr) provides post-discharge credit counseling to assist in this process.

5. Costs and Legal Aid

5.1 Court Filing Costs

Court fees (stamp duty and service fees) typically run in the range of several hundred thousand Korean won, depending on the case. Attorney fees add to this cost.

5.2 Korea Legal Aid Corporation (Free Support)

The Korea Legal Aid Corporation (klac.or.kr) provides free or low-cost legal consultation and representation for eligible individuals (income-tested). Services cover both personal rehabilitation and bankruptcy filings. Contact: 132 (no area code needed) or visit any of 132 offices nationwide.

5.3 Credit Counseling and Recovery Service

Before entering court proceedings, consider the Credit Counseling and Recovery Service (ccrs.or.kr) for private debt restructuring (individual workout / pre-workout). This avoids the public credit record of court proceedings and has a simpler process, though creditor consent is required and interest reductions may be more limited.

Conclusion: A Legal Right, Not a Moral Failure

Both personal rehabilitation and bankruptcy are legal rights enshrined in Korean law — tools designed to give struggling individuals a genuine economic fresh start. The choice of which path to take depends heavily on your income, assets, debt type, and history. This article provides general information only.

Always consult the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (132), a court civil service desk, or a licensed attorney for advice specific to your situation.
