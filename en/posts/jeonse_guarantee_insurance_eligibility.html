Korean Jeonse Deposit Return Guarantee - Eligibility Across HUG, HF and SGI

Jeonse ratio, senior liens, filing windows, premiums, and the reasons applications get turned down (as of August 2026)

Aug 18, 2026 Finance & Economy
This article explains a public scheme in general terms and is not financial or legal advice. Confirm eligibility requirements and review outcomes with the guarantee institution concerned. The figures below were checked against institutional and government sources on August 18, 2026. Requirements and rates are subject to change.

Introduction: One Sheet of Paper That Decides a Lot

Before going further, a word on what jeonse is, because it has no close equivalent outside Korea. Under a jeonse lease a tenant hands the landlord a very large lump sum, often a majority of the property value, pays no monthly rent, and receives the entire sum back at the end of the term. The landlord holds and invests that money in the meantime. It is, in effect, an interest free loan from tenant to landlord, secured by nothing more than the landlord ability and willingness to repay. Deposits of several hundred thousand US dollars on an ordinary flat are unremarkable.

That structure produces one anxiety above all others: will the money come back? The jeonse deposit return guarantee is the market answer. If the landlord does not repay, an institution pays the tenant instead.

Two misconceptions travel with this product. First, enrollment is not a form you fill in but an underwriting decision you have to pass, and what decides it is mostly the property, not the tenant. Second, there is no single product. Three institutions run schemes under similar names with different eligibility, different limits and different premiums. Any explanation of the conditions that does not name the institution is half wrong.

This article looks at the product itself: what it actually covers, where the three providers diverge, how long the filing window stays open, what sets the premium, and why certain properties get refused. Choosing a property and the basic safeguards of resident registration and fixed date stamping are covered in the jeonse fraud prevention guide and in chonsegwon registration versus fixed date; reading the property register is in what the Korean property register proves. This piece stays off that ground.

1. What the Guarantee Actually Does

The mechanism is simple. The tenant pays a premium. If a guarantee event occurs, the institution pays the deposit in the landlord place. The institution then pursues the landlord for that money. In the phrasing of KDI research, the guarantor collects a premium, discharges the landlord obligation to return the deposit when a guarantee event arises, and afterwards exercises a right of indemnity against the landlord.

That one sentence contains almost everything about the product character.

  • It advances money; it does not erase a debt. The obligation survives intact, only the creditor changes
  • The institution is looking at recoverability. Indemnity is the premise, so a file with nothing to recover from does not get written
  • Hence the property is the axis of review. Collateral headroom and encumbrances rank ahead of tenant income or credit
  • It is a guarantee more than an insurance policy. People call it jeonse insurance, but the HUG and HF products are named guarantees; only the SGI product carries the word insurance

Why does the distinction matter? Because it tells you what to change when a file is refused. If the problem sits with the applicant, another attempt may work. If the problem is the senior liens or the valuation of that particular flat, the same property will fare much the same at any provider. A property that fails the test fails it; switching counters does not fix it.

2. Three Institutions, Not One Product

As of August 2026, three bodies run deposit return guarantees: HUG (Korea Housing and Urban Guarantee Corporation) with the jeonse deposit return guarantee, HF (Korea Housing Finance Corporation) with the jeonse jikim guarantee, and SGI (Seoul Guarantee Insurance) with jeonse deposit protection credit insurance.

The sharpest divide is the route in. HF general jeonse jikim guarantee requires the applicant to be a tenant already using, or about to simultaneously use, the corporation jeonse loan guarantee, and to be a head of household who has paid at least 5 percent of the deposit. It is tied to borrowing. A lease funded from savings never reaches that counter. The HUG product carries no such precondition and is filed by the tenant directly.

On paper HUG and HF look alike, but they feel different in practice. At HF the loan assessment and the guarantee assessment effectively run as one flow at the bank counter, while the HUG route is a separate decision the tenant makes and files. With a loan in the picture you can weigh both routes; without one, the field narrows.

3. The Filing Window: Half the Term

The most commonly missed element of this product is timing. Meeting every substantive requirement counts for nothing once the window has closed on that contract.

Per government policy briefing guidance, a new jeonse contract must be filed before half of the contract term has elapsed, counted from the later of the balance payment date shown on the contract and the resident registration date. Note that the clock does not start at signature. HF likewise requires that its general jeonse jikim guarantee be applied for while the lease term is one year or longer and before half of that term has passed.

A renewal gets its own window. On the HUG side it runs from one month before the expiry of the prior contract until half of the renewed term has elapsed.

  • On a two year lease, the usable window is roughly the first year after moving in
  • Because the clock starts at balance payment or registration rather than signature, a long gap between signing and completion means you should work out your own window
  • Miss it and there is no route within that term; you wait for the next renewal
  • And if values have fallen by then, the limit formula turns against you and that route may close too

One decision rule is worth fixing here. If you are even considering the guarantee, settle it right after the balance is paid. Delay buys you a few months of premium and costs you the option itself. The senior lien position discussed below can deteriorate over time, and it does not improve on its own.

4. Eligible Properties and Excluded Ones

The range of eligible housing is wider than most people assume. The Seoul Housing Portal lists the HUG deposit return guarantee as covering detached and multi household houses, multi family houses, row houses and multi unit dwellings, senior welfare housing, residential officetels, and apartments. The impression that only apartments qualify is wrong.

The obstacle is not the type but the condition of the specific property. On its eligibility checklist HF excludes properties recorded as illegal structures on the building ledger and cases where the deposit is subject to third party interference such as seizure or provisional attachment. Both are settled by a single document, leaving no room for negotiation.

  • Illegal structure - once recorded on the building ledger it is disqualifying on its own. Rooftop extensions and neighbourhood facilities converted to residential use without permission are common causes
  • Seizure and provisional attachment - entries in section gap of the register signal a live debt dispute on the landlord side
  • Trust registration - with property held in trust, the registered owner is the trustee. Who signed the lease and where the letting authority sits become immediate questions
  • Multi household houses - one building holds several tenancies, so the deposits of other households are aggregated when collateral headroom is assessed. HF applies a separate cap of 80 percent of property value for detached and multi household housing
  • Landlord status - HF requires the tenant to be a Korean national and the landlord to be a national or a corporation, excluding public rental operators and foreign corporations

Here is where visibility splits. Illegal structure status shows on the building ledger; seizures, attachments and trust registration show on the certified copy of the register. Both can be obtained before signing. The aggregate deposits of other households in a multi household building, by contrast, are hard for a tenant to establish independently, and that opacity is the structural weakness of multi household leases.

5. What Sets the Premium

The premium is not a flat fee but a rate tied to risk, and in 2025 both institutions overhauled their rate structures within a month of each other.

HUG applied new rates to applications from March 31, 2025. As reported, the previous band of 0.115 to 0.154 percent per year widened to 0.097 to 0.211 percent, with a jeonse ratio of 70 percent as the pivot: up to 20 percent cheaper below it, up to 30 percent dearer above. The debt ratio brackets, previously split at 80 percent, were rebuilt into three: 70 percent or less, above 70 up to 80 percent, and above 80 percent.

HF introduced LTV banded rates for new guarantee applications from March 1, 2025. The rates published in the corporation notice are as follows.

HF calculates its LTV as (total senior claims + lease deposit) / property value. It shares a name with mortgage LTV but not a numerator, so the two should not be conflated. The corporation also stated that term extensions of existing guarantees without an increase in the deposit keep the previous rate.

Discounts exist. In the 2025 HUG revision the 60 percent discount for low income households was retained, the discount for socially considered groups such as multi child, disabled, elderly and newlywed households was adjusted from 50 to 40 percent, and the discount for one home owners was abolished with a no home ownership condition added. HF also publishes rate reductions for preferential households.

Some invented figures to calibrate the sense of scale. On a deposit of KRW 300 million at 0.15 percent a year, the annual premium is KRW 450,000, or KRW 37,500 a month. The same deposit at 0.04 percent gives KRW 120,000; at 0.211 percent it gives KRW 633,000. A single rate band is worth hundreds of thousands of won a year. These numbers are illustrative only and actual assessment differs by institution.

6. Why Applications Get Refused

Rejection grounds look scattered but share one root: would anything be left to recover if a guarantee event occurred? Seen that way, the conditions line up.

Start with the formula. HUG sets the guarantee ceiling at property value × 90 percent LTV − senior claims; HF requires the deposit to sit at or below 90 percent of property value − total senior claims. Above that line, the requirement is not met.

Non apartment housing carries an extra wrinkle. Unlike apartments with transparent comparables, villas and multi unit dwellings raise the question of what the value even is. During the 2023 response to jeonse fraud, the officially assessed price multiplier was reduced from 150 to 140 percent and the LTV was capped at 90 percent, so their product, 126 percent of the assessed price, became the effective ceiling for non apartment guarantees. This is the so called 126 percent rule. In June 2024 the government announced it would keep the standard while adding a remedy: where a landlord disputes the assessed price, an appraisal commissioned by HUG may be used as the reference property value.

Read the table down the right hand column and a rule appears. Every line but one is checkable before signing. Most refusals are therefore predictable, and they surprise people only because the checking was deferred until after the contract. Looking for ways around the requirements is the wrong direction. A property that fails these tests is one an institution has judged unrecoverable, and that judgment is just as valid a warning for the tenant.

7. Landlord Consent Is No Longer Required

This is where the scheme today differs most from its early form. KDI research records that when the guarantee launched in 2013 a tenant needed the landlord consent to enroll, that the consent obligation was abolished in February 2018 to protect tenant rights, and that enrollment surged afterwards.

The practical weight of that change is considerable. While consent was a requirement, a refusal ended the matter, and the properties where consent was hardest to obtain were precisely the ones where cover was most needed. Today the tenant files alone.

Two things still need separating, though.

  • Consent is not required, but landlord side facts are assessed. The landlord status, the encumbrances on the property and the landlord debt position all enter the review
  • Some stages still need cooperation. Establishing the aggregate deposits of other households in a multi household building is the clearest case
  • Special clauses promising not to obstruct enrollment are common in contracts, but a clause does not substitute for meeting the underwriting requirements

8. When a Guarantee Event Occurs

Being covered does not mean money arrives automatically at expiry. You have to claim, and the claim has prerequisites. Reverse the order and you lose time or rights.

First, the definition. HF describes two types of guarantee event.

  • Type 1 - the lease has been terminated or has expired, one month has passed, and the tenant has still not had the deposit returned
  • Type 2 - during the lease the property went to auction or public sale, and after the distribution process concluded part of the deposit remained unrecovered

Type 1 is the ordinary route. Expiry alone does not create the event; there is a one month grace period. And that month is not spent waiting. Laid out in order, the sequence HF publishes runs as follows.

  1. At least two months before expiry, notify the landlord by certified mail that you are declining renewal
  2. Assign the deposit return claim to the guarantee institution
  3. File with the court for a lease registration order, available from the day after expiry, and complete the registration
  4. Once one month has passed after termination and the guarantee event has arisen, submit the claim form
  5. The institution conducts its claim review, notifies the outcome, and makes the subrogated payment

Missing step one hurts most in practice. Under the Housing Lease Protection Act, avoiding implied renewal requires the tenant to give notice of refusal within a set period before expiry; without it the contract is treated as renewed and the expiry date itself moves back. If expiry moves, no guarantee event arises.

Step three is equally order sensitive. Moving out before the lease registration is entered surrenders possession and resident registration, which can collapse the opposing power and priority repayment right. That is precisely why the lease registration order exists. Confirm the registration is complete, then move. The legal background to this stage is in the tenant rights protection guide, and recovery without a guarantee is covered in the deposit return lawsuit guide.

9. Covered but Still Unpaid

There are places where a claim stalls even with a certificate in hand. Usually it is not a defect in the product but a tenant side requirement that collapsed mid term.

  • No renewal refusal notice, so the contract renewed - expiry moves back and no guarantee event arises
  • Moving out before the lease registration - without opposing power and priority repayment right intact, the claim requirements wobble
  • Missing resident registration or fixed date stamp - HF explicitly requires possession, registration and date stamping to secure opposing power and priority repayment
  • Contract terms diverging from what was filed - an unreported deposit increase or amendment puts the covered contract out of step with the real one
  • Covered up to the limit rather than in full - whatever the formula trims sits outside the guarantee

That last point is the most misunderstood. Being enrolled and having the whole deposit covered are not the same statement. The ceiling is property value × LTV − senior claims, so a property carrying a prior mortgage may leave only part of the deposit inside the cover. Put the guaranteed amount on the certificate next to the contract deposit and compare them as soon as you are enrolled.

10. Read Every Requirement With Its Date Attached

These conditions do not hold still. The trajectory of recent years alone shows it.

  • February 2018 - landlord consent obligation abolished
  • 2023 - during the jeonse fraud response, the assessed price multiplier moved from 150 to 140 percent and the LTV from 100 to 90 percent, producing the 126 percent rule
  • June 2024 - the government announced the non apartment 126 percent rule would be retained, adding that where a landlord disputes the assessed price an appraisal commissioned by HUG may serve as the reference value
  • November to December 2024 - proposals to cut the LTV from 90 to 80 percent were reported; HUG stated at the time that it was not considering implementation
  • March 2025 - new premium structures took effect, at HF from March 1 and at HUG from March 31

Memorising the conditions is therefore of little use. Handle them this way instead: carry the institution and the date alongside every figure, and re confirm on the provider page when a contract is imminent. A rate quoted in an article two years old is unlikely to match today invoice, and the gap runs to hundreds of thousands of won.

12. Frequently Asked Questions

Who pays the premium?

The tenant files the application, so the tenant bears the premium as the default structure. A contract may allocate it to the landlord, but that is an arrangement between the parties and does not alter the relationship with the guarantee institution.

Does it apply to a monthly rent lease?

The product name says jeonse, but whether a tenancy with a deposit and monthly rent qualifies turns on each institution requirements. The materials checked here address jeonse and lease deposits, so they cannot be assumed to carry over to a small deposit monthly rental. Confirm with the institution directly.

Is the premium refunded on early termination?

Treatment of the unexpired portion is a matter for the policy terms. The refund basis was not confirmed for this article.

Can two institutions cover the same deposit?

There is no benefit in duplicate cover on one deposit, and each institution underwrites within its own limit formula. It is better framed as choosing one.

If the application is refused, should the lease be cancelled?

It depends first on the ground. A missed window says nothing about the property; a jeonse ratio or senior lien failure says the risk on that property is genuinely high. Whether the contract can be cancelled depends on its clauses and the individual facts, so take professional advice.
