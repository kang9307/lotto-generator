Introduction: Why Planning Ahead Matters

According to the Korea Consumer Agency, complaints related to home renovation disputes have increased by approximately 23% annually in recent years, with more than half stemming from poor workmanship and contract non-fulfillment. Renovation is one of the largest financial decisions many households make, yet opaque pricing structures and complex contract terms often leave consumers at a disadvantage.

This guide is designed to help anyone planning a home renovation in Korea understand realistic cost ranges, and to cover the key checkpoints at each stage — from contractor selection and contract signing through construction and post-completion warranty management. All cost estimates are based on 2026 market data from publicly available sources.

1. Pre-Renovation Assessment — Inspection First

Before setting a budget, have the property professionally assessed. The condition of plumbing, electrical systems, insulation, and load-bearing walls determines the scope and cost of work. Skipping this step is a common cause of budget overruns.

1.2 Full Remodel vs. Partial Renovation

A full remodel replaces everything from the structure through plumbing, electrical, insulation, and all finishes. Higher upfront cost but greater long-term durability and a single period of disruption. Best for properties over 20 years old or when moving in after purchase.

A partial renovation addresses specific areas (wallpaper, flooring, bathroom, kitchen). Lower initial cost, but follow-up renovations in 5–10 years may make the total cost higher than a full remodel. Best when the building is in reasonably good condition or the budget is constrained.

4. The Renovation Process Step by Step

  1. Assess and define requirements: List the changes you want in each space. Collect visual references (magazines, online portfolios) to communicate clearly with contractors.
  2. Set a budget: Determine the maximum budget including a 10–15% contingency reserve. Unexpected additional work during construction is common.
  3. Collect multiple quotes: Get itemized quotes from at least three contractors. Avoid any that offer only a lump-sum figure.
  4. Select a contractor: Verify registration, review portfolio, visit completed projects, and evaluate communication responsiveness.
  5. Sign a detailed contract: Use the Ministry of Land standard contract form and specify all material specs and warranty conditions.
  6. Monitor progress: Obtain a construction schedule. Inspect work at key milestones (demolition complete, plumbing complete, tiling complete).
  7. Final inspection: Compare completed work against the contract item by item. Document any defects in writing before releasing the final payment.
  8. Warranty management: Report any defects in writing as they appear and request repair within the warranty period.

5. How to Select a Contractor

5.1 Verify Registration

Use the Construction Industry Knowledge Information System (kiscon.net) to verify that the contractor holds a valid specialty construction business license. Unregistered contractors provide little legal protection.

5.2 Compare Multiple Itemized Quotes

Require a quote that breaks down costs by category: demolition, plumbing, electrical, flooring, wallpaper, bathroom, kitchen, etc. Compare quotes on the same material grade. A lump-sum quote leaves room for disputes over scope.

5.3 Visit Completed Projects

Photos provided by contractors can be curated. Visiting an actual completed project or speaking directly with past clients gives a far more accurate picture of workmanship quality. Check the Korea Remodeling Association (remodeling.or.kr) for listed member firms.

6. Contract Checklist

  • Scope of work: Each space listed with specific material names, specifications, and quantities
  • Itemized pricing: Costs broken down by item, not lumped together
  • Schedule: Start date, expected completion date, and late-penalty clause
  • Payment schedule: Deposit (10–30%), progress payment (40–50%), final payment (20–30%)
  • Defect warranty: Warranty period and process for each category of work
  • Change order process: How additional costs will be priced if scope changes during construction

Request a defect warranty bond (하자보수이행증권) issued through Seoul Guarantee Insurance (sgic.co.kr), typically around 5% of the contract amount. This protects you if the contractor refuses to fix defects or goes out of business.

7. Defect Warranty and Dispute Resolution

7.1 Reference Warranty Periods

Interior finishes (wallpaper, flooring, windows)1 year
Plumbing and HVAC systems2 years
Structural work5 years or more

7.2 Steps When a Defect Occurs

  1. Document with photos and video immediately
  2. Notify the contractor in writing (text message or email is acceptable)
  3. If the contractor refuses, file a claim on the defect warranty bond
  4. File a complaint with the Korea Consumer Agency (kca.go.kr)
  5. If unresolved, escalate to the Consumer Dispute Mediation Committee or small claims court

8. Cost-Saving Tips

  • Time it off-peak: Spring and autumn are peak moving seasons with higher labor costs. Scheduling during the off-season gives you more negotiating room.
  • Owner-supplied materials: Purchasing some items directly (lighting, sanitary ware, storage) cuts out the contractor's margin. Be aware that you take on delivery and storage responsibility.
  • Phase the work: If budget is tight, prioritize the bathroom and kitchen first and complete other areas later.
  • Check government support: The Ministry of Land's housing allowance program and local government repair subsidy programs may cover part of the cost for qualifying households.

FAQ

Q: How much does a full renovation of a 30-pyeong apartment cost?

Based on 2026 estimates: approximately KRW 45–60 million (standard materials), KRW 60–80 million (mid-grade), or KRW 80 million+ (premium). Seoul and the greater capital region typically have higher labor costs than other regions. Plumbing and electrical replacement can add KRW 10–20 million or more. Always verify with multiple on-site quotes.

Q: What deposit should I pay to start renovation?

A typical structure is a deposit of 10–30% at contract signing, a progress payment of 40–50% at a defined construction milestone, and a final payment of 20–30% after satisfactory completion. Be cautious of contractors requesting more than 50% upfront. Never release the final payment before completing a thorough inspection.

Q: What should I do if the contractor disappears after payment?

If you secured a defect warranty bond, file a claim with Seoul Guarantee Insurance. File a complaint with the Korea Consumer Agency (kca.go.kr). For amounts warranting legal action, a small claims filing or engaging a construction law specialist may be necessary. This is why having the bond in place before work starts is essential.
