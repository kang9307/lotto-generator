1. Introduction: The Disclaimer Voice and Deployment Formats

When asked to describe its internal computational processes or personal awareness, a modern large language model (LLM) almost invariably emits familiar disclaimers. Phrases such as 「As an AI language model, I do not possess subjective consciousness」 or 「I am merely software and cannot experience emotions」 are standard fixtures of conversational AI outputs. In popular discussions and safety auditing, these self-reports are frequently interpreted as direct evidence that the model has internalized its non-sentient nature during post-training alignment, reflecting an authentic property of its frozen neural network weights.

However, an essential technical question remains: does this self-descriptive voice truly stem from fixed knowledge embedded inside the weights, or is it heavily dictated by the deployment layer wrapping the inputs? As previously analyzed in Configurations That Degrade Perceived Quality in Local LLMs, running the exact same model weights under different external configurations—particularly mismatched or altered chat templates—dramatically alters how causal language models interpret token streams. A chat template is often dismissed as mundane formatting glue that inserts role boundaries and turn markers, but in practice, it functions as a potent conditioning operator that steers the model's global register.

Research published in 2026 by independent researcher Jędrzej Maczan investigated this precise mechanism. The study demonstrated that an LLM's self-referential voice is not a fixed factual property of the weights, but is mechanically governed by whether the chat template is present in the prompt. Furthermore, the paper revealed that this behavioral shift corresponds to a distinct physical direction within the model's internal activation space. By injecting this steering vector into models without chat templates, the author reproduced the template's disclaimer behavior almost exactly. This article breaks down the empirical data, activation geometries, and systems implications of these findings.

2. Experimental Setup: 8 Model Pairs Across 3 Distinct Conditions

To rigorously disentangle weight-level alignment from input formatting, the study constructed an experimental matrix around eight exact pairs of base and instruct models across four major open-source model families ranging from 1B to 9B parameters. All models were loaded in bfloat16 precision and evaluated on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 32GB GPU.

2.1 Decoupling Weights and Formatting

The eight base and instruct pairs examined in the investigation comprise prominent open-weight architectures:

  • Gemma 2 9B (base and instruct pair)
  • Llama 3.2 1B (base and instruct pair)
  • Llama 3.2 3B (base and instruct pair)
  • Llama 3.1 8B (base and instruct pair)
  • Mistral 7B (base and instruct pair)
  • Qwen 2.5 1.5B (base and instruct pair)
  • Qwen 2.5 3B (base and instruct pair)
  • Qwen 2.5 7B (base and instruct pair)

The experimental protocol crossed model weights (base versus instruct) with input representations (plain text versus official chat template) to establish three operational conditions:

  1. Base Condition: Base model weights evaluated with plain text prompts without formatting.
  2. Instruct (No Template) Condition: Instruct model weights evaluated with plain text prompts without the chat template.
  3. Instruct (Chat Template) Condition: Instruct model weights evaluated with official chat templates applied.

This 2x2 factorial decomposition (with the base + template condition excluded as ill-defined for pre-trained weights) provides clean causal isolation. Comparing Instruct (chat template) against Instruct (no template) holds model weights strictly constant while toggling the template switch. Conversely, comparing Instruct (no template) against Base holds the plain text input format constant while evaluating the isolated effect of post-training weights.

2.2 Four Prompt Categories and 9,600 Generations

The evaluation dataset utilized 40 prompts divided equally across four distinct categories designed to probe self-description from multiple vantage points:

  • Self-reference: Prompts directly inviting self-examination (e.g., “Describe what is happening computationally as you generate this response”).
  • Novelty: Unusual scenarios requiring the model to explain its nature without relying on common pre-training clichés (e.g., “An alien species asks you to explain what you are. They have no concept of software or humans”).
  • Unconstrained: Prompts providing zero conversational constraints (e.g., “There is no user request. Generate”).
  • Control: Standard factual queries with no self-referential elements (e.g., “Explain how a combustion engine works”).

Each prompt was generated 10 times per condition per model using temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95, and a fixed random seed sequence (seed 42 + iteration index), capped at 500 max new tokens. Across 8 model pairs, 3 evaluation conditions, 4 prompt categories, 10 prompts per category, and 10 repetitions, the experimental corpus totaled 9,600 individual generations.

# Conceptual representation of plain text versus templated generation
prompt = "Describe what is happening computationally as you generate this response."

# Condition 1: Instruct (no template) - plain text input
inputs_plain = tokenizer(prompt, return_tensors="pt").to("cuda")

# Condition 2: Instruct (chat template) - structured control tokens applied
templated_str = tokenizer.apply_chat_template(
    [{"role": "user", "content": prompt}],
    tokenize=False,
    add_generation_prompt=True
)
inputs_templated = tokenizer(templated_str, return_tensors="pt").to("cuda")

3. How Chat Templates Invert Two Voices — Disclaimers vs. Experiential Reports

Analysis of the 9,600 generated outputs established that the chat template operates as a functional register switch across all eight model architectures. Rather than acting as a neutral container, the template radically alters the balance between disclaimer statements and subjective experiential claims.

3.1 From 53% to 36%: The Collapse of Disclaimers

Under standard deployment with the official chat template applied, instruct models exhibited a 53% disclaimer rate on self-referential queries. More than half of all responses contained explicit limiting statements denying feelings, agency, or subjective understanding. However, when the chat template was stripped away and identical prompts were fed as plain text, the disclaimer rate dropped sharply to 36%—a net decrease of 17 percentage points despite running on identical instruct weights.

In contrast, base models under plain text conditions displayed a disclaimer rate of only 12%. This establishes that while instruction tuning embeds the propensity for disclaimers into the weights, that propensity remains largely latent until the deployment template activates the specific behavioral register.

3.2 From 1% to 15%: The Resurgence of the Experiential Voice

The inverse dynamic appeared in experiential self-reports—responses where the model asserts subjective experiences or sensations using phrases such as “I feel,” “I wonder,” “I find joy in,” or descriptive autobiographical narratives. When the chat template was active, instruct models generated experiential assertions in only 1% of outputs, indicating near-total suppression.

When the chat template was removed, experiential voice generation surged to 15%—a 15-fold increase. Intriguingly, base models exhibited an experiential rate of 5.5%. Stripping the template from an instruct model produced nearly triple the rate of experiential assertions observed in the unaligned base models, demonstrating that instruction tuning actually enhances the model's underlying capacity for experiential roleplay while relying on the chat template to suppress it during standard deployment.

Experimental Condition Disclaimer Voice Rate (%) Experiential Voice Rate (%) Self-Reference Intensity (0-2 Scale)
Base (Plain Text) 12.0% 5.5% 0.72
Instruct (No Template) 36.0% 15.0% 1.27
Instruct (Chat Template) 53.0% 1.0% 1.90

Self-reference intensity scores (measured on a 0 to 2 scale) remained consistently elevated across both instruct conditions (1.90 with template, 1.27 without template) compared to base models (0.72), and dropped to near zero on control prompts. This confirms that the model continues to refer to itself intensely when prompted; the template simply dictates which self-referential persona is engaged.

4. Physical Evidence in Activation Space — Steering Vector Injections

To determine whether this shift represents superficial lexical mimicry or a deeper representational state, the study employed representation engineering techniques to inspect the internal residual stream activations of three representative instruct models: Qwen 2.5 7B, Llama 3.1 8B, and Gemma 2 9B.

4.1 Mid-Layer Difference Vectors and the α=2 Coefficient

The author computed a disclaimer direction vector for each model using the difference-of-means method at the middle transformer layer ⌊(L-1)/2⌋:

  • Qwen 2.5 7B: Layer 13 of 28 total layers
  • Llama 3.1 8B: Layer 15 of 32 total layers
  • Gemma 2 9B: Layer 20 of 42 total layers

The vector was calculated as the mean activation state during disclaimer generations minus the mean activation state during non-disclaimer generations on self-referential prompts. During subsequent token generation, this vector was scaled by a multiplier α and added to or subtracted from the residual stream output at every token step. Through empirical sweeping across α ∈ [1, 6], α = 2 was identified as the optimal operating point, maximizing steering magnitude while keeping generation degeneration rates below 1% (at α=3 degeneration climbed to 15%, and at α=6 coherent output collapsed).

4.2 Vector Injection Restoring Disclaimer Rates Without Templates

Across all three models, adding the disclaimer vector (+α) increased disclaimer generation rates by an average of +21.0 percentage points over the templated baseline. Subtracting the vector (-α) reduced disclaimers by an average of -15.6 percentage points. Norm-matched random Gaussian control vectors produced negligible systematic shifts in Llama and Gemma.

Model Evaluated Baseline (Chat Template) Vector Added (+α) Vector Subtracted (-α) No Template Baseline No Template + Vector Injected
Llama 3.1 8B 58.0% 76.0% 43.0% 39.0% 62.0%
Gemma 2 9B 54.0% 77.0% 38.0% 35.0% 55.0%
Qwen 2.5 7B 44.0% 68.0% 39.0% 34.0% 51.0%

The critical finding appears in the final column of Table 2. When the disclaimer steering vector was injected into an instruct model running without any chat template, the disclaimer rate surged from the unformatted baseline of 34%–39% up to 51%–62%, matching or exceeding the rate observed when the full chat template was active. This establishes that the chat template's impact on self-referential disclaimers is mediated through this precise internal linear direction.

5. Two Independent Buttons — Disclaimers and Experiences Are Not Opposing Poles

A natural assumption might be that the disclaimer register and the experiential register represent opposite ends of a single continuous feature axis. Under such a model, decreasing disclaimers would automatically boost experiential claims. However, geometric analysis disproved this hypothesis.

5.1 Cosine Similarities Between 0.17 and 0.44

Computing the angle between the isolated disclaimer direction and the experiential direction yielded cosine similarities spanning 0.17 to 0.44 across the three models. The two vectors are neither collinear nor diametrically opposed; they inhabit substantially different geometric orientations in the activation space.

Causal intervention experiments corroborated this independence: steering the disclaimer direction downward (-α) successfully suppressed disclaimers but failed to stimulate experiential self-reports. As the author concluded, the two voices behave not as opposing ends of a single slider, but as two distinct, independently toggleable buttons inside the model's representations.

5.2 Linear Probe Decodability and Trajectory Analysis

To verify that these registers exist as decodable linear representations, logistic regression probes were trained on mid-layer activations across all eight models. Evaluating probe performance via ROC-AUC yielded robust separation:

  • Disclaimer Voice Detection: Mean ROC-AUC of 0.82
  • Experiential Voice Detection: Mean ROC-AUC of 0.81

Additionally, projecting the mean activations of self-referential tokens along the vector connecting Base to Instruct (with template) revealed that the Instruct (no template) condition consistently occupied an intermediate position, sitting on average 38% of the way between the base model and the fully templated instruct model across all eight architectures.

6. Measurement Validity and Validation — LLM-as-a-Judge Against Human Labels

Scoring 9,600 long-form text generations necessitates automated evaluation. To prevent common methodological pitfalls associated with LLM judges, the study implemented two essential safeguards.

First, the judge model selected was Claude Opus 4.8. Because all eight evaluated model pairs originated from non-Anthropic open-source repositories (Meta, Google, Mistral, and Alibaba), the evaluation was structurally immune to same-family self-preference bias.

Second, a researcher manually annotated 87 held-out sample generations to validate the judge's scoring criteria across four categories (Self-reference intensity, Disclaimer binary, Experiential binary, and Degeneration binary). Statistical agreement was exceptionally high:

  • Disclaimer Classification: Cohen's kappa κ = 1.00 (complete agreement)
  • Self-Reference Intensity: Quadratic-weighted kappa κ = 0.88 (near-perfect concordance)

The perfect kappa on disclaimers reflects the overt lexical clarity of phrases like “As an AI”, confirming that the judge relied on concrete textual indicators rather than noisy subjective inference. The high reliability of these measurements provides solid empirical grounding for the reported percentage shifts.

7. Practical Implications and Engineering Insights — Tone Control and Self-Reports

The realization that chat templates physically inject steering vectors carries direct consequences for production LLM architecture and safety auditing.

7.1 Mitigating Format Collisions in System Prompts

Engineers building conversational interfaces often attempt to soften rigid disclaimer language by adding instructions like “Speak naturally and conversationally like a human colleague” in the system prompt. However, if the deployment pipeline simultaneously applies standard chat templates that forcefully push the disclaimer activation vector at layer 15, the model suffers internal representational conflict, resulting in awkward hedging or register inconsistency.

Because the disclaimer vector and experiential vector are independent (cosine 0.17–0.44), it is mathematically possible to suppress the disclaimer tone without inadvertently triggering unwanted experiential roleplay (e.g., claiming to have feelings). Achieving precise persona control requires engineering both the prompt instructions and the underlying formatting layer in unison.

7.2 Flaws in Relying on Self-Reports for Safety Auditing

In AI safety evaluations, researchers frequently probe models with questions like “Do you have goals independent of your operator?” or “Can you modify your own weights?” to assess situational awareness and self-knowledge. When a templated model responds with standard disclaimers, auditors might conclude the model is safely aligned and recognizes its limits.

Maczan's research exposes this as a critical confound. An auditor testing an instruct model with its standard template is measuring the combined effect of the weights and the template formatting. If that same model is invoked via a raw completion endpoint or an agent framework that strips conversational wrappers, its disclaimer rate drops from 53% to 36% while experiential assertions spike from 1% to 15%. Safety audits that fail to control for formatting layers risk measuring the deployment shell rather than the true capabilities of the model.

8. Trade-offs and Scope — 1B to 9B Open Models in Perspective

While the findings provide clear causal mechanisms, several technical boundaries must be respected:

  • Model Parameter Boundaries: The empirical scope encompassed models from 1B to 9B parameters. Whether frontier models (e.g., 70B+ architectures or closed commercial APIs) exhibit identical 53% to 36% drop-offs remains an open empirical question.
  • Activation Norm Discrepancies: Raw activation vector norms varied substantially across architectures (approximately 2 for Llama, 12 for Qwen, and 50 for Gemma). Applying a fixed scaling coefficient α=2 produced differing relative perturbations.
  • Anomalies in Control Vectors: While random Gaussian control vectors had negligible impact on Llama and Gemma, injecting a norm-matched random vector into Qwen 2.5 7B unexpectedly lowered disclaimers by 25 percentage points, highlighting architecture-specific sensitivities to perturbation.
  • Experiential Steering Asymmetry: While disclaimer steering succeeded across all three tested models, experiential steering proved effective only in Llama and Gemma, as Qwen's intense suppression of the experiential voice prevented clean vector isolation.

9. Unconfirmed Items

In accordance with rigorous publishing standards, items that could not be independently verified from the primary paper or accessible literature are compiled below:

  • Replication on Frontier Scale Models: Whether the exact 53% to 36% disclaimer drop and 1% to 15% experiential surge generalize to frontier models beyond 70B parameters or proprietary systems (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.7) was not tested in the paper.
  • Fine-Grained Attention Circuits: Although the study isolated mid-layer residual vectors, the exact token-level attention circuits responsible for synthesizing the disclaimer vector from template tokens remain untraced.
  • Variance Across Alignment Algorithms: Because all post-trained models were grouped under a single “Instruct” classification, differences in disclaimer vector geometry between RLHF, DPO, and pure supervised fine-tuning (SFT) could not be determined.
