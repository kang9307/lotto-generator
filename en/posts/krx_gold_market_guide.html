The Complete Guide to Trading on Korea's KRX Gold Market

From Opening a Brokerage Gold Account to the Tax-Free Structure

Introduction: Same Gold, Different Taxes

Whenever gold prices climb, interest in owning gold climbs with them. What is less widely understood is that where and how you buy the same gold can change the tax and fees dramatically. A physical bar carries VAT from the moment you buy it, and bank gold accounts or gold-related ETFs tax the gains you make. Among the options, Korea's KRX Gold Market - the physical gold market run by the Korea Exchange - is frequently cited as a particularly tax-advantaged route. Open a gold account at a brokerage and you can trade gold in 1g units much like stocks, and as long as you trade within the account, the trading gains are described as untaxed.

This article walks through what the KRX Gold Market is, how to start trading, the core tax-benefit structure, the VAT that applies when you withdraw physical gold, and how it compares with bars, gold ETFs, and gold banking, along with the risks. The goal is not to recommend a specific product or entry point, but to help you understand the decision criteria behind each method. Rates and rules are current as of July 2026 and may change, so always reconfirm with current sources and a professional before trading.

1. What Is the KRX Gold Market?

The KRX Gold Market is a physical gold trading market operated by the Korea Exchange (KRX). It was established as a public market to bring gold trading into the regulated system and make it transparent, and it is described as forming real-time quotes during market hours and letting standardized gold be bought and sold, much like the stock market. The gold traded is described as quality-verified standard gold of 99.99% purity (1kg and 100g formats) supplied by domestic refiners, which investors can trade in units as small as 1g.

A key point is that you do not need to store the physical metal yourself. Gold you buy is described as being kept safely through a public custody institution, so you can hold gold as an account balance without worrying about theft or loss. You withdraw physical gold through a separate procedure only when you actually need it.

2. What Sets the Price: International Quotes, FX, and Local Premium

The domestic gold price is quoted in Korean won, but its roots are in the international market. According to the Korea Exchange, the benchmark for the domestic gold price is the international gold price quoted in US dollars per troy ounce together with the KRW-USD spot rate traded in the Seoul foreign exchange market. A troy ounce is the weight unit used for precious metals and equals roughly 31.1g, so dividing the international price by 31.1 and multiplying by the exchange rate gives an approximate won-per-gram equivalent. The actual traded price then settles slightly above or below that theoretical figure as local supply and demand come into play.

In other words, three axes move the domestic gold price. Understanding this structure is what lets you interpret a situation like "the international gold price rose, so why is my account flat?"

  • The international gold quote: Real interest rates, inflation expectations, central-bank gold purchases, and safe-haven demand all feed into it. This is not an asset whose direction can be predicted from any single indicator.
  • The KRW-USD exchange rate: If the won weakens, the won-converted domestic price rises even when the international quote is unchanged. Conversely, a stronger won can offset a rise in the international price. Buying gold domestically effectively means taking exposure to gold and the dollar at the same time.
  • Local premium or discount: When domestic buying crowds in, trades print above the theoretical converted price (a premium); when selling dominates, the reverse appears. The Korea Exchange and private quote services publish the gap against the international price alongside the local price.
  • Bid-ask spread and volume: In quiet periods the gap between bid and ask widens, so pushing an order through at market can fill at a worse price than expected.

Practically, it helps to build the habit of checking the international quote, the exchange rate, and the local traded price together before pressing buy, so you can gauge where today's price sits relative to the theoretical conversion. Right after a headline about a gold spike, the local premium tends to widen temporarily, so rushing in at market on the news alone can leave you with a worse entry. That said, no indicator tells you the future price, and a low premium does not imply that the price will subsequently rise. 3. How to Start Trading

3.1 Opening a Dedicated Gold Account

To trade on the KRX Gold Market, you first open a dedicated KRX gold account at a brokerage that is a member of the Korea Exchange gold market. A gold account is often separate from a regular equity account, so it helps to check first whether your usual brokerage supports KRX gold trading and whether the account can be opened remotely. Major brokerages are reported to support gold trading, but availability and terms can change over time, so confirm with each brokerage's latest guidance.

3.2 How Trading Actually Works

After depositing funds, you search the gold symbol in the HTS (PC) or MTS (mobile) and place buy and sell orders in much the same way as stock trading. The minimum trading unit is 1g, and quotes move in real time during market hours. Because you can start small, a dollar-cost-averaging approach - buying a set amount of gold each month - is often mentioned as feasible. That said, it remains a fluctuating investment asset whose price can fall. The trade-offs of splitting purchases over time are covered separately in our Dollar-Cost Averaging Guide.

3.3 Practical Notes on Symbols, Hours, and Settlement

The KRX Gold Market is described as listing two symbols with different specifications: a 1kg contract and a 100g contract (mini gold), both at 99.99% purity. You can trade either in 1g units, but the minimum unit for later physical withdrawal differs - 1kg and 100g respectively. So if you have any intention of eventually taking delivery, it is natural to consider the 100g symbol from the start, since its withdrawal threshold is lower.

As for hours, brokerage guidance describes a regular session flanked by off-hours sessions before and after, generally falling within a window running from around 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Settlement times are also described as differing depending on which session a trade filled in. However, the session names and exact times are presented differently by different brokerages and at different points in time, so always confirm with the latest notices from the Korea Exchange and your brokerage before placing an order. The sequence for opening an account for the first time looks like this.

  1. Confirm that the brokerage you use (or plan to use) supports KRX physical gold trading.
  2. Open a dedicated gold account through the remote app or a branch. It is often separate from a regular equity account.
  3. Before opening, check and compare the online trading fee rate, the physical withdrawal fee, and the terms on deposit interest.
  4. Fund the account, then search the gold symbols (1kg spec / 100g spec) in the HTS or MTS.
  5. For your first order, fill a small quantity with a limit order rather than a market order to learn the screens and the process.
  6. After the fill, check your balance and your average purchase price. That figure later becomes the basis for the VAT calculation on physical withdrawal, so it is worth recording.

4. The Core: KRX Gold Market Tax Benefits

The main reason the KRX Gold Market draws attention is tax. The benefits commonly cited across sources are:

  • Capital-gains tax exemption: Gains from buying and selling gold through the account are generally reported to carry no capital-gains tax.
  • No dividend income tax: The dividend income tax applied to gold banking and gold ETF gains is reported not to apply to in-account KRX physical gold trading.
  • VAT waiver: VAT is waived at the in-account buy-and-sell stage. (Physical withdrawal, covered below, is the exception.)
  • Excluded from comprehensive financial-income taxation: These gains are reported to be excluded from aggregation, which some describe as easing the burden especially for investors with high financial income.
  • The statutory basis: The VAT waiver on trades executed inside the physical gold market is explained as resting on Article 126-7 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, which sets out special taxation for gold bullion traded on the physical gold market. The article itself is always subject to amendment.
  • Which stage the benefit attaches to: The benefit attaches to the in-account trading stage only. Other stages, such as physical withdrawal and fees, can carry their own taxes, so a flat claim that KRX gold involves no tax at all is not accurate.

In short, as long as you trade only within the account, the tax burden on trading gains is described as essentially minimal. Because this rests on tax law, however, it can change with future amendments and may apply differently by individual circumstance, so do not treat the content here as absolute and always verify with current tax law and a professional.

One point is frequently missed. Press and industry guidance describe direct KRX physical gold trading as not eligible for inclusion in an ISA (Individual Savings Account) or a pension savings account. In other words, pairing physical gold with the separate tax shelters those accounts offer - the ISA's exempt and separately taxed allowance, or the tax credit on pension contributions - generally does not work. That is why guidance points to vehicles such as physical gold ETFs when you want to reach gold while also using those account-level benefits. Read the other way, KRX physical gold carries no tax at the trading-gain stage without consuming any separate account allowance, which can actually simplify planning if you would rather allocate your ISA capacity to other assets. Eligibility and limits are subject to rule changes, so confirm with current guidance before trading. The structure of the ISA itself is covered in our Complete ISA Guide.

Two things stand out. First, more than the headline rate, whether the income is aggregated into comprehensive financial-income taxation can drive the real burden. A domestically listed gold ETF or gold banking looks as though 15.4% withholding ends the matter, but once annual financial income from interest and dividends passes a threshold (commonly cited at KRW 20 million), the excess is combined with other income and recalculated at progressive rates. The more financial income you already have, the larger this gap becomes. Second, a physical bar looks favorable if you only note the absence of tax on gains, but the entry cost is heavy. Guidance describes 10% VAT at purchase plus a fabrication margin of roughly 5%, which is why several sources explain that the gold price needs to rise by something on the order of 15% just to break even. Without weighing tax and cost together, a conclusion can flip.

To restate the caveat: every rate, deduction, and condition above is subject to amendment, and exceptions exist depending on product structure. The derivatives rate in particular sits under a flexible-rate mechanism and can change over time. For the figures you will actually file with, rely on National Tax Service guidance and a tax professional.

6. Withdraw Physically, and 10% VAT Applies

Here is a trap you must know. In-account trading is VAT-exempt, but the moment you withdraw your holdings as physical gold such as a bar, a VAT of generally 10% is reported to apply to the withdrawn amount. Physical withdrawal can usually be requested only in 1kg or 100g units, and bar-fabrication and withdrawal fees (cited at tens of thousands of KRW per bar) plus shipping and insurance costs are added on top.

Accordingly, the common advice is that if your goal is trading gains, keeping the gold inside the account without physical withdrawal is more tax-efficient. Unless you actually need the physical metal itself - for a gift, an heirloom, or the like - there is little reason to pay VAT to withdraw it. Confirm the exact VAT rate, withdrawal costs, and available units with your brokerage and at the time of the transaction.

The basis on which the VAT is computed is also worth knowing. Brokerage guidance and tax interpretation materials explain that the VAT on withdrawal is assessed not on the market price at the moment of withdrawal but on the average purchase price multiplied by the withdrawn quantity. In other words, for the same quantity, gold bought at a lower price produces a lower VAT base. Read the other way, if you have not recorded your purchase price, you cannot estimate the real cost of taking delivery. This calculation method and the invoicing treatment can shift with amendments and official interpretations, so confirm directly with your brokerage before committing to a withdrawal.

Timing is another variable. Published examples indicate that, depending on when you file the request and where you collect, delivery may be available the same day or may roll to the next business day or later, with some explanations noting longer timelines for collection outside the capital region. So if you need the metal in hand by a specific date, file with room to spare, and do not assume you can sell today and receive a bar today.

7. What to Weigh on Withdrawal and Storage

Physical withdrawal is not settled by the tax question alone. The moment you withdraw, the asset changes character from an account balance into an object you are responsible for. If you are considering it, check the following in advance.

  • The unit threshold: Even if you accumulated in 1g increments, withdrawal is described as available only in 100g or 1kg units depending on the symbol. Any remainder below the unit stays in the account.
  • How costs stack: On top of VAT come bar fabrication and release fees, then shipping and insurance. The smaller the withdrawn quantity, the larger the share taken by these largely fixed costs.
  • Collection method and schedule: The delivery date can shift with your request time and collection location. Check the cut-off time for withdrawal requests in advance.
  • Custody responsibility transfers: While the gold sits in the account a public custody institution manages it, but once withdrawn, theft, loss, and damage are entirely your risk. If you use a bank safe-deposit box, fold that rental cost into your real expense.
  • Friction on resale: When selling a physical bar back, guidance describes conditions such as the certificate of authenticity being present and the weight and outward form being preserved. Damaging the packaging or losing the certificate can worsen the terms.
  • Re-depositing is not a given: Do not assume that converting withdrawn metal back into an account balance is always possible. Confirm eligibility, conditions, and cost beforehand.
  • Separate your purposes: Decide first whether the goal is a gift, an heirloom, or an emergency physical reserve, or whether it is trading gains. Mixing both purposes in one account makes it hard to judge cost against benefit.

10. Fees and Custody Costs

A tax advantage does not mean zero cost. KRX gold trading still involves a brokerage trading fee, generally cited at around 0.2-0.5% of the trade value. Online trading fees differ by brokerage and are sometimes lowered through promotions, so compare the actual fee rate at the brokerage you plan to use. In-account custody is described as handled through a public institution with little separate custody fee, but physical withdrawal adds the VAT and fabrication/shipping costs noted above.

A few points are easy to get wrong when comparing fees. First, the fee itself carries VAT. Brokerage disclosures sometimes present a total burden figure that adds the fee rate and the 10% VAT on that fee. In other words, your real cost is slightly higher than the headline rate on screen. Second, the spread between brokerages is wider than people assume. Published online rates range from the 0.2% area to the 0.4% area, so assuming they are all about the same is risky. Third, the fee is paid once on the buy and once on the sell - a round trip. The more frequently you trade in and out, the more the fees eat into what the tax treatment saved you.

When comparing against an ETF's expense ratio, remember the costs are different in nature. An ETF's total expense ratio is a holding-period cost deducted from net assets a little each day, while a physical gold trading commission is a point-of-trade cost. So the longer you hold, the more the former accumulates against you; the more often you trade, the more the latter does. The right sequence is to fix your expected holding period and trading frequency first, then compare costs. Beyond these, it is worth checking any account opening or maintenance fees, the physical withdrawal fee, and the terms on interest paid on idle cash.

11. A Pre-Purchase Checklist

The items below are not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. They are questions to answer for yourself before deciding. If any item stops you, it is safer to resolve that point first and then proceed.

11.1 Baseline checks before starting

  • Is this capital you genuinely will not need for another purpose for at least one to three years?
  • Can you state the reason you want to buy gold in one sentence, beyond "it looks like it will go up"?
  • Have you set a ceiling in advance for how much of your total assets gold may occupy?
  • If the price fell substantially below your entry, could you absorb that loss?
  • Have you confirmed that the brokerage you intend to use supports KRX physical gold trading?
  • Have you compared the online trading fee and the physical withdrawal fee at two or more firms?
  • Do you intend to withdraw physically, and if so, have you decided between the 100g and 1kg spec?
  • Do you know whether your ISA and pension account allowances are already committed to other assets?

11.2 Criteria for choosing a route

  • If the goal is trading gains, have you tested whether the untaxed in-account structure is genuinely the better fit?
  • If the goal is holding metal, have you calculated the total entry cost including VAT and the fabrication margin?
  • Is your annual financial income from interest and dividends near the comprehensive taxation threshold? If so, the dividend-taxed routes get heavier.
  • If you want the tax benefits of a pension account or ISA alongside, have you checked which product types are eligible?
  • Can you accept simultaneous exposure to gold and the dollar, meaning the exchange-rate variable?
  • At the time of purchase, did you check the international quote and the KRW-USD rate together?
  • Have you decided between buying in one lot and splitting purchases over time?
  • Have you decided whether to rule leveraged derivative routes out of consideration from the start?

11.3 Execution and ongoing management

  • Did you place your first order as a small limit order rather than at market, to verify the process?
  • Do you plan to avoid the quiet periods when the bid-ask gap tends to widen?
  • Are you recording your average purchase price? It becomes the VAT basis on physical withdrawal.
  • Have you written down your selling criteria in advance - target, loss limit, and rebalancing interval?
  • Have you set an interval at which you will check for tax-law amendments and fee changes?
  • Do you have a plan to consult a tax professional once the amounts grow?

12. Risks: Do Not Enter on Tax Alone

The tax benefit is a clear advantage, but KRX physical gold remains an investment asset where principal loss is possible. The main risks are:

  • Price risk: Gold prices rise and fall with international quotes, supply and demand, and rate and inflation expectations. Even with no tax, you lose money if the price falls below your purchase price.
  • Exchange-rate risk: The domestic gold price is influenced by both the international gold quote and the KRW-USD exchange rate. Even if the international price is unchanged, currency moves can shift the domestic price.
  • Withdrawal-related costs: Choosing physical withdrawal lowers your effective return via VAT and fees. Do not conflate a physical-holding purpose with an investment purpose.
  • Opportunity cost: Gold generates no interest or dividends. Holding a portion for diversification is the commonly discussed approach; concentrating your entire net worth in one asset is not advisable.
  • Rule-change risk: The exemptions and waivers rest on statutory provisions, so if the articles are amended or the scope of the special treatment is adjusted, the premise itself can change. Assuming that what is favorable now will stay favorable is risky.
  • Liquidity and execution risk: Trading volume is small relative to the equity market, so orders may not fill immediately at the price and size you want, and the bid-ask gap can widen. That works against you if you need to liquidate a large position quickly.
  • Limits as an inflation hedge: There is no guarantee that gold always offsets rising prices. There have been periods when gold lagged while real interest rates rose, so the simple premise that gold is unconditionally a safe asset deserves a second look.

13. How to Decide

The KRX Gold Market can be summarized as a tax-efficient route for gold investment, but the optimal method depends on your purpose. First, if your goal is trading gains, KRX physical gold - traded within the account without physical withdrawal - is cited as tax-advantaged. Second, if you actually need the physical metal (for a gift or heirloom), weigh the VAT and costs and consider physical withdrawal or buying a bar. Third, if small amounts and automation matter and opening a brokerage account is cumbersome, a gold ETF or gold banking may be an alternative, but weigh the tax difference too. Fourth, whatever the method, approach it within spare-capital limits and diversified, on the premise of price and exchange-rate risk.

14. Conclusion

The KRX Gold Market lets you trade gold in 1g units just by opening a brokerage account, and in-account trading is reported to be exempt from capital-gains, dividend, and value-added tax and excluded from comprehensive financial-income taxation - which is why it is frequently presented as an attractive route on tax grounds. Keep in mind, though, that 10% VAT applies the moment you withdraw physically, and that the absence of tax does not erase price and exchange-rate risk. The right order is to first fix your purpose (trading gains vs. physical holding), your funding situation, and the volatility you can tolerate, then choose a method.
