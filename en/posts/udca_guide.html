Ursa (UDCA): From Gallstone Dissolution to Proper Usage

What Is Ursa (UDCA)?

The active ingredient in Ursa (known as Woorusa in Korea) is UDCA (ursodeoxycholic acid), a type of bile acid originally discovered in bear bile - hence its name derived from "Ursus," the Latin word for bear. Today it is produced synthetically and is widely used as a medication for liver diseases and gallstone treatment.

History and Discovery of UDCA

  • 1902: First discovered in bear bile in Japan
  • 1954: Chemical structure fully elucidated
  • 1970s: Synthetic production method developed; approved as a pharmaceutical
  • 1987: Launched in South Korea by Daewoong Pharmaceutical under the brand name "Ursa" (Woorusa)

How UDCA Works

1. Improving Bile Acid Composition

UDCA works in the body through the following mechanisms:

Conversion to Hydrophilic Bile Acids

  • Replacing hydrophobic bile acids: Converts toxic hydrophobic bile acids to hydrophilic forms
  • Improving the bile acid pool: Shifts the overall bile acid composition to a safer profile
  • Cell membrane stabilization: Protects liver cell membranes

2. Gallstone Dissolution Mechanism

Effect on Cholesterol Gallstones

Normal bile: Balance of cholesterol + bile acids + phospholipids
Gallstone formation: Cholesterol supersaturation
UDCA action: Increases cholesterol solubility

How It Works

  1. Increased bile acid concentration: Enhances cholesterol-dissolving capacity
  2. Cholesterol secretion inhibition: Reduces cholesterol secretion from the liver
  3. Gallbladder motility promotion: Prevents bile stasis

Major Indications and Effects

1. Gallstone Dissolution Therapy

Eligible Patients

  • Cholesterol gallstones: Radiolucent stones
  • Size limitation: Stones 15mm or smaller in diameter
  • Number limitation: 3 or fewer stones
  • Normal gallbladder function: Gallbladder contractility must be preserved

Treatment Effectiveness

  • Dissolution rate: 60-80% for small cholesterol gallstones
  • Treatment duration: Typically 6 months to 2 years
  • Recurrence rate: Approximately 50% within 5 years after discontinuation

2. Liver Disease Treatment

Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC)

  • FDA-approved indication: Most well-established therapeutic effect
  • Liver function improvement: Reduces ALT, AST, and ALP levels
  • Improved survival: Significantly better long-term survival rates

Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)

  • Symptom relief: Reduces itching and fatigue
  • Reduced bile duct inflammation: Slows bile duct fibrosis progression
  • Adjunctive therapy: Not the primary treatment but provides benefit

3. Liver Protection Effects

General Liver Protection

  • Hepatocyte protection: Shields liver cells from toxic substances
  • Antioxidant effect: Scavenges reactive oxygen species
  • Anti-inflammatory effect: Reduces inflammation within the liver

Drug-Induced Liver Injury Prevention

  • During chemotherapy: Mitigates side effects of anticancer drugs
  • During anti-tuberculosis medication: Prevents hepatotoxicity
  • Alcoholic liver disease: Protects against alcohol-induced liver damage

Proper Dosage and Administration

1. Basic Administration Principles

Standard Dosage

For Adults:
- Gallstone dissolution: 10-15 mg/kg/day (divided into 2-3 doses)
- Liver disease: 13-15 mg/kg/day (divided into 2-3 doses)
- Liver protection: 300-600 mg/day (divided into 2-3 doses)

Timing

  • Take after meals: Within 30 minutes after eating for better absorption
  • Regular schedule: Maintain consistent intervals to keep stable blood levels
  • Bedtime dose: Helps prevent nighttime bile stasis

2. Condition-Specific Dosing

For Gallstone Treatment

Dose: 10-15 mg per kg of body weight
Administration: Full dose after dinner, or divided into 2 doses
Duration: Minimum 6 months; continue 3 months after complete dissolution
Monitoring: Ultrasound every 3 months

For Liver Disease Treatment

Dose: 13-15 mg per kg of body weight
Administration: 2-3 times daily after meals
Duration: Long-term use (under physician guidance)
Monitoring: Liver function tests every 1-3 months

3. Precautions

When Dose Adjustment Is Needed

  • Elderly patients: May need reduced dose if kidney function is impaired
  • Renal impairment: Adjust based on creatinine clearance
  • Severe liver failure: Use is contraindicated

Optimizing Administration

  • Do not take on an empty stomach: Increases risk of gastrointestinal issues
  • Stay well hydrated: Aim for 8-10 glasses of water daily
  • Eat regular meals: Maintains the natural rhythm of bile secretion

Side Effects and Precautions

1. Common Side Effects

Gastrointestinal Side Effects (by frequency)

  1. Diarrhea: Most common side effect (10-15%)
    • Mild cases: Usually resolves on its own within 1-2 weeks
    • Severe cases: Reduce dose or temporarily discontinue
  2. Abdominal discomfort: Bloating, gas
  3. Nausea and vomiting: Primarily at higher doses
  4. Indigestion: Fat digestion difficulties

Other Side Effects

  • Headache: Rare (1-2%)
  • Dizziness: Temporary occurrence
  • Skin rash: Allergic reaction (very rare)

2. Serious Side Effects (Emergencies)

Discontinue Immediately If:

  • Severe diarrhea: Accompanied by signs of dehydration
  • Worsening jaundice: Rapid deterioration of liver function
  • Severe abdominal pain: Possible bile duct obstruction
  • Allergic reactions: Hives, difficulty breathing

3. Contraindications

Absolute Contraindications

  • Acute cholangitis: Active infection present
  • Complete bile duct obstruction: UDCA cannot be excreted
  • Calcified gallstones: Stones visible on X-ray
  • Non-functioning gallbladder: Gallbladder does not contract

Relative Contraindications

  • Inflammatory bowel disease: Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis
  • End-stage cirrhosis: Child-Pugh class C
  • Early pregnancy: Use with caution during the first trimester

Monitoring and Evaluating Effectiveness

1. Monitoring During Gallstone Treatment

Regular Examination Schedule

First 3 months: Monthly liver function tests
After that: Abdominal ultrasound every 3 months
After 1 year: Follow-up every 6 months

Criteria for Evaluating Effectiveness

  • Complete dissolution: Gallstones completely gone on ultrasound
  • Partial dissolution: Stone size reduced by 50% or more
  • No effect: No change in size after 6 months

2. Monitoring During Liver Disease Treatment

Blood Test Parameters

  • Liver enzymes: ALT, AST, ALP, GGT
  • Bilirubin: Total bilirubin, direct bilirubin
  • Protein: Albumin, prothrombin time
  • Autoimmune markers: AMA, ANA (for PBC patients)

Indicators of Improvement

  • Liver enzyme normalization: Returns to normal range within 3-6 months
  • Itching relief: Symptom improvement within 2-4 weeks
  • Reduced fatigue: Increased energy within 1-3 months

Drug Interactions

1. Medications Requiring Caution

Medications That Impair Absorption

  • Antacids: Space doses at least 2 hours apart
  • Cholestyramine: Significantly reduces UDCA absorption
  • Calcium supplements: High doses may interfere with absorption

Medications That Reduce Effectiveness

  • Estrogen: Promotes gallstone formation
  • Clofibrate: Increases biliary cholesterol secretion
  • Cyclosporine: Interferes with bile excretion

2. Compatible Medications

Potential Synergistic Effects

  • Silymarin (milk thistle): Enhanced liver protection
  • Vitamin E: Boosted antioxidant effect
  • UDCA + SAMe: Improved liver function

Lifestyle and Dietary Recommendations

1. Diet During Gallstone Treatment

Recommended Foods

  • Low-fat diet: Keep daily fat intake below 30g
  • High-fiber foods: Vegetables, fruits, whole grains
  • Adequate hydration: 2-3 liters per day recommended
  • Regular meals: Three meals a day at consistent times

Foods to Avoid

  • High-fat foods: Fried foods, fatty meats
  • High-cholesterol foods: Egg yolks, organ meats
  • Simple sugars: Cakes, cookies, soft drinks
  • Alcohol: Increases the burden on the liver

2. Lifestyle Habits for Liver Health

Recommendations

  • Regular exercise: Aerobic exercise 3-4 times per week, 30+ minutes
  • Weight management: Maintain BMI of 18.5-24.9
  • Adequate sleep: 7-8 hours per night
  • Stress management: Meditation, yoga, and other relaxation techniques

Things to Avoid

  • Excessive drinking: Avoid drinking more than twice per week
  • Smoking: Complete cessation strongly recommended
  • Overwork: Ensure adequate rest time for your liver
  • Unvetted supplements: Consult your doctor before taking any health supplements

Use in Special Circumstances

1. Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Use During Pregnancy

  • Safety rating: FDA Category B
  • First trimester: Best avoided if possible
  • Second and third trimester: Use cautiously under physician guidance
  • Dosage: Use the minimum effective dose

Use During Breastfeeding

  • Breast milk transfer: Small amounts transfer but not at harmful levels for the infant
  • Recommendation: Take the medication immediately after breastfeeding to minimize concentration
  • Monitoring: Watch for diarrhea or digestive issues in the infant

2. Pediatric Use

Pediatric Indications

  • Primary biliary cholangitis: Effective in children as well
  • Intrahepatic cholestasis: Congenital liver conditions
  • Cystic fibrosis: Prevention of liver complications

Dose Adjustment

Pediatric dose: 15-20 mg/kg/day
Neonates: 10-15 mg/kg/day (use with caution)
Weight-based dose calculation is essential

Latest Research and Future Outlook

1. Research into New Indications

Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD)

  • Recent findings: Anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects
  • Clinical trials: Histological improvement with long-term use
  • Outlook: Potential expansion of approved indications

Neurodegenerative Diseases

  • Alzheimer's disease: Neuroprotective effects under investigation
  • Parkinson's disease: Neuroprotection through anti-inflammatory effects
  • Early stage: Still in experimental research phase

2. Formulation Development

New Formulation Research

  • Extended-release formulations: Fewer doses per day
  • Nano formulations: Improved absorption rates
  • Combination products: Paired with other liver-protective ingredients

3. Personalized Medicine

Genetic Testing-Based Treatment

  • Metabolic genes: CYP7A1 and other bile acid metabolism-related genes
  • Response prediction: Predicting individual treatment response
  • Dose optimization: Determining optimal dose based on genotype

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: How long do I need to take Ursa (UDCA)?

A: It depends on the purpose:

  • Gallstone treatment: At least 6 months to 2 years (until stones are completely dissolved)
  • Liver disease: Long-term use (potentially lifelong, as determined by your doctor)
  • Liver protection: Continue as long as risk factors are present

Q2: Can I drink alcohol while taking Ursa?

A: Strongly not recommended.

  • Alcohol puts extra strain on the liver and can counteract the effects of UDCA
  • Complete abstinence is strongly advised during liver disease treatment
  • Even during gallstone treatment, alcohol can promote stone formation

Q3: Can I take probiotics together with Ursa?

A: Yes, but space them apart.

  • Take probiotics at least 2 hours after taking Ursa
  • UDCA can alter the intestinal environment, potentially affecting probiotic survival
  • Consult your doctor for personalized advice

Q4: I'm experiencing severe diarrhea while taking Ursa. What should I do?

A: Consider adjusting the dose or temporarily discontinuing.

  • Mild diarrhea: Observe for 1-2 weeks (usually resolves on its own)
  • Moderate diarrhea: Reduce the dose by half
  • Severe diarrhea: Stop taking it and consult your doctor
  • If you show signs of dehydration, visit the hospital immediately

Conclusion

Ursa (UDCA) is a highly effective medication for gallstone dissolution and liver protection. Its effectiveness has been firmly established, particularly in dissolving cholesterol gallstones and treating primary biliary cholangitis.

However, as with all medications, following proper usage guidelines and precautions is extremely important. Since the dosage and duration of treatment can vary depending on individual circumstances, it is essential to take this medication only under the diagnosis and prescription of a qualified healthcare professional.

For those suffering from gallstones or liver disease, Ursa can certainly be a valuable treatment option. Continue taking it consistently and monitor your treatment progress through regular check-ups.
