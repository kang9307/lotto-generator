1. Two Types of Divorce in Korea

Under Korean law, divorce falls into two main categories: consensual divorce (협의이혼) and contested divorce through litigation (재판상 이혼). The path available to a couple depends on whether both parties agree and whether legal grounds for divorce exist. This guide covers the South Korean legal system, which applies to Korean nationals and may also apply to foreigners in certain circumstances.

1.1 Consensual Divorce

Consensual divorce, governed by Articles 834–836-2 of the Korean Civil Act, allows couples who mutually agree to divorce to proceed without proving fault. However, the divorce does not take effect through private agreement alone — the couple must appear together at a family court and receive official confirmation. Key matters including child custody, parental authority, child support, and property division must be agreed upon and documented before the court's confirmation.

1.2 Contested Divorce (Litigation)

Contested divorce requires one spouse to file a lawsuit citing grounds defined in Article 840 of the Korean Civil Act. These grounds include:

  • Infidelity or extramarital affair by a spouse
  • Malicious desertion by a spouse
  • Severe mistreatment by a spouse or their lineal ascendant
  • Severe mistreatment of the petitioner's lineal ascendant by the other spouse
  • Three or more years of unknown whereabouts of a spouse
  • Other significant reasons making it impossible to continue the marriage (e.g., severe irreconcilable differences, domestic violence, persistent financial misconduct)

Under the Family Litigation Act, parties must generally attempt court mediation (조정) before a full trial proceeds. If mediation fails or is deemed unsuitable, the case moves to formal litigation. Alimony, property division, child custody, and child support can all be decided in the same proceedings.

2.1 Required Documents

  • Consensual Divorce Confirmation Application Form
  • Certificate of Family Relations (issued within the past 3 months)
  • Certificate of Marriage
  • Resident Registration Certificate
  • Child Support Obligation Document (required if minor children exist)
  • Government-issued identification for both spouses

2.2 Cooling-Off Period Waiver

In cases involving domestic violence or imminent danger, a spouse may request the court to shorten or waive the cooling-off period. Approval is at the court's discretion based on evidence presented.

4. Property Division

Under Articles 839-2 and 843 of the Korean Civil Act, property division (재산분할) applies to assets jointly accumulated during the marriage. It is a separate claim from alimony and must be filed within 2 years of the divorce date.

4.1 What Is Subject to Division?

  • Generally included: Real estate, bank accounts, retirement funds, pension entitlements, stocks, and investment accounts acquired during the marriage
  • Generally excluded: Pre-marital assets, property inherited or gifted to one spouse individually (separate property)
  • Note: Separate property may become partially divisible if the other spouse contributed to its maintenance or increase in value

4.2 How Contribution Is Assessed

Korean courts recognize a broad definition of contribution. In addition to direct financial income, homemaking, childcare, emotional support, and career sacrifices made for the family are all recognized as forms of marital contribution. Courts have consistently affirmed that stay-at-home spouses are entitled to substantial shares of marital property.

4.3 Debts During the Marriage

Debts incurred jointly for family purposes (mortgage, household expenses) may be deducted from the divisible estate. Debts incurred unilaterally for personal purposes generally cannot be assigned to the other spouse.

5. Alimony

Alimony (위자료) is compensation for emotional harm caused by the at-fault spouse. It is grounded in Articles 806 and 843 of the Korean Civil Act and is distinct from property division — both can be claimed simultaneously.

5.1 Statute of Limitations

The right to claim alimony expires 3 years after the divorce is finalized or after the injured party becomes aware of the wrongdoing, whichever applies. Failure to claim within this period forfeits the right.

5.2 Factors Affecting Alimony Amount

  • Length of the marriage
  • Nature and severity of the misconduct (infidelity, domestic violence, abandonment)
  • Presence of children
  • Financial situation of both parties
  • Post-divorce living circumstances of both parties

There is no fixed alimony schedule in Korean law. Courts determine amounts on a case-by-case basis, weighing all relevant factors.

6. Child Custody, Parental Authority, and Child Support

When minor children are involved, divorce proceedings must address three related matters: parental authority (친권), physical custody (양육권), and child support (양육비).

6.1 Parental Authority and Custody

In consensual divorce, the spouses agree on who holds parental authority and physical custody. In contested divorce, the court decides based on the best interests of the child, considering:

  • Age and expressed preferences of the child (if old enough)
  • Parenting capacity and financial stability of each parent
  • Continuity of the child's existing care arrangements
  • Whether siblings would be separated

Parental authority and physical custody can be held by different parents. The non-custodial parent retains the right to regular visitation and communication with the child (면접교섭권).

6.2 Child Support

Child support is established by agreement or by court order. Korean family courts apply the Supreme Court's Child Support Guidelines, which set base amounts by parental combined income and child age. The non-custodial parent typically pays a monthly amount to the custodial parent until the child reaches adulthood at 19. Either party may request a modification if circumstances change materially.

6.3 Enforcement of Child Support

If the obligated parent fails to pay child support, the custodial parent can apply for enforcement orders, wage garnishment, suspension of the obligor's driver's license, and passport restrictions. The Child Support Enforcement Agency (양육비이행관리원, tel. 1644-6621) also provides enforcement support services.

7. Legal Aid and Resources

Legal fees can be substantial for contested divorce cases. Those with limited income may qualify for free or reduced-cost legal representation through the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (대한법률구조공단). Services include legal consultation, document preparation, and court representation.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What if my spouse refuses to appear at court for consensual divorce?

Consensual divorce requires both spouses to appear together at the family court. If one spouse refuses, the consensual process cannot proceed. In that case, the other party may need to file for contested divorce through litigation.

Q2. Can a foreigner divorce a Korean national in Korean courts?

Yes, in many cases. Korean courts generally have jurisdiction if either spouse is a Korean national or if both spouses are residing in Korea. The specific rules vary by nationality and circumstances; professional legal advice is strongly recommended for international divorce cases.

Q3. Does a short marriage affect property division?

The length of the marriage is a factor in assessing how much joint property was accumulated and each party's contribution. Short marriages typically result in smaller divisible estates, but the length of the marriage alone does not automatically exclude a party from property division rights.

Q4. Can child custody be changed after divorce?

Yes. If circumstances change significantly — such as a parent's living situation, health, or the child's preferences — either parent may apply to the family court to modify the custody arrangement in the child's best interests.

Q5. How can I get financial support during divorce proceedings?

Even while divorce proceedings are ongoing, spouses have a legal obligation to support each other. If the other party refuses financial support, you may apply for a provisional order or injunction from the family court to secure temporary alimony or child support during the proceedings.
