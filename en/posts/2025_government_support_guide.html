Korean Government Support Programs: What Closed and What's Still Open (August 2026)

Programmes closed to new applicants, programmes whose amounts changed, and figures that could not be confirmed

2025.07.15 · updated 2026.08.11 Finance & Economy ⏱️ 12 min read

🏛️First: what changed in this article

On 11 August 2026 the current status of every programme in this article was re-checked against the official guidance of the agency that runs it, and the article was corrected. Two programmes that the July 2025 version described as open for applications — the Youth Leap Account and the Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction — have closed to new applicants. Programmes whose amounts rose, such as the Basic Pension, now show current figures. Amounts that could not be confirmed against a primary source were removed and collected under "What could not be confirmed" below. Every programme here is run by the South Korean central or local government.

🚫 1. Programmes closed to new applicants

⚠️ What the earlier version of this article got wrong

The programmes below can no longer be applied for as a new entrant.

  • Youth Leap Account — new signups ended with the December 2025 intake
  • Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction — closed under the 2024 programme sunset
  • Youth Future Savings — the first application window closed on 7 August 2026

📌 Youth Leap Account — new signups closed

  • Status: Closed to new applicants. The December 2025 intake was the last one, and the "apply by 31 December 2025" line in the earlier version of this article referred to that final round.
  • Existing holders: Accounts continue as they are, and the government contribution and the tax exemption on interest are described as running through to maturity.
  • Successor: Youth Future Savings, launched by the Financial Services Commission in 2026, is effectively the replacement product.
  • Switching over: This was allowed only during the first Youth Future Savings application window, and only in one order: open the new account first, then close the Youth Leap Account through a special early termination. Anyone who held a Youth Leap Account to its full five-year maturity is not eligible for Youth Future Savings.
  • Read more: Korea's Youth Leap Account - New Signups Closed: Holding, Early Exit and the Successor

📌 Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction — no new enrolment

  • Status: The subscription site of the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency states that enrolment is currently unavailable because the programme sunset in 2024.
  • Earlier wording: This article presented "up to 18,000,000 KRW accumulated over three years" as something you could still sign up for. That is not the case.
  • Existing members: Contributions and maturity proceed as agreed. How early termination is handled differs by enrolment cohort, so check your own contract on the subscription site.
  • Successor route: The same site points members towards re-enrolling in or extending the Ministry of SMEs and Startups' Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction (3 to 5 years) after maturity.

👨‍🎓 2. Programmes for young adults

🌟 Youth Future Savings (successor to the Youth Leap Account)

Ages 19–34 · income and household conditions apply

First application window 22 Jun – 7 Aug 2026 (closed)

📌 What is confirmed about Youth Future Savings

  • Who qualifies: People aged 19 to 34 who meet the income and household conditions are described as eligible. The first intake covered those born between 1 January 1991 and 7 August 2007.
  • Income check: Applicants are described as needing verifiable income for the previous year, 2025.
  • Household condition: Assessed against household members on the resident registration record — spouse, parents, children and minor siblings.
  • First window: 22 June to 7 August 2026. On 11 August 2026 the Financial Services Commission reported 1.385 million final signups from that window.
  • Can you apply now: The first application window has already closed. Whether there will be a further intake has to be checked in notices from the Financial Services Commission and the Korea Inclusive Finance Agency.
  • Monthly cap, government match rate, contract term: These could not be pinned down from a primary source, so no figures are given here. Check the product guidance from the Korea Inclusive Finance Agency.

🎯 Local youth programmes (2026 basis)

  • Seoul Youth Allowance: 500,000 KRW a month for up to six months. In 2026 there were two intakes, in March (around 20,000 places) and May (around 6,000 places), and both have closed.
  • Gyeonggi Youth Basic Income: 250,000 KRW per quarter, up to 1,000,000 KRW a year, paid in local currency vouchers to 24-year-olds. The first-quarter 2026 application ran from 3 March to 1 April.
  • Gyeonggi residency condition: Described as holding resident registration in the province and having lived there continuously for at least three years, or for ten years in total.
  • Caution: Local programmes depend on each municipality's ordinance and annual budget, so whether a given city or county runs one can differ. Check the announcement for where you live.

👷‍♂️ 3. Programmes for employees

🏖️ Worker Vacation Support Programme (running in 2026)

Employee pays 200,000 KRW → 400,000 KRW of credit per person

2026 applications 30 Jan – 30 Dec · first come, first served

📌 The 2026 Worker Vacation Support Programme

  • Status: Still running in 2026. The earlier version's "150,000 places in 2025" and "valid through 31 December 2025" referred to the previous round.
  • Who qualifies: Described as employees of mid-sized firms, small and medium enterprises, small business owners, non-profit civic groups, and social welfare corporations and facilities.
  • Cost split: 100,000 KRW from the government, 100,000 KRW from the employer and 200,000 KRW from the employee, giving 400,000 KRW of credit per person.
  • Different split: For mid-sized firms and for SMEs in their fifth or later year of cumulative participation, the split is 50,000 KRW government, 150,000 KRW employer and 200,000 KRW employee.
  • Places: Around 145,000 in total — roughly 135,000 for SMEs and small business owners, and up to 10,000 for mid-sized firms.
  • When and how to apply: From 14:00 on 30 January 2026 to 23:55 on 30 December 2026, first come first served. Applications are made by the employer through the programme website, not by individual employees.

💼 Related income and employment support

  • National Employment Support Programme: Korea's National Employment Support Program 2026 - Who Qualifies and How Much Is Paid sets out the conditions and payments by category.
  • Earned Income Tax Credit: See Korea Earned Income Tax Credit - Who Qualifies and How Much 2026.
  • Energy costs: See Korea Energy Voucher 2026 - Who Qualifies and How Much You Get.
  • What was removed: The support rates and limits for living stability loans, children's tuition aid, health checkups and vocational skills development could not be confirmed from a primary source, so the figures were deleted. See section 7 below.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 4. Family and housing programmes

👶 Childcare support (2026 basis)

  • Parental Benefit: 1,000,000 KRW a month at age 0 (months 0–11) and 500,000 KRW a month at age 1 (months 12–23). The "700,000 KRW a month" in the earlier version was the pre-increase figure.
  • Child Allowance: From 2026 the age range widened from under 8 to under 9, at 100,000 KRW a month.
  • Regional top-up: Children outside the capital region and in population-decline areas are described as receiving an extra 5,000 to 30,000 KRW a month.
  • First Meeting Voucher: For children born on or after 1 January 2024, 2,000,000 KRW for a first child and 3,000,000 KRW for a second or later child. The voucher is described as usable for two years from the date of birth.
  • How to apply: Described as in person at a community service centre, or online through the Bokjiro and Government24 portals.

🏠 Housing support (2026 basis)

  • Housing Benefit threshold: For 2026 it is 48% of the standard median income, notified as 3,117,474 KRW a month for a four-person household.
  • Correction: This article said "bottom 47% income bracket". The 47% figure was the 2025 basis. The government has stated a direction of raising it in stages towards 50%.
  • Reference rent: The 2026 reference rent rose by between 17,000 and 39,000 KRW against 2025, depending on rent zone and household size.
  • The "160,000 KRW a month average": That figure gave no year and no basis, so it was removed. The actual payment varies by rent zone, household size and the rent you actually pay, so it is more accurate to look up your own case in the reference rent table.
  • Jeonse loans: The limits and rates for newlywed and multi-child jeonse loans could not be confirmed and were removed. See section 7 below.

👴 5. Programmes for older adults

💰 Basic Pension (2026 basis)

  • Standard pension amount: 349,700 KRW a month. It rose by 7,190 KRW from 342,510 KRW in 2025, reflecting the previous year's 2.1% consumer price change, and applies from January 2026.
  • Income threshold: 2,470,000 KRW a month for a single-person household and 3,952,000 KRW for a couple household — up by 190,000 KRW and 304,000 KRW respectively from 2025.
  • How eligibility works: People aged 65 and over whose household recognised income is at or below the threshold are described as eligible. Recognised income converts both income and assets, so it cannot be judged from a salary or pension figure alone, and the actual decision is made by the National Pension Service and your local district office.
  • Newly eligible: Those born in 1961, who turn 65 in 2026, can apply for the first time.
  • Couple reduction: A 20% reduction applies when both spouses receive the pension. A direction has been set out to ease this in stages for low-income households from 2027.

🧑‍🌾 Senior employment programme (2026 basis)

  • Scale: 1,152,000 places in 2026, up from 1,098,000 in 2025.
  • Public interest activity track: Open to people aged 65 and over who receive the Basic Pension. Described as at least 30 hours a month within a three-hour daily cap, averaging 11 months, with an activity allowance of 290,000 KRW a month.
  • Correction: The "up to 710,000 KRW a month" in the earlier version did not say which track it referred to, so it was removed. Hours and allowances differ by track, so identify the track first.
  • Excluded from selection: Recipients of the livelihood benefit, workplace-based health insurance subscribers, people graded 1 to 5 under long-term care insurance, and people already in two or more government or local job programmes are described as excluded.
  • Dementia centres and health checkups: These are delivered as services rather than cash, so no amounts are given. Check with your district health centre and the National Health Insurance Service.

🏢 6. Local government programmes

🗺️ Local programmes that were confirmed

  • Seoul Youth Allowance: Described as 500,000 KRW a month for up to six months for unemployed Seoul residents aged 19 to 34, or those working 30 hours a week or less.
  • Gyeonggi Youth Basic Income: 250,000 KRW per quarter, up to 1,000,000 KRW a year, paid in local currency vouchers to 24-year-olds.
  • Local programmes not confirmed: The Busan Youth Digital Dividend and the Daegu youth startup grant named in the earlier version could not be confirmed for status or amount, so the figures were removed.
  • Timing: Local programmes usually announce early in the year and run in separate rounds. Once a round closes you have to wait for the next announcement.

🎯 The order to check local programmes in

Start with this year's original announcement on your city or provincial youth portal or your district website, then call the community service centre. Amounts quoted in blogs and summary articles are often from a previous round. That is exactly what happened to the earlier version of this article.

❓ 7. What could not be confirmed

⚠️ No amounts or deadlines are given for the items below

The earlier version of this article listed amounts and deadlines for the items below. As of 11 August 2026 they could not be confirmed in the responsible agency's own guidance, so the figures were deleted. Leaving them out is better than guessing. Check whichever ones you need at the source given.

📌 Unconfirmed items and where to check

  • Youth monthly rent special support: Sources disagree on whether the time-limited programme has ended or been converted into a standing one. Check the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, Bokjiro, and your local announcement.
  • K-Digital Training: The scope of tuition coverage and the training stipend could not be confirmed. Check Employment24.
  • Startup grants (general, tech, small business, youth): The earlier figures of 300 million, 2 billion, 70 million and 100 million KRW could not be confirmed. Check the K-Startup announcements from the Korea Institute of Startup and Entrepreneurship Development and from the Small Enterprise and Market Service.
  • Worker living stability loans and children's tuition aid: Rates, limits and coverage ratios could not be confirmed. Check the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service.
  • Vocational skills development and worker health checkups: Coverage ratios could not be confirmed. Check Employment24 and the National Health Insurance Service.
  • Home childcare allowance: The 2026 amount could not be confirmed. Check Bokjiro.
  • Newlywed and multi-child jeonse loans: Limits and rates move with market interest rates. Check the Housing and Urban Fund guidance.
  • Busan and Daegu youth programmes: Check the announcements on those cities' youth portals.

📝 8. Points to watch when applying

⚠️ Do not decide your own eligibility or amount

  • The agency decides: Conditions in this article mean "people who meet this are described as eligible". The final decision rests with the responsible agency, based on recognised income and the documents you submit.
  • Improper claims are clawed back: Money obtained through inaccurate declarations or documents is recovered, and depending on the programme further sanctions and legal liability follow.
  • Overlap rules differ by programme: Combining benefits is neither always blocked nor always allowed, so read the overlap clause in the specific announcement.
  • Look for the year: Amounts and thresholds change annually. If a guide does not state which year it is based on, treat the figure with suspicion.
  • There are two kinds of deadline: A closing date is not the same as running out of budget or places. First come, first served programmes can close while time still remains.

📋 Where to check

  • Government24: The Subsidy24 service lets you look up in one place what you may be entitled to.
  • Bokjiro: Sets out eligibility, amounts and application methods for welfare benefits and services, programme by programme.
  • Employment24: Covers employment, training and incentive programmes.
  • The responsible agency's own site: Where money is involved, read the original announcement for that year rather than a summary article.
  • Your local district office: Local programmes are not all listed on the national portals.

💡 Documents to prepare

  • Photo ID: Resident registration card, driver's licence or passport
  • Proof of income: Withholding receipt for earned income, business registration certificate, or whatever the programme asks for
  • Bank account copy: The account the payment will go to
  • Certificate of employment: Sometimes required for employee-targeted programmes
  • Resident registration abstract and family relation certificate: Used where household members must be verified

🌟 Summary

Three things came out of this check. First, the Youth Leap Account and the Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction are closed to new applicants. Knowing that a programme has ended is more useful than knowing how to apply to one that has. Second, the Basic Pension, Parental Benefit, Child Allowance and Housing Benefit are still running, but their amounts and thresholds have changed, and they now appear here on a 2026 basis. Third, figures that could not be confirmed were deleted. The most dangerous thing in an article about government money is an old number that still looks current. Whatever guidance you read, check which year it is based on first.
