Introduction: Why a hub comparison now

The first half of 2026 left the AI model market more crowded than anyone expected a year ago. Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8, OpenAI's GPT-5.5, Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro and xAI's Grok 4.3 fight for the top of the closed flagship tier, while beneath them Chinese open-weight models like DeepSeek V4, GLM 5.2, Kimi K2.7-Code and MiniMax-M3 attack from the flank on price and openness. With more choices, the question "so which one should I use?" has only gotten harder.

This article is the hub guide to that question. It puts each model's benchmarks, context, pricing and strengths in one table, then resolves them into use-case picks: this for coding, that for reasoning, another for real-time. It leans on public benchmarks and prices rather than marketing copy, and where something is uncertain it says "as of" explicitly. Deep dives on coding-tool comparisons, image-generator comparisons, ChatGPT vs Claude, and API pricing live in separate posts; this one draws the whole map.

2. Claude Opus 4.8 - the coding and long-agent leader

Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 sits at #1 overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at around 61 points as of July 2026. Its standout territory is coding and autonomous agents. With SWE-bench Verified 88.6% and the harder SWE-bench Pro 69.2%, it ranks near the top of standard coding benchmarks, and it is especially praised for staying coherent across long, multi-step agent loops without losing the thread.

The sibling lineup is clear too: Opus 4.8 for the hardest work, balanced Sonnet 4.6, fast and cheap Haiku 4.5, and creative-leaning Fable 5. Standard API pricing runs roughly $5 input / $25 output per million tokens, considered reasonable for its coding tier. For tasks where accuracy and persistence matter most, like code migrations, refactors and agent backends, it is the first candidate. For a deeper coding-tool breakdown, see the coding AI tools comparison.

3. GPT-5.5 - agentic workflows and multimodality

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 holds the very top tier at around 60 on the Intelligence Index, just behind Claude Opus 4.8. Its strengths are agentic workflows and broad multimodal handling. It does particularly well on terminal- and tool-call-heavy tasks, and the breadth of the OpenAI ecosystem (plugins, tools, integrations) is a real adoption advantage.

Reported pricing is roughly $5-10 input / $30-40 output per million tokens, with variation across sources, so confirm the latest official price before adopting. If you value general-purpose breadth and ecosystem, and you are already tied into OpenAI tooling, GPT-5.5 is the natural pick. A head-to-head ChatGPT vs Claude piece covers that matchup in more detail.

Update (July 2026): On July 9, 2026, OpenAI shipped GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna as the successor to GPT-5.5, a three-tier lineup. All three share roughly the same 1.05M-token context and 128K-token output limit with a February 16, 2026 knowledge cutoff, and only the top tier, Sol, unlocks max reasoning effort and ultra mode. Reported per-million-token pricing is $5/$30 for Sol, $2.50/$15 for Terra, and $1/$6 for Luna. Notably, Terra reportedly matches GPT-5.5-level performance at about half the price, which could shift the default pick for general-purpose/ecosystem work from GPT-5.5 toward Terra. The Responses API also gained Programmatic Tool Calling, letting the model write code to orchestrate tool calls directly. That said, OpenAI's own benchmark claims (such as Sol reportedly beating Claude Fable 5 on Agents' Last Exam) remain unverified by independent parties. See the dedicated GPT-5.6 launch analysis for more.

4. Gemini 3.1 Pro - reasoning, multimodality and long context

Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro leads on pure reasoning. It posts GPQA Diamond 94.3% and ARC-AGI-2 77.1%, top of the hard reasoning benchmarks, and remains competitive in coding with SWE-bench Verified 80.6%. Add its 1M-token context and you can feed hundreds of pages of documents or a large codebase whole, without chopping it up.

Its other differentiator is native multimodality: text, image, audio, video and code in one model, plus native integration with Google Workspace (docs, mail, sheets) so it slots straight into work. API pricing is roughly $2 input / $12 output per million tokens, rising to about $4 / $18 for prompts over 200K tokens. For hard reasoning, long documents and multimodal input, Gemini 3.1 Pro is the strongest pick.

Update (July 21, 2026): Google added a small, fast, low-latency tier beneath Gemini 3.1 Pro. Gemini 3.6 Flash prices at $1.50 input / $7.50 output per million tokens, down from a prior $9.00 output price, with a March 2026 knowledge cutoff; its coding and agentic benchmarks improved (DeepSWE 49%, OSWorld-Verified 83.0%), making it well suited to latency-sensitive agent workloads. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite prices at $0.30 input / $2.50 output, generates roughly 350 output tokens per second, and offers adjustable thinking effort (minimal/low/high). A security-focused pilot, Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber, targets vulnerability detection and patching (tied to CodeMender) and is available only to government and trusted partners in limited release. Gemini 3.5 Pro, the next flagship tier, remains in partner testing and has not shipped yet.

5. Grok 4.3 - real-time data and social

The decisive differentiator for xAI's Grok 4.3 is real-time access. It taps live X (Twitter) data directly, making it strong for research and social content that reflects events, sentiment and trends as they happen. While other models are pinned to a training cutoff, Grok can answer "what is happening right now."

Pricing is aggressive too: roughly $1.25 input / $2.50 output per million tokens with a 1M context, so large-scale real-time research stays affordable. That said, Claude and Gemini lead on absolute coding and reasoning quality, so there is no need to route general work to Grok unless real-time data or social flow is central. Split by use case.

6. Chinese open-weights - the flank attack on price and openness

The loudest place below the closed flagships is the Chinese open-weight camp. DeepSeek V4 ships a 1M context by default while breaking price to roughly $0.14-0.44 input / $0.28-0.87 output per million tokens. GLM 5.2 (Zhipu) is an open-weight model focused on a 1M context and autonomous coding agents; Kimi K2.7-Code (Moonshot) leans on token efficiency; and MiniMax-M3 claims price and performance at once.

The real value of open weights is not only unit price. You can take the weights and self-host on your own infrastructure, so code and prompts never leave for an external API. For privacy- and regulation-sensitive teams, and for those who want to control the break-even on high-volume workloads, that is decisive. Absolute top quality still favors closed flagships, but the gap has closed faster than most predicted. The API pricing post and per-model deep dives cover the price dynamics in more depth.

7. Use-case picks - what to use when

The conclusion of any comparison is not "pick one" but "deploy by use case." Here are the per-workload picks.

  • Coding & autonomous agents: Claude Opus 4.8. Long-loop reliability and top-tier SWE-bench are the differentiators. To save cost, DeepSeek V4 / GLM 5.2 are alternatives.
  • Hard reasoning & research: Gemini 3.1 Pro. Leads GPQA / ARC-AGI-2 with a 1M context for long sources whole.
  • Real-time, social, trends: Grok 4.3. Direct live X data, aggressive pricing.
  • Multimodal (image, audio, video): Gemini 3.1 Pro or GPT-5.5. Multiple modalities in one model.
  • Low-latency, lightweight, cost-efficient: Gemini 3.6 Flash / 3.5 Flash-Lite (added July 2026, roughly $1.50-$0.30 input / $7.50-$2.50 output). Comparable small-model options include GPT-5.6 Luna (~$1/$6) and Claude Haiku 4.5 (~$1/$5).
  • General-purpose & ecosystem: GPT-5.5 (or the July 2026 GPT-5.6 Terra if cost matters, ~2x cheaper than GPT-5.5). Broad tools/plugins and strong agentic workflows.
  • Cost-optimal, privacy, self-hosting: Chinese open-weights (DeepSeek V4 / GLM 5.2 / Kimi / MiniMax). Low token price, weights in your hands.
  • Long documents & large codebases: 1M-context models (Gemini 3.1 Pro / Grok 4.3 / DeepSeek V4 / GLM 5.2).

The realistic operating mode is a dual/multi stack. Design behind a provider-agnostic gateway and switching the backend per task type becomes a config change, not a rewrite. Image generation, a different family of models, is covered separately in the image-generator comparison.

8. Pitfalls when comparing

Benchmark numbers are useful but have traps. First, a benchmark is not your workload. A high SWE-bench score does not guarantee it solves your codebase's nasty edge cases. Second, list price is not effective price. Caching, batching, long-context surcharges and reasoning-effort settings change the real bill a lot. Third, versions move fast. New releases and price cuts will keep coming after this article's date.

So the recommended process is simple: build a golden set of 50-200 of your real tasks, run the candidate models under identical conditions, and measure quality, token spend, latency and cost yourself. Your data, not vendor marketing, is the most trustworthy evidence.

9. Conclusion - the July 2026 map

The July 2026 AI landscape is an era of "axis champions," not a single strongest. Coding and agents go to Claude Opus 4.8, reasoning, multimodality and context to Gemini 3.1 Pro, general-purpose and ecosystem to GPT-5.5 and the new GPT-5.6, real-time to Grok 4.3, and price and openness to the Chinese open-weights. The answer is not "use only one" but "deploy the best-fit model per workload."
