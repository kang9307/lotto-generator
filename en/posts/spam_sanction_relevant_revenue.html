Korea Moves Spam Sanctions From Senders to Conduits - Article 50-9 and the Revenue Base

Effective 1 October 2026 in the Republic of Korea - what the statute settles and what it leaves to the Decree

2026-09-11 Law & Legal

When a regulatory change becomes news, usually one number survives the retelling. This time it was six percent. Yet in a penalty surcharge regime a percentage is only half the information. The other half sits in what that percentage is multiplied by. The same six percent produces figures that differ by orders of magnitude depending on whether the base is a company entire turnover or a narrow slice of it.

And the weight of this Korean amendment is not really in the money. It is in the fact that the sanction has widened from the party that sends to the pipe the sending travelled through. Liability attached to a conduit has a different shape. What is asked is not the outcome that an advertisement went out, but the procedure that followed once it did.

What follows lays the provisions out first, then works through how the base is set, what the conduit test actually requires, and where the sending side and the receiving side each stand. Everything here is the law of the Republic of Korea; a different jurisdiction sets different requirements.

This summary draws on the text of the Korean Act on Promotion of Information and Communications Network Utilization and Information Protection and on government press material, and is provided for general information. It is not legal advice, and whether particular conduct meets a particular requirement is settled by investigation from the competent authority and by the courts. The provisions cited are as they read on the 1 October 2026 enforcement date, and where the statute hands a question to the Decree that is noted as such rather than filled in.

The trigger - what is statute and what is still Decree

This change is not a single block. The statute is already fixed; the Presidential Decree and the implementing public notice follow behind it. If those three layers are not kept apart, it becomes very easy to read something that has not been decided yet as though it had been.

The statutory history is recorded in government press material. The amendment bill to the Act on Promotion of Information and Communications Network Utilization and Information Protection cleared the Cabinet meeting on 24 March 2026, and was described as imposing a surcharge of up to six percent of related revenue on senders of illegal spam and on operators that were lax in their duty to prevent illegal spam, and as providing for the confiscation and collection of unjust gains from those who transmit advertising information for unlawful activity. The same material recorded that the bill was scheduled to take effect six months after promulgation, and that the specific ceiling and the calculation standards were delegated to the Presidential Decree.

The Decree side is later. The briefing on the 34th plenary meeting of the Korea Communications and Media Commission described the agenda item as an amendment to the Enforcement Decree, stating that the Decree would prescribe the standards for measures by information and communications service providers and the calculation standards for the surcharge by type of violation, and that the related public notice would prescribe the detailed standards and procedures for imposing the surcharge together with the grounds for aggravation and mitigation.

The name of the regulator is worth pinning down as well. The body named in the provision is the Korea Communications and Media Commission, the successor to the older commission name, and using the former name no longer matches the statutory text.

LayerWhat it settlesPresent state
Statute - the Network ActThe legal basis for the surcharge, the ceiling percentage, the basic structure of calculation, the mandatory factors, the arrears surcharge and collectionArticle 50-9 newly inserted and Article 50-4, paragraph 4 amended, both dated 31 March 2026 and returned for an enforcement date of 1 October 2026
Presidential DecreeThe revenue that forms the basis of the calculation, calculation standards by type of violation, and the standards for measures by service providersThe briefing records an amendment handled as a commission agenda item. The final wording is not reproduced here

One thing follows from that table. A large share of the rules that actually determine an amount still sit outside the statute. The six percent ceiling is statutory, but the thing it is multiplied by and the width of aggravation and mitigation are decided a layer down.

Start with the text - the nine paragraphs of Article 50-9

The new provision is Article 50-9, headed imposition of penalty surcharges for violations relating to the transmission of advertising information. It runs to nine paragraphs, and each one carries a distinct job.

Paragraph 1 reads: the Korea Communications and Media Commission may impose on a person who has violated Article 50, Article 50-4, Article 50-5, Article 50-7 or Article 50-8 a penalty surcharge not exceeding the amount obtained by multiplying revenue prescribed by Presidential Decree by six one-hundredths.

Two things already show through. First, the surcharge does not hang off one provision but off five. Second, the statute did not fix the base itself; it handed it to the Decree.

ParagraphWhat it settlesWhere practice turns on it
Paragraph 1The imposing body, the five predicate provisions, the six one-hundredths ceiling, and the delegation of the baseThe list of predicate provisions is the scope. A violation of a provision outside the list is not a basis for this surcharge
Paragraph 2Calculation on total revenue less revenue unrelated to the violation, with a proviso for refusal to submit data or submission of false dataThe direction of the subtraction and the degree of cooperation both move the number
Paragraph 3Where there is no revenue or revenue is difficult to calculate, in cases prescribed by Presidential Decree, a surcharge of up to two billion wonThe fallback when the percentage mechanism cannot operate
Paragraph 4Seven matters that must be taken into account when imposingThis is where what a business kept on record becomes visible
Paragraph 5Specific calculation standards and procedures to be prescribed by Presidential DecreeThe rules that actually produce a figure live a layer down
Paragraph 6An arrears surcharge of six one-hundredths per annum on non-payment, capped at sixty months of collectionShares a number with paragraph 1 but is a different thing entirely
Paragraph 7After a demand, unpaid amounts are collected in the same manner as delinquent national taxesThe regime does not rely on voluntary payment
Paragraph 8Where a refund arises from a court judgment, an additional refund amount is paidThe outcome of a dispute is settled in money
Paragraph 9Where a disposition is revoked and a new surcharge imposed, the additional refund is calculated only on the differenceRe-imposition is anticipated by the text

That paragraph 7 borrows the national tax collection mechanism, and that paragraphs 8 and 9 contemplate revocation and re-imposition in advance, shows that this regime was drafted on the assumption of litigation. A disposition is not the end of the road.

The base for six percent - the statute never writes 「related revenue」

This is the heart of the provision. The phrase that travelled through the press material and the coverage is related revenue. That phrase does not appear in the statutory text. Paragraph 1 says only revenue prescribed by Presidential Decree, and paragraph 2 is the provision that narrows it.

Paragraph 2 reads: where the Korea Communications and Media Commission imposes a penalty surcharge under paragraph 1, it shall calculate the surcharge on the basis of total revenue less revenue unrelated to the violation.

The two formulations sound interchangeable. They point in opposite directions.

  • Related revenue implies a base built by gathering the sales that can be tied to the violation. The starting point is zero, and only what is shown to be connected gets added.
  • Total revenue less unrelated revenue puts the starting point at the whole turnover. What leaves the base is the share shown to be unconnected.
  • Where the starting point sits changes who has to explain what. In a subtractive structure the explanation that has to be produced is that a given stream of revenue has nothing to do with the violation.
  • The proviso to paragraph 2 conditions that channel expressly. Where a provider, without justifiable grounds, refuses to submit data for calculating revenue or submits false data, the calculation is made on the basis of that provider total revenue.
  • The same proviso adds that revenue may be estimated on the basis of accounting materials such as financial statements and business status materials such as subscriber numbers and usage charges, for that provider and for providers of comparable size. Absent data does not stop the calculation.
SituationBaseProvision
The ordinary caseTotal revenue less revenue unrelated to the violationArticle 50-9, paragraph 2, main text
Refusal to submit data without justifiable grounds, or submission of false dataTotal revenueArticle 50-9, paragraph 2, proviso
The same situation where the data itself is thinAn estimate drawn from accounting materials, subscriber numbers and usage chargesArticle 50-9, paragraph 2, latter part of the proviso
No revenue, or revenue difficult to calculate, in cases prescribed by Presidential DecreeNot a percentage but a fixed ceiling of two billion wonArticle 50-9, paragraph 3
Specific calculation standards and proceduresNot in the statute. Delegated to the Presidential DecreeArticle 50-9, paragraph 5

So the only thing calculable today is the shape of the ceiling. Which period of revenue is looked at, which service unit the base is cut along, and how multiple violations are grouped are all matters for the Decree. Producing an estimated figure from the statute alone would have nothing standing behind it.

The definition of the base is delegated by paragraph 5 to the Presidential Decree, and the final wording of that Decree is not reproduced in this article. That is why no estimated surcharge figure appears in any section below. Calculation in an individual case is settled by the judgment of the competent authority and by review in the courts.

When there is no revenue - the two billion won alternative

Percentage-based sanctions always carry the same gap: where turnover cannot be traced there is nothing to multiply. Paragraph 3 fills that space. Notwithstanding paragraph 2, where there is no revenue or revenue is difficult to calculate, in cases prescribed by Presidential Decree, a surcharge of up to two billion won may be imposed.

That single line does more than it looks.

  • It means the surcharge is designed on two axes, one proportional and one fixed. Which axis applies is decided first, and the amount comes afterwards.
  • The circumstances in which the fixed axis applies are themselves prescribed by Decree. The scope of revenue being difficult to calculate is not settled in the statute either.
  • A small turnover does not translate into a small figure. Two billion won is a ceiling, not a starting amount, and the actual figure passes through the seven factors in paragraph 4 and the calculation standards in the Decree.
  • Nor does a large turnover automatically put a party on the proportional axis. The order in the text is fixed: paragraph 2 first, paragraph 3 as the exception.

「Neglect」 is not in the text - Article 50-4, paragraph 4 is the test

The phrase that stands out in the press material is operators that were lax in their duty to prevent illegal spam, and once summarised it usually becomes something like neglectful operators. Yet the word neglect does not appear anywhere in the statutory text. The conduit-side test lives in Article 50-4, and specifically in its paragraph 4.

Article 50-4 is headed restrictions on the provision of information transmission services. Paragraph 1 provides that an information and communications service provider may refuse to provide the relevant service where the transmission or receipt of advertising information causes or threatens to cause an obstruction to the provision of the service, and where a user does not wish to receive advertising information. Paragraph 2 requires the refusal to be written into the service contract with the user. Paragraph 3 requires interested parties to be told that a refusal has been made, or told without delay afterwards where advance notice is impracticable.

Up to that point the register is may. Paragraph 4 changes it.

An information and communications service provider shall, where the service it provides to users under a service contract is being used for the transmission of commercial advertising information in violation of Article 50 or Article 50-8, take the following measures in accordance with the types and standards prescribed by Presidential Decree.

Unpacked into elements, that sentence has three layers.

  1. The service must be one provided under a service contract. The contractual relationship is the doorway to the requirement.
  2. That service must be being used for commercial advertising transmission in violation of Article 50 or Article 50-8. The text is in the present continuous.
  3. In that case the listed measures must be taken, in the types and by the standards the Decree prescribes. This is the part that is a duty to perform.

And Article 50-4 sits inside the list in Article 50-9, paragraph 1. Failure to take the paragraph 4 measures is therefore a basis for the surcharge. The question asked is not did you send but did you act.

SubparagraphMeasureCharacter
1Immediate suspension of the transmission of advertising informationAn immediacy measure that cuts a transmission already under way
2Refusal of service or termination of the service contractA measure that clears the contractual relationship itself
3Inspection and improvement of security system vulnerabilities related to intrusion incidentsA technical measure so the same route is not reused
4Improvement of the terms of use and the service contractA documentary measure that writes the condition into the next contract
5Establishment and inspection of a plan to prevent recurrence of the violationA measure that demands two stages, a plan and an inspection of it

Set side by side the five subparagraphs divide cleanly. The first and second cut off what is happening now; the third through fifth are about the same thing not happening again. The statute says only that the measures shall be taken, and leaves the types and standards, once again, to the Decree.

Procedural liability - what has to survive for you to say the measures were taken

To say the test is procedure rather than outcome is to say, turned around, that the procedure has to survive somewhere. The bare fact that an advertisement went out does not complete the requirement, and what was done afterwards, if it is not written down anywhere, stays a claim.

The table below sets out only what can be read directly from the subparagraphs of Article 50-4, paragraph 4. The types and standards that the Decree will prescribe are not reflected in it.

Measure in the textRecords that would naturally surviveQuestion the record answers
Immediate suspension - subparagraph 1Time of becoming aware, time of execution, identifier of what was stopped, who executed itHow long was it between learning of it and cutting it
Refusal or termination - subparagraph 2The decision document, the contractual clause relied on, the record of notice to interested partiesWas there a contractual basis and was notice given
Vulnerability inspection - subparagraph 3Scope and date of inspection, findings, confirmation that remediation closedHas the same route been shut
Terms and contract improvement - subparagraph 4Before and after comparison of wording, date of revision, which contracts it reachesDid the documents actually change
Recurrence plan and inspection - subparagraph 5The plan document, the inspection interval, inspection findings and follow-upDid the plan turn into an inspection
Awareness channels generallyIntake history of the recipient reporting channel, records of external notifications, internal detection alert logsWhen and how did you come to know

The last row of that table is really the first. Until the moment of awareness is fixed, none of the other timestamps mean anything. Without a channel that takes reports there is no recorded moment of awareness, and without that moment there is no way to say what the immediate in immediate suspension is immediate to.

One more record follows directly from the text. Paragraph 2 of Article 50-9 speaks of data for calculating revenue. That refusing to submit it or submitting false data returns the base to total revenue means that how cleanly accounting streams are partitioned in the ordinary course has a direct effect on the figure.

Who is covered - four layers

Rolled up into the word business, the requirements disappear. Spread the five provisions listed in Article 50-9, paragraph 1 into layers and it becomes visible what catches each one.

LayerWhat it doesProvision that mainly bitesCharacter of the requirement
The senderTransmits commercial advertising information through an electronic transmission mediumThe paragraphs of Article 50Conduct requirements - consent, time of day, disclosure, prohibited acts
The advertiser of prohibited subject matterTransmits advertising information about goods or services the law forbidsArticle 50-8A requirement attaching to the subject matter of the transmission itself
The provider of the transmission serviceProvides a service under a contract, and that service is used for transmissionArticle 50-4, especially paragraph 4A procedural requirement about whether the measures were taken
Programs and posting spacesThe subjects of Articles 50-5 and 50-7, also listed in Article 50-9, paragraph 1Articles 50-5 and 50-7The text of those two provisions is not reproduced in this article

The list itself carries one more piece of information. The provisions named in Article 50-9, paragraph 1 are Articles 50, 50-4, 50-5, 50-7 and 50-8, five in all. Article 50-3, which deals with entrusting the transmission of commercial advertising information, is not among them. In a provision drafted as a closed list, absence from the list is itself a fact. Whether a party that carried out sending under an entrustment arrangement can be assessed as a violator of Article 50 in its own right is a question for the individual case, so what can be stated flatly here stops at the contents of the list.

Duties on the sending side - the eight paragraphs of Article 50

The duties of the sending side are not scattered. They are organised into eight paragraphs inside Article 50, and each paragraph governs a different moment.

ParagraphContentWhat to watch in the wording
Paragraph 1Anyone intending to transmit commercial advertising information through an electronic transmission medium shall obtain the explicit prior consent of the recipientThe qualifier explicit. Two exceptions are attached as subparagraphs
Paragraph 1, subparagraph 1A person who collected the contact details directly from the recipient through a transactional relationship in goods, transmitting within the period prescribed by Presidential Decree, about goods of the same kind as those it handled and transacted with the recipientCollection route, period, the handling party and sameness of the goods must all hold at once
Paragraph 1, subparagraph 2A telemarketer under the Act on Door-to-Door Sales who states the source of the personal information by voice and then solicits by telephoneBy voice and disclosure of the collection source are joined together
Paragraph 2Where the recipient has expressed refusal or withdrawn prior consent, transmission is prohibitedThis paragraph prevails even where consent under paragraph 1 exists
Paragraph 3A person intending to transmit between 9 p.m. and 8 a.m. the following day shall obtain separate prior consentGeneral consent and night-time consent are distinct. Media prescribed by Decree are excepted
Paragraph 4The sender name and contact details, and matters concerning measures and methods enabling easy refusal or withdrawal of consent, shall be stated specifically as prescribed by Presidential DecreeThe detailed form of the disclosure lives in the Decree
Paragraph 5Five prohibited acts - evading or obstructing refusal or withdrawal, automatically generating contact details, automatically registering them for transmission purposes, concealing identity or the source of the advertisement, and deceiving recipients into respondingThese five are prohibited separately even where consent was obtained
Paragraph 6Measures shall be taken so that the recipient does not bear monetary costs such as telephone charges arising from refusal or withdrawalThe cost of refusing sits with the sending side
Paragraph 7The result of processing a consent, a refusal or a withdrawal shall be notified to the recipientThe fact of processing must itself be communicated
Paragraph 8A person who obtained consent shall periodically confirm whether the recipient consent still stands, as prescribed by Presidential DecreeConsent obtained once does not last indefinitely

The exception in paragraph 1, subparagraph 1 rewards a second reading. Four conditions bind simultaneously: the contact details were collected directly through a transactional relationship, the transmission falls within the period prescribed by Decree, the goods are ones the sender itself handled, and they are of the same kind as what the recipient transacted. Miss one and the exception is gone and the general rule returns.

The five prohibitions in paragraph 5 have a different character again. That paragraph operates regardless of consent. Even an advertisement sent under valid consent runs into subparagraph 1 of paragraph 5 if the refusal path was made hard to use.

Consent is a record, not a claim

Read Article 50 as a procedure and one axis runs through the whole of it: the history of consent and refusal. Paragraph 1 wants explicit prior consent, paragraph 3 wants separate consent for the night-time window, paragraph 7 wants notice of how the request was processed, and paragraph 8 wants periodic re-confirmation. All four are the kind of thing that cannot be verified unless it was kept.

  • The time of consent. When it was obtained is the starting point for judging the period prescribed by Decree in paragraph 1, subparagraph 1.
  • The scope of consent. What kind of goods the consent covered connects to the sameness requirement in the same subparagraph.
  • The route of consent. Which screen and which wording it was obtained through is what the qualifier explicit attaches to.
  • Whether night-time consent was separate. Paragraph 3 demands a consent distinct from the general one, so if the two are collapsed into a single checkbox the distinction disappears.
  • The time a refusal or withdrawal was received. That is the reference point for when the prohibition in paragraph 2 starts to bite.
  • The notification history. What paragraph 7 requires is not the processing itself but the fact that the result was communicated.
  • The interval and results of periodic confirmation. Paragraph 8 leaves the interval to the Decree, so what has to survive is not the interval but the fact that a confirmation was run and recorded.
  • The cost structure of refusing. Paragraph 6 demands measures so the recipient bears no monetary cost, which ties the channel offered for refusal directly to this paragraph.

One property runs through the whole list. None of it can be reconstructed afterwards. The fact that consent was obtained has to be written at the moment of obtaining, and the fact that a refusal was processed at the moment of processing. Which makes this less a matter for the legal function than a matter of data design.

How the amount is fixed - seven factors and two different six percents

A ceiling is not an amount. Paragraph 4 lists seven matters that must be taken into account on imposition. Read from the other side, that list doubles as an inventory of what a business would want to have kept.

SubparagraphFactorCharacter of the matching record
1The number of violations relating to the transmission of advertising informationHow counts are aggregated and how periods are cut
2Whether the information and communications service provider acted intentionally or negligentlyThe moment of awareness and the decisions taken after it
3The scale of the violating transmissionVolume, breadth of recipients, duration
4Whether measures to remedy harm and to prevent its spread were carried outTime of suspension, notification history, confirmation that follow-up closed
5The business form and scale of the providerMaterials on the structure of the service and the size of the business
6The effect of the violation on users and the scale of user harmThe basis on which the affected scope was estimated
7Efforts to prevent violations and to respond quicklyPreventive controls in the design, a reporting intake channel, elapsed response time

Subparagraphs 4 and 7 are the notable pair. Neither asks whether there was a violation; both ask what was done around it. The efforts to prevent and respond quickly in subparagraph 7 are accumulated while nothing is happening, and are not the sort of thing that can be manufactured after an incident.

And the fact that six percent appears twice inside the provision is an easy place to get tangled.

  • The six one-hundredths in paragraph 1 is the ceiling percentage for the surcharge. It is multiplied by revenue.
  • The six one-hundredths per annum in paragraph 6 is the interest rate on the arrears surcharge that attaches when the surcharge is not paid by the deadline. It attaches to the unpaid amount, not to the surcharge as such.
  • Paragraph 6 also caps the arrears mechanism: the period for collecting the arrears surcharge may not exceed sixty months.
  • Paragraph 7 provides that where payment is still not made after a demand, collection follows the example of the collection of delinquent national taxes.
  • Paragraphs 8 and 9 set the additional refund amount where a judgment produces a refund, and confine that calculation to the difference where a disposition is revoked and a new surcharge imposed.

The receiving side - the reporting channel, the evidence, and the reality of process

Everything above concerns the sending side and the conduit. The receiving side has a different axis. Blocking happens on the handset and at the carrier, and how to block spam calls and texts is set out in a separate article rather than repeated here. What this section covers is reporting.

The official channel in Korea is the Korea Internet and Security Agency spam response centre. The web channel is spam.kisa.or.kr, and telephone consultation is on 118 without an area code. The centre guidance asks a report to carry the sending number, the date and time of receipt, the substance of the report, and a screen capture.

Evidence itemWhy it mattersHow it disappears
Sending numberThe starting point for identifying the transmitting partyIt goes with the message if the received list is cleared

Mail headersFor electronic mail, they carry the transmission pathNot saved when only the body is kept

And one point about the reality of process. A report is not a disposition. A report is a lead for an investigation, and the surcharge is fixed by the Korea Communications and Media Commission after passing through the requirements in Article 50-9 and the factors in its paragraph 4. Whether an individual who reports is notified of the outcome, and if so how far that notification extends, is not something the provisions themselves answer.

What the receiving side actually gains from this amendment lies elsewhere. A duty to act attached to the conduit means there is now one more place a report can land. Because Article 50-4, paragraph 4 requires the service provider to act where its service is being used for a violating transmission, telling that provider is itself an act that creates a moment of awareness.

The last row is the point of this section. An effective date arriving does not make every rule visible at once. The skeleton set by the statute is as the table shows, and the rules that actually generate an amount live in the Decree and the public notice.

How the change applies to conduct before the effective date, and whether a separate grace period exists, are settled not in the body of the provisions but in the supplementary provisions and in notices from the competent authority. Those points are not covered here, so they are gathered with their reasons in the section below.
