Standards Are Written as Requirements, Not Features - Korea's Supervised FSD Finding and the Shape of the FTA Exception

What one line about the turn signal decides, where self-certification moves the moment of judgement, and how far the exception actually reaches

2026-09-05 Tech & IT

When a feature crosses a border its name stays the same but its treatment changes. In one place it simply switches on. In another it acquires a sentence saying it does not match the regulations. The technology does not mutate at the frontier. What changes is the text that was written down to receive it.

Regulations are not drafted by looking at what a feature is called. They record, in seconds and in numbers, who operates what and when, which signal must be emitted and for how long, and what is cut off automatically when a condition breaks. That is why a feature bearing one name passes under one set of provisions and catches on another. What follows walks those sentences provision by provision.

This article is general information and does not replace legal advice on any particular matter. The provisions quoted here are the texts currently published in Korea's national legal information service together with the treaty texts they point to, and whether any particular vehicle or any particular software complies is a determination for the competent administrative and judicial authorities. It is not a piece that ranks products or pronounces on their safety.

The trigger - non-compliance and an exception in the same answer

In early September 2026 the discussion opened when reporting surfaced the content of an answer given by Korea's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport through the public petition channel. It came down to two statements. First, that supervised FSD and FSD V14 Lite, as activated on ordinary roads, do not conform to a particular provision of the domestic motor vehicle safety standards. Second, that vehicles of United States origin benefit from mutual recognition of safety standards under a free trade agreement.

Read quickly, the two statements look like they collide. If something does not meet the standard, the reasoning goes, how can it be used at all. Yet the two statements speak to different layers. The first concerns a requirement inside a rulebook. The second concerns which country's standards are used to judge compliance with that rulebook. Different layers, so both can hold at once.

How the feature rollout was sequenced, and what the label supervised demands of the person behind the wheel, was already covered in Tesla FSD v14 Lite arrives in Korea. What follows sits one storey above that: how the norms are assembled, and through which opening the exception enters.

Where the finding sits - from statute down to annex item 10

The address named in the reported answer is item 10 of Annex 6-2 to the Rules on Performance and Standards for Motor Vehicles and Motor Vehicle Parts. Establishing what that address hangs from settles most of the rest. Motor vehicle safety standards in Korea do not end at one line of statute. They descend four steps.

Standards are written as requirements, not features

Item 10 of Annex 6-2 is titled, in translation, criteria for vehicles fitted with advanced driver assistance steering systems. There is no product name in it and no marketing vocabulary. Instead it splits functions that move the steering automatically into three branches and attaches requirements to each: the automatically commanded steering function, the corrective steering function, and the emergency steering function.

That drafting choice matters because it makes the requirement, rather than the product, the unit of assessment. Whether a given piece of software conforms is not settled by its name or its advertising copy. It is settled by whether the behaviour the function produces satisfies each item written into the text. Broadly the text deals with four kinds of item.

  • Activation conditions — what must be present before the function may begin. Whether a deliberate act by the driver is needed, and which operation counts as that act
  • Warnings and indications — which state must be signalled and how. Which of visual, audible and haptic signals must be emitted, at what moment, and within how many seconds
  • Intervention and priority — what happens first when the driver touches the controls, and on what threshold the system stands down
  • Monitoring and automatic deactivation — how the vehicle detects that the driver is holding the steering control, and what is cut off after how many seconds if the driver is not

Nowhere among the four is there a slot for grading autonomy with an adjective. It is all a matter of who operates what, at what time, and with which signal. That is exactly why treatment diverges from country to country. Each jurisdiction has nailed these four items down with different numbers in a different order, and a function is lawful in a given jurisdiction only where it was designed against that jurisdiction's numbers and order.

Categories A, B and C - three boxes for steering assistance

The automatically commanded steering function splits again into three categories. The definitions at the end of the annex record them as follows. Category A assists at the driver's request during low speed or parking manoeuvres. Category B influences the lateral movement of the vehicle so as to keep it within the selected lane. Category C, where the driver activates and commands it, assists in carrying out a single lateral manoeuvre such as a lane change.

CategoryWhat it doesSpeed and road conditionsLocation in the text

Category BKeeps the vehicle inside the selected laneBetween the minimum and maximum speeds specified by the manufacturer. No separate restriction on road type is prescribedItem 10, sub-item (a)(2)
Category CA single lateral manoeuvre such as a lane changeRoads where pedestrians and cyclists are prohibited, where opposing traffic is physically separated, and which have two or more lanes in the direction of travel. All three must holdItem 10, sub-item (a)(3)

Changing lanes on the vehicle's own initiative belongs to Category C, and Category C carries two layers of constraint. One is the road condition and the other is the procedure. On the road condition side the text requires the system to verify by at least two separate means that the road satisfies the conditions, and requires the function to deactivate automatically when the vehicle moves onto a road that does not. An ordinary road where pedestrians may walk is not, to begin with, an operating environment for Category C.

Run down the table and it becomes visible what this text is measuring. Not how well the vehicle drives, but the order in which control passes back and forth between driver and system. The direction indicator has been placed as the starting point of that order. Under this text the indicator is therefore not merely a lamp. It is evidence that the driver expressed an intention, and the signal that opens the procedure.

The word supervised and the SAE levels

The familiar yardstick for talking about driving automation is SAE J3016. Korean regulation does not adopt that scale verbatim. Article 111 of the vehicle standards rules divides driving automation systems into three kinds: partial, conditional full, and full. The names differ, but there is a correspondence, and more importantly the dividing criterion is the same: whether the driver's intervention is required.

On the manufacturer's own labelling, a function carrying the qualifier supervised is a driver assistance function premised on continuous supervision. The maker itself communicates that it is not autonomous driving, and the ministry's motor vehicle policy division has been reported as saying the feature is classified domestically at Level 2. The ministry's own division of work follows the same line: automation of Level 3 and above sits with the automated driving policy division, and driver assistance at Level 2 and below with the motor vehicle policy division.

The label itself carries no normative force. A level designation does not decide which provision applies. What decides the applicable provision is what the function actually does. Where there is a manoeuvre that changes lanes on the vehicle's own initiative, the Category C requirements of Annex 6-2 item 10 attach. Where the character becomes one of operating the vehicle in defined conditions without driver intervention, the Article 111 family of automation standards attaches instead. How often level analogies are misused as the language of judgement was taken up separately in AI agent autonomy levels.

Assistance and automation are governed by different provisions

In the Korean rules the two live in different parts of the book altogether. The assistance family sits inside the device safety standards of Chapter 3, while the automation family sits in a separate section titled safety standards for driving automation systems. The density of what is demanded differs as well.

The right-hand column is far heavier. Once the premise is accepted that a system takes over driving for a stretch, the obligations that follow from that premise arrive all at once. The left-hand column, by contrast, stands on the premise that the driver is still driving. Its requirements are therefore filled with devices for confirming the driver's presence and handing priority back. That is why the gap between the two columns cannot be crossed by naming alone.

Self-certification - who decides compliance, and when

Once the requirements are established, the next question is procedural. Who determines conformity, and at what moment. This is where regimes diverge sharply. Under type approval, an administrative authority examines and approves the type before sale. Under self-certification, the manufacturer certifies conformity itself, puts the vehicle on the market, and the authority verifies afterwards.

Korea uses the latter. Article 30 paragraph 1 of the Motor Vehicle Management Act requires anyone manufacturing, assembling or importing a motor vehicle to certify for themselves that the vehicle type conforms to the motor vehicle safety standards. Paragraph 2 requires registration of manufacturing, testing and inspection facilities, and paragraph 3 requires those who do not meet the self-certification capability requirements to obtain technical review and safety inspection from the designated performance testing agent. Three consequences follow from that structure.

  • The moment of judgement moves later — with no pre-sale approval gate, conformity is contested after the product reaches the market. With software that changes by over the air update, that interval widens further still
  • The manufacturer is the first decider — primary responsibility for reading the provision rests with the manufacturer, and the administrative view arrives afterwards
  • Post-market tools carry the weight — the defect remedy regime of Article 31 and the defect investigation carried out by the performance testing agent take over the role that pre-market scrutiny would otherwise play

The passage in the reported answer stating that the ministry does not pre-verify the software in question is a consequence of that design. The department is not postponing a judgement. The statute arranged the order of judgement that way.

How a trade agreement reaches into safety standards

Now the exception. The route by which a trade agreement enters domestic safety standards is not vague. It is a single identified provision. Article 114 of the vehicle standards rules collects the special rules on applying the standards, and paragraph 13 is the opening. It provides that where a free trade agreement concerning safety standards has been concluded and put into effect, then notwithstanding these rules, safety standards recognised under that agreement shall be treated as conforming to these rules, and that the minister may issue a notice setting out the details.

That notice is the one titled Motor Vehicle Safety Standards Recognised through the Conclusion of Free Trade Agreements. Article 1 identifies its basis as Article 114 paragraph 13. Article 2 lists the covered vehicles by origin. Article 3 designates, for each origin, the instrument that applies. For the European Union and the United Kingdom it points to provisions of the respective protocol annexes, and for Canada to Article 6.7 paragraph 2 of Chapter 6. For vehicles of United States origin it designates the Protocol between the Government of the Republic of Korea and the Government of the United States of America amending the February 10, 2011 Exchange of Letters.

In other words the notice contains no requirements of its own. The notice is a pointer that names which document to open, and the substantive requirements live in the treaty text. To know the scope of the exception one has to open that treaty.

The scope and conditions of the exception - not a blanket waiver

The instrument concerned is a protocol signed in New York on 24 September 2018 and in force from 1 January 2019, which replaced the whole of Section B of the 2011 letters with new text. Its paragraph 1 reads as follows.

Section B: Safety Standards

1. In lieu of paragraphs 2(a) and 2(b) of the self-certification provisions of the
   letters the Parties exchanged on June 30, 2007 regarding Chapter Nine of the KORUS,
   Korea shall provide that an originating motor vehicle produced by a manufacturer
   that sold no more than 50,000 originating motor vehicles in the territory of Korea
   during the previous calendar year shall be deemed to comply with Korean Motor
   Vehicle Safety Standards (KMVSS) if the manufacturer certifies that the motor
   vehicle complies with U.S. Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS).

The sentence is long but the structure is simple. Where a manufacturer meets the condition and certifies compliance with the United States federal standards, that certification is deemed to be compliance with the Korean standards. It does not say the Korean standards are set aside. It says satisfaction of the Korean standards may be evidenced by satisfaction of another country's. And the same section leaves a number of places where that substitution does not reach.

Read the six rows together and the shape of the exception appears. It is a device that changes the reference point for the ex ante conformity judgement. It is not a door out of the safety regime as a whole. Paragraph 5 in particular addresses technology that is not yet regulated. Lane change assistance is already written into Annex 6-2 item 10, which places it in a different situation from the unregulated space.

What the number 50,000 actually counts

This figure changes meaning as it gets summarised. The phrasing one usually meets is that up to 50,000 units a year may be imported. The treaty text is not built that way. What the text counts is the manufacturer's sales in Korea during the previous year.

Precisely, the subject is an originating motor vehicle produced by a manufacturer that sold no more than 50,000 originating motor vehicles in the territory of Korea during the previous calendar year. This is not a volume quota that switches off once a ceiling is reached. It is an eligibility test under which, for as long as the manufacturer sits below the threshold, the deeming effect attaches to that manufacturer's originating vehicles. The difference is plain from the grammar: the subject of the conditional clause is the manufacturer, not the vehicle.

The number also has a history. The original 2011 text said 25,000, and the 2018 protocol raised it to 50,000 when it replaced the whole section. Then the joint fact sheet released on 14 November 2025 recorded that Korea would abolish that 50,000 ceiling. However, no subsequent instrument further amending the safety standards section appears in the treaty listing of the national legal information service, and the ministerial notice still stands in its 2020 version. An announced agreement and its reflection in domestic norms are separate stages, and the two should not be read as the same moment.

The point where origin divides

The treaty text applies to originating motor vehicles, defined as vehicles qualifying as an originating good of the United States for the purposes of the agreement. Origin is not settled by the location of the assembly plant alone; it is determined under the rules of origin of the agreement. What is clear, however, is that the deeming effect of this provision does not extend to a vehicle that is not a United States originating good. Where the same company builds the same model in another country and ships it in, the governing provision is a different one.

The reported answer put the emphasis on whether the manufacturer had obtained official certification and approval rather than on origin as such, which reads consistently with the same structure. The starting point of the exception is what the manufacturer has certified, and against which standard, while origin is one of the tests that decides whether that certification will be deemed to satisfy the domestic standards. A situation in which uncertified software becomes active is not directly covered by either test.

The post-market stage sits outside the exception

The clarifying language in paragraph 1 of the treaty states that nothing prevents Korea from applying the provisions of its Motor Vehicle Management Act on post-market verification. That single line draws the boundary of the exception. The ex ante conformity judgement can be substituted, but the procedure for identifying defects afterwards and requiring their remedy stays where it is.

On the domestic side the basis is Article 31 of the Motor Vehicle Management Act. Where a manufactured vehicle or part does not conform to the safety standards, or has a defect that impairs safety through a design, manufacturing or performance problem, the manufacturer must disclose that fact without delay from the day it becomes known and carry out remedial measures. Paragraph 3 requires the minister to order remediation where a manufacturer fails to disclose or remedy. Paragraph 4 allows the minister to have the performance testing agent conduct an investigation into whether a defect exists. Paragraph 6 goes further and provides that where accidents caused by a device operating differently from the driver's intention recur in vehicles of the same kind, and other prescribed conditions are met, a defect is presumed if the manufacturer fails to submit the requested materials.

The part of the reported answer stating that defects will be met with investigation and recall irrespective of origin belongs to these provisions. It does not conflict with the exception; the two stand side by side.

What is actually left with the driver

Come all the way down through the layers and what remains at the bottom is the driver's own obligations. This part does not change according to which feature is switched on, or whose standards were used to judge its conformity. The basis here is not the safety standards but the Road Traffic Act.

  • Duty to signal — Article 38 paragraph 1 requires the driver of every vehicle changing course while proceeding in the same direction to signal by hand, direction indicator or lamp until the manoeuvre is complete
  • Duty of safe driving — Article 48 paragraph 1 requires accurate operation of the steering and braking systems and prohibits driving at a speed or in a manner that endangers others, having regard to traffic conditions and to the structure and performance of the vehicle
  • Duty to respond to a takeover request — Article 56-2 paragraph 1 requires the driver of a vehicle equipped with a driving automation system that is not a full driving automation system to respond without delay to the system's demand for direct driving and to operate the controls directly
  • Definition of driving — Article 2 subparagraph 26 provides that driving includes the use of a driving automation system. Leaving a system engaged does not put a person outside the state of driving

To that, one practically important fact should be added. When something goes wrong, what feeds the assessment is not the name of the feature but the record that remains. The warning signals, hands-on detection and automatic deactivation timings that Annex 6-2 item 10 requires are themselves an operating history of the system. Which warning was issued at which moment, and whether the steering control was being held, are handled as records quite independently of any marketing designation. That is one reason regulatory texts nail down signals and timings so tightly.

Reading the headline again

Back to the two statements. Having followed the provisions, the conditions that ought to travel with each of them become visible.

The reach of the word non-compliant is not the whole feature. What the reported answer named is Annex 6-2 item 10, and within it the requirement for opening the lane change procedure. Expanding that into a verdict on steering assistance generally, or on lane keeping as such, makes the claim broader than the original sentence.

The reach of the word exception is likewise bounded. The treaty text gates it on two tests, the manufacturer's previous year sales and origin, carves out commercial vehicles and replacement parts, and preserves post-market verification and temporary emergency measures. Summarising it as a blanket waiver deletes the conditional clause entirely.

The identity of the number is different too. The figure of 50,000 is not a permitted import volume but a previous-year sales threshold that sorts manufacturers, and whether the abolition agreed in the 2025 fact sheet has been reflected in domestic norms is a separate matter to establish.

In the end what this episode illustrates is not a story about one company or one ministry. Standards do not measure features; they measure requirements. That is why the same software crossing a border changes not in performance but in conformity. When and by whom that conformity is judged is set by the self-certification regime, and by whose standards it is judged is set by the trade agreement. Three layers overlap, so a conclusion drawn from one layer alone is usually wrong. Before debating how fast regulation follows technology, the thing to establish is which provision the feature is currently standing under. The method of reading institutional texts provision by provision was laid out in the same order in preparing for Korea's AI Framework Act.

The summary above reproduces publicly available statutes, notices and treaty texts, and is not a determination about any particular vehicle or any particular software. Conformity assessments and defect findings in individual cases follow from investigation by the competent administrative authority and from judicial determination, and because provisions are amended, the text in force at the time of application should govern. Nothing here offers guidance on operating a vehicle.

What could not be confirmed

  • The original text of the ministry's answer — the content was reportedly delivered through the public petition channel, and no press release or notice carrying the same content was located through public channels. Every passage above that quotes the answer rests on reporting dated 1 September 2026 and is therefore a secondary citation; the exact wording of the finding and its precise scope were not checked against an original
  • The implementation status of the abolition of the 50,000 ceiling — that the fact sheet of 14 November 2025 records the intention to abolish it is established, but no amending treaty instrument or amended notice reflecting that appears in the national legal information service. Whether the agreement text has already been amended with the database lagging, or whether implementation is still pending, could not be separated
  • The practice of applying the threshold — which statistics are used, at what point in time, and by whom, to determine the previous-year figure of 50,000, and on which side of the threshold any given manufacturer currently sits, were not established. The reported answer contained no reference to unit numbers either
  • The item list in the commercial vehicle annex — the list of domestic safety standard items in the attached annex referred to by paragraph 3 of Section B did not open together with the treaty text, so the individual items were not checked
  • Comparison with other jurisdictions — how European regulations or United States federal standards frame lane change assistance was outside the scope of the original-text comparison undertaken here. That the domestic provisions were built with reference to international standards is evident from their structure, but the correspondence was not fixed clause by clause
