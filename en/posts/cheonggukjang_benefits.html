10 Amazing Health Benefits of Cheonggukjang

A traditional Korean fermented soybean paste packed with nutrition

Jun 2, 2025 Health

Cheonggukjang is one of Korea's most iconic fermented foods - a traditional fermented soybean paste rich in nutrients and associated with a wide range of health benefits. Recent scientific research has drawn attention to its diverse properties, earning it a reputation as a superfood. This article takes a closer look at the main health benefits of cheonggukjang.

What Is Cheonggukjang?

Cheonggukjang is a traditional Korean fermented food made by fermenting cooked soybeans wrapped in rice straw. It undergoes a short fermentation period of 2-3 days, which gives it a distinctive strong aroma and a characteristic sticky, stringy texture. The bacterium Bacillus subtilis plays a central role in fermentation, and this process makes cheonggukjang even more nutrient-dense than the raw soybeans it's made from.

History and Cultural Background

Cheonggukjang has a history spanning thousands of years and has long served as an important source of protein in Korea, especially during times when protein was scarce. Because it can be made relatively quickly - even in cold weather - it became a prized winter staple. It occupies an important place in Korean cuisine and carries significant cultural meaning.

10 Major Health Benefits of Cheonggukjang

1. Better Digestion

The probiotics and enzymes in cheonggukjang promote digestion and improve the gut environment. The Bacillus bacteria produced during fermentation encourage the growth of beneficial gut flora, while the digestive enzymes help break down food and absorb nutrients.

Studies suggest that regular consumption of cheonggukjang may help with indigestion, constipation, and diarrhea.

2. Blood Pressure Regulation

The isoflavones and peptides in cheonggukjang support healthy blood pressure. In particular, they have ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitory activity, which can help lower blood pressure.

One study reported that patients with hypertension who consumed cheonggukjang consistently for 8 weeks saw their systolic blood pressure drop by an average of 5-10 mmHg.

3. Improved Cholesterol

The saponins and isoflavones produced during fermentation help lower blood cholesterol. They tend to reduce "bad" LDL cholesterol while raising "good" HDL cholesterol.

Clinical research shows that groups consuming cheonggukjang regularly saw total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol drop by about 8% and 12%, respectively.

4. Stronger Immunity

The probiotics, vitamins, and minerals in cheonggukjang help strengthen the immune system. Bacillus bacteria in particular enhance immune cell activity and promote antibody production.

Experimental studies show that cheonggukjang extracts increase the activity of natural killer (NK) cells and macrophages, boosting immune response.

5. Antioxidant Activity

Cheonggukjang is rich in isoflavones, phenolic compounds, and vitamin E, all of which have strong antioxidant activity. These compounds protect cells from free-radical damage and help slow aging.

Research suggests that the antioxidant activity of cheonggukjang is about 2-3 times higher than that of plain soybeans.

6. Better Bone Health

The vitamin K2 and isoflavones in cheonggukjang are especially important for bone health. Vitamin K2 helps ensure calcium is effectively deposited into bones, while isoflavones can help protect against osteoporosis.

In studies of postmenopausal women, those who regularly consumed fermented soy products including cheonggukjang experienced a slower decline in bone density than control groups.

7. Weight Management and Better Metabolism

As a high-protein, relatively low-calorie food, cheonggukjang fits well into weight-loss diets. The beneficial bacteria and enzymes produced during fermentation also promote fat metabolism and improve insulin sensitivity, supporting weight management.

Experimental studies have shown that cheonggukjang extracts inhibit the differentiation of fat cells and promote fat breakdown.

8. Anti-Cancer Potential

The isoflavones (such as genistein and daidzein) and saponins in cheonggukjang are reported to have anti-cancer properties. They may help with prevention of breast, prostate, and colorectal cancers in particular.

Laboratory studies show that cheonggukjang extracts can inhibit cancer cell growth and induce apoptosis (programmed cell death).

9. Better Skin Health

The antioxidants, protein, and vitamin E in cheonggukjang support skin health. These components promote skin regeneration, protect against UV damage, and help collagen production.

Clinical studies suggest that regular intake of fermented soy products is associated with improved skin elasticity and reduced wrinkles.

10. Anti-Inflammatory Effects

The polyamines and peptides formed during fermentation have anti-inflammatory effects. They can help reduce chronic inflammation and may ease symptoms of inflammatory conditions such as arthritis.

Experimental studies have shown that cheonggukjang extracts downregulate inflammation-related genes and suppress the production of inflammatory cytokines.

Things to Keep in Mind

Despite its many health benefits, there are a few things to be mindful of when eating cheonggukjang:

  1. Allergic reactions: people with soy allergies should avoid cheonggukjang.
  2. Sodium content: cheonggukjang stew and other dishes often include salt or soy sauce, so watch your overall sodium intake.
  3. Purine content: cheonggukjang is relatively high in purines, so people with gout may need to limit their intake.
  4. Drug interactions: the vitamin K in cheonggukjang can affect anticoagulants like warfarin, so consult your doctor if you take these medications.

How to Include Cheonggukjang in Your Diet

Here are some ways to enjoy cheonggukjang:

  1. Cheonggukjang jjigae (stew): the most traditional way - simmered with tofu, vegetables, and meat.
  2. Cheonggukjang bibimbap: rice topped with cheonggukjang and assorted vegetables, mixed together before eating.
  3. Cheonggukjang fried rice: quickly stir-fried with rice and vegetables for a convenient meal.
  4. Cheonggukjang and doenjang soup: using both cheonggukjang and doenjang (regular soybean paste) adds extra depth of flavor.
  5. Cheonggukjang spread: use it as a spread on bread or crackers.

Conclusion

Cheonggukjang is more than just a traditional food - it's a superfood that offers a wide range of health benefits. Research continues to support its roles in digestion, blood pressure, cholesterol, immunity, and antioxidant activity.

Incorporating cheonggukjang into a healthy diet can support overall well-being and help prevent various chronic conditions. That said, it's important to adjust your intake based on your own health status and any medications you're taking.

Cheonggukjang embodies traditional Korean wisdom and is still recognized for its value through the lens of modern nutrition science. Consider making it a regular part of your healthy eating plan.
