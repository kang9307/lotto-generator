Introduction: How Do You Know If AI Is Citing Your Content?

Open Google Analytics and you can see at a glance how many people visited yesterday, what they read, and where they came from. But the real question of 2026 never shows up on that screen. Right now, when someone asks ChatGPT a question in your field, did the AI cite your article as a source? The user never reached your site, nothing was logged in your analytics, yet your content may well have been consumed. This invisible exposure is the blind spot of measurement.

Running a four-language tech blog (braindetox.kr), I feel this blind spot growing. Search traffic plateaus even though my content quality and visibility have not declined. The explanation is simple. People are getting their information from AI answer boxes instead of search bars, and inside those answers my work is used as a source while the click never happens. You cannot improve what you cannot measure, which is exactly why "AI citation tracking" has emerged as a new category of analytics.

This article is a practical guide to how you track and measure AI citations. Starting from Citly, a tool that drew attention for tracking Korean sites' AI citations, it covers the new concept of GEO (Generative Engine Optimization), the five metrics worth tracking, how citation differs across models, a comparison of similar tools, and above all how to build citation-friendly content yourself. I write this not as reporting but from the seat of someone who actually wants to measure and improve.

1. What Is GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)? The Next Step After SEO

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization. For two decades we have been comfortable with SEO, search engine optimization, whose goal was to rank high in Google or Naver results and earn the click. GEO has a different goal: to make generative AI like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude cite your content as a trustworthy source when they compose their answers.

The decisive difference is the destination. In SEO, the user eventually arrives at your site. In GEO, the user need never arrive at all. Your information melts into the AI answer, and at best you get a single line as a source link. This structure breaks the core assumption of SEO, that exposure leads to a click.

So in GEO the target of optimization shifts. More than keyword density or backlinks, what matters is whether you serve facts in a form an AI can parse and trust. Clear definition sentences, structured data, self-contained claims, and verifiable first-party data become GEO assets. SEO does not disappear. AI still crawls the web, so basic SEO is the foundation of GEO. But a new layer, optimizing to be cited by AI, is laid on top of it.

2. What Is Citly? An AI Citation Tracker Overview

Citly is a tool that gained attention in the Korean developer community (GeekNews) in May 2026 for tracking Korean sites' AI citations. Its core idea is simple yet powerful: automatically ask the same questions to multiple generative AIs and tally whether your domain appears as a source in their answers.

Where traditional analytics looks at "after the user reaches your site," an AI citation tracker like Citly looks at "before the user reaches your site, inside the AI answer screen." The thing being measured is fundamentally different. Conceptually it works in roughly this flow.

  • Define a query set: Build a bundle of representative questions in your topic. For a tech blog, that might be questions like "how to use Claude Code."
  • Multi-model querying: Repeatedly ask the same questions to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and others.
  • Source parsing: Extract the cited sources, links, and domains from each answer.
  • Matching and aggregation: Tally how often your domain appears as a source, where, and how it varies by model.
  • Reporting: Show citation trends over time and share of voice versus competitors on a dashboard.

The important point is that Citly is not the proprietary feature of one tool, but rather a Korea-born instance of a new tool category called "AI citation tracking." Its specialization in Korean-language queries and Korean domains differentiates it from global tools. Understanding the "object" the tool measures matters more, and lasts longer, than the tool itself.

3. Five Metrics for AI Citation Tracking

To track AI citations seriously, you must move beyond the binary of "AI mentioned me or not." Here are the five metrics I actually watch for my own site.

3.1 Citation Frequency

The share of your query set in which your domain appears as a source. If your domain shows up in 12 of 100 answers, citation frequency is 12%. This is the foundational north-star metric of GEO, and the trend over time matters more than the absolute value.

3.2 Source Position

Even the same citation has different value depending on whether it was used as a core piece of evidence in the body or squeezed into a reference-link list at the end. A citation used as a primary source is a far stronger authority signal than a passing mention, and more likely to drive clicks.

3.3 Per-Model Variance

If you are cited often in ChatGPT but never in Gemini, that itself is diagnostic information. Models differ in training data and search backends, so knowing where you are weak tells you where to reinforce.

3.4 Topic-Level Citation

Look at which topics earn citations rather than the overall average. If you are cited only on topics where you already have authority but not on new topics you are pursuing, your content-investment priorities become visible.

3.5 Share of Voice

Your citation share relative to competing domains across the same query set. Citation is close to zero-sum: an AI does not stuff unlimited sources into one answer, so who occupies that slot is the competition.

4. How ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude Cite Differently

Lumping "AI cites" into one thing makes tracking inaccurate. The four models handle citation quite differently.

ModelSource styleLive web searchTracking difficulty
ChatGPT (search mode)Inline + source cardsYes (when search on)Medium
PerplexityNumbered footnotes + source listAlwaysLow (explicit sources)
GeminiIntegrated in body + some linksGoogle Search-linkedMedium to high
ClaudeCites on search, none by defaultWhen tool enabledHigh (nondeterministic)

Perplexity is the easiest to track. It is source-first by design, attaching numbered footnotes to every claim and always exposing a source list. It is the model an AI citation tracker can parse most reliably.

ChatGPT varies by whether search mode is on. With search active you get source cards and inline links, but when it answers from pure trained knowledge no source is shown. Your content may be reproduced from training data with no attribution, which makes this a gray area of measurement.

Gemini is tied to Google Search and tends to integrate information smoothly into the body, so explicit sources can be sparse. Claude cites only when its web search tool is active and shows no sources in default answers, making it the most nondeterministic to track. The takeaway: measurement reliability differs by model, so it is more accurate to break the data out per model than to trust a single number.

My recommendation is a staged approach. Start with manual tracking. 6. Seven Ways to Make Content AI-Citation Friendly

Tracking is only half the job. After you measure, you must actually raise the probability of being cited. Here are seven practices I have compiled and apply myself.

  • 1. Add structured data. FAQPage, HowTo, and BlogPosting schemas make it easier for models to parse and attribute your content. This article uses FAQPage schema too.
  • 2. Write FAQs. The question-answer format mirrors the structure of AI answers, making it the form AI most readily lifts and reuses.
  • 3. Put clear definitions up front. A one-sentence definition like "GEO is..." is the easiest unit for AI to cite. Define key terms cleanly on first mention.
  • 4. Use citable formats. Tables, numbered lists, and short paragraphs are easy to lift wholesale. Decompose claims instead of mashing them into one paragraph.
  • 5. Make authority signals explicit. Author identity, publish date, and source attribution are the trust signals that decide which source an AI picks among similar pages.
  • 6. Keep it fresh. Date markers like "as of May 2026" and periodic updates raise your odds of being cited in time-sensitive questions.
  • 7. Widen reach with multiple languages. Offering the same article in several languages puts you in the citation pool of each language's models. This is why braindetox.kr publishes in four languages.

7. GEO vs Traditional SEO - What Is Different

Treating GEO and SEO as opponents is a misread. They are layers at different levels. Still, the optimization focus is clearly different.

  • Goal: SEO targets rank and clicks; GEO targets citation and source adoption.
  • Measurement: SEO uses rank, CTR, sessions; GEO uses citation frequency, source position, share of voice.
  • Unit: SEO's unit is the "page"; GEO's is the "self-contained, citable factual statement."
  • Battleground: SEO is backlinks and keywords; GEO is clarity, originality, and structure.
  • Reach: SEO reaches search users; GEO reaches users who see (and may never click) an AI answer.

The key is that GEO does not replace SEO but layers on top of it. AI still crawls the web and prefers authoritative pages, so good SEO is a prerequisite for GEO. But a well-ranked page is not automatically well-cited. To aim for citation you must redesign content so AI can "lift it one piece at a time."

8. AI Citation = Traffic? Revenue? (The Link to Citation Compensation)

Here we must answer the most uncomfortable question. If you track and grow citations, does that become traffic or revenue? The honest answer is "partly, and increasingly so."

Perplexity and Gemini surface clickable source links that send a steady stream of referral traffic. But with many zero-click answers, the "citation equals traffic" equation has weakened. Valuing citations by traffic alone undersells them. A citation is itself brand visibility and an authority signal, and a domain repeatedly cited on a topic gets etched into user perception.

And there is a bigger picture. Naver recently began experimenting with linking AI answer citation counts directly to creator compensation (see the related article in the references below). The direction is one where citations become a reward unit in their own right, independent of clicks. If this model spreads, citation tracking becomes revenue tracking. Whoever builds the habit of measuring citations now will be ready when citation compensation goes mainstream. GEO is not merely a visibility game but a pre-emptive investment in a future revenue base.

9. Application for Site Owners - The braindetox.kr Case

Let me share the actual process of applying the theory to my own site. braindetox.kr is a four-language tech blog that publishes a Korean original alongside English, Japanese, and Chinese translations.

  • Query set: I picked about 20 representative Korean and English questions on topics where I have authority (Claude Code, AI search, GEO).
  • Manual tracking first: Each week I feed the same questions to every model and log whether the braindetox.kr domain appears in a spreadsheet. Korean queries surface most often in Perplexity.
  • Structural improvement: Every article gets FAQPage schema and clear definition sentences. The opening definitions in this article are examples.
  • Multilingual leverage: Publishing in four languages puts me in the citation pool of each language's models simultaneously. I have observed Korea-origin content cited in Japanese and Chinese markets.
  • Publishing workflow: On publish I automate sitemap, RSS, and structured-data validation so AI crawlers discover new posts quickly.

The key lesson is that you can start without any grand tool. Twenty questions, a spreadsheet, and the habit of adding an FAQ to every article already get you 80% of the way through citation tracking and GEO.

10. Risks - The Limits of Citation Tracking and Measurement Accuracy

Pure optimism would be a dereliction of duty. AI citation tracking has real limits.

  • Nondeterminism: AI answers the same question differently each time. Being cited once does not mean being cited always; tracking is inherently a probabilistic sample.
  • Unattributed use: Your content can be absorbed into training data and reproduced with no source attribution. No tracker catches this use.
  • Sample bias: Your query set is the limit of your measurement. Choose questions poorly and you get a distorted view of your real citation reality.
  • Personalization and localization: AI answers vary by user, region, and time, so the results you see are not the results every user gets.
  • Cost versus benefit: A paid tracker can be over-investment for a small site. The stage where manual tracking suffices lasts a long time.

These limits do not render tracking useless. They simply mean you should not blindly trust a single number and should instead adopt the posture of reading trends, per-model breakdowns, and qualitative observation together. Measurement here is less a precise science than a compass for direction.

11. Conclusion + Three Things to Apply Today

The web is shifting from a click economy to an answer economy, and who gets cited inside those answers becomes the new currency of visibility. Tools like Citly and the concept of GEO have finally made this invisible exposure measurable. It is not perfect and it is nondeterministic, but the very fact that we have begun tracking the untrackable is a turning point. Even in an AI answer economy, a path is opening for those who write good content to be rewarded.

If you forget everything else, here are three things you can do this week.

  1. Pick 10 core questions and start tracking yourself. Feed the same questions to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude, and log whether your domain appears in a spreadsheet. In a week you will have your first citation frequency.
  2. Add FAQs and clear definitions to your top three articles. Turning vague paragraphs into self-contained factual statements and adding FAQPage schema immediately raises your citation odds.
  3. Track citation as a metric separate from traffic. A citation is valuable even with no click. Start accruing citation data now to be ready when citation compensation models arrive.

Just as those who watched analytics early got ahead in the SEO era, those who track citations early will get ahead in the GEO era. Tools merely assist; the core is the habit of asking, every week, "is AI citing my content?"
