This article relies on Apple Support’s “How to use AirDrop on iPhone and iPad” page (119857) and the AirDrop topic in the Mac User Guide (mh35868). Anything those two pages do not state is collected in the section on what could not be confirmed.

Two Kinds of Symptoms and the Basic Premises in Apple’s Pages

Reports that AirDrop does not work fall into two groups. In the first, the other person never appears on the share screen. In the second, the person appears, but the transfer stops at the accept or send stage. Apple’s pages do not describe these two symptoms separately, but deciding which one applies makes it faster to choose the order of checks.

Not Listed Versus Listed but Not Progressing

The Mac User Guide describes the recipients shown in the share list as people who are nearby, have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on, and can accept files sent from your device. If the other person is missing from the list, one of those three conditions, which are distance, radios on, and willingness to receive, is not met.

If the person’s name appears but nothing progresses, the pages support three candidates: the recipient has not accepted the prompt, a contact requirement or the AirDrop code procedure is blocking the transfer, or a device moved out of range during the transfer. The checks below follow that order. Whether the name appears in the list is the starting point: if it does not, look at connections and settings, and if it does, look at acceptance and conditions.

The Four Things Apple Says to Check First

  • Both people turn on Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. This is the first sentence under “Before you begin” in the iPhone and iPad page.
  • If either person has Personal Hotspot on, disconnect it.
  • Both the sender and the recipient must be using an iPhone, iPad, or Mac, according to the “If AirDrop doesn’t work” section of the Mac User Guide.
  • Check that the recipient has AirDrop set to receive from Everyone or Contacts Only, and if that does not help, restart the devices and try again.

Step 1 — Receiving Settings and the 10-Minute Rule

The receiving setting is changed on the receiving person’s device. To exchange items with someone who is not in the contacts, that person has to open the setting on their own device, so having the other person change their setting first when the list is empty matches the flow of the iPhone and iPad page.

Setting Names and Where to Open Them

  • Path: the iPhone and iPad page says to open Control Center, touch and hold the connectivity controls, and tap AirDrop. The Mac User Guide describes touching and holding the group of controls at the top left of Control Center.
  • Contacts Only: allows only items sent by people in the contacts.
  • Everyone for 10 Minutes: the setting the page tells the other person to choose when sharing with someone who is not in the contacts.
  • Receiving Off: in the iPhone and iPad page it appears as the value a device that is not signed in to an Apple Account reverts to after 10 minutes.

The Mac User Guide adds that the Contacts Only and Everyone for 10 Minutes options are available only on devices that meet the minimum system requirements. If the options themselves are missing, check the device’s software version first.

The Setting Changes After 10 Minutes

In iOS 16.2 and later, the Everyone for 10 Minutes setting changes after 10 minutes. If the person is signed in to an Apple Account, it reverts to Contacts Only. If not, it reverts to Receiving Off.

If AirDrop worked yesterday but the other person does not appear today, that device may already have reverted to Contacts Only or Receiving Off. If the person does not meet the contact requirement from Step 4, the setting has to be opened to 10 minutes again.

Step 2 — Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Personal Hotspot

The basis for this step is a single paragraph under “Before you begin” in the iPhone and iPad page, together with the same section of the Mac User Guide.

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on Both Sides

The page says to make sure both people have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on. Turning them on for only one side does not meet the condition.

  • Both items must be on for the other person’s device as well, so have the other person look in Control Center to see that both are on.
  • If a Mac is on either side, the same condition applies to the Mac’s Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The Mac User Guide says they must be on for each device.
  • If the settings are fine but the problem remains, restarting the devices and trying again is the Mac User Guide’s general remedy.

Disconnect Personal Hotspot

The sentence in the iPhone and iPad page is that if either of you has Personal Hotspot on, you should disconnect. The one action the page asks for is to disconnect.

  • It applies to either person. It is not enough to look only at your own hotspot.
  • The action the page asks for is to disconnect. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth still need to be on for both people after the hotspot is off.
  • After disconnecting the hotspot, retry from the first sharing step: tap AirDrop on the Share button.

Step 3 — Distance and Leaving Range Mid-Transfer

Within About 10 Meters

Under “Before you begin,” the Mac User Guide says the devices must be within 30 feet (10 meters) of each other, and its troubleshooting list repeats the distance. The Korean edition writes it as about 10 meters. Moving both devices close together in the same room and trying again is a check that stays within the range the page states. The distance condition can be met without touching any setting, just by keeping the two devices close.

If You Move Away After the Transfer Starts

The Mac User Guide’s note says that if you move out of Bluetooth or Wi-Fi range after an AirDrop transfer has started, the transfer continues over the internet. The distance condition before starting and leaving range after starting are treated differently in the page.

  • Before starting: the two devices must be within about 10 meters.
  • After starting: the page says the transfer continues over the internet even if you leave range.

Step 4 — Contact Cards and the Same Apple Account

Conditions When Sending to a Contacts Only Recipient

The Mac User Guide says that when you send to someone who accepts items only from contacts, you must be in the recipient’s contacts, and the recipient’s contact card for you must include your Apple Account email address or phone number. The iPhone and iPad page says the same in substance: the other person’s contact card needs your Apple ID email address or phone number.

  • The place to check is the recipient’s Contacts app. What the pages require is registration on the recipient’s side.
  • The value that must be on the card is the email address or phone number the sender uses for the Apple Account.
  • If the condition is hard to meet, the recipient can open the setting to Everyone for 10 Minutes (Step 1).

Devices Signed In to the Same Apple Account

The Mac User Guide says that when items are sent between devices signed in with the same Apple Account, for example from a Mac to an iPhone, they are accepted and saved automatically.

  • If you sent between your own devices and no accept prompt appeared, that can be normal.
  • The condition for automatic acceptance is that both devices are signed in to the same Apple Account.

Step 5 — AirDrop Codes (26.2 and Later)

When a Code Is Needed and the Procedure

The iPhone and iPad page says that when both devices use iOS 26.2 or later and you share with someone who is not in your contacts, you are prompted to use an AirDrop code. The Mac User Guide says a code is required when a person who is not in your contacts tries to send to a device running iOS 26.2, iPadOS 26.2, or macOS 26.2 or later.

  • Sender: tapping the other person under People brings up the prompt to use a code.
  • Recipient: choosing Continue or tapping Get AirDrop Code shows a code on the device, which the recipient tells the sender.
  • When the sender enters that code on their own device, the item is shared.

The 30-Day Rule

A person connected by code is added to the Other Known List in your contacts for 30 days, and during that time you can keep exchanging items without another code. A code is needed when you first exchange items with someone outside the contacts, and within that period you do not enter one again for the same person. To stop before the 30 days are up, the iPhone and iPad page says to mark the person as unknown in the Contacts app, and the Mac User Guide says to choose Remove This Person.

Step 6 — Turning On and Receiving on a Mac

Where to Turn It On and Who Can Send

  • Click AirDrop in Control Center in the menu bar. When it turns blue, it is on.
  • Click and hold next to AirDrop, or click the arrow beside it, and choose Contacts Only or Everyone.
  • This setting decides who can send items to the Mac. The Mac User Guide uses Everyone, not the iOS wording Everyone for 10 Minutes.

The Finder Sidebar, Accept Prompts, and Save Locations

  • Finder: open a Finder window from the Dock and click AirDrop in the sidebar, and you can drag the item you want to send onto the recipient.
  • Notifications: accept or decline notifications appear in Notification Center. If AirDrop is open in Finder, they appear in that window instead.
  • Saving: most items are saved in the app they were sent from, and photos are saved in the Photos app. On iPhone and iPad some items are saved in the Files app, and on a Mac some items are saved in the Downloads folder.
  • Volume: the Mac User Guide says AirDrop works best for sharing a small number of files, and points to syncing or iCloud for large numbers of files.

Holding Devices Together in iOS 17 and Communication Safety

Sharing by Bringing Devices Close Together

The iPhone and iPad page says that if both devices are on iOS 17 or later, you can try holding them together to share via AirDrop. The Mac User Guide says you can start the same way when you are near someone in your contacts, and limits it to supported models with iOS 17 or later.

Communication Safety

  • If a child receives or attempts to send certain sensitive content via AirDrop, Communication Safety can warn the child, blur the content, and provide helpful resources.
  • The pages describe this feature as protection for children and do not present it as a cause of transfer failures.
  • The settings path is covered in Apple’s separate page (105069).

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • Why Personal Hotspot has to be disconnected. The pages give only the action, not the reason.
  • Whether a transfer that has already started continues when there is no internet connection.
  • How walls or nearby radio interference affect the 10-meter distance. The pages give only the distance figure.
  • How to choose Receiving Off directly, and an equivalent value on the Mac; in the iPhone and iPad page it appears only as the value a device reverts to after 10 minutes. The screen path for entering values on a contact card, the handling of an accept step not finished within 10 minutes, whether Everyone on a Mac reverts automatically, and whether cellular data is in use are not in the pages either.
  • Interoperability with Android and other platforms. This topic is outside the scope here; syncing between a smartphone and a PC is covered in the smartphone and PC sync guide.
