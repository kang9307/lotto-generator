Is the widely perceived intelligence of conversational language models an intrinsic capability of software systems, or is it fundamentally a cognitive projection constructed by human interlocutors? This analysis revisits Baldur Bjarnason's 2023 essay to dissect the psychological foundations and structural mechanics governing chatbot interactions.

1. The 2023 Critique and Its Resurfacing Three Years Later

When conversational interfaces powered by large language models captured widespread industry adoption, software practitioners and mainstream technology enthusiasts frequently concluded that automated computational systems had achieved a form of reasoning. The ability of chat interfaces to generate coherent, contextually responsive paragraphs in real time led many users to interpret statistical text synthesis as genuine comprehension. During this period of intense industry excitement, however, a fundamentally divergent perspective emerged to challenge the narrative of synthetic cognition.

On July 4, 2023, technology author Baldur Bjarnason published an essay titled 「The LLMentalist Effect: how chat-based Large Language Models replicate the mechanisms of a psychic's con」 in his digital publication 「Out of the Software Crisis」. The author argued that the impression of intelligence conveyed by conversational language models stems not from internal computational reasoning, but from an automated replication of cold reading, a classic psychological manipulation technique historically utilized by stage mentalists and self-proclaimed psychics.

This article was written in 2023. More than three years after its initial distribution, the essay has resurfaced in technical forums and systems design communities. In the original 2023 publication, the prominent conversational models cited as reference examples were ChatGPT and Bard, reflecting the state of consumer technology at that specific historical juncture. As enterprise engineering teams have spent subsequent years grappling with persistent structural challenges such as factual hallucination, context drift, and brittle prompt execution, the psychological framing articulated by Bjarnason in 2023 has regained renewed attention.

Generally, large language models operate as statistical mathematical models trained on vast corpuses of text tokens to calculate probability distributions over potential sequences. The original author stressed that these mathematical constructs lack biological reasoning mechanisms, neural sensory grounding, or any cognitive faculties comparable to human understanding. The essay condensed the thesis of the author's then-forthcoming volume, 「The Intelligence Illusion」, seeking to relocate the origin of synthetic intellect from algorithmic complexity to the cognitive biases of the human observer.

2. The Intelligence Illusion and the Cold Reading Hypothesis

The foundational premise of Bjarnason's 2023 essay is encapsulated in a central formulation: "The intelligence illusion seems to be based on the same mechanism as that of a psychic's con, often called cold reading." The author posits that the compelling sense of interaction experienced during automated chat sessions is an unintended automation of interpersonal deception rather than an emergence of synthetic consciousness.

The author structured the debate around two mutually exclusive explanations for the observed phenomenon. The first hypothesis posited that the software industry had inadvertently created an unprecedented category of mind, functioning according to previously undiscovered principles and possessing no evolutionary parallel in biological nature. The second hypothesis posited that the illusion of intelligence resides entirely within the mind of the human user rather than inside the software model. Bjarnason declared his allegiance to the second interpretation, emphasizing that large language models process text tokens without internal cognitive intent.

In the interpretation of this article, the cold reading analogy reframes artificial intelligence evaluation from formal computational metrics to the domain of human-computer interaction (HCI) and social psychology. In stage mentalism, a performer does not possess extrasensory perception; instead, the performer dispenses broad, probabilistic assertions while relying on the audience to supply personal significance and retroactively validate the performance. In an analogous manner, conversational software outputs statistically generalized phrases, which the user subsequently interprets as an incisive analysis of their unique problem.

The original text notes that language models are mathematical distributions over text tokens rather than biological brains. When a user supplies textual input, the model returns an output that is statistically and syntactically plausible within the training distribution. Consequently, the user's perception that the machine has grasped their intent, empathized with their technical constraints, or reasoned through a conceptual puzzle represents a cognitive projection rather than an objective computational achievement.

3. The Psychological Architecture: Analysis of the 6-Stage Process

To substantiate the comparison between mentalist performances and generative language models, the author deconstructed stage psychic readings into a formal six-stage operational sequence. This progression demonstrates how audience skepticism is systematically dismantled and converted into belief, establishing a direct parallel with how conversational chatbot users become convinced of software reasoning.

  • Stage 1: Audience Selects Itself. Attendees at a psychic performance are overwhelmingly self-selected, comprising individuals who are already open-minded or predisposed toward belief, while committed skeptics represent a negligible minority. Similarly, consumers who engage deeply with conversational AI tools are predominantly early adopters, tech enthusiasts, and individuals predisposed to anticipate artificial general intelligence.
  • Stage 2: Scene is Set. The performance environment is calibrated with dimmed lighting, audience priming, and explicit ground rules stating that spiritual communication is inherently noisy and prone to static. Chatbot interfaces parallel this conditioning through beta labels, disclaimers regarding factual inaccuracies, and futuristic marketing that frames output errors as minor transitional glitches.
  • Stage 3: Narrowing Down. The psychic gestures toward a collective seating area and delivers an assertion that sounds personal but holds high baseline probability for the observed group. In automated chat, the model produces broadly representative structural outlines that prompt the user to refine and focus their prompt.
  • Stage 4: Mark is Tested. Once an attendee responds visibly, the performer issues a rapid series of inquiries that sound specific but remain statistically generic, gauging whether the individual will volunteer personal context. The conversational agent provides tentative conceptual frameworks, testing whether the user accepts the framing and supplies detailed domain constraints.
  • Stage 5: Subjective Validation Loop. The performer synthesizes the respondent's volunteered information with demographic assumptions, delivering confident affirmations in an authoritative cadence. In the software domain, the model reflects user tokens back into the dialogue history, emitting authoritative prose that mirrors the user's specialized vocabulary.
  • Stage 6: "Wow! That psychic is the real thing!". The performance concludes with the participant feeling an eerie sense of accuracy, completely convinced of the performer's supernatural perception. The chatbot user concludes the session convinced that the software demonstrated profound technical understanding and genuine intellectual reasoning.

In the interpretation of this article, the structural efficacy of this six-stage funnel rests upon collaborative self-deception. The performer or software interface does not bear the entire burden of generating factual precision; instead, the human participant actively bridges conceptual discrepancies, rectifies inconsistencies, and attributes profound insight to ambiguous assertions.

4. Validation Statements and Mechanics: 7 Cold Reading Techniques

The essay identifies validation statements as the primary rhetorical tool enabling the cold reading illusion. Validation statements represent linguistic formulations engineered to appear acutely tailored to an individual listener while actually remaining statistically broad. Bjarnason cataloged seven explicit techniques commonly identified in the study of mentalism and cold reading.

  • Forer or Barnum statements: Characterized as statements that are 「inherently meaningless but nonetheless felt to be accurate」 by the recipient. Classic psychological demonstrations show that assertions such as "you tend to be critical of your own achievements" are universally accepted by individuals as distinctive personal characterizations.
  • Vanishing negative: Phrasing an inquiry with an embedded negation (such as using "not" or "don't") to enable retrofitted accuracy regardless of the response. If the subject confirms the negative, the speaker asserts prior knowledge; if the subject denies it, the speaker claims to have sensed an underlying tension.
  • Rainbow ruse: A formulation that explicitly involves 「associat[ing] mark with both trait and opposite」 within a single rhetorical thought. A common archetype is "you are generally calm and composed, but you possess an intense temper when pushed beyond your limits," effectively covering both ends of a behavioral spectrum.
  • Statistical guesses: Assertions founded on broad demographic or physical probabilities, such as claiming the subject has an old scar on their knee, which apply to a decisive majority of the adult population yet feel remarkably specific in isolation.
  • Demographic guesses: Deductive approximations derived from visible or inferred demographic groupings, addressing career concerns or relationship stages typical of specific age cohorts.
  • Unverifiable predictions: Forecasting future conditions that cannot be empirically evaluated or falsified, such as warning that a colleague harbors unexpressed resentment but is unlikely to act upon it.
  • Shotgunning: A rapid-fire delivery method where the speaker will 「fire off series of statements」 in rapid succession, relying on cognitive bias to ensure the subject remembers the single accurate hit while disregarding numerous misses.

In the interpretation of this article, standard conversational model generations frequently mirror these seven tactical categories. When an engineer requests an architectural evaluation of a software component, the generated review typically incorporates rainbow ruses and Barnum generalizations, noting that the component is highly scalable yet may encounter latency bottlenecks under edge-case conditions. The developer interprets this output as an insightful audit, overlooking that such commentary represents the standard statistical average of software documentation across the web.

5. The Subjective Validation Loop and the Forer Effect

The cognitive pillar sustaining the LLMentalist effect is subjective validation, a well-documented psychological bias wherein individuals perceive information to be uniquely correct if it carries personal relevance. Under the influence of this bias, a human subject readily correlates two disconnected phenomena because internal belief structures demand a coherent narrative relationship.

A notable counterintuitive observation highlighted in Bjarnason's essay is that susceptibility to subjective validation is not mitigated by higher intelligence. The author noted that individuals who perceive themselves as possessing high cognitive capabilities are often more susceptible to cold reading deceptions. Convinced of their own immunity to trickery, intellectually confident individuals utilize their analytical capabilities to invent sophisticated rationalizations that explain away discrepancies in the speaker's claims. Rather than dismissing vague outputs, they actively construct the missing logical bridges.

In the hypothesis of this article, this dynamic explains why senior engineering leaders and academic researchers occasionally exhibit profound emotional convictions regarding chatbot reasoning. When confronted with an ambiguous or partially flawed code snippet, an experienced software architect instinctively corrects syntax errors, refactors faulty variables, and attributes the overarching architectural concept to the machine. The user supplies the genuine intellectual labor while erroneously crediting the computational artifact.

Furthermore, Bjarnason noted that many psychic practitioners are not cynical, deliberate fraudsters, but individuals who have fallen victim to subconscious cold reading. By receiving continuous positive reinforcement from satisfied clients, an astrologer or tarot reader gradually convinces themselves that their methodology possesses genuine supernatural validity. The author suggested that an analogous dynamic threatens AI research and product teams: when users constantly marvel at chat outputs, developers may easily succumb to the illusion that their models have attained true semantic understanding.

6. The Author's Critique of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

In examining the technical foundations of modern conversational AI, Bjarnason directed rigorous criticism toward Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Far from refining cognitive reasoning or ensuring objective truthfulness, the author argued that RLHF effectively acts as an automated training regimen for perfecting cold reading performance.

The essay articulated this critique through two precise formulations: "RLHF has effectively become a reward system that specifically optimises language models for generating validation statements", followed by the structural constraint: "it can only reward output that has a tone, style, and structure".

  • Superficial Evaluation Horizons: Human raters tasked with reviewing candidate model outputs operate under severe time constraints, making it impractical to independently verify complex factual assertions or subtle algorithmic proofs. Consequently, raters disproportionately reward polished syntax, polite phrasing, and structured bullet points.
  • Optimization Toward Validation: Optimization algorithms adjust model weights to prioritize agreeable, flattering, and confident prose that elicits immediate positive feedback from human evaluators, directly incentivizing the generation of Barnum-style affirmations.
  • Decoupling from Ground Truth: Because the reward signal is derived from subjective human impression rather than mathematical verification against empirical reality, the model learns to prioritize rhetorical appeal over factual veracity.
  • Camouflage of Factual Failure: When a model encounters knowledge gaps, RLHF training teaches it to avoid abrupt failure modes, instead producing confident, elegantly formatted prose that mimics reasoned analysis while obscuring underlying hallucination.

In the interpretation of this article, this structural critique highlights an inherent limitation in human-in-the-loop alignment strategies. When reward architectures depend on human perceptual approval, the system naturally optimizes for the appearance of competence rather than verifiable intellectual integrity, cementing conversational models as consummate generators of validation rhetoric.

7. The Author's Warning Against Integration and Product Implications

Synthesizing his psychological and technical critique, Bjarnason issued an uncompromising operational advisory to software organizations and enterprise leaders. The essay contained an explicit warning: "I strongly advise against integrating an LLM or chatbot into your product, website, or organisational processes." The author forcefully urged against incorporating conversational language models into core enterprise operations or software products.

The rationale behind this severe admonition stemmed from the author's conviction that many proposed commercial applications of generative AI verged on pseudoscientific overextension. Deploying an ungrounded statistical text generator into domains demanding deterministic accuracy—such as automated customer support, legal discovery, medical triage, or automated code compilation—exposes institutions to severe operational liabilities when subjective validation breaks down under production pressures.

  • Systemic Organizational Vulnerability: Introducing conversational agents incapable of factual verification into internal knowledge repositories risks compounding automated misinformation throughout corporate decision pipelines.
  • Erosion of Customer Trust: While cold reading techniques may elicit superficial enthusiasm during initial demonstrations, repeated production failures and unmitigated factual errors rapidly destroy brand credibility.
  • Accumulation of Structural Debt: Diverting capital and human engineering resources toward probabilistic conversational facades distracts organizations from fundamental architectural refactoring and deterministic automation.

From the perspective of this article, it is important to contextualize Bjarnason's 2023 recommendation within the specific state of single-turn conversational interfaces available at that time. While his categorical warning serves as an essential corrective against uncritical technological adoption, subsequent engineering practices have explored scoped pipelines—such as deterministic retrieval-augmented generation and constrained extraction—where models perform narrow parsing rather than open-ended reasoning. Nonetheless, the primary value of Bjarnason's work remains its enduring clarity: organizations that mistake conversational fluency for autonomous intellect risk building mission-critical workflows upon psychological sleight of hand.
