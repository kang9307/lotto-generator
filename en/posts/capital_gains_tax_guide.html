Introduction: the wall you hit when you sell a home

When you buy and sell property in Korea, or cash out certain shares, one term always follows the deal: capital gains tax (yangdo sodeukse). Two people can sell at the same price, yet one pays nothing while the other pays tens of millions of won. The difference is not luck. It comes down to requirements, deductions, holding period and filing. Capital gains tax is widely regarded as one of the most complex parts of the Korean tax system, tangled with exemptions, reliefs, surcharges and special cases, so small differences turn into large amounts.

This article is an informational guide to help newcomers grasp the big picture at once. It walks through what Korean capital gains tax is, what it applies to, the conditions for the one-household one-home exemption, how the long-term holding deduction and basic deduction work, how rates are set, and when you must file and pay and how to reduce the tax lawfully. Note that tax law changes often, and real estate taxation in particular moves quickly with policy. In 2026 alone, the multi-home surcharge in adjustment-target areas came back into force from 10 May, and in August the government published a tax reform plan that would reshape capital gains tax on homes. The figures here reflect the rules in force as of 29 September 2026, and proposals that have not yet passed the National Assembly are marked separately.

This article is general information, not tax advice, and does not replace a professional. All figures and explanations here, including tax rates, exemption and relief conditions, long-term holding deduction rates, the basic deduction, filing and payment deadlines, and whether a multi-home surcharge applies, are general standards based on official guidance such as the National Tax Service, and tax law changes frequently, so the current rules may differ depending on the time. Application varies greatly with each person's circumstances, such as holding period, residence, household composition, number of homes, adjustment-target-area status, and acquisition date. Aggressive tax saving is never guaranteed and raises the risk of penalties. Before any actual transaction, filing or payment, always confirm the latest rules for your situation with the National Tax Service (call 126), Hometax, or a licensed professional such as a certified tax accountant. All decisions and responsibility rest with you.

What is capital gains tax?

Capital gains tax is a tax on the profit (the taxable gain) made when you transfer an asset. The key is that it is charged on the gain, not on the full sale price. If you bought for 500 million and sold for 700 million, the starting point is the 200 million gain (then reduced further by necessary expenses), not the full 700 million. The National Tax Service also explains that no capital gains tax arises when a transfer produces no income or a loss.

What is taxable

Taxable assets are mainly real estate such as land and buildings, rights related to real estate such as sale rights and redevelopment membership rights, and certain shares and equity interests, along with assets like memberships. For listed shares, tax applies when a major shareholder sells, or when a minority shareholder sells outside the securities market (NTS), and the holding-value test for a major shareholder remains 50 billion won or more per stock (government announcement of September 2025; the 2026 reform summary lists no change to it). Everyday secondhand sales or ordinary bank interest are not subject to capital gains tax. Rates, deductions and exemption conditions all differ by asset type, which is the first reason the tax is so complex.

The meaning of transfer

Here, transfer means a change of ownership for consideration, through sale, exchange, or contribution in kind to a corporation. The essence is handing over the asset for value. A gift or inheritance, which passes property without consideration, falls under gift tax or inheritance tax instead. However, according to the NTS, the debt portion of a gift with assumed debt is treated as a transfer, and a sale between spouses or lineal relatives is presumed to be a gift. Even when property changes hands, the applicable tax differs entirely depending on whether value is exchanged and between whom.

In one line: capital gains tax is charged on the gain you earned, not the price you sold for. The devices that reduce that gain are deductions and exemptions.

How the tax is calculated

Capital gains tax is not set in one step. It runs through several stages, and understanding the flow shows where the tax falls.

From gain to taxable base

First, sale price minus acquisition cost and necessary expenses gives the taxable gain. Subtract the long-term holding special deduction to get the capital gains income, then subtract the basic deduction (2.5 million won per year) to reach the taxable base to which rates apply. Multiplying the base by the rate gives the computed tax.

Stage by stage

  • 1. Taxable gain = sale price − acquisition cost − necessary expenses (brokerage, acquisition tax, capital improvements, etc.)
  • 2. Capital gains income = taxable gain − long-term holding special deduction
  • 3. Taxable base = capital gains income − basic deduction (2.5 million won/year)
  • 4. Computed tax = taxable base × rate − progressive deduction

So the core of saving is to fully recognize necessary expenses in stage 1, maximize the holding deduction in stage 2, use the basic deduction in stage 3, and manage the rate brackets that shift with holding period and number of homes in stage 4.

The one-household one-home exemption - the strongest benefit

By far the biggest benefit in capital gains tax is the one-household one-home exemption. Meeting the conditions can mean little or no tax on the gain, which makes it the single most important item for the many people who own one home to live in.

Core conditions - holding and residence

According to the NTS and the government's Easy Law service, you must meet the following. First, on the date of transfer, one household owns only one home in Korea. Second, the home has been held for at least two years. Third, if the home was acquired on or after 3 August 2017 and was in an adjustment-target area at the time of acquisition, at least two years of residence during the holding period is added. What matters is whether the area was designated at the time of acquisition, not today. Some areas were newly designated on 16 October 2025, so for homes bought after that date, check the designation status on the acquisition date.

The 1.2 billion won high-price exception

Even a single home is not exempt without limit. A high-priced home whose actual transfer price exceeds 1.2 billion won is excluded from the exemption, and tax falls on the portion of the gain attributable to the amount over 1.2 billion won. Even then, the long-term holding deduction described below can apply heavily, easing the actual burden. The 1.2 billion won threshold is unchanged as of September 2026; the 1.2-to-1.4 billion won increase in the 2026 reform plan concerns the comprehensive real estate holding tax, not the capital gains high-price threshold.

The one-home exemption has many special cases such as temporary two-home situations, inheritance, marriage, combining households to care for elderly parents, and moving to or from farming, and they change often. This article covers only the basic skeleton, so never assume you are eligible; always confirm with the NTS or a tax professional.

Long-term holding special deduction - a reward for holding

The long-term holding special deduction removes a percentage of the taxable gain when you have held an asset for a long time. It is understood to ease the burden of taxing, in a single year, a gain that accumulated over many years.

Ordinary real estate - up to 30%

Under the NTS rate table (transfers on or after 1 January 2021), ordinary land and buildings get 6 percent from three years of holding, rising by 2 percent per year to a maximum of 30 percent at 15 years or more. The longer you hold, the larger the deduction, so the system is designed to favor long holding over short-term flipping.

One-household one-home - up to 80%

A qualifying one-household one-home enjoys a much larger rate. If the home has been held three years or more and the owner lived in it for at least two years during the holding period, a holding-based deduction (4 percent per year, up to 40 percent at 10 years) and a residence-based deduction (4 percent per year, up to 40 percent at 10 years) combine for up to 80 percent. Residence of two to under three years gives 8 percent. Even a high-priced single home above 1.2 billion won can see its taxable amount fall substantially thanks to this large deduction. If the two-year residence is not met, the ordinary rate (up to 30 percent) applies instead. And a home subject to the multi-home surcharge in an adjustment-target area gets no long-term holding deduction at all.

The 2026 tax reform plan (government proposal) - not yet in force

The 2026 Tax Reform Plan, announced by the Ministry of Economy and Finance on 3 August 2026 and finalized as the government bill on 1 September, would substantially change capital gains tax on homes. This is a proposed change, and the parts that amend statutes take effect only after the National Assembly passes them. The main items listed in the government material are:

  • Converting the long-term holding deduction from a holding-based to a residence-based deduction (up to 80 percent), with no deduction for homes not lived in
  • A new cap on the deduction: deferred for 2027, then 2 billion won in 2028 and 1 billion won from 2029 (a 1 billion won residence-deduction cap for homes worth over about 3 billion won)
  • Raising the basic deduction from 2.5 million to 25 million won a year for a one-home household that has lived there 10+ years and sells for 3 billion won or less
  • A reduction (up to 50 percent, capped at 500 million won) when a one-home owner aged 65+ moves from the capital region to a non-capital region
  • Capital gains changes deferred for 2027, then phased in over 2028 and 2029

Figures and timing may change in parliamentary review, so do not read this list as the rules that apply today.

Basic deduction and tax rates

After the holding deduction, you subtract the basic deduction and apply the rate. These two set the final tax.

Basic deduction - 2.5 million won per year

Capital gains income gets a basic deduction of 2.5 million won per person per year (per tax period). If you sell several assets in one year, they are combined and the deduction applies once, so spreading sales across different years to use the deduction multiple times is a frequently cited saving point. As of September 2026 the amount is 2.5 million won; the 25 million won figure in the reform plan is a proposal limited to qualifying long-term resident single homes.

Base rates - 6% to 45% progressive

For assets held two years or more, the base rate is progressive from 6 percent to 45 percent of the taxable base, using the same table as the comprehensive income tax. The brackets applying from 2023 are:

Short holding and multi-home surcharges

Short holding sharply raises the rate. Under the NTS rate table (transfers on or after 1 June 2021), ordinary land and buildings are taxed at 50 percent under one year and 40 percent from one to under two years. Homes and redevelopment membership rights are heavier at 70 percent under one year and 60 percent from one to under two years, and sale rights are 70 percent under one year and 60 percent from one year on, regardless of holding period. Where two or more rates apply, the one producing the larger tax is used.

For multiple-home owners in adjustment-target areas, the law adds 20 percentage points for two homes and 30 points for three or more to the base rate. From 10 May 2022 to 9 May 2026 this surcharge was temporarily waived for homes held two years or more, but on 12 February 2026 the government announced it would end on schedule without extension. So for transfers from 10 May 2026, the multi-home surcharge in adjustment-target areas applies again, and surcharged homes also lose the long-term holding deduction. Transitional relief applied, for example, where a sale contract was signed by 9 May and the transfer completed within four or six months of the contract depending on the area (Korea Policy Briefing, 2026-02-12 and 2026-04-24). Separately, the 2026 reform plan (government proposal) would temporarily ease the surcharge to 5 points for two homes and 10 points for three or more in 2027, and 10 and 15 points in 2028, returning to 20 and 30 points from 2029; the government Q&A says the eased surcharge rates would also apply to transfers made after the surcharge resumed on 10 May. Until the National Assembly passes it, the current rule is still 20 and 30 points.

Short-holding rates and multi-home surcharges are the parts that change most frequently with real estate policy. In particular, the May 2026 resumption of the surcharge overlaps with the reform plan's temporary easing, so the rate can depend on when you sell. Always confirm the current rules with the NTS or Hometax before a transaction.

Filing and payment - miss the deadline and penalties follow

Capital gains tax is self-assessed: you calculate, file and pay it yourself. Missing the deadline brings penalties, so managing the schedule matters.

Preliminary return - two months from the end of the month of transfer

According to the NTS, when you transfer real estate you must file and pay a preliminary return with your local tax office within two months from the last day of the month of transfer. For example, if you received the balance on July 15, the deadline is two months after that month-end (July 31), namely September 30. The transfer date is in principle the day the price is fully settled, and if the deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday, public holiday or Workers' Day, it moves to the next day. For shares, the preliminary return is due within two months from the end of the half-year of transfer. Failing to file triggers a non-filing penalty of 20 percent of the tax due and a late-payment penalty of 0.022 percent per day.

Final return - May of the next year

If you transferred several properties in one year, you file a combined final return with your local tax office between 1 and 31 May of the following year. If you had only one transfer and completed the preliminary return, no final return is needed. You can file electronically on Hometax or through your district tax office.

Installment payment

When the tax due exceeds 10 million won, part of it can be paid within two months after the payment deadline (NTS). This helps when paying a large sum at once is difficult. The amount you can defer depends on the size of the tax, so check the Hometax guidance before filing.

Lawful ways to reduce capital gains tax

The point is not to avoid tax you owe, but to reduce it within what the rules allow. Here are commonly cited lawful principles.

Meet the exemption and deduction conditions

The surest saving is meeting the one-home exemption conditions (two years held, and two years resided where required) or the long-term holding deduction. People often pay heavy tax by selling just days before meeting a requirement, so simply timing the sale after the conditions are met can change the result dramatically.

Document necessary expenses fully

Acquisition tax, legal and brokerage fees, and capital improvements such as balcony extensions may count as necessary expenses deducted from the gain. Documentation (tax invoices, receipts, contracts) is required, so keep property-related receipts as a habit. Some items, such as routine repairs, are not recognized, so the distinction matters.

Use sale timing and the basic deduction

If the holding period is just crossing the one-year, two-year, or long-term thresholds, a few days can change the rate and deduction. Because the 2.5 million won basic deduction applies each tax period, spreading sales across years lets you use it multiple times. Realizing gaining and losing assets in the same year so they offset is also discussed. If you own several homes, the 10 May 2026 resumption of the surcharge overlaps with the reform plan's temporary easing, so it is safer to confirm the rate fixed for your intended sale date with a tax professional before deciding.

The principles above are general directions, not guaranteed results. Eligibility, special cases and surcharges differ for each person, and aggressive attempts (under-reporting contracts, false residence registration) are illegal and carry heavy penalty and criminal risk. Always discuss actual application with a tax professional or the NTS.

Foreigners and overseas Koreans selling Korean property

Foreigners not resident in Korea, and overseas Koreans, are also taxed when they transfer Korean real estate. However, exemptions and deductions can differ depending on whether you are classified as a resident or a non-resident.

The NTS describes the one-home exemption on the premise of a resident transferring one home, so a non-resident may have limited access to such benefits. Procedures where the buyer withholds tax from the price, and tax treaties between countries, may also be involved, so the outcome varies greatly with nationality, place of residence and length of stay. The residence-based deduction in the 2026 reform plan (government proposal) is also built around actual residence, which could work against a home held while living abroad. Foreigners and overseas Koreans selling Korean property should always get individual advice from a Korean tax professional.

Conclusion - conditions and timing decide the tax

Capital gains tax is the most complex part of the Korean tax system, yet also a tax you can reduce as much as you understand it. Three points stand out. First, it is charged on the gain, not the price, and that gain is cut step by step by exemptions, the long-term holding deduction, the basic deduction and necessary expenses. Second, the one-home exemption and the long-term holding deduction are the strongest devices, sharply lowering the burden once you meet the requirements of holding, residence and number of homes. Third, missing the preliminary return deadline (two months from the last day of the month of transfer) brings penalties, so schedule management matters.

2026 added two more points. The multi-home surcharge in adjustment-target areas applies again to transfers from 10 May, and a tax reform plan (government proposal) that would shift the home holding deduction toward residence is awaiting parliamentary review. The rates, deduction rates, conditions and deadlines here are the rules in force as of 29 September 2026, and the proposal may change depending on what the National Assembly decides.

## 2. One-household one-home exemption check

- [ ] On the transfer date, the whole household owns only one home in Korea
- [ ] The home has been held for two years or more
- [ ] If acquired on or after 2017-08-03 and it was in an adjustment-target area **at acquisition**, you lived in it for two years or more during the holding period
- [ ] If it is in an area newly designated on 2025-10-16, you re-checked the designation status as of the acquisition date
- [ ] The actual transfer price is 1.2 billion won or less (above that, the gain on the excess is taxed)
- [ ] You confirmed with a professional whether a special case applies (temporary two homes, inheritance, marriage, caring for parents, etc.)
