AI Companies Are Hiring Electricians and Plumbers by the Thousands - The Jobs AI Investment Creates Are Not Desk Jobs

Data centers are built by hand, and this demand is a construction cycle

2026-07-31 Trends/Culture

Opening: The Top Listing Is an Electrician

When you open the careers page of one of the largest AI companies in the world and the role at the top is not a machine learning researcher but an electrician, it is easy to assume you misread something. You did not. What the big American technology firms announced one after another in the first half of 2026 was not research recruiting but direct investment in skilled trades training.

What makes this interesting is that it runs opposite to the narrative we have been fed for years. The sentence «AI destroys jobs» almost always assumes desk work: summarizing documents, writing code, building slides. Yet the compute that runs all that automation sits inside physical buildings, and those buildings are built by human hands. Pouring concrete, pulling conductors, welding pipe, fabricating duct.

This article looks at that other side. It has no interest in inflating the boom. There is one sentence it holds onto to the end.

AI investment is physical infrastructure investment. And infrastructure demand is a construction cycle. During the build and after commissioning are different things.

Without holding both sentences together the story tips one way. Saying only «AI is lifting the trades» leaves out what happens after commissioning; saying only «it is all a bubble» ignores the capital spending actually happening right now.

1. What Is Actually Happening - Confirmed by Official Announcements

Start with what can be verified. There are two items.

1.1 Meta - America's Workforce Academy

On June 8, 2026 Meta published America's Workforce Academy in its newsroom (the document carries update marks through June 10). The items confirmable directly from the official document are these.

  • It states a 115 million dollar first-year investment as the starting point. The phrase «first year» reads as presupposing a multi-year plan
  • It names fiber technicians, welders, plumbers and electricians plus other skilled trades as targets. The order of that list already resembles a data center schedule
  • The program is cost-free to participants, supports them while they learn, and guarantees a job for every graduate
  • The 2026 pilot locations are Louisiana, Ohio, Indiana and Texas. That these are places with large data center projects underway is hard to read as coincidence
  • Graduates receive an NCCER credential alongside the program's own certificate, and both are described as designed to travel with the worker across employers and industry sectors. That sentence matters again in section 7
  • Meta notes that its earlier fiber installation training program drew 35,000 applications in its first seven days. That is a signal about supply-side interest, not only demand
  • Meta calls this the largest private-sector commitment to the skilled trades with a job guarantee in American history. That is the company's claim, not third-party verification

2. Why These Trades Specifically - Servers Arrive Last

Look at the trade lists in section 1 again and a pattern appears. Electrical, plumbing, welding, sheet metal, fiber. Those five are close to reading a data center schedule from the front. And the crucial part is that no server-handling occupation appears on the list.

The common misconception is to picture a data center as «a warehouse you put servers in». The reality is closer to the opposite. A data center is a large electrical and heat-exchange installation with a roof over it, and servers are the last freight to arrive once all of that is in place.

Three things should be read out of that order.

  • Electrical appears twice. The electricity coming in at [4] (service and substation) and the electricity spreading out at [6] (distribution) involve different voltages and different qualifications. So they collapse into «electrician» as a word while in practice requiring several distinct streams of labor
  • Piping includes fire protection. Chilled water piping at [7] and suppression piping at [8] are separate systems installed separately. That is why piping demand is not explained by cooling alone
  • Why carpenters are needed sits in [2] and [3]. Carpentry in a data center is not interior finish, it is concrete formwork and temporary structure. Before you can pour the foundations and slabs that carry heavy equipment, the forms have to stand first

2.1 Floor Loading, the Constraint Nobody Sees

One decisive difference between an office building and a data center is floor loading. High-density racks, battery banks, transformers, generators and chillers are all heavy. And that load does not spread evenly across the building, it concentrates in specific zones.

  • Foundation and slab design becomes possible only after the equipment layout is fixed. Which means a cooling decision reaches backwards and changes the civil design
  • Battery rooms and generator rooms carry not just load but separate ventilation, exhaust, noise and fuel requirements, making them their own work packages
  • Equipment delivery routes (large cranes, knockout openings, haul roads) must be secured during structural design. Projects really do end up unable to get equipment in later
  • So data center projects have tightly coupled trade dependencies, and a labor gap in one trade delays every downstream package. That is precisely why companies are putting money directly into training

3. Electricity Is the Bottleneck

«There is not enough power» comes up constantly in data center discussion, but what exactly is short, and how, rarely gets separated. Three different problems hide in there.

3.1 Generation Shortage, Grid Capacity Shortage and Interconnection Delay Are Different

  • Generation shortage means there is not enough plant to make the electricity. It is solved by building generation
  • Grid capacity shortage means the electricity exists but the lines and substation equipment to deliver it to that point do not. It is solved by new and upgraded transmission and substations
  • Interconnection delay means the equipment is planned but permitting, compensation and the construction queue are backed up. Money does not resolve it immediately

What large data centers actually run into is usually the second and third. And those two directly create electrician demand, because installing lines and substation equipment is itself electrical construction.

3.2 Why Data Centers in Particular Create Grid Problems

At equal capacity, the shape of the load changes the burden on the grid. Data center load has three characteristics.

  • The load factor is high. A commercial building peaks during business hours and drops off at night, while a data center runs nearly flat 24 hours a day. From the grid's side, a load with no peak-sharing headroom attaches all at once
  • It concentrates at a single point. The same energy spread across a city is a completely different problem from the same energy in one campus. The latter directly demands regional substation capacity
  • It grows in large increments. Data centers expand one building at a time, and one building is already large. This is not gradual growth but a staircase jump

3.3 Long-Lead Equipment as a Separate Bottleneck

Labor is not the only bottleneck. The core equipment of an electrical system has long delivery times after order.

  • Large transformers are frequently built to specification rather than pulled from stock. Global manufacturing capacity for them is itself limited
  • High-voltage switchgear behaves the same way. If delivery slips, phase [4] waits in its entirety
  • Standby generators and large uninterruptible power supplies also carry long lead times
  • So project schedules are often set by delivery, not by construction capacity. A site can alternate between electricians waiting and electricians being short

Why does this structure matter? Because the statement «electrical labor is short» alone tells you neither when nor how much. When equipment delivery slips, the timing of labor demand slips with it. Section 8 reflects this when choosing indicators to watch.

4. Cooling and Water - Piping Reaches the Server

Power in means heat out. Essentially all electricity entering a data center turns into heat, so the power problem and the cooling problem are two faces of the same problem. And as cooling methods shift, the role of the piping trades has grown fundamentally larger.

4.1 Why Heat Once Carried by Air Is Now Carried by Water

The reason is simple. Air is a poor carrier of heat. When a rack draws modest power, blowing enough cold air at it works. But a rack packed with accelerators for AI training and inference draws far more power in the same footprint. Then you hit two walls.

  • The airflow wall. As required air volume rises, fan power climbs steeply and noise and vibration follow. Past some point you enter a regime where the electricity spent on cooling is itself the problem
  • The path wall. When the heat source is concentrated at a chip surface, air cannot reach it well enough. The heat has to be taken right at the point of generation
  • So the industry moves to cold plate direct liquid cooling or immersion cooling. The heat transfer medium changes from air to liquid
  • The construction implication is large. Previously only electricity reached the server; now water reaches the server too. Piping no longer stops in the plant room, it runs between the rack rows

4.2 The Water Question Is More Complex Than «It Uses a Lot»

Data center water use is routinely oversimplified. The real structure is a trade between water and electricity.

  • Evaporative cooling towers carry heat away as water evaporates, which makes them efficient. In exchange they consume water and bring concentrated blowdown and water treatment along with them
  • Air-cooled chillers use almost no water. In exchange they spend more electricity to reject the same heat. Where power is already the bottleneck, that choice is a burden
  • In other words, save water and you spend more electricity; save electricity and you spend more water. Local climate, water resources and grid conditions decide the choice
  • A liquid cooling closed loop does not continuously consume the circulating coolant. But at the stage where that heat is finally rejected outdoors, the same choice reappears
  • And one new risk appears: leaks. Liquid piping now runs right beside electronics, so workmanship, joining method, leak detection and isolation procedure all become safety requirements. It is a structure in which welder and pipefitter craftsmanship is directly an operating risk

That explains why Google's announced trade list places pipefitters, sheet metal workers and HVAC and refrigeration service technicians side by side. When the cooling method changes, the kind of hand skill required changes with it.

5. The Korean Situation - Structure and Contested Points

From here the subject is Korea. But this section contains almost no figures. Per the rule stated earlier, numbers whose primary government or public-agency statistical pages were not opened directly are not used. Instead it lays out which structures generate the contested points. It takes no position for or against any policy.

5.1 Demand and Supply Are Geographically Misaligned

Compress the Korean data center problem into one sentence and it reads: the places that make electricity and the places people want to build data centers are different.

  • Large generation sits on the coasts and in the provinces. Nuclear, coal and large-scale renewables all do
  • Data center demand, by contrast, concentrates in the capital region. The reasons are partly technical and partly not. Major customers and carrier circuits cluster there, network latency is lower, operations and construction labor is easier to find, and real estate and financing decisions live there too
  • The result is that electricity must be transmitted over long distances, and building transmission lines runs the long path of permitting, local acceptance and compensation
  • This is the structural cause behind «we secured the site but cannot get power»

5.2 Permitting Is Not a Single Gate

The approvals needed to build a data center are not one kind, and they sit with different authorities. So if any one of them blocks, progress on the rest is worthless.

  • Power supply side: an electricity supply application goes through technical review of grid conditions, which sets whether and on what conditions supply is possible. A finding of «not available» or a conditional finding changes the business plan itself
  • Land and building side: zoning, development permits, building permits, and environmental and traffic reviews each apply separately
  • Local government and community side: separate from legal requirements, local acceptance is a real variable. Arguments over noise, generator exhaust, transmission equipment and actual local economic contribution happen here
  • The key point is that these three axes are interlocked rather than parallel. Change the power conditions and the design changes; change the design and building permits are walked again

5.3 What Is Actually Being Argued in the Decentralization Debate

The relocation debate is often consumed as «capital-region restriction versus provincial revitalization», but the points actually argued in practice are narrower and more concrete. This article does not judge which side is right. It lists the contested points only.

  • Who bears grid reinforcement cost. Whether the cost of expansion required by a specific customer falls on that customer or is folded into general tariffs is the central dispute
  • How real the disadvantage of a provincial site is. Network latency is measurable; staffing and maintenance response time are hard to quantify
  • How large the local benefit is. A data center absorbs a great deal of labor during construction but is a facility with far fewer resident staff during operation. That gap creates the distance between local expectation and outcome
  • The nature of the instruments used. Incentives such as tariff or fee adjustment and restrictions on siting itself differ in both effect and side effect
  • Whether the time axes match. Data center decisions run on one to two year cycles while transmission and substation assets run far longer. That mismatch itself keeps regenerating the dispute

Why no figures appear in this section. Numbers for domestic power supply applications, capital-region concentration ratios, rates of unavailable findings and long-term supply targets do exist in news coverage. But while writing this article the primary materials from the utility and the relevant ministries were not opened and verified. Citing them without that check strips away decisive qualifiers such as which date they refer to and whether they count applications or approvals. So they were not used. The verification paths are collected in section 8.

6. Skilled Labor Supply - Very Few Confirmed Numbers

«So is Korea short of skilled trades too?» is the natural next question. The honest answer is this: widely cited figures exist, but the primary statistics were not opened and verified for this article. So no numbers are given. What is more useful instead is to name why these statistics are tricky.

6.1 Indicators That Look Alike Count Different Things

  • Number of licenses issued counts people who passed an exam. It does not tell you whether that person works in the trade. Korean electrical national technical qualifications are also earned for promotion points, bidding credentials and business registration requirements beyond employment, so they can diverge widely from employment counts
  • Construction trades workforce statistics have different populations depending on who surveys and on what basis. Records based on retirement mutual-aid enrollment and figures based on sample surveys will produce different numbers for the same year. Neither is wrong; they count different things
  • Unfilled positions by occupation is what employers reported. The threshold at which an employer answers «short» already embeds wage conditions, so the indicator means shortage at current terms, not raw physical shortage
  • Aging indicators get summarized as an average age, but what matters operationally is not the average, it is the distribution by age band and the rate of new entry. Two workforces with the same average age look nothing alike if one has new entrants and the other does not

6.2 What Can Still Be Said Structurally

Some structure holds without any figures. It comes from process and institutions rather than statistics.

  • Craft accumulates only through time. An exam can be passed in months; competence on high-voltage service gear or large chilled water piping cannot. That supply cannot follow a demand spike immediately is a fact prior to any statistic
  • Trade labor follows projects. When a large site opens in a region, nearby sites lose crew. So «national shortage» and «regional shortage» are different problems, and where data centers cluster, the latter shows up first
  • There is one more bottleneck inside training. The people who teach are skilled workers too. Expanding training pulls skilled workers off sites and into classrooms, which thins field labor further in the short run
  • This explains why Meta and Google went through existing union and association training systems or built their own academy. They bought a faster path than changing school curricula

7. As a Career Choice - Conditions and Risks Together

This section calls for the most caution. This article does not recommend a career path. It does not say to go get a particular license or apply to a particular company, and it recommends no schools or courses. What it does instead is put the conditions and the risks side by side.

7.1 The Shape of the Korean Qualification Path

Entry into this field in Korea is organized around national technical qualifications. In broad strokes it looks like this.

  • Electrical track: engineer, industrial engineer and craftsman grades in electrical work, plus the electrical construction variants on the installation side. The engineer grade carries education or practical experience prerequisites in its eligibility rules, so not everyone can simply sit it
  • Mechanical and building services track: HVAC and refrigeration machinery, building services and energy management grades border the cooling and air-conditioning side
  • Piping and welding track: piping and welding craftsman and industrial grades exist, and on this side actual installation skill and a record of passing inspection tend to be judged more directly on site than the certificate itself
  • There is also a part where regulation creates demand. Rules on appointing electrical safety managers and on contractor registration requirements generate structural demand for license holders. That demand exists independently of any data center boom

7.2 Difficulty of Entry Is Not Set by the Exam Alone

  • The exam and the field are separate gates. Between obtaining a qualification and being trusted with work sit further layers: experience requirements, crew affiliation, completed safety training
  • Working conditions differ from desk work. Outdoor, elevated, high-voltage and confined-space work, night and weekend commissioning, and relocation that follows the job are the actual conditions. Judging by pay alone leaves this out
  • This article gives no wage figures. Domestic wage statistics by occupation were not verified from primary sources, and overseas reported figures come from different markets, dates and job definitions, so transplanting them creates misunderstanding
  • Safety arrives as a personal matter, not a statistic. Injury risk in these trades is higher than in desk work, and it belongs on the scale together with the pay

7.3 How Long This Lasts - As Conditions, Not a Prediction

This is the most important passage. Whether this demand persists is a question of conditions, not forecasting. It rests on four things.

  1. Does capital spending continue. Whether the large operators hold or cut their capex plans is the first variable, and it is tied to the pace of AI monetization
  2. Does grid reinforcement keep up. Even with the will to invest, no power means no construction start. How fast the bottleneck in section 3 clears sets the actual volume
  3. Does ground actually get broken domestically. Overseas investment announcements do not automatically become domestic work. Domestic construction start statistics are the only verification
  4. What remains after commissioning. This is the item most often left out

On the fourth. Structurally, in a construction cycle the labor demand during the build and the operating headcount afterwards differ in scale. A large site absorbs many trades simultaneously, but after commissioning what remains is facilities operation, electrical safety management and equipment maintenance, at a far smaller scale. It is the same point made in 5.3 about resident staff dropping sharply during operation.

So the practical criterion shifts. The question is not «how many years will this boom run» but «is this a skill that survives the boom».

  • Electrical fundamentals such as high-voltage service equipment, distribution and grounding have demand outside data centers. Industrial plants, hospitals, semiconductor fabs and the grid itself are customers
  • Refrigeration, HVAC and piping likewise retain maintenance demand as long as buildings exist
  • Conversely, craft optimized only to one client's proprietary method stops when that client's investment stops
  • Meta emphasizing that its credential is designed to travel with the worker across employers and industry sectors can be reread in this light. The party providing the training also knows this is a cycle, and appears to have written portability into the design requirements

As a note, the structure of the semiconductor industry upstream of this flow is not covered here. That is set out separately in the shape AI chips gave the industry.

8.1 Rules for Reading the Indicators

  • Read with lag assumed. Several quarters pass between ① turning and ③ turning. Healthy construction start data does not mean investment is still being sustained
  • Separate applications from approvals, and announcements from groundbreaking. These four are entirely different numbers with heavy attrition between them. Citing without checking which stage a figure refers to is dangerous
  • Look at the regional level. National totals cancel out overheating in one region against stagnation in another and erase both
  • Read corporate announcements in the original. Qualifiers such as «first year», «target» and «cumulative» disappear between original text and summary. The gap between the verified sum and the reported sum in section 1 is an example
  • Negative signals come from the same place. Project scale-backs and delays are posted on the same channels. Tracking only positive announcements means missing the turn

  • Wage figures. Reported average annual pay for data center construction workers and high-pay examples from specific regions were not verified against primary statistics

  • Korean data center power applications, concentration ratios and unavailable-supply rates. Multiple reports exist, but the source materials from the utility and ministries were not opened and verified
  • Korean trades workforce age and headcount statistics. Average age and headcount figures are cited in coverage, but they were not verified against the issuing body's own materials

The unverified items share something. Most of them are numbers expressing scale. And this article's argument does not depend on scale. That servers arrive last in the data center schedule does not change whichever number is correct, and neither does the fact that construction-phase and operating-phase labor demand differ.

Conclusion: AI Investment Shows Up First as Concrete and Copper

To summarize: in the first half of 2026 two large technology companies made official announcements putting money directly into skilled trades training. Meta with its own academy at 115 million dollars in the first year, Google with a 50 million dollar fund run alongside unions and industry associations. The target trades are electrical, plumbing, welding, sheet metal, fiber and HVAC/refrigeration. Not research roles.

That list is itself the explanation. A data center is an electrical installation with a roof, and servers occupy the last cell of the schedule. Ahead of them are the incoming service and substation room, the chilled water piping and duct, and the foundations and formwork that carry it all. And as rack density rises, water now reaches the server too. That is why the piping trades have grown in weight.

At the same time, two things must be written alongside to avoid overstating it. First, this is a construction cycle. The crew during the build and the staff remaining after commissioning differ in scale. Second, how much of this materializes in Korea is a separate question. Overseas announcements do not convert automatically into domestic work, and domestically there are separate gates called grid conditions and permitting.

So this article does not end with «go get an electrical license now». It changes the question instead. Not «how many years will this boom run» but «is this a skill that survives the boom». Grids, industrial plants, hospitals and factories keep needing electricity and piping regardless of data center investment. Portable skill crosses the cycle; craft optimized to one client does not.

Finally, there is one thing to do right away. Next time you see a large AI investment announcement, skip the summary and open the company's own original text. Even here, the reported combined figure and the figure verified in the original differed. «First year» versus «cumulative», «target» versus «committed» — these vanish in summarization. Those qualifiers are in fact the most important information.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are AI companies really hiring electricians and plumbers?

What official announcements confirm is training investment rather than direct hiring. On June 8, 2026 Meta stated in its newsroom a 115 million dollar first-year investment in its own academy, named fiber technicians, welders, plumbers and electricians as target trades, and said it guarantees a job for every graduate. On June 11, 2026 Google stated on its official blog that 50 million dollars would help prepare more than 300,000 workers across more than 20 states. The two verified items sum to 165 million dollars; the larger combined figure that appears in coverage rests on a primary source that was not opened.

Why do power and piping come before the servers?

Because a data center is less a building than a large electrical and heat-exchange installation. Energizing racks requires incoming service, transformers, switchgear, standby generators, uninterruptible power supplies, batteries, busway, panelboards and grounding in order, and removing the heat requires chillers, cooling towers, chilled water piping, air handlers and suppression piping. Because that equipment is heavy, floor loading and foundations must be designed first, which is civil and structural work. As a result IT equipment comes last on the schedule.

Why does electricity become the bottleneck?

Because of load shape and equipment lead time. A data center holds a nearly flat load all day, leaving no peak-sharing headroom, and that load concentrates at one point, directly demanding regional substation capacity. What is needed is often not more generation but new transmission lines and substation equipment, a long path that includes permitting and compensation. Then long-lead equipment such as transformers and switchgear stacks on top. That is how a site with land and capital still cannot start for lack of power.

Should I go get an electrical license right now?

This article does not say that. Three things belong together. First, licensing and field craft differ, and the engineer grade carries education or experience prerequisites for eligibility. Second, this demand is a construction cycle, so headcount during the build and after commissioning differ. Third, persistence depends on capital spending, grid reinforcement and domestic groundbreaking. So the useful test is not «get one or do not» but whether the skill travels beyond a single client. Wage figures are not given here because they were not verified against primary statistics.

How do I judge how long this demand will last?

Watch indicators, not announcements, in the order money → power → shovels → people. Capex guidance from the large operators is the first signal, grid connection requests and reinforcement plans lead construction starts, building starts and construction orders confirm the conversion from announcement to physical work, and unfilled positions by occupation and licenses issued show the supply response. Because these four layers move with a lag, read them expecting later layers to turn after earlier ones.

## 3. Cooling Method × Where Piping Reaches

| Method | Heat carrier | Piping reaches | Water | Trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Air + evaporative tower | air→chilled water→ambient | Plant room | Evaporative use | Sheet metal, pipefitting, HVAC |
| Air + dry chiller | air→refrigerant→ambient | Plant room | Low (power↑) | HVAC, electrical |
| Rear door heat exchanger | air→chilled water | Rack rear | System dependent | Pipefitting, welding |
| Cold plate direct liquid | coolant→CDU→chilled water | **Inside server** | Closed loop, leak control | Pipefitting, welding, controls |
| Immersion | dielectric fluid→exchange | **Whole server** | Fluid recovery system | Pipefitting, mechanical |

- Save water and you spend electricity; save electricity and you spend water (a trade)
- More liquid cooling = pipefitting and welding craftsmanship becomes operating risk

## 5. Career Checklist

- [ ] Understand that licensing and field craft are **separate gates**
- [ ] Engineer grade carries **education or experience prerequisites** to sit the exam
- [ ] Outdoor, elevated, high-voltage, confined space, night commissioning and relocation are real conditions
- [ ] This demand is a **construction cycle** (far fewer resident staff after commissioning)
- [ ] Ask first whether the chosen skill **travels beyond one client**
- [ ] Grids, industrial plants, hospitals and factories keep demand regardless of data centers
- [ ] Check wages against **domestic primary statistics** (do not transplant overseas figures)

## 6. Do Not

- ❌ Cite a reported combined figure without opening the original
- ❌ Read «number of applications» as «construction started»
- ❌ Transplant overseas wage figures onto a domestic market
- ❌ Mistake construction-phase demand for operating-phase demand
- ❌ Concentrate all craft in one client's proprietary method
