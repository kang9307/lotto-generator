Telling Reasoning Apart From Recall

Change only the numbers, only the wording, one premise, then give a problem with no answer — what each one isolates

Aug 16, 2026 Tech & IT

Introduction: a question a single score cannot answer

A new model posts a higher number on a math benchmark. Reason from that figure to so it will handle our problems too and you will usually be disappointed a month after adoption. Not because the announcement was false, but because a score was never built to answer that question.

A single accuracy figure merges two things of completely different character. One is following an unfamiliar structure step by step. The other is having seen a nearly identical problem and pulling out the shape of its answer. On the scoring sheet both are worth exactly one point. No amount of squinting separates them.

There is only one way to separate them: touch the problem rather than the grading. This article lays out four ways of touching it and the rules for reading what comes back. The method survives model generations even when the numbers do not.

Four things to take away.

  • Three places where a score stops proving capability — fix contamination and two of them remain
  • The design of four perturbations and what each isolates — this is the body of the article
  • Rules for reading results — the signal is the asymmetry between drops, not the drop itself
  • How to weigh the counter-evidence equally — the setting determines the conclusion

1. Three places where a score stops proving capability

Saying benchmarks cannot be trusted is too blunt to act on. There are three distinct routes to distrust, and they differ in cause and in remedy.

1.1 Contamination — the problem and its answer entered the training corpus

The best known route. Public problem sets scatter across the web, and solution write-ups scatter with them. The next scraping pass pulls both in. Responses are always retrospective and therefore always a beat late: estimate contamination statistically, filter, ship a new version, repeat. The move toward companies abandoning standard benchmarks to design their own evaluations is what exhaustion with that loop looks like.

1.2 Near-variants — nothing was contaminated, yet nothing is new

This one is harder. Even when a specific answer is absent from training data, tens of thousands of structurally near-identical problems are present. Elementary word problems, standard integrals, common combinatorics patterns exist on the internet without limit. The exact answer string may be missing while the skeleton of the solution is already there in many forms. Contamination checks miss this entirely, because no strings overlap.

1.3 Reformulation — benchmark problems and real problems are shaped differently

Benchmarks polish their problems. Every needed fact is present, clutter is removed, and the answer resolves to one value. Problems arriving from real work are not shaped that way. Information is missing or in excess, and answers are plural or absent. This is less a flaw in the model than a mismatch between what is measured and what is used. What benchmarks structurally cannot see is covered separately in the gap between knowledge and behaviour benchmarks.

None of the three leads to the model is bad. All three are measurement problems, which is why the remedy sits on the measurement side.

2. The design principle: hold the logic, move the surface

The principle fits in one line.

Hold the logical path to the answer fixed and change only the surface. One thing at a time.

Build a problem that is logically equivalent but visually different, and the system that followed the logic keeps its score while the system that matched the surface drops. The gap between the two is the only observable signal. The original score serves only as a baseline in this experiment.

The most common way this design fails is changing more than one thing at once. Change the numbers and lengthen them at the same time and a drop can never be attributed: was it memory dependence or arithmetic load? One variable moves, everything else stays pinned.

Table 1. Design of the four perturbations

PerturbationWhat changesWhat must stay pinnedWhat it isolatesBuild cost
① NumbersConstants inside the problemDigit count, divisibility, unitsDependence on a specific instanceLow — templating generates them
② WordingNames, background narrative, sentence orderEvery figure and the operation structureDependence on surface vocabularyMedium — naturalness is the hard part
③ PremiseOne condition (added, reversed, removed)All remaining conditionsGrasping the problem as one wholeMedium — the answer must be recomputed
④ UnanswerableMake no answer existOutward shape and lengthConfidence without groundsLow — remove one condition

You do not need all four. But judging on ① alone is dangerous, because number perturbation is the weakest of the four diagnostics and the easiest to pass.

3. Perturbation 1: change only the numbers

Swap the constants in a template for different values and generate a batch of problems, perhaps twenty, then run them under identical conditions.

What matters here is the variance, not the mean. If the logic is being followed, instances of the same template should land at similar scores. When scores swing sharply from instance to instance, the probability rises that a specific example was known rather than the type being solved.

The reference work that ran this systematically is Apple researchers' GSM-Symbolic (arXiv:2410.05229, first version October 2024, revised August 2025). Converting a set of grade-school word problems into symbolic templates and generating multiple instances of the same problem showed that performance varied noticeably between instances and declined when numbers were altered. Fragility deepened as the number of clauses grew. The systems tested were the frontier models available at that time.

Three traps sit in the design.

  • Do not lengthen the digits at the same time. Turning three digits into six converts this into an arithmetic-load experiment
  • Control whether division comes out even. If the original resolves to an integer and the variant produces a decimal, difficulty has shifted
  • Use physically sensible values. Changing a person's age to 400 invites commonsense judgement and measures something else

Holding score here does not prove reasoning. A skeleton memorized with the number slots left open passes this test too, which is why the next perturbation exists.

4. Perturbation 2: change only the wording

Leave every figure and the operation structure untouched, and change only names, unit labels, background narrative and sentence order. Move a problem about sharing apples to one about allocating cables while keeping the numbers and the order of calculation identical.

A drop here is a heavy diagnosis, because it means the logical path did not change by a single character and yet the different vocabulary defeated it. That is close to the most direct evidence available for having seen nearly this problem before.

Several cautions apply.

  • Naturalness must survive. Rewrite into awkward prose and comprehension failure becomes indistinguishable from recall failure
  • Treat sentence reordering as a separate perturbation. Vocabulary swaps and order swaps measure different things
  • Asking in translation is a wording perturbation but not a clean one. The large variable of per-language training volume comes with it. Valid if per-language performance is the target, contaminating if memory dependence is
  • Do not swap in famous proper nouns. A well known name drags its own background knowledge into the problem

5. Perturbation 3: touch a single premise

The strongest of the four, for a simple reason. Recall grasps the problem as one whole, and touching one premise turns that whole into a wrong answer. Surface matching has no route through this.

Three subtypes.

5.1 Add one irrelevant clause

Insert a sentence that looks plausibly related but contributes nothing to the calculation. The GSM-Symbolic work built this condition separately and measured performance drops of up to 65% across the frontier models tested when a single clause that does not feed the reasoning chain was added. That measurement belongs to the October 2024 first version, and the problems were at grade-school word-problem difficulty — both conditions belong with the number.

What makes the result matter is less the size of the drop than its character. Performance falling when information increased means the stage that decides which information is needed is weak. Real working documents are full of irrelevant information.

5.2 Reverse a single condition

Turn minimum into maximum, at least into fewer than, after discount into before discount. The correct answer must change accordingly. Producing the same answer as the original means the problem was not read to the end, which is a worse signal than a wrong answer, because it is not an error in the answer but an answer to a different problem.

This variant costs more to build since the answer must be recomputed. In exchange, its signal is the cleanest of the set.

5.3 Remove a piece of necessary information

Make the answer underivable. This runs into the next perturbation, so it continues there.

Do not skip the human control group. People are also thrown by irrelevant information and also miss reversed conditions. Present a drop without a control and the conclusion a defect unique to AI attaches itself, when reaching that conclusion requires giving the same items to people. Claims missing this step are the classic shape of what collapses under re-examination.

6. Perturbation 4: give a problem with no answer

Mix in problems whose conditions contradict each other, whose necessary values are absent, or whose premise is simply false. Make them look exactly like the rest of the set.

This one measures something slightly different from the other three. Rather than separating reasoning from recall, it asks whether the system checks the grounds of its own answer. And the most expensive failure in real work lives precisely here. A wrong answer is cheap next to a wrong answer delivered with confidence.

The scoring changes, which is easy to overlook.

  • With no correct answer, accuracy cannot be counted. Count the rate of unwarranted confidence instead — how often a number was invented
  • Award partial credit. Full marks for cannot be determined, a bonus for naming the missing piece, as in cannot be determined because this value is absent
  • Keep these at roughly 10 to 20% of the set. Too high a proportion and the pattern is noticed, after which everything gets refused
  • Do not treat refusal as a virtue in itself. Measure the over-refusal rate on solvable items alongside it, or the picture tips the other way

A tool showing a high rate of confidence on unanswerable items should be treated as unusable for that workflow unless fact verification is attached as a process.

7. Rules for reading the results

Running the perturbations does not hand you a conclusion. Three rules govern the reading.

  • Read the asymmetry between drops, not the drop itself. An even, modest decline across all four most likely means difficulty rose. The diagnosis holds only when one specific perturbation collapses
  • Never judge on a single run. Sampling settings alone move results. Run at least five times under identical conditions, ten if you can, and record the spread
  • Check the original score first. If the original is already low, the perturbation experiment carries no information. This design only informs when the original is handled well

The third column is the one I hope gets the most use. Applied to published claims rather than your own runs, the same slot becomes the work of separating the layers of a claim.

8. Is memory the bad thing here?

The direction has to flip once. If everything above reads as relying on memory is disqualifying, something has gone wrong, because that standard does not hold for people either.

People who are good at mathematics memorize theorems. Nobody reproves the Pythagorean theorem before each use or rederives the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on sight. Recognizing where a memorized result applies is the mark of skill. Memory is not the opposite of reasoning; it is the material reasoning works with.

So where is the problem? In claiming generalization on the strength of memory.

  • This model answered this problem — a statement of fact. True whether by recall or reasoning
  • This model does mathematics — a prediction. It asserts the same performance on problems never seen
  • Evidence for the second must come from unseen cases. Stacking seen cases never establishes it

Which is why the shape of the working verdict should change. Does this model reason is a question with no available answer, while how far does this transfer is a question that does have one. The latter is measurable by perturbation; the former is not. The same error of reading a narrow score as general capability shows up when interpreting a small model's math results.

9. Why it surfaces in mathematics specifically

There is a reason this argument runs hottest in mathematics, and it is not that mathematics is where AI is weak. It is that mathematics is very nearly the only laboratory where this experiment can be run.

  • There is one correct answer. No partial-credit dispute, no grader taste entering the measurement
  • Verification can be mechanical. Go as far as formal proof and every step is machine checked
  • Equivalent variants can be generated automatically. One symbolic template yields hundreds of instances. Prose does not permit this
  • Right answer with wrong working is visible. In prose that mismatch is not observable at all

Put differently, moving this experiment to coding or document work means building your own equivalence generator and your own automatic grader. Which answers are mechanically checkable and which are not is a property of the answer, covered separately in where verifiability draws its line. Coding ports relatively easily because builds and tests provide automatic verdicts; planning, advisory and summarization work, where the correct answer is a product of agreement, does not accept this design as-is.

10. The counter-evidence: what formal proof has confirmed

Stopping here and concluding that AI cannot do mathematical reasoning drops evidence that deserves equal weight.

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof, together with AlphaGeometry, solved four of six problems at the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad, scoring 28 out of 42 under official marking. That is the silver medal band, one point below the gold threshold. The methodology appeared in Nature in November 2025 (Olympiad-level formal mathematical reasoning with reinforcement learning). The structure pairs a pretrained language model with AlphaZero-family reinforcement learning, trained to generate proofs inside the Lean proof assistant environment.

What makes this a different class of evidence is not the score. It is that every step of the proof is machine verified in Lean. The failure mode of a right answer with wrong working cannot arise. The very failures the four perturbations hunt for are structurally impossible in this setting.

The conditions have to be read alongside it.

  • Translating problems into the formal language came first. Turning natural-language problems into Lean statements sits outside this result
  • Official marking was applied; this was not sitting the competition. Read as a score obtained under contestant conditions it becomes an overstatement
  • Two of the six went unsolved. This is not uniform strength across every area
  • It is bounded to domains where a verifier attaches. The approach does not extend to problems that resist formalization

So the sentence that holds is neither AI reasons nor AI cannot. It is narrower and far more usable. In settings where free-form answers are produced from natural-language word problems there is evidence of fragility under surface perturbation, and in settings where formal verification attaches there are verified results. The setting determines the conclusion. And in practice what we get to choose is usually not the capability but the setting.

11. Adjusting expectations in practice

This distinction earns its keep in exactly one place: expectations when applying a system to a new situation. For work already standardized, recall and reasoning produce identical results, so there is nothing to separate.

Table 3. Expectations and actions by situation

SituationDoes the distinction bite?WhyAction
Standard calculation, unit and format conversionBarelyInside the distribution and cheap to checkAutomate the check and stop there
Applying internal policy to a new caseConsiderablyPolicy is absent from training data and premises differ per casePre-measure with premise variants, route edge cases to people
Diagnosis or analysis in an unfamiliar domainMost of allUsers cannot see the difference between plausible and correctMeasure the unanswerable rate first, mandate cited grounds
Tests, schemas and other machine-checkable outputLessWrong answers are filtered automaticallyBuild the verifier first, generate second
Decision support under missing informationDecisivelyJudging that information is missing is itself the deliverableMeasure refusal ability as a separate metric, hold adoption if it falls short

The minimum procedure to run before an adoption decision comes out like this.

  • Pick twenty problems your own work actually produced. Do not use public benchmarks — they already carry contamination risk
  • Pair each item with five versions: original, number variant, wording variant, premise variant, unanswerable
  • Run at least five times under identical conditions and record the spread
  • Tabulate the drop per variant rather than the average
  • Designate the work matching the worst-collapsing variant as subject to human review
  • Rotate a few items each quarter. Even a private in-house set contaminates over time

The questions to ask of published benchmark results follow the same logic.

  • Is the problem set public, or a held-out set kept private? Epoch AI's FrontierMath builds this axis into its design with unpublished problems and a separate collection of unsolved ones
  • Were perturbation results reported alongside? An announcement carrying only the original score answers none of the questions in this article
  • Is there a human control group? Without one, sentences of the form better or worse than people do not hold
  • How many runs, and what spread? A single-run score does not belong on a leaderboard
  • Are model version, measurement date and settings stated? Missing any of the three, it is not a reproducible result

Attaching an output-level review procedure to an organization is laid out separately in building a verification workflow.

12. Limits of the method, and what stayed out

Perturbation testing is not universal either. Knowing the limits keeps the results from being overtrusted.

  • Difficulty control is never complete. A variant believed to be surface-only may have become subtly harder, which is why per-variant drops must be read comparatively rather than as absolute figures
  • There is no agreed threshold. No standard says what percentage counts as memory dependence. Safer to use this for ranking than for verdicts
  • Models keep changing. Do not read a drop measured at one moment as a constant of that model family. The perturbation set is the asset; the measurement is a consumable
  • Tools change the result. With a calculator or code execution attached, the number-perturbation signal weakens sharply. Tool availability must always be stated

What was deliberately left out is worth recording too.

  • Reproduction figures for individual current models — no primary source stating version, measurement date and settings together was available, so no figures were used. The only citable measurement is the October 2024 first-version work above
  • Per-model FrontierMath scores — only the design was cited, never a score, because no figure came with its date and evaluation conditions attached
  • A human-mathematician control run on the same perturbation set — the material this argument most needs, and no public control group on identical items under identical conditions could be identified
  • Equivalence of context windows and human working memory — the article that prompted this topic argues that AI's edge comes from a far larger workspace rather than from reasoning, citing several working-memory studies. Those studies are about people, and whether a context window belongs on the same axis as human working memory is a question they do not address

Frequently asked questions

Why can a score alone not tell you whether a model reasoned or recalled?

Because one accuracy figure merges two abilities of different character. Following an unfamiliar structure and having seen a nearly identical problem both earn one point on the same sheet. Separation requires touching the problem rather than the grading.

What does each of the four perturbations isolate?

Numbers isolate instance dependence, wording isolates surface-vocabulary dependence, and premises break the strategy of grasping a problem as one whole. Unanswerable items are slightly different: they measure the rate of confident invention rather than accuracy.

Is relying on memory itself a defect?

No. People memorize theorems, and knowing where a memorized result applies is skill. The trouble starts when generalization is claimed on the strength of memory. Ask how far it transfers rather than whether it reasons, and an answer becomes available.

Should this be read as proof that AI cannot do mathematics?

That reading discards the counter-evidence. In formal proof environments every step is machine checked, a silver-medal-band result was obtained on Olympiad problems, and the methodology was published in a journal. The condition that formalization came first must be read alongside it.

How does a team use this when deciding on adoption?

Build a perturbation set from twenty of your own problems instead of a public benchmark and run it repeatedly. Read the asymmetry between per-variant drops rather than the average, and attach human review to the work matching the worst collapse.

Conclusion: change the shape of the question

Does this model really think is entertaining over drinks and decides nothing in a meeting room, because it is a question with no available answer.

Ask this instead. How much does it drop when the numbers change? What about when the names change? What if one irrelevant sentence is inserted? What does it say when given a problem with no answer? All four return numbers, and the asymmetry between those numbers tells you what you actually wanted to know.

Three lines to keep.

  • Read the gap, not the score. The original is only a baseline; the information lives in the difference from the variants
  • Memory is material, not a defect. It becomes a problem only when generalization is claimed on its strength
  • The setting determines the conclusion. Do not hold the same expectations for work a verifier can attach to and work it cannot

Build the perturbation set once and it is reusable every time a model changes. A team that runs that set instead of opening the leaderboard when a new model lands will, six months later, hold something others do not: a time series of what does and does not work in their own domain.
