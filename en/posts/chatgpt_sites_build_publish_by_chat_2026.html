Background — Bridging Conversational Creation to Live Hosting with ChatGPT Sites

OpenAI has released ChatGPT Sites into public beta, providing an integrated environment where users can converse, preview, host, and publish websites, lightweight web applications, and interactive dashboards entirely within the ChatGPT interface. While our previous analysis of the OpenAI Codex Sites plugin examined developer-centric prompt deployments, ChatGPT Sites consolidates conversational generation, in-app rendering, and public distribution into an accessible unified workflow.

Users can initiate creation simply by describing the layout, functional purpose, and audience of their proposed site or by invoking @Sites directly. In addition to conversational prompts, creators can attach design mockups, local source documents, or existing project repositories to guide generation, testing the resulting interface within an interactive in-app browser to refine components iteratively.

However, lowering friction around web deployment increases the author responsibility regarding operational security. Creators must thoroughly understand the public surface of their chosen domain, the nature of data gathered from visitors, and the governance boundaries separating workspace members before publishing live interactive tools.

Default-Private Architecture and Workspace Visibility Boundaries

According to official OpenAI Help Center articles, every new site initialized via ChatGPT Sites is configured as private by default. This preventative safeguard prevents unfinished prototypes, proprietary draft text, or experimental application logic from accidentally leaking to search indexers or unauthorized external observers.

The core visibility and governance parameters documented by OpenAI include:

  • Initial access is strictly confined to the site owner and authenticated workspace administrators.
  • General colleagues within the organization workspace cannot view the draft project until explicit sharing is enabled.
  • Owners can selectively invite specific individuals based on workspace governance guidelines and subscription capabilities.
  • Internal hubs, team progress monitors, or departmental dashboards can be promoted to workspace-wide visibility.
  • External viewer invitations are rolling out progressively across eligible paid plans and may require administrative authorization.
  • Publishing a site to the general public requires an explicit configuration review covering authentication gates and public links.

This progressive disclosure pipeline provides essential assurance in enterprise settings, guaranteeing that an accidental click of a publish button does not instantly broadcast confidential operational material to the global web.

Collaborative Editing and Role Segregation — Owners, Editors, and Viewers

ChatGPT Sites accommodates multi-stakeholder workflows by allowing project owners to designate collaborators. Owners can invite active members from their shared workspace to assist in maintaining application assets.

Official support guidelines distinguish operational roles across several tiers:

  • Owner: Retains exclusive rights to perform the initial deployment, manage custom domain bindings, and invite workspace collaborators.
  • Editor: Permitted to refine visual components, modify logic prompts, and publish revisions once the owner has deployed the first live build.
  • Editor Data Visibility: Crucially, editors are granted read access not only to codebase prompts but also to all user data stored within the site backend.
  • Viewer: Confined exclusively to rendered front-end interactions, completely isolated from administrative consoles, prompt configs, and backend records.
  • Workspace Administrator: Maintains oversight over all generated assets across the organization, tracking publishing statuses and permission posture.
  • Access Revocation: When a user departs the workspace or is removed by the owner, their editing capabilities and access to backend storage terminate instantly.

Because editors inherently gain visibility into collected site databases, team leads must vet collaborator assignments carefully whenever an application ingests customer inquiries, lead submissions, or sensitive internal feedback.

Visitor Identity and Underlying Data Storage Mechanics

To move beyond simple static pages toward interactive applications, ChatGPT Sites bundles dedicated user authentication with persistent storage capabilities. The cornerstone of this capability is Sign in with ChatGPT integrated alongside managed backend storage engines.

Documented mechanics governing authentication and state persistence include:

  • Visitors authenticating via their ChatGPT accounts maintain continuous state across browsing sessions to resume workflows seamlessly.
  • Structured application records—such as game scores, configuration settings, and calculation logs—are preserved within relational databases (powered by D1).
  • User-uploaded documents, profile photos, and file attachments are isolated within high-durability object storage (powered by R2).
  • Within Business and Enterprise workspaces, visitors can grant read-only authorization allowing the site to read their pre-connected ChatGPT tools.
  • Tool integrations strictly inspect information the visitor already possesses permission to see, never inheriting the creator broader credentials.

This turnkey architecture eliminates the need for developers to provision bespoke databases or configure complex OAuth flows. Nevertheless, because visitor inputs reside directly within OpenAI managed cloud infrastructure, creators must construct input forms in strict compliance with corporate data protection policies.

Hosting Subdomains, Custom Domains, and Routing Considerations

When a site is published, OpenAI handles global hosting automatically without requiring server provisioning. Each project receives an automated subdomain under OpenAI managed namespaces, with the option to attach dedicated custom domains.

Critical hosting and domain verification points include:

  • Standard deployments receive assigned subdomains under namespaces such as *.chatgpt.site.
  • Site creators can customize and rename their assigned hosting subdomain prefix to reflect the project identity.
  • Renaming an active hosting URL triggers automated HTTP redirection, seamlessly routing incoming visitors from the legacy address to the new target.
  • Organizations seeking to preserve branding continuity can map fully qualified custom domains to their sites.
  • Establishing custom domain routing requires configuring designated DNS verification records with the authoritative domain registrar.
  • Operators should conduct post-binding smoke tests to confirm that traffic resolves smoothly across desktop and mobile browsers.

The presence of automated redirection when modifying subdomains offers valuable resilience, protecting published hyperlinks from breaking abruptly. When deploying public-facing production tools, mapping an organization official custom domain remains best practice to reinforce user trust.

Access Control Tiers and Security Perimeter Analysis

Establishing clear boundaries regarding who may inspect or interact with an application is fundamental to digital security. The four discrete access tiers provided by ChatGPT Sites are compared below.

Access Control Tier Target Audience Scope Authentication Requirement Collaborative Editing and Data Rights
Default Private Site creator and workspace administrators exclusively Authenticated ChatGPT workspace login required Creator holds exclusive edit rights; data access restricted
Invited Members Explicitly invited individuals specified by email address Invited account authentication mandatory Branching rights determined by granted role (Editor vs Viewer)
Workspace Sharing All active members belonging to the organizational workspace Active corporate workspace account credentials Colleagues access as viewers; designated editors retain write access
General Public Any global web user possessing the published link Accessible anonymously (optional login can be enforced) External visitors strictly read-only; editing completely barred

Adhering to these distinct perimeters prevents unintended exposure. Internal tools should remain firmly anchored within the workspace tier, reserving general public status solely for promotional pages, outward-facing calculators, or public documentation.

What Could Not Be Verified

  • Quantitative storage quotas (in gigabytes) and monthly bandwidth caps allocated across individual subscription plans.
  • The definitive calendar date marking the conclusion of the public beta and transitioning to general availability.
  • Underlying technical protocols governing SSL/TLS certificate renewal cycles for attached custom domains.
  • Whether free-tier ChatGPT users will eventually receive read-only browsing access or basic publishing capabilities.
  • The availability of automated scheduled database export mechanisms or tools for migrating stored records off-platform.
  • Sandboxing constraints and script execution policies governing third-party client-side analytics scripts.
