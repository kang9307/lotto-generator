This article covers what to do when the electronic door lock on your front door will not open. It is written for the keypad digital door locks common in Korean homes, and it is based on one Korean manufacturer's official FAQ, the Korean Agency for Technology and Standards' external heat-shock test announcement (June 27, 2019), and the Korea Consumer Agency's smart door lock test results (April 2021). The reference date is September 29, 2026. The figures and procedures from the manufacturer's FAQ apply to that manufacturer's products. Door locks work differently by maker and model, so read this alongside the manual for your own lock.

There is an order to what you do at the door. First read the symptom, then take the step that fits that symptom, and if that does not work, call someone. Forcing, shaking, or breaking the door appears nowhere in that sequence. Security issues such as smartphone integration and hacking are covered separately in our Smart Home & IoT Security Complete Guide.

1. When the Door Will Not Open - What to Check First by Symptom

When a door lock stops cooperating, the symptoms fall into a handful of groups. The keypad shows no response at all. An alert sound plays every time the door opens and closes. After several wrong PINs or keys, the lock cannot be used for a while. An alarm will not stop. The door does not lock when closed. Or the PIN is correct but the door still will not open. The table below lists what to check first and what you can do yourself for each symptom. Only the cells marked "manufacturer FAQ" are backed by official material.

1.1 No Response Is Different From an Alert Sound

If pressing the keypad produces no light and no sound, the first thing most people think of is dead batteries. But a lack of response alone does not establish the cause, so this is a general assumption, not a confirmed diagnosis.

According to one manufacturer's FAQ, its products that run on 1.5V alkaline AA batteries (four or eight of them) can be used for about a year based on 10 uses a day, and when replacement is due they play an alert sound, like a children's bicycle song melody, every time the door is opened and closed. The same FAQ adds that battery life varies with temperature, humidity, frequency of use, and battery quality. The type of alert and how it is delivered differ by product, so check your manual. Our view is that the easiest way to avoid a dead battery is to replace the batteries while the door still opens, as soon as the alert starts.

1.2 Stopped After Wrong Entries vs. Will Not Open With the Right PIN

Some products disable themselves for a set time after an unregistered PIN or key is entered several times. In one manufacturer's FAQ, the rule is five consecutive wrong entries for five minutes, and it varies by model between 30 seconds, 3 minutes, and 5 minutes (see section 3).

If the PIN is correct but the door still will not open, you might suspect that the locking mechanism (the mortise or deadbolt) is stuck. However, the causes and symptoms of a stuck mechanism do not appear in the official material this article relies on. Our view is that in this case it is better to leave it to the manufacturer's service or a professional than to take it apart or force it yourself.

2. Dead Battery - Emergency Power Steps and Cautions

Some products have an emergency power terminal on the outside of the door in case the batteries die. Whether there is a terminal, where it is, and how it works all differ by manufacturer and model, so the steps below follow one manufacturer's FAQ.

2.1 Opening From Outside With a 9V Battery (One Manufacturer's FAQ)

That FAQ states that the door can be opened using an emergency battery and lists the following steps.

  • Get an alkaline 9V battery. The FAQ notes that one can be bought at a convenience store or stationery shop.
  • Touch the battery to the emergency power terminal at the bottom of the outdoor front body (the outside of the door).
  • Touch the outdoor front body (or press the * button).
  • Enter your PIN, then touch the front body (or press the * button).

The FAQ does not say which way the battery should face or how long it should be held in place. You need to check your own product manual for where the terminal is and whether this method works on your lock. Do not assume that touching a 9V battery to any door lock will open it.

2.2 Once the Door Is Open, Replace All the Indoor Batteries

The same FAQ says that after opening the door you must open the indoor battery cover and replace all the batteries with new 1.5V AA alkaline batteries. The 9V battery is only a temporary power source for opening the door once. Replace the indoor batteries as soon as you get inside so you do not end up in the same situation next time.

There are also things to watch when inserting the batteries. That manufacturer lists malfunctions from improper battery insertion or use of defective batteries, and cover bolts broken when the customer replaces batteries, as paid-service cases even within the warranty period. Check the battery orientation and do not force the cover.

3. Handling Wrong-Entry Lockouts and Alarms

Sometimes the batteries are fine but the lock will not accept a PIN for a while, or an alarm will not stop. Everything below comes from one manufacturer's FAQ, and other products may work differently.

3.1 A Wrong-Entry Lockout Releases If You Wait

That FAQ states that if an unregistered PIN or key is entered five times in a row, a five-minute lock is set and the door lock cannot be used during that time.

  • If you wait five minutes without touching it, it plays an operating sound and returns to normal mode.
  • If someone is home, pressing the indoor open/close button releases the lock immediately.
  • The lockout time varies by model (30 seconds, 3 minutes, or 5 minutes), and the FAQ says to check the manual for details.

Since the lock cannot be used during the lockout, it is better to use that time to recall the correct PIN and enter it once the time has passed.

3.2 Three Ways to Stop an Alarm

The same FAQ gives three ways to stop an alarm.

  1. On the outdoor front body (the outside of the door), enter your PIN and then touch it with your palm (or press the * button).
  2. Touch a registered key (card) to the key (card) touch area on the outdoor unit.
  3. From inside, remove the batteries and put them back in. If that does not stop it, press and hold the indoor OPEN/CLOSE button for five seconds.

The FAQ does not say what causes the alarm. If the alarm keeps recurring and you do not know why, read the manual, and if that does not help, it is better to contact the manufacturer's service.

4. When the Door Will Not Lock - Checking Settings and Safety Rules for Changes

Sometimes the door does not lock automatically when closed. The lock may be set to manual locking, or it may be a symptom related to how it was installed.

4.1 When Auto-Lock Does Not Work and Opening the Door Sets Off an Alarm

One manufacturer's FAQ addresses the case where, after installation, the lock does not auto-lock and an alarm sounds when the door is opened. It describes this as a symptom that can appear when the locking system is changed on main-lock models with an integrated handle (a left/right handing change) and explains how to switch it back using model-specific button operations. The button sequence differs by model, so follow the manual for your own product.

Also, if the lock is set to manual locking, the door will not lock automatically. The same FAQ asks users of manual locking to be sure to lock the door themselves when going out. If the door does not lock, the first step is to check whether it is set to automatic or manual.

4.2 Keep the Door Open When Changing Settings

A mistake while changing settings can leave you with the door closed and a new PIN that does not work. That is why one manufacturer's FAQ attaches the same warning to every settings change.

  • Changing the PIN: the FAQ says to be sure to register a new PIN with the door open, just in case. It also says to check, with the door still open, that the new PIN opens the lock after the change.
  • Setting auto-lock: the FAQ likewise says to be sure to do this with the door open, just in case. The same warning applies to setting manual locking.
  • The FAQ repeatedly notes that registration methods differ by model and that the product manual has the exact steps.

5. Fire and High-Temperature Safety - A False Rumor and Government Test Results

Because door locks can open automatically in a fire, a rumor spread that heating one from outside with a lighter would open the door. There are government test results on this.

5.1 Automatic Unlocking on High Heat and the Indoor Manual Release

One manufacturer's FAQ says that when high heat occurs, its locks detect the internal temperature, sound an alarm, and switch from locked to unlocked, and that in an emergency the door can be opened with the internal manual release device. The same FAQ gives the fire sensor's operating temperature as around 60°C and compares it to the temperature of a Korean sauna room.

The sensing temperature and the way the lock responds can differ by product. So it is worth learning from your manual, ahead of time, where your door lock's manual release is and how to operate it.

The Korea Consumer Agency's 2021 smart door lock test results (republished by the Seoul Metropolitan Government's Media Hub) also stated that automatic release of the lock in a fire, making escape easier, met the relevant standard. That result applies to the products in that test.

5.2 Heating From Outside Did Not Open Them - The Agency for Technology and Standards Test

On June 27, 2019, Korea's Agency for Technology and Standards announced the results of an external heat-shock test on 15 digital door lock products. The condition was that a 10 cm by 10 cm square hot plate (temperature 100°C ±10°C) is placed in contact for 10 minutes with the outer surface closest to the temperature sensor inside the digital door lock, and a product passed only if the lock stayed shut in every one of at least three runs.

All 15 products met the external heat-shock safety standard, and the announcement stated that it was confirmed that none of the 15 products opened even when the digital door lock was heated with a lighter flame from outside the front door. One manufacturer's FAQ also explains that fire doors are designed so that fire does not pass through, so heat applied from outside has difficulty reaching the inside.

In the smart door lock test of eight brands announced by the Korea Consumer Agency in April 2021, the result was that no product suffered damage or malfunction from a 150 kg external force, a 100°C heat shock, an electric shock, or electromagnetic waves. For battery life, measured with eight AA batteries, six products could open and close more than 30,000 times and two products more than 15,000 times. The agency advised that if you mainly use a card key or a remote control or home network to open the door, you should check whether a security program is installed for each opening method, and that you should not register transit cards, credit cards, or smartphone NFC cards in place of the dedicated card key.

6. When to Call Someone - Warranty and Paid-Service Rules

If the steps above do not solve the problem, or the cause is unclear, as when the PIN is correct but the door will not open, the next step is to call the manufacturer's service or a professional. This article does not cover ways to open a door without the key or PIN.

6.1 One Manufacturer's Free and Paid Service Rules

One manufacturer's FAQ states that the product warranty period is basically one year from purchase, and that within the warranty period, free service is available for all symptoms except those caused by customer fault or environmental effects (typhoons, condensation, and so on). The items it lists as "user inexperience," which are charged even within the warranty period, are as follows.

  • Visits to explain how to use the product, and inspection requests where there is no obvious defect
  • Service requests caused by a forgotten PIN (or lost key)
  • Malfunctions caused by the customer's own installation
  • Malfunctions from improper battery insertion or defective batteries, and cover bolts broken when the customer replaces batteries
  • Damage from external impact, malfunctions from chemical products, and cosmetic damage caused by force

Warranty periods and paid-service rules differ by manufacturer, so check the warranty for your own product. Amounts such as call-out fees differ by manufacturer and can change, so this article does not list them.

6.2 Do Not Take It Apart or Force It Open Yourself

We do not recommend taking a door lock apart yourself or forcing it open just because the door will not open. Even under the manufacturer rules above, damage from external impact or force, and problems caused by the customer's own installation, are charged even within the warranty period. The official material does not describe what damage disassembly or forced opening leaves on the parts and the door, but our view is that you risk losing both your warranty and your safety.

If there is no response even with emergency power, if the door will not open with the correct PIN, or if the alarm keeps going, contact the manufacturer's service center. For handing over door lock PINs and keys when you move, see The Complete Moving Guide.

7. What We Could Not Confirm

The following could not be pinned down in the official sources, so this article does not state them as figures or firm claims.

  • Causes and symptoms of a stuck mortise or deadbolt: the manufacturer's FAQ has no entry on this, and we did not see the manufacturer's manual.
  • Other manufacturers' official standards: we could not compare the emergency power terminal, alert sounds, lockout time, or fire unlocking method against other manufacturers' official material. The figures in this article come from one manufacturer.
  • The Korea Consumer Agency's 2025 announcement on fire risks of overseas direct-purchase door locks: there is press coverage, but we could not obtain the agency's original text, so no figures are included.
  • The Korea Consumer Agency's 2024 digital door lock quality and safety test results: we did not see the original.
  • Battery orientation and contact time when applying 9V emergency power: not stated in the manufacturer's FAQ.
