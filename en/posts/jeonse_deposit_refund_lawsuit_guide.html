Introduction: Deposit Refunds After the Jeonse Fraud and Reverse-Jeonse Wave

Since the jeonse fraud cases and the reverse-jeonse crunch that took off in 2022, more tenants in Korea have been unable to get their deposits back on time even after their leases end. Guarantee-accident statistics change every year, so check the latest figures in announcements from HUG (Korea Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation) and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport. If you are a victim of jeonse fraud, the validity period of the Special Act on Support for Victims of Jeonse Fraud and Housing Stability has been extended to May 31, 2027, and you can apply for a victim determination until then. However, tenants who first signed their lease on or after June 1, 2025 are excluded (Korea Policy Briefing, 2025-05-02).

Even after the contract expires, it has become common for landlords to delay refunds with excuses like "I can only return it once the next tenant is found" or "I don't have the funds." Tenants may be unable to secure a new home because the deposit is tied up, or may watch loan interest pile up. In these circumstances, simply waiting is rarely enough to protect a tenant's rights.

Below, step by step, are the Korean procedures for recovering a deposit: the lease registration order (imchagwon-deunggi-myeongryeong), certified mail (naeyong-jeungmyeong), the payment order (jigeupmyeongryeong), the main lawsuit, and compulsory execution. Statutes, stamp fees and service fees follow the current provisions on the National Law Information Center and the Easy-to-Find Everyday Law Information service as of September 29, 2026; durations and costs without an official basis are flagged separately. Tenant rights in general are covered in Tenant Rights and Lease Protection, and checks at the contract stage in 2026 Jeonse & Wolse Contract Checklist.

1. Preparation Steps Before Demanding the Deposit Refund

Some preparations must be in place before litigation. Missing this stage can lead to the lease being renewed automatically or to losing your opposing power (daehangryeok) and priority repayment right, leaving you at a legal disadvantage.

1.1 Notice of Refusal to Renew Before the Lease Ends

Article 6(1) of the Housing Lease Protection Act provides that if the landlord does not give notice of refusal to renew between six months and two months before the lease term ends, or if the tenant does not give notice by two months before the lease term ends, the lease is deemed renewed on the same terms (implied renewal). In that case the term is deemed to be two years (Article 6(2)).

  • Timing: The tenant must give notice of non-renewal no later than two months before expiry. This "by two months before" wording comes from the June 9, 2020 amendment (National Law Information Center, checked 2026-09-29)
  • Method: Text message, KakaoTalk and email are possible, but certified mail provides the most solid evidence of delivery and content
  • Sample wording: State both the refusal and the refund request clearly, for example "I will not renew the contract and request the full return of my deposit on the expiry date"
  • If implied renewal has occurred: The tenant may give notice of termination at any time, and termination takes effect three months after the landlord receives the notice (Article 6-2)
  • Preserving evidence: Keep mailing receipts, delivery records and screenshots of replies in a dedicated folder

1.2 Applying for a Lease Registration Order (Preserving Opposing Power)

If the lease has ended and you must move out before the deposit is returned, consider a lease registration order first. Once the lease registration is completed, you do not lose the opposing power and priority repayment right you already acquired even if you later move and lose the requirements for opposing power (Housing Lease Protection Act Article 3-3(5)). Conversely, moving your registration before the registration is completed can cost you this protection, so it is safest to confirm "housing lease right" on the register before moving.

  • Court: The district court, branch court, or city/county court with jurisdiction over the leased house (Article 3-3(1))
  • Requirements and documents: You may apply when the deposit has not been returned after the lease ends, and must make a prima facie showing of the reasons and the facts giving rise to the registration (Article 3-3(1)-(2)). Check the exact list of attachments, such as a copy of the lease, the resident registration certificate and the building register, with the court
  • Execution before service: Under the April 18, 2023 amendment, Article 292(3) of the Civil Execution Act applies mutatis mutandis, so the registration can be executed even before the order is served on the landlord (Article 3-3(3))
  • Cost: You can claim the costs of the application and registration from the landlord (Article 3-3(8)). Check the actual amounts of stamps, service fees and registration tax with the court.
  • Duration: The time to decision and registration depends on the court, so it is safest to schedule the move after confirming the registration is complete
  • Caution: Moving out before the registration is complete may extinguish your opposing power. Always confirm "housing lease right" on the register before transferring your resident registration

1.3 Sending Certified Mail

Certified mail has no coercive force by itself, but it becomes evidence in a later lawsuit that the tenant formally demanded the deposit. Certified mail demanding performance can serve as a "peremptory notice" under Article 174 of the Civil Act, but it has no effect of interrupting prescription unless a judicial claim, seizure or provisional attachment follows within six months. Drafting and sending are covered in detail in Complete Guide to Naeyong-Jeungmyeong (Certified Mail) in Korea.

  • Contents: Contract summary, expiry date, amount to be refunded, deadline (e.g., 14 days after delivery), and the legal steps planned if unpaid
  • Sending method: At a post office counter or through the Internet Post Office
  • Cost: According to Korea Post (checked 2026-09-29), ordinary postage 500 KRW (standard size up to 25 g) + registered mail 2,400 KRW + certified-content fee of 1,300 KRW for the first page (650 KRW for each additional page). One A4 page costs 4,200 KRW and two pages 4,850 KRW; weight and extra services change the total
  • Storage: Keep the copy returned to you after mailing and the receipt, as they serve as evidence in litigation

2. Filing a Payment Order (Simplified Procedure)

A payment order is an order the court issues on the creditor's application alone, without examining the debtor, for claims for the payment of money and the like (Civil Procedure Act Articles 462 and 467). It is worth considering first in deposit cases where the amount and the relationship are clear. However, it is available only when service can be made in Korea by a method other than service by public notice (proviso to Article 462), so you need to know the landlord's address.

2.1 Filing Method and Required Documents

A payment order can also be filed through the Korean courts' Electronic Litigation System (ecfs.scourt.go.kr), without visiting the court.

  • Court: Exclusive jurisdiction lies with the district court of the place of the debtor's (landlord's) general forum, or a court with jurisdiction under Articles 7-9, 12 or 18 of the Civil Procedure Act (Article 463)
  • Documents: Submit the application together with supporting materials such as a copy of the lease, a copy of the certified mail, lease-registration documents and the real estate register
  • Drafting tip: State the principal and delay damages. Damages for non-payment of money follow the statutory rate of 5% per annum under the Civil Act unless otherwise agreed (Civil Act Articles 379 and 397), and 12% per annum from the day after a document equivalent to a complaint, such as the payment order application, is served on the debtor (Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition of Legal Proceedings Article 3(1)). The start date can vary with the facts
  • Electronic filing: File and track progress without visiting the court. Documents filed electronically bear stamps equal to nine-tenths of the normal amount (Act on Stamps Attached for Civil Litigation Article 16)

2.2 Stamp Fees and Service Fees (Checked September 2026)

A payment order application bears stamps equal to one-tenth of the stamp amount for a complaint (Act on Stamps Attached for Civil Litigation Article 7(2)). As of September 29, 2026, the calculation is as follows.

  • Complaint stamp amount: Under 10 million KRW, 50/10,000 of the claim; 10 million to under 100 million KRW, 45/10,000 plus 5,000 KRW; 100 million to under 1 billion KRW, 40/10,000 plus 55,000 KRW; 1 billion KRW or more, 35/10,000 plus 555,000 KRW. Amounts under 1,000 KRW become 1,000 KRW, and above that, fractions under 100 KRW are dropped (Article 2)
  • Example (100 million KRW deposit): One-tenth of the 455,000 KRW complaint stamp amount, i.e. 45,500 KRW, is the payment order stamp fee. Filed electronically, it is nine-tenths of that (about 41,000 KRW)
  • Service fee: One service fee unit × number of parties × 6 units. One unit is listed as 5,640 KRW (Easy-to-Find Everyday Law Information, as of 2026-09-15), so for a tenant and a landlord (two parties) it is 67,680 KRW
  • If an objection turns it into a lawsuit: You must top up the difference between the complaint stamp amount and the stamps already paid (Civil Procedure Act Article 473(1); Stamp Act Article 7(3)). In the 100 million KRW example that is 455,000 − 45,500 = 409,500 KRW, and if not paid within the set period the application is dismissed (Article 473(2))
  • Total example (100 million KRW deposit, paper filing): 45,500 KRW in stamps + 67,680 KRW in service fees = about 113,000 KRW at the payment order stage

2.3 Objection and Transition to a Lawsuit

If the debtor files an objection within two weeks of being served with the payment order, the order loses effect to that extent (Civil Procedure Act Article 470(1)), and a lawsuit is deemed filed at the time the payment order was applied for (Article 472(2)).

  • Objection period: Within two weeks of service of the payment order; this is a peremptory period (Article 470)
  • If there is no objection: The payment order has the same effect as a final judgment (Article 474). Compulsory execution based on a final payment order is, in principle, carried out on the authentic copy of the order without an execution clause (Civil Execution Act Article 58(1))
  • If there is an objection: The case moves to litigation, and once the stamps are topped up the record is sent to the competent court (Article 473(3)). The costs of the payment order procedure become part of the litigation costs (Article 473(4))
  • If service fails: A payment order cannot be served by public notice. When the court orders the creditor to correct the address, the creditor may request that the case proceed as a lawsuit, and if service is possible only by public notice, the court may refer the case to litigation on its own motion (Article 466). If you have lost contact with the landlord, consider filing the main lawsuit from the start

3. The Main Lawsuit for Deposit Refund

If the landlord objects to the payment order or you choose litigation from the start, the case proceeds as a formal civil suit. It takes more time and money, but ends in a judgment after evidence review and hearings. First-instance claims for money of 30 million KRW or less are handled as small claims (Small Claims Trial Rules Article 1-2); that procedure is explained in Small Claims Court Complete Guide 2026.

3.1 How to Draft the Complaint

The complaint must state the prayer for relief and the cause of action clearly, with supporting evidence attached.

  • Sample prayer for relief: "The defendant shall pay the plaintiff KRW ○○○ with interest at 5% per annum from ○/○/○ until the date of service of a copy of this complaint, and at 12% per annum from the following day until full payment"
  • Cause of action: Describe in chronological order the conclusion of the lease, expiry of the term, the refund obligation, the refund demand, and non-performance
  • Attached evidence: Lease agreement, certified mail, real estate register, lease registration order ruling, text/KakaoTalk messages, bank transfer records
  • Delay damages rate: 12% per annum from the day after the complaint or an equivalent document is served on the debtor (Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition of Legal Proceedings Article 3(1); statutory rate regulation in force since June 1, 2019). It does not apply to the extent the court finds it reasonable for the debtor to dispute the existence or scope of the obligation (Article 3(2))
  • Hiring a lawyer: Recommended for large deposits or cases involving suspected jeonse fraud. Fees are set by each firm, so confirm them in a written agreement before retaining counsel

3.2 Expected Duration

The durations below are general examples from the earlier edition, not figures backed by court statistics (as of April 2026, not re-verified). Actual durations vary widely with the court and the complexity of the case.

  • Filing to the defendant's answer: About 1-2 months
  • First hearing: About 2-3 months after filing
  • Further hearings and evidence: 2-3 sessions, 1-2 months apart
  • Judgment: 2-4 weeks after the close of hearings
  • Overall: Around 6 months for simple cases; a year or more when the landlord contests or service by public notice is needed
  • On appeal: Several more months to over a year

3.3 Winning Is Not the Same as Collecting

In cases with good evidence such as the lease, lease registration and certified mail, actually collecting the money after judgment is often harder than winning. The earlier edition's "first-instance win rate of about 92%" and "actual recovery rate of 55-65%" were removed because their source could not be confirmed.

  • Main reasons recovery fails: (1) Landlord insolvency or bankruptcy, (2) excessive senior mortgages, (3) repeated failed auctions
  • Preservation: If you fear the landlord will dispose of assets before judgment, consider a provisional attachment
  • Preparing for execution: Once you hold an enforcement title for payment of money, you can apply to the court of the debtor's general forum for an order to disclose assets (Civil Execution Act Article 61)

4. Compulsory Execution (After Judgment)

Once you hold an enforcement title such as a final judgment or a final payment order, you can apply for compulsory execution based on it. This is the stage where the deposit is actually recovered.

4.1 Compulsory Real Estate Auction

A compulsory auction of the leased house or other real estate owned by the landlord is the most direct means of recovery.

  • Court: The district court with jurisdiction over the property
  • Documents: An enforceable authentic copy (a judgment with an execution clause; a final payment order, in principle, as is), certificate of service, real estate register, and so on
  • No counter-performance required: When a tenant applies for an auction of the leased house based on a final judgment for return of the deposit or a similar title, performance or tender of the counter-obligation (vacating the house) is not a requirement for starting execution (Housing Lease Protection Act Article 3-2(1)). However, to receive the deposit through the priority repayment right, the tenant must hand the house over to the buyer (Article 3-2(3))
  • Cost: Registration license tax on the auction-application registration is 2/1,000 of the claim (Local Tax Act Article 28(1)(1)(d)), and local education tax is 20/100 of that (Article 151(1)(2)), for a total of 0.24% of the claim. The court sets the advance deposit for the auction, so ask the court where you apply
  • Distribution priority: Senior claims such as mortgages and tax claims come first; the tenant receives distribution according to the priority based on opposing-power requirements and the fixed date. For the small-deposit top-priority rules, see Tenant Rights and Lease Protection
  • Duration and failed auctions: The time from application to distribution and the rate at which the minimum sale price is reduced after a failed auction vary by court and case, so check the sale-date notice. With heavy senior claims you may receive nothing

4.2 Seizure of Deposits and Wages

Besides real estate, execution can target the landlord's bank deposits, wages, business income and other monetary claims. However, amounts the law treats as living expenses cannot be seized, and those thresholds rose on February 1, 2026.

  • Claim seizure and collection order: An order allowing you to collect directly from the third-party debtor (a bank, etc.). How long a decision takes depends on the court
  • Asset inquiry: Where, for example, the asset list the debtor submitted in the disclosure procedure is insufficient, the court may, on the creditor's application, query financial institutions and others about assets in the debtor's name. You must specify the institutions and pay the costs in advance (Civil Execution Act Article 74)
  • Wage seizure limits: Half of a wage claim cannot be seized. If that half is below 2.5 million KRW per month, seizure is barred up to 2.5 million KRW; if it exceeds 3 million KRW per month, seizure is barred only up to 3 million KRW plus half of (the half amount − 3 million KRW) (Civil Execution Act Article 246(1)(4); Enforcement Decree Articles 3 and 4). The 2.5 million KRW threshold applies to seizure orders filed on or after February 1, 2026 (previously 1.85 million KRW)
  • Protected bank deposits: Deposits with a per-person balance of 2.5 million KRW or less cannot be seized (Enforcement Decree Article 7), and a new "living-expense account" system holds only the protected living-expense amount (Civil Execution Act Article 246-2; Enforcement Decree Article 8)
  • Business revenue: Card-sales receivables, rental income and trade receivables can also be targets
  • Practical tip: If you know which banks the landlord uses, filing against several banks at once is effective

4.3 Using a Jeonse Deposit Return Guarantee

If you enrolled in a jeonse deposit return guarantee with HUG or SGI Seoul Guarantee when signing the lease, you can claim performance of the guarantee from the guarantor instead of a long lawsuit against the landlord. Requirements and documents follow the guarantee terms, so confirm the current standards with the guarantor before claiming.

  • Claim requirements: These follow the guarantee terms and HUG guidance. The earlier edition listed "one month after expiry, lease registration order completed" (as of April 2026, not re-verified); check the current requirements with HUG before claiming
  • Documents: Guarantee certificate, lease agreement, lease-registration documents, resident registration certificate, etc. (as of April 2026, not re-verified)
  • Payment timing: After a guarantee accident it can take several months to receive the deposit, so leave slack in your moving and loan schedules
  • Right of recourse: After paying the tenant, the guarantor exercises its right of recourse against the landlord
  • Eligibility: HUG's jeonse-to-price ratio cap of 90% has applied to new contracts since May 1, 2023 (Korea Policy Briefing, 2023-02-02). The premium rate of 0.115-0.154% per year comes from November 2022 Policy Briefing material, so check the current rate before enrolling
  • Other guarantors: Products from SGI Seoul Guarantee and others have different requirements and limits, so check that institution's terms

Conclusion: A 5-Step Strategy for Quick Deposit Recovery

Recovering a jeonse deposit is a matter of procedure, not emotion. Rather than spending time only on negotiations with the landlord, taking the legal steps on schedule usually brings recovery forward. The general flow is as follows.

  • Step 1 (6 to 2 months before expiry): Give notice of non-renewal no later than two months before expiry, and check whether you have a return guarantee
  • Step 2 (around expiry): Send certified mail requesting the refund and plan your new lease and move
  • Step 3 (right after expiry): Apply for a lease registration order (confirm registration before moving); guarantee holders should check the claim procedure with the guarantor
  • Step 4 (after that): Without a guarantee, file a payment order if you know the landlord's address, or sue if you don't or the dispute is serious. If you sent a peremptory notice by certified mail, take follow-up action within six months
  • Step 5 (after obtaining an enforcement title): Apply for a compulsory auction and claim seizure, and use asset disclosure and asset inquiry to find targets if needed

The most important principle is to proceed "without delay and with evidence." What is covered here is general information, not legal advice. The same procedure can lead to different outcomes depending on the facts of each case, so if handling it alone is difficult, consult a lawyer, the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (dial 132), the Housing Lease Dispute Mediation Committee, or the HUG Jeonse Victim Support Center.

# Jeonse Deposit Recovery Checklist (Korea)

Reference date: 2026-09-29. Statutes, stamp fees and service fees were checked against the National Law Information Center and Easy-to-Find Everyday Law Information. General information, not legal advice. For your case, consult a lawyer or the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (132).

---

## 1. Step-by-step checklist

### Before expiry
- [ ] Gave notice of non-renewal no later than 2 months before expiry (Housing Lease Protection Act Art. 6(1))
- [ ] Used a method that records delivery and content (e.g., certified mail)
- [ ] Checked whether a return guarantee exists and with which guarantor
- [ ] If implied renewal occurred: termination takes effect 3 months after notice (Art. 6-2)

### Right after expiry, before moving
- [ ] Applied for a lease registration order (district court, branch court or city/county court for the house's location)
- [ ] Will move only after confirming "housing lease right" on the register
- [ ] Kept receipts for application and registration costs (claimable from the landlord, Art. 3-3(8))
- [ ] Sent certified mail demanding the refund (no interruption of prescription unless follow-up within 6 months, Civil Act Art. 174)

### Recovery procedure
- [ ] Landlord's address known → consider a payment order (not available if service is possible only by public notice)
- [ ] Address unknown or serious dispute → consider the main lawsuit
- [ ] Claim of 30 million KRW or less → small claims (Small Claims Trial Rules Art. 1-2)
- [ ] Worried about asset disposal before judgment → consider provisional attachment

### After obtaining an enforcement title
- [ ] A final payment order is, in principle, enforced on its authentic copy without an execution clause (Civil Execution Act Art. 58)
- [ ] No need to vacate first when applying to auction the leased house (Housing Lease Protection Act Art. 3-2(1))
- [ ] But the house must be handed over to receive distribution by priority (Art. 3-2(3))
- [ ] If no assets are visible: asset disclosure (Art. 61) → asset inquiry (Art. 74)

## 4. Seizure exemptions (seizure orders filed from 2026-02-01)

| Target | Exempt from seizure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Wages | One-half; if that is under 2.5M KRW/month, up to 2.5M KRW | Civil Execution Act Art. 246; Decree Art. 3 |
| High wages | Only up to 3M KRW/month + half of (the half amount − 3M KRW) | Decree Art. 4 |
