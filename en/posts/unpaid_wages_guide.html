Unpaid Wages in Korea: Complete Guide — Filing, Claims, and Lawsuits

From gathering evidence to enforcement — everything you need to know about recovering unpaid wages in Korea, as of June 2026

2026-06-29 Legal

Introduction

If your employer has failed to pay your wages, salary, or severance in Korea, you have multiple legal avenues to recover what you are owed. Korea's Labor Standards Act (근로기준법) and related legislation provide strong protections for workers, backed by a government enforcement system and public legal aid programs.

This guide covers the practical steps: gathering evidence, filing a complaint with the Ministry of Employment and Labor (MOEL), accessing the Simplified Wage Guarantee Fund, pursuing civil litigation, understanding the statute of limitations, and obtaining free legal support. All information reflects Korean law and practice as of June 2026.

1. Gather Evidence First

Every effective wage recovery case starts with documentation. Whether you use the administrative or civil route, you need proof of the employment relationship and the amount owed.

Key documents to collect

  • Employment contract — States the agreed wage, payment schedule, and duration. If none exists, text messages, emails, or chat logs discussing wage terms serve as substitutes.
  • Pay slips and bank statements — Show what was paid and what period is missing.
  • Attendance and work records — Access card logs, fingerprint records, work messenger timestamps, or email activity proving you were working.
  • Wage demand records — Messages or emails in which you asked the employer for payment are strong supporting evidence.
  • Social insurance records — National Health Insurance or National Pension enrollment history confirms the employment relationship.

Even without a written contract, you can still file a complaint if you can demonstrate that employment existed and wages were agreed upon. Compile your evidence chronologically with dates and amounts to prepare a clear arrears summary.

2. Filing a Complaint with the Ministry of Employment and Labor (MOEL)

The first and most accessible step is submitting a complaint (jinjeongseo) to MOEL. It is free, and a labor inspector will investigate and order the employer to pay.

Complaint vs. criminal complaint

  • Complaint (jinjeo): An administrative request for mediation and correction. The labor inspector contacts the employer and orders payment.
  • Criminal complaint (goso): Requests criminal prosecution of the employer for violating the Labor Standards Act. Can result in fines or imprisonment for the employer.

Most workers start with a complaint. If the employer refuses to comply with the correction order, you can escalate to a criminal complaint or pursue civil litigation simultaneously.

How to file

  • Online: MOEL Labor Portal at labor.moel.go.kr — select "Wage Arrears Complaint"
  • In person: Visit the Regional Employment and Labor Office (지방고용노동관서) covering your employer's business address
  • By mail: Send a written complaint to the relevant office

What happens after filing

The statutory processing period is 25 business days. A labor inspector is assigned and summons the employer for investigation. If wage arrears are confirmed, a correction order is issued. If the employer pays, the case closes. If the employer refuses, the case is referred to the prosecutor's office for criminal proceedings.

3. Wage Arrears Confirmation and Employer Penalties

When the labor inspector confirms wage arrears, a Wage Arrears Confirmation Certificate is issued. This document is essential for applying to the Wage Guarantee Fund and is strong evidence in civil proceedings.

Under Article 36 of the Labor Standards Act, employers must pay all wages and severance within 14 days of a worker's departure. Violation carries a penalty of up to 3 years imprisonment or a fine of up to 30 million KRW (Article 109). Criminal exposure often motivates employers to settle and pay voluntarily.

4. The Simplified Wage Guarantee Fund (Gan-i Daejigeukkeum)

If you cannot recover wages from the employer directly, the Simplified Wage Guarantee Fund allows the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service (COMWEL) to pay on the employer's behalf — even if the employer has not gone bankrupt.

Eligibility

  • You must have worked at a business covered by the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act.
  • The employer must have operated continuously for at least 6 months prior to your departure.
  • Separated workers: Apply within 2 years of separation with a court-issued enforcement title, or within 1 year if a MOEL complaint was filed.
  • Current employees: Same deadlines measured from the date the wage was due.

Coverage and limits

The fund covers unpaid wages, severance pay, and certain other benefits, up to a maximum of 10 million KRW. COMWEL then exercises subrogation rights against the employer to recover the amount paid.

How to apply

  1. File a MOEL complaint and obtain the Wage Arrears Confirmation Certificate.
  2. Submit a Simplified Wage Guarantee Fund application to your nearest COMWEL regional office.
  3. Payment is typically made within a few weeks of approval.

Online applications are available at comwel.or.kr.

5. Civil Lawsuit and Enforcement

The MOEL complaint process can result in criminal penalties for the employer but does not guarantee you actually receive the money. If the employer has assets but refuses to pay, a civil lawsuit leading to compulsory execution (forced enforcement) is the direct route to recovery.

Small claims and payment orders

For claims under 30 million KRW, small claims court (soaek sageonsimpan) offers a simplified procedure that workers can often handle without a lawyer. A payment order (jigeupmyeong) is even faster — if the employer does not object within 2 weeks, it carries the same force as a court judgment at a fraction of the cost.

Civil litigation steps

  1. File a statement of claim (sojang) at the competent district court.
  2. The court serves the claim on the employer (30-day response period).
  3. Oral argument and evidence examination.
  4. Judgment issued.
  5. Apply for compulsory execution once judgment is final.

Compulsory execution

With a final judgment in hand, you can enforce against the employer's assets: bank account garnishment, real estate seizure, or movable property seizure and auction. To prevent asset concealment, consider filing a preliminary injunction (gaapryu) as soon as you begin legal proceedings.

6. Statute of Limitations — Act Within 3 Years

Wage claims in Korea expire after 3 years from the date payment was due (Labor Standards Act, Article 49). Severance pay follows the same rule. After 3 years, the right to claim is legally extinguished regardless of the facts.

Do not wait to see if the employer will eventually pay. File a formal complaint or lawsuit to stop the limitations clock. A verbal request to the employer does not interrupt the statute of limitations.

Filing an MOEL complaint or initiating civil proceedings interrupts the limitations period. Once interrupted, a new 3-year period begins from that point.

7. Free Legal Aid

You do not have to navigate this process alone. Several public programs provide free support.

Korea Legal Aid Corporation (KLAC) — Phone 132

KLAC offers free legal consultation and full litigation support (including attorney representation) to workers whose average monthly wage over the last 3 months was under 4 million KRW. Foreign workers residing in Korea are included. Apply by phone (132), online at klac.or.kr, or in person at any KLAC branch.

MOEL free legal aid referral

After you file an MOEL complaint, the labor office can refer you to KLAC for free legal support in civil proceedings. Ask about the referral program when visiting the labor office.

Certified labor attorney (gonginchamusa)

Many certified labor attorneys (노무사) offer free initial consultations. They can assist with wage calculation, complaint drafting, and evidence organization. The Korea Association of Labor Consultants can help locate one in your area.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. I worked without a written contract. Can I still file a complaint?

Yes. The absence of a written contract does not bar you from filing. Prove the employment relationship through bank records, social insurance enrollment, or written communications. Note that an employer's failure to provide a written contract is itself a violation of the Labor Standards Act.

Q. What if the company has closed down?

Even if the company has shut down, you can pursue the employer personally if they have assets. If the business meets the insolvency criteria for the general Wage Guarantee Fund (not the simplified version), COMWEL may pay a broader range of arrears. Consult KLAC or a labor attorney for your specific situation.

Q. Am I entitled to severance pay as a part-time worker?

Under the Act on the Guarantee of Retirement Benefits (근로자퇴직급여 보장법), any worker who has been employed for at least 1 year with an average of 15 or more working hours per week is entitled to severance pay. This applies to part-time workers as well.

Q. Should I accept a settlement offer?

You are free to settle at any time. However, signing a settlement agreement typically extinguishes your future legal claims. Review the terms carefully — if it involves accepting less than the full amount owed, consult a labor attorney or KLAC before signing.
