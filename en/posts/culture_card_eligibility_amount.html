Korea Culture Voucher Card - Eligibility and Grant Amount

Who gets the 150,000 won, when to apply, where the card stops working, and when the money disappears - as of August 2026

August 5, 2026 Finance & Economy

Read this first - the cut-off date and the limits of this article

This is a South Korean welfare program available only to residents of South Korea, and the information below reflects the situation as of August 2026. It does not substitute for an eligibility determination or any administrative decision. The grant amount, eligible groups, application window, and merchant rules of the Culture Voucher Card change with each program year. Whether a particular individual qualifies cannot be settled by this article; the final determination rests with the eup, myeon or dong community service center of your resident registration address or with the Culture Voucher Card support center (1544-3412). Before applying, check the notice for the current year on the official site (www.mnuri.kr).

What the card actually gives you - not cash, but a purpose-bound subsidy

The formal name of the program is the Integrated Culture Voucher, known in everyday Korean as the munhwa nuri card. The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism runs the program, the Arts Council Korea operates it, and regional operators across the seventeen metropolitan cities and provinces, along with municipal district offices, handle actual issuance and budget execution. Because it looks and behaves like a payment card, people tend to picture it as a welfare point balance. Its legal character is closer to a national subsidy whose spending categories are fixed by rule. That distinction is exactly what drives the merchant restrictions and the misuse penalties described later.

The physical card is issued as an NH Nonghyup card, and two BIN ranges are in circulation: numbers beginning 9463-16 and numbers beginning 9463-17. The grant sits loaded on the card itself, and each purchase at a participating merchant draws down that balance. You cannot withdraw it as cash, and you cannot have a leftover balance transferred to a bank account.

The key idea: this is not a cash-equivalent benefit but a subsidy usable only in the culture, tourism and sports categories. Misjudge the merchant category and the payment is declined; work around the restriction and you become subject to clawback.

Three checks that tell you whether you qualify

Run the eligibility question through three gates in order and you will make far fewer mistakes.

  1. Income status: are you a Basic Livelihood Security recipient under the National Basic Living Security Act, or a member of the near-poverty class?
  2. Age: are you 6 years old or older? For the 2026 program year that means anyone born on or before December 31, 2020.
  3. Budget: does the municipality of your resident registration address still have budget left for the year? Funds are allocated by region, so once a district exhausts its allocation, issuance can close early even for people who qualify.

Gate one is where most people stall. Basic Livelihood Security recipients almost always know their own status, but the near-poverty class covers several distinct sub-types, and plenty of households never realize they belong to one. The categories that local governments generally group under near-poverty are as follows.

  • Participants in near-poverty self-sufficiency work programs
  • Recipients of the disability allowance or the disabled children allowance
  • Recipients of the supplementary benefit portion of the disability pension
  • People designated for near-poverty medical co-payment relief
  • Members of low-income single-parent families receiving support
  • Holders of a near-poverty status confirmation certificate
  • Other household members in a family with a student receiving the education benefit

Recognizing yourself on that list means you may be eligible; it is not a confirmation. If you are unsure whether you fall into a near-poverty category at all, the fastest route is to take your identification to the welfare desk at your local community service center and ask directly. The near-poverty status confirmation certificate can also be applied for online through the Bokjiro welfare portal or its app, rather than only in person.

The grant is calculated per person, not per household. If four members of one household qualify, four separate cards are issued and each carries its own amount. Having only one family member apply leaves money on the table.

How much lands on the card - the 150,000 won and the extra 10,000

For 2026 the base grant is 150,000 won per person per year, up 10,000 won from the 140,000 won of the previous year. On top of that, anyone who falls into a life-stage supplement group receives an additional 10,000 won, for a maximum of 160,000 won.

These are the points where expectations most often diverge from reality.

  • The 10,000 won supplement is not a separate application item. It is determined automatically from your birth year and loaded together with the base grant.
  • The supplement has its own budget line and can close early once exhausted, independently of the base grant.
  • The qualifying birth years shift by one each year because they are anchored to the program year. Copying last year's table is a reliable way to get it wrong.
  • The amount is an annual lump sum loaded at once, not a monthly installment.

When the money arrives without you applying at all

Many people assume a fresh application is required every year. If you meet the conditions, it is not. Automatic reload applies when all three of the following hold.

  1. You were issued and still hold a Culture Voucher Card from 2025.
  2. Your 2025 spending on that card totaled 30,000 won or more.
  3. You still hold qualifying status as a Basic Livelihood Security recipient or near-poverty class member in 2026.

Meet all three and the 2026 grant is loaded onto your existing card with no application step at all. There is no new card to collect and no visit to make.

Conversely, the following situations push you out of automatic reload, and you have to apply directly.

  • Your prior-year spending came in under 30,000 won - people who took the card and barely used it get caught here.
  • Your welfare status changed during the year and is no longer maintained.
  • You lost or damaged the card and no longer hold it.
The automatic reload conditions are adjusted from program year to program year. Do not assume that because it happened automatically last year it will happen again. Confirm your own reload status directly on the official site, in the mobile app, or through the support center at 1544-3412.

Applying for the first time - four routes and how long each really takes

For 2026 the issuance window, covering both new cards and reloads, runs from Monday, February 2, 2026 through Monday, November 30, 2026, with the final day closing at 18:00. There are four routes, and they differ in how you receive the card and how long it takes.

The in-person route at the community service center is straightforward.

  1. Bring identification to the eup, myeon or dong office of your resident registration address.
  2. Fill in and submit the card application form.
  3. Staff verify your eligibility and register your details.
  4. You receive the card on the spot.

The online route runs in this order.

  1. Open the official site or the mobile app and go to the card issuance menu.
  2. Pass the eligibility check and identity verification.
  3. Register your details, including the delivery address.
  4. Receive the card by registered mail, or if this is a new issuance, collect it at a Nonghyup branch with your identification.

The phone reload route carries conditions.

  • You must be 14 or older and use a mobile phone registered in your own name.
  • The card you hold must have an expiry date in 2027 or later.
  • Residents of welfare facilities cannot reload by phone.

Applicants under 14 who apply online need guardian consent and phone verification. In practice, handling a young child's card at the community service center alongside the rest of the family is faster.

The one step people skip after the card arrives

A card that arrives in the mail will not pay for anything as-is. You must complete receipt registration on the official site or in the mobile app before the card becomes usable. A large share of the complaints along the lines of the card arrived but payment is declined trace back to this skipped step. If you collected the card in person at the community service center, registration is handled at the counter and no separate action is needed.

Delivery by mail → receipt registration → the card starts working. Remember that order. Before registration the balance may display but payments will still be blocked.

Where it works and where it stops working

Permitted spending falls into three families: culture, tourism and sports.

  • Culture: books, albums and music, film, broadcast and video content, performances, exhibitions, crafts, photo studios, and cultural experience programs.
  • Tourism: domestic transport including rail, intercity bus and air travel, travel agency products, national parks, theme parks, and lodging.
  • Sports: spectator tickets for professional football, baseball, basketball and volleyball, sporting goods, and sports facilities such as swimming pools, gyms, yoga studios and taekwondo schools.

The following business types are not permitted, and this is where most people are caught off guard at the register.

  • Large multi-category supermarkets and department stores, and grocery purchases at convenience stores or supermarkets.
  • Restaurants and cafes, including food delivery app payments.
  • General clothing, footwear, accessories and cosmetics, and household consumables.
  • Hair and beauty services, and general exam-prep or supplementary academies.
  • Tobacco and alcohol.
  • Gift certificates and mobile gift vouchers, and any overseas payment.

Even within the same business type, whether a payment goes through depends on merchant registration. If you are planning a larger purchase, it is safer to look the specific store up in the merchant search on the official site or the app before paying. Online merchants participate as well, so booking books and performances online is often the smoother path.

Cancellations have their own rule. Same-day cancellations are generally refunded the same day, but transport payments such as rail and air tickets are not refunded on the day of cancellation. Booking something in a rush at the end of the year and then cancelling can leave the balance unrestored past the deadline.

Cross December 31 and the money is gone - deadlines and balance checks

The 2026 grant is spendable from February 2, 2026 until 23:59 on December 31, 2026. Past that moment, any unspent balance is returned automatically to the national treasury. It does not roll into the following year, and it is not refunded in cash.

There are three ways to check the balance.

  • The card usage and balance menu on the official site, after identity verification.
  • The Culture Voucher Card mobile app.
  • The support center at 1544-3412, weekdays 09:00 to 12:00 and 13:00 to 18:00, excluding public holidays. Card-specific matters such as loss reports go to NH Nonghyup Card at 1644-4000.
Planning to spend everything in December is risky. Popular performances and lodging fill up at year end, and transport payments cannot be refunded on the day of cancellation, so a cancelled booking may not restore your balance in time. Aim to use the grant up during November.

If you lose the card - the reissue deadline and its restrictions

A lost or damaged card can be reissued, but the rules differ from a first issuance.

  • A reissue request with delivery by registered mail must be completed by 23:59 on December 15, 2026 in order to spend that year's grant.
  • Reissuance is not available once qualifying status has been lost, whether Basic Livelihood Security or near-poverty.
  • Reissuance is blocked for 10 days after a card issuance request, including immediate issuance at a community service center.
  • Branch pickup at Nonghyup applies to new issuance only and is not available for reissuance.

The gap between the reissue deadline of December 15 and the spending deadline of December 31 matters. Lose your card on December 20 and, balance or not, there is no way to spend it that year.

Spend it this way and it gets clawed back - three forms of misuse

Because the card carries a national subsidy, spending outside the permitted purpose does not end as a simple rule violation. Official guidance defines three forms of misuse.

  1. Buying non-permitted items: paying for miscellaneous goods, accessories, groceries or household consumables through a permitted merchant category code.
  2. Cashing out in collusion with a merchant: charging the card without an actual purchase, or charging more than the real amount and taking the difference back in cash.
  3. Transferring the card: handing the card to someone other than the registered holder to use.

Where misuse is established, the legal basis and the consequences are as follows.

  • Subsidy Management Act, Article 31-2 - exclusion from performing subsidized projects.
  • Same Act, Article 33 - clawback of the subsidy from the recipient.
  • Same Act, Article 33-2 - imposition and collection of penalty surcharges and additional dues.
The card may be used only by the person to whom it was issued. Letting a family member spend it can amount to transfer. Merchants sometimes propose the cash-out arrangement themselves, but a cardholder who goes along with it also becomes subject to clawback and penalty surcharges.

Six traps that are easy to walk into

  1. Early budget exhaustion - funds are allocated by region, so applying late can mean the window is shut for the year even if you qualify. Applying early in the year is the safest move.
  2. The 30,000 won wall - spend less than that in a given year and you drop out of automatic reload the next year, which adds an application step you did not have before.
  3. Skipping receipt registration - a card delivered by mail will not pay for anything until it is registered.
  4. The December 31 cliff - no carry-over, no refund. Unspent balance returns to the treasury.
  5. Misreading the merchant category - supermarkets, restaurants and academies feel like cultural spending but are not permitted.
  6. The lodging restriction for minors - a card held by someone born on or after January 1, 2008 cannot pay for lodging, so family trips need to be booked on an adult's card.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Can I use it right after applying?

Collect the card in person at a community service center and you can spend the same day. Apply online for delivery by registered mail and it takes 3 to 5 business days, stretching to 1 or 2 weeks if the mail is returned. After a mail delivery, receipt registration must be completed before payments open.

Q. Can a whole family apply together?

The grant is per person, so if several members of a household qualify, each receives their own card. Whether a single counter visit can process the whole household at once varies by local operation, so ask at your community service center to be sure.

Q. Does it work at online shopping sites?

It works at online businesses registered as Culture Voucher Card merchants. Even within one shopping site, though, whether a payment goes through depends on the item category, so checking the merchant search on the official site or the app before paying is the safer route.

Q. Can I use it from a phone without the physical card?

Physical card issuance is the default. Balance lookups, merchant search, and issuance or reload requests can all be handled in the mobile app. How payment itself works can vary by card type and merchant, so the support center is the accurate place to confirm.

Q. I did not use it last year - can I still get it this year?

Yes. But if your prior-year spending fell short of 30,000 won, you drop out of automatic reload and will need to apply directly through a community service center or the official site.

Q. I have a small balance left - can I take it in cash?

No. Cash withdrawal and transfer to a bank account are both unavailable, and the balance returns to the treasury once the deadline passes. If the remainder is small, spending it somewhere that accepts low-value payments, such as a book purchase or a film booking, is the better outcome.

Q. What happens to money already loaded if my status changes mid-year?

Balance handling and reissue restrictions after a status change are areas where the local authority's judgment comes into play. Because reissuance is unavailable once status has been lost, the safe move if your circumstances change is to inform your community service center first and follow their guidance.

Q. I missed the application window.

Issuance for 2026 closes at 18:00 on November 30. Past that point the grant for that year cannot be received and you have to wait for the next program year's notice. Regional budgets can run out before the deadline, so it is unwise to leave it to the last moment.

In short - three things to check now

The Culture Voucher Card is not a difficult program once the conditions line up. What it does have is very clear points at which money disappears. Three checks cover most of it. First, confirm at your community service center whether you and each family member individually qualify. Second, look up whether you are on automatic reload through the official site or the support center; if you are, nothing else is required, and if you are not, apply before November 30. Third, review your balance and deadline during November. A balance that crosses 23:59 on December 31 does not come back.

Deciding in advance where to spend it helps in practice too. Locking in categories that are clearly permitted, such as books, film, performances, domestic transport and sports facilities, keeps you out of the year-end scramble that ends with a declined payment.

The amounts, requirements and dates in this article reflect August 2026 and change with each program year. Do not treat anything written here as a confirmation of your own eligibility. Before applying, check the current year notice on the official site (www.mnuri.kr), and confirm eligibility with the community service center of your resident registration address or the support center (1544-3412).
