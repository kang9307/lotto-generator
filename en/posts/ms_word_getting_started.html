Scope and Notation for Working in Word

Making a single document in Word breaks down into six features: starting a new document, applying styles, page numbers, a table of contents, saving as PDF, and AutoSave. The applies-to list that Microsoft gives differs a little from feature to feature, so even the same menu can look different depending on the version of Word.

  • New document: The applies-to list of the article Create a document in Word is Word for Microsoft 365, Word 2024, and Word 2021. The article says its content and steps apply to Word for Microsoft 365 and to other desktop versions of Word for Windows.
  • Page numbers, table of contents, and fields: The page number article lists Word for Microsoft 365, Word 2024, 2021, 2019, and 2016 together with their Mac versions, and has separate Windows and macOS steps. The table of contents article applies to Word for Microsoft 365, Word 2024, and 2021 and has Windows, macOS, and Web steps. The Update fields article applies to Word for Microsoft 365, Word 2024, 2021, 2019, and 2016.
  • Styles and PDF: The styles article applies to Word and Outlook in Microsoft 365, 2024, 2021, 2019, and 2016. The PDF article covers several Office apps at once, and for Word it lists Microsoft 365, 2021, 2019, and 2016.
  • AutoSave: The article describes AutoSave as a feature available to Microsoft 365 subscribers. For people who are not subscribers, AutoRecover keeps working as usual.

Keyboard shortcuts use only the Windows combinations written in the article Keyboard shortcuts in Word. Menu paths follow the article's wording, as in Insert > Page Number. Because the supported versions differ from article to article, the applies-to line of each article is worth checking before its steps are followed on an older version of Word.

The Order for Finishing One Document at a Glance

The work proceeds in this order: open a new document, apply styles to headings and body text, add page numbers and a table of contents, and then export to PDF or decide where the file is saved. The table below gathers, for each step, the menu location, what the article says, and the applies-to list.

Task Menu location What the Microsoft article says Applies to or condition
Start a new document File tab > New Select Blank document, double-click a template image, or type the kind of document into the Search for online templates box and press Enter Word for Microsoft 365, Word 2024, Word 2021
Apply a style The Styles gallery on the Home tab Select the text to format and click the style you want in the gallery Word 365, 2024, 2021, 2019, 2016
Insert page numbers Insert > Page Number Choose a location and style, and hide the number on the first page with Different First Page Word 365, 2024, 2021, 2019, 2016

The sections that follow take the steps of this table in order and set out the procedure and conditions that each article describes.

Starting a New Document - Blank Documents and Templates

A new document starts by selecting New on the File tab. As things Word can do, the article lists creating a document (from scratch or from a template), adding text, images, art, and videos, researching a topic and finding credible sources, accessing documents from a computer, tablet, or phone through OneDrive, sharing and collaborating, and tracking and reviewing changes.

  • Blank document: On the New screen, select Blank document.
  • Template: On the same screen, double-click a template image.
  • Online template search: Type the kind of document into the Search for online templates box and press Enter.
  • Typing and formatting: Click the new blank page and type some text, then select the text to format and choose font options such as Bold, Italic, Bullets, and Numbering on the Home tab.
  • Insert tab: Add a table (hover over the size wanted and select it), pictures (an image on the computer, an online stock image, or a Bing image search), shapes, icons, 3D models, SmartArt, a chart, or a screenshot.

The article adds that older versions of Word may have Online Pictures on the ribbon next to Pictures. A new document can also be created with the keyboard shortcut Ctrl+N. For practicing Word features, the article recommends learning guides such as Welcome to Word and Insert your first table of contents.

Styles - the Gallery, Modifying, and Keyboard Shortcuts

Styles let a set of formatting choices be applied quickly and consistently across a whole document, and the styles article covers both Word and Outlook. The Styles gallery is the visual menu on the Home tab, and a style is applied by selecting the text to format and clicking the style wanted in the gallery. Unlike changing the font size and weight by hand, a heading given a style becomes the basis for the table of contents covered later.

  • Modify a style: In the Styles gallery on the Home tab, right-click a style and select Modify. In the Formatting section, change the font style, size, color, alignment, line spacing, or indentation, and choose whether the change applies to the current document only or to all future documents.
  • Update a style to match formatting: Select text that already has a style, such as Heading 1, change its formatting, then in the Styles group on the Home tab right-click that style and choose the Update [Style Name] to Match Selection command. All the text that uses that style changes to the new formatting. The article's example is changing Heading 1 from 16 points to 14 points.
  • Create a new style: Right-click formatted text, choose Styles on the mini toolbar and then Create a Style, and give it a name. To make it appear in all new documents, open Modify in the gallery and select New documents based on this template at the bottom of the dialog box.
  • Keyboard shortcuts: Heading 1 is Ctrl+Alt+1, Heading 2 is Ctrl+Alt+2, and the Normal style is Ctrl+Shift+N.

The table of contents article explains that missing entries often happen because headings are not formatted as headings. In that case, select the heading text to include, choose Heading 1 under Home > Styles, and then update the table of contents.

Page Numbers and Headers and Footers

Page numbers start on the Insert tab. Below are the Windows steps from the page number article.

  • Insert: On the Insert tab, select Page Number, then choose the location and style you want.
  • Hide the first-page number: If the page number should not appear on the first page, select Different First Page.
  • Starting value: To start numbering with 1 on the second page, go to Page Number > Format Page Numbers and set Start at to 0.
  • Finish editing: Select Close Header and Footer or press the Esc key.
  • Edit again: To get back to a header or footer to make changes, double-click in the header or footer area.

The macOS steps are a little different. On the Insert tab, select Page Number and then choose Page Number again, then pick a location and an alignment style. Word automatically numbers every page except designated title pages. The numbering style is changed by selecting Format on the top toolbar, and OK is selected twice to close both dialog boxes.

For a document with a cover page, the two Windows steps can be applied one after another. Selecting Different First Page keeps a number off the first page, and setting Start at to 0 makes the second page start at 1.

Creating a Table of Contents and Updating Fields

A table of contents in Word is based on the headings in the document. When the heading text, sequence, or level changes, an automatic table of contents can be updated to match.

  • Insert: Put the cursor where the table of contents should go (usually near the beginning of the document), select References > Table of Contents, and then choose an Automatic Table of Contents style from the list.
  • Format and customize: The font, the number of heading levels, and whether to show dotted lines between entries and page numbers can all be changed.
  • Manual style: With a Manual Table of Contents style, Word does not use the headings to create the table of contents and cannot update it automatically. It uses placeholder text to create the look of a table of contents, so each entry has to be typed in by hand.
  • Update the table of contents: After a change that affects the table of contents, right-click it and choose Update Field. The Update Table of Contents dialog box asks whether to update page numbers only or the entire table; updating page numbers only changes the numbers of the existing sections and does not add new sections to the table of contents.
  • Update all fields: Press Ctrl+A and then F9. Tables that hold fields or formulas may need to be selected one at a time with F9. To avoid forgetting to update the table of contents before printing, select Update fields before printing under Printing options in File > Options > Display.
  • Word for the web: The Web steps in the table of contents article are to select References and then Insert Table of Contents.

Fields are used for the table of contents, headers and footers, cross-references, bookmarks, and formulas in tables, and they sometimes have to be updated manually. If it is hard to tell which parts of the text are fields, Field shading under Show document content in File > Options > Advanced can be set to Always so they show on a gray background. A field might not update because the item it refers to no longer exists, or because a link or bookmark is broken.

Saving as PDF - Procedure and Options in Word

An Office program can save or convert a file to PDF, and no other software or add-in is needed. The article says to use the PDF format when a document needs to look the same on most computers, to be small in file size, or to follow an industry format, and it gives resumes, legal documents, newsletters, files for reading and printing, and files for professional printing as examples. Sharing as a PDF, however, does not guarantee that the recipient cannot edit it, because many programs, including Word, can open and edit PDF files. The article splits its procedures for Access, Excel, OneNote, PowerPoint, Project, and Word across a single page, so the part that applies to Word is only one portion of it.

In Word, if the file was saved before, choose File > Save a Copy; if it has not been saved, choose Save As or Save a Copy. Browse to pick the location, choose PDF in the drop-down list, and save. Selecting More options > Options customizes the PDF.

Option What the article says
Page range Used to create the PDF from only some pages of the document
Publish what Select the document showing markup to include tracked changes; otherwise select the document
Create bookmarks using Creates a set of bookmarks in the PDF from headings or from Word bookmarks
Document properties Includes the document properties in the PDF
Document structure tags for accessibility Makes the document easier to read in screen-reading software
ISO 19005-1 compliant (PDF/A) Outputs the PDF as version 1.7, an archiving standard, and helps the document look the same when opened later on another computer
Bitmap text when fonts may not be embedded If fonts cannot be embedded, uses bitmap images of the text so the PDF looks like the original; without it, a PDF reader may substitute a different font
Encrypt the document with a password Restricts access so that only people who know the password can open the PDF
  • Opening a PDF: Under File > Open, browsing to a PDF file makes Word open the PDF content in a new file, and the content needed, including images and diagrams, can be copied.
  • Mac: Under File > Save As or Save a Copy, choose PDF as the file format at the bottom of the window. The article notes that using Best for printing might not convert hyperlinks correctly, which it calls a known issue in Word for Mac.
  • Word for the web: Choose File > Print and then Save as PDF in the printer drop-down. If more PDF options such as bookmarks are needed, choose Open in Desktop App from the Editing drop-down to convert in the desktop app. Online services can use only fonts that Microsoft has legal rights to, so a custom font may be substituted.

The same PDF article covers PowerPoint as well, but its steps and options differ. Saving slides as PDF is covered separately in the PowerPoint getting-started guide, and for a Word document the procedure and options above apply.

AutoSave and Version History - When It Turns On

AutoSave saves a file every few seconds as you work in Excel, Word, and PowerPoint, and the article describes it as available to Microsoft 365 subscribers. The storage location decides when it turns on and when it is disabled, and every condition in the list below comes from the AutoSave article.

  • When it turns on: If a file is stored on OneDrive, OneDrive for Business, or SharePoint Online, AutoSave is on by default in Microsoft 365. A subscriber with a current version of Word for Windows sees the AutoSave switch at the upper left of the title bar.
  • When it is disabled: It is disabled for files on a SharePoint on-premises site, a file server, some other cloud storage services, or a local path such as C:\, and for files that have not been saved yet. Other reasons listed include an older format such as .doc, a paused OneDrive sync, an older OneDrive sync app, and a file embedded in another Office file.
  • Manual saving: Ctrl+S, the Save button, or File > Save always saves manually. Even for people who are not subscribers, AutoRecover protects the file in a crash, and after reopening, the version with recent changes appears in the Document Recovery pane.
  • Version history: Click the file name at the top of the window and select Version History, review the dates and times, then select Open Version and Restore. The first change after opening a file adds a new version to the history, and after that a new version is added about every 10 minutes while editing continues.
  • Save a Copy: When a document is opened from OneDrive and similar locations, Save As disappears from the File menu and Save a Copy appears in its place. To keep changes in a copy rather than the original, it is safer to use File > Save a Copy before editing.

If AutoSave is turned off for an individual file, the program remembers that and keeps it off every time the file is reopened. To turn it off by default for all files, clear the check box next to AutoSave OneDrive and SharePoint Online files by default in File > Options > Save, and restart Word. To try changes out without showing them to others, turn the AutoSave switch off before saving, and to discard them, close the file and select No when asked whether to save. An owner can also prevent accidental changes with Always Open Read-Only under File > Info > Protect Document.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • The differences in ribbon screens and menu locations among Word 2024, 2021, 2019, and 2016. The articles list the applies-to versions but do not describe screen differences by version.
  • The individual style names shown in the table of contents gallery, and the features of the Design and Layout tabs. The article says only an Automatic Table of Contents style and does not cover those two tabs.
  • The step in Word's PDF procedure for choosing Standard or Minimum size optimization. That choice appears only in the Access, Excel, and PowerPoint procedures of the same article.
  • Whether AutoSave can be used in non-subscription versions of Word 2021 and Word 2024. The applies-to list names those versions, but the body describes AutoSave as a Microsoft 365 subscriber feature.
  • Which services are meant by the some other cloud storage services for which AutoSave is disabled. The article does not name the services.
