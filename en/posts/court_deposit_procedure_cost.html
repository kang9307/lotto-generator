Court Deposit Procedure and Cost Guide in Korea — Requirements, Filing, Retrieval, and Statute of Limitations

Complete Guide to Korea's Court Deposit System When Creditors Refuse Payment or Cannot Be Identified
2026-09-29 Law & Legal
⚠️ Legal Disclaimer — This guide is provided for general informational purposes based on South Korean statutes and court regulations, and does not constitute formal legal advice for individual cases. Please consult the competent court deposit office or a certified legal professional in Korea for specific legal matters.

This guide explains the statutory court deposit system (Gongtak, 공탁) operated under the judicial system of the Republic of Korea. In commercial and personal civil relationships, situations frequently arise where a debtor attempts in good faith to perform a monetary or property obligation, but the creditor arbitrarily refuses to accept payment, remains unreachable due to absence, or cannot be determined without fault on the part of the debtor due to competing assignment notices. In these circumstances, if the debtor had no legal remedy, they would suffer the unjust burden of accruing late payment interest (delay damages) and potential default liabilities. To address this exact problem, South Korean civil law provides a statutory mechanism allowing debtors to discharge their obligations by depositing the subject matter with a court deposit official.

Under the Korean court deposit framework, once the debtor properly lodges the funds or property with the designated deposit officer at a court deposit office, the debtor is discharged from the debt (subject to the depositor's right of retrieval before the creditor accepts), and the creditor acquires an administrative claim to withdraw the deposited funds directly from the court. This article provides a comprehensive legal overview of the statutory categories of deposits, the strict tripartite requirements for performance deposits, step-by-step application procedures, verified filing costs, electronic filing ceilings, retrieval and withdrawal rules, and the strict 10-year statute of limitations under the Korean Deposit Act and Supreme Court Deposit Rules.

1. Concept of Court Deposits and Major Statutory Categories

Under South Korean law, a court deposit is a public legal arrangement wherein a depositor (Gongtakja, 공탁자), pursuant to grounds defined in relevant statutes, places money, securities, or other movable articles into the custody of a court deposit officer (Gongtakgwan, 공탁관), allowing a designated payee (Pigongtakja, 피공탁자) or entitled party to collect the property, or allowing the depositor to recover it upon the occurrence of specified statutory events. Under statutory rules, eligible subject matter is confined to money, securities, and other tangible goods. Crucially, the term "money" in Korean monetary deposits denotes exclusively the lawful currency of South Korea (KRW). Foreign currencies (such as USD or EUR) are not eligible as monetary deposits; instead, they are treated as tangible property under the rules governing article deposits and must be stored in designated warehouse facilities or bank vaults.

Korean statutes categorize court deposits according to their functional purposes. Beyond the common performance deposit used to discharge debts, deposits are utilized across civil litigation, preliminary attachment, and compulsory enforcement procedures. The primary types recognized under the Korean Deposit Act and administrative guidance are summarized below.

Deposit Category Purpose and Circumstances of Use Governing Statutory Provisions (Verified)
Performance Deposit (변제공탁) When a debtor seeks to discharge an obligation by depositing the subject matter after the creditor refuses or cannot receive payment, or cannot be ascertained without fault. Civil Act Article 487; Deposit Act Article 4
Criminal Performance Deposit (형사변제공탁) When a criminal defendant deposits restitution funds for a crime victim whose personal identifying details are unknown to the defendant. Deposit Act Articles 5-2 and 9-2
Security / Guarantee Deposit (담보·보증공탁) When a party deposits funds pursuant to a court order to secure potential future damages, such as for provisional attachment, provisional injunction, or stay of execution. Civil Execution Act Article 19, Paragraph 1
Execution Deposit (집행공탁) When an enforcement officer or third-party debtor deposits property under the Civil Execution Act in the course of compulsory execution or provisional attachment. Civil Execution Act Article 248
Custody Deposit (보관공탁) When property is deposited purely for safekeeping (e.g., depositing bearer bonds to request the convening of a bondholders' meeting, per Easy Law). Individual statutes
Forfeiture Deposit (몰취공탁) When funds are deposited so that the state may forfeit the deposit if certain statutory violations or conditions occur. Civil Procedure Act Article 299, Paragraph 2
Mixed Deposit (혼합공탁) When grounds for a performance deposit (e.g., uncertain creditor) and an execution deposit (e.g., competing attachments) concurrently coexist. Classification in practice (article number omitted)

1.1 Principal Deposit Types Encountered in Practical Disputes

In routine civil affairs, individuals and corporate entities most frequently encounter performance deposits and judicial security deposits. For instance, when a residential lease terminates and the tenant refuses to accept the return of the deposit while refusing to vacate, the landlord, who owes the deposit back, can use a performance deposit to discharge that obligation. Similarly, when a plaintiff applies for a preliminary attachment (Gayuamryu, 가압류) against a defendant's real estate or bank accounts, the civil court routinely issues a security order requiring the plaintiff to deposit cash as security against wrongful attachment damages.

Furthermore, a debtor whose assets have been frozen by a preliminary attachment order may deposit the full claim amount specified in the court order to lift the freeze; this is known as a "release deposit for provisional attachment" (가압류 해방공탁). For procedural steps concerning underlying small claims lawsuits or lease deposit litigation, readers can refer to dedicated resources such as the Small Claims Court Guide or the Jeonse Deposit Refund Lawsuit Guide. This article focuses specifically on the statutory criteria, filing mechanics, and fund retrieval rules governing the court deposit institution itself.

1.2 Scope and Eligibility of Deposit Objects

Under Korean statutory rules, the subject matter of a deposit is strictly limited to money, securities, and specified physical goods. When cash is deposited, it must strictly be legal tender of the Republic of Korea. Foreign currency cannot be processed as a standard monetary deposit; applicants must proceed under the rules for article deposits, which entail specialized warehousing arrangements and associated storage tariffs.

In performance deposits, the deposited amount must strictly equal the entirety of the debt owed under the principle of complete performance. Under the position taken in Supreme Court of Korea precedents, partial deposits (일부공탁) do not discharge the underlying obligation unless the creditor explicitly accepts the partial payment without reservation. Therefore, if interest or delay damages have accrued up to the date of deposit, the debtor must calculate and deposit the exact total of the principal together with all accrued statutory interest; otherwise, the debt remains undischarged.

2. Three Statutory Grounds for Performance Deposits and Debt Discharge Effects

The substantive cornerstone of performance deposits is Article 487 of the Korean Civil Act. This provision allows the person performing an obligation to be discharged by depositing the subject matter of performance for the creditor when the creditor does not or cannot receive performance, or when the performer cannot identify the creditor without negligence. A deposit filed without satisfying one of these three explicit statutory grounds is legally ineffective and fails to discharge the debtor's liability.

2.1 Three Statutory Grounds: Refusal, Inability, and Non-Negligent Uncertainty

To establish a valid performance deposit, the applicant must clearly substantiate at least one of the following statutory conditions:

  • Creditor's Refusal of Performance (수령거절): Arises when the debtor offers genuine performance according to the true intent of the obligation at the due date, but the creditor unreasonably declines to accept payment. A common example occurs when a dispute exists over the exact calculation of interest or damages, prompting the creditor to block bank transfers and declare they will not accept the money. In practical proceedings, debtors substantiate this by submitting certified mail notices proving that tender was formally made and rejected (Certified Mail Writing and Sending Guide).
  • Creditor's Inability to Receive Performance (수령불능): Pertains to objective situations where the creditor is incapacitated from receiving payment due to factual or legal obstacles. This includes cases where the creditor is missing, has moved abroad without leaving contact information, or lacks a designated representative.
  • Uncertainty of Creditor Without Negligence (과실 없는 채권자 불확지): Applies when an obligation objectively exists, but the debtor, despite exercising the due care of a good administrator, cannot determine with certainty who the true creditor is. What is accepted for a performance deposit is relative uncertainty (e.g., knowing that either Party A or Party B is the creditor, but not which one). A deposit where the creditor is entirely unknown (absolute uncertainty) is in principle not allowed; the Supreme Court recognized an exception only where a statute provides for it, such as land expropriation compensation (Supreme Court en banc decision 96Da11747, October 16, 1997). A typical scenario is competing assignment notices for the same claim where the debtor cannot determine the true creditor. Where an assignment notice and a provisional attachment order are served together, the case may instead become a mixed deposit, combining performance-deposit and execution-deposit grounds. Subjective doubt alone without objective justification does not satisfy this statutory test.

2.2 Legal Effects: Discharge of Debt and Possibility of Retrieval

Once a lawful performance deposit is established, the debtor is discharged from the debt (Easy Law). However, until the creditor approves the deposit or notifies the deposit office of intent to receive it, or until a judgment confirming the validity of the deposit becomes final, the depositor may retrieve the deposited property (Civil Act Article 489; Deposit Act Article 9(2)(1)).

As a consequence, the debtor can stop further late interest (delay damages) from accruing, and the creditor acquires a right to claim the deposited funds from the court deposit office, known as the "right to claim withdrawal of deposit" (공탁물 출급청구권).

3. Jurisdictional Rules and Electronic Deposit System Thresholds

Determining the correct court deposit office to receive an application is known as "deposit jurisdiction" (공탁 관할). An application filed with a deposit office lacking jurisdiction may be refused, and even if accepted, the validity of the performance deposit may be disputed. Applicants must therefore check the jurisdictional rules established by law.

3.1 Criteria for Determining the Competent Deposit Office

The territorial jurisdiction of court deposit offices is established under the Civil Act and specialized procedural statutes:

  • Standard Jurisdiction for Performance Deposits: Easy Law explains that a performance deposit must be made with the deposit office at the place of performance of the obligation (Civil Act Article 488, Paragraph 1). Because monetary debts under Korean civil law are generally portable obligations (obligations payable at the creditor's domicile), the competent deposit office is ordinarily the district court, branch court, or municipal court having jurisdiction over the registered address of the creditor (payee).
  • Special Rule for Foreigners and Non-Resident Citizens: Under Article 5, Paragraph 1 of the Korean Deposit Act, when a performance deposit is made for the benefit of a foreigner or overseas Korean national who possesses no registered domestic address or temporary residence in Korea, the application may be filed with the deposit office at the seat of the Supreme Court (the Seoul Central District Court Deposit Office).
  • Convenience Access to Non-Jurisdictional Deposit Offices: For the convenience of citizens, Korean court administration permits applicants to use nearby non-jurisdictional court offices under specific statutory restrictions. According to official guidelines, this applies exclusively to monetary performance deposit filings and all monetary deposit payment claims, provided the applicant appears in person and the claim amount does not exceed 10 million KRW.

3.2 Scope of Supreme Court Electronic Deposit System and the 50 Million KRW Cap

To modernize judicial administration, the Supreme Court of Korea operates an online electronic deposit portal (ekt.scourt.go.kr), allowing parties to submit applications remotely. However, the procedural scope of electronic processing is strictly circumscribed by administrative regulations.

Article 69 of the Supreme Court Deposit Rules provides: "Applications or petitions regarding monetary deposit cases may be submitted as electronic documents using the electronic deposit system; provided, however, that this shall not apply to petitions for withdrawal (출급) or retrieval (회수) of deposited money exceeding 50 million KRW."

This means that while the initial deposit of funds into court may be conducted electronically without any monetary ceiling, applications to withdraw or retrieve deposited money exceeding 50 million KRW cannot be conducted online. Such claims cannot be made through the electronic deposit system and are filed in writing with the deposit office (Deposit Rules Article 69).

4. Step-by-Step Filing Procedures and Statutory Filing Expenses

The execution of a court deposit proceeds through structured statutory phases pursuant to Article 4 of the Deposit Act and the Deposit Rules. Article 4 establishes that "any person who intends to make a deposit shall prepare a deposit application and submit it to a deposit officer in accordance with the Supreme Court Rules, and then pay the deposit property to a designated bank or warehouse operator." Officially, this comprises: (1) Application, (2) Receipt and Review, and (3) Payment of Property.

Procedural Phase Operational Tasks and Detailed Steps Statutory Citation (Official Number) Applicable Expense Provisions
Step 1: Application Prepare and submit 2 copies of deposit application, attach deposit notices matching number of payees, and attach proof of address. Deposit Rules Article 20(1), Article 21, Article 23(1) Prepayment of delivery-certified postal fees (Deposit Rules Article 23(2))
Step 2: Receipt & Review Formal examination by deposit officer (checking jurisdiction, required statements, attachments). Issuance of deposit number and payment deadline upon acceptance. Deposit Act Article 4; Deposit Rules Articles 25 and 26 No statutory filing fee prescribed in Act or Rules
Step 3: Payment Deposit funds or property with designated depository bank or warehouse within specified payment deadline. Deposit legally takes effect upon confirmation. Deposit Act Article 4; Official Guidelines Warehouse storage fees may be charged for article deposits (Deposit Act Article 8)

4.1 Documentation and the Mandatory Submission of Two Application Copies

When applying in person, Article 20, Paragraph 1 of the Deposit Rules strictly mandates that the applicant submit "two copies of the deposit application" (공탁서 2통) to the deposit officer. One copy is retained in the permanent archives of the court, while the second copy is marked with the acceptance, the deposit number, and the payment deadline, and then given to the depositor (Deposit Rules Article 26, Paragraph 1). The depositor presents this stamped copy to the designated on-site depository bank when transferring the funds. In electronic filings, this document is generated digitally, and payment is remitted via virtual account.

The standard accompanying documentation includes:

  • Deposit Notices (공탁통지서): When the depositor is required to give notice to the payee, a number of deposit notice forms exactly equal to the number of payees must be attached (Deposit Rules Article 23, Paragraph 1).
  • Proof of Payee Address: In performance deposits, certified documentation establishing the payee's residential address (such as a certified copy or abstract of resident registration) must be submitted; if the address is objectively unknown, an explanatory statement substantiating the search efforts must be provided (Deposit Rules Article 21, Paragraph 3).
  • Corporate and Agency Proof: If a party is a corporation, certified proof of corporate representative qualifications (Article 21, Paragraph 1) is required; if filed by an agent, a formal power of attorney proving authority (Article 21, Paragraph 2) must be attached.
  • Validity Window for Public Certificates: Any official certificate issued by a public administrative agency must have been issued within 3 months prior to the date of submission (Deposit Rules Article 16).

4.2 Verified Statutory Filing Costs and Absence of General Application Fees

A frequent point of confusion among foreign entities and domestic citizens is the extent of administrative fees charged by the court for filing a deposit. A thorough examination of the entire text of the Korean Deposit Act and Supreme Court Deposit Rules reveals that the statutory text contains no mention of any general "application fee" (수수료) whatsoever.

The only verified statutory expense explicitly imposed by the rules is the delivery-certified postage fee (배달증명 우편료) under Article 23, Paragraph 2 of the Deposit Rules. Because the court deposit officer must formally transmit the deposit notice to the payee via registered mail with delivery certification, the applicant must prepay the requisite postal tariff per recipient. For physical goods deposits, Article 8 of the Deposit Act establishes that the depository bank or warehouse operator may charge the recipient customary storage fees. Any other actual expenses should be confirmed with the competent deposit office. The Act and the Rules contain no provision setting a filing fee, and no primary source confirms that filing costs nothing at all.

5. Requirements for Withdrawal, Retrieval, and Criminal Deposit Restrictions

Retrieving property held in court custody is divided into two distinct statutory procedures: "withdrawal by the payee" (Chulgeup, 출급) and "retrieval by the depositor" (Hoesu, 회수). Withdrawal refers to the collection of the deposit by the designated creditor or entitled beneficiary, whereas retrieval refers to the recovery of the property by the original depositor upon the realization of specific legal conditions.

Classification Entitled Claimant Primary Statutory Grounds and Conditions Required Documentation and Standards
Withdrawal (출급) Payee (designated creditor or entitled party) Proof of right to receive deposit pursuant to Supreme Court Rules (Deposit Act Article 9(1)); proof of counter-performance (Article 10). Original deposit notice (may be omitted under 50M KRW), proof of entitlement, seal certificate (with statutory exemptions).
Retrieval (회수) Depositor (debtor or original applicant) 1. Civil Act Article 489 (prior to acceptance); 2. Mistaken deposit; 3. Extinction of deposit cause (Deposit Act Article 9(2)). Original deposit application copy (may be omitted under 50M KRW), proof of retrieval grounds, seal certificate (with exemptions).

5.1 Requirements for Payee Withdrawal and Counter-Performance Proof

Under Article 9, Paragraph 1 of the Deposit Act, any person seeking to receive deposited property must prove their entitlement in accordance with Supreme Court Rules. To claim withdrawal, the claimant must submit two copies of the withdrawal petition pursuant to Article 32, Paragraph 1 of the Deposit Rules.

If the deposit was made subject to a conditional obligation—such as requiring the tenant to deliver vacant possession of a building or deliver registration documents—Article 10 of the Deposit Act provides that where the person entitled to receive the deposit must make counter-performance, that person cannot receive the deposit unless the counter-performance is proven by a document of the depositor, a judgment, a notarial deed, or another official document prepared by a public agency. Thus, the payee must prove counter-performance with such documents to collect the funds.

Claimants must generally submit the original deposit notice and a certified seal certificate. However, where the deposited amount does not exceed 50 million KRW, submission of the deposit notice may be waived under administrative rules. Furthermore, under Article 37, Paragraph 3 of the Deposit Rules, if an individual claimant appears in person to claim an amount not exceeding 10 million KRW and verifies their identity via government-issued photographic identification, the submission of a seal certificate is entirely exempted. In electronic claims submitted under Article 79, Paragraph 1, digital certificate authentication replaces the physical seal certificate.

5.2 Three Statutory Retrieval Grounds and Criminal Deposit Restrictions

A depositor cannot arbitrarily cancel or claw back deposited funds. Under Article 9, Paragraph 2 of the Deposit Act, retrieval is permitted strictly upon proving one of three statutory grounds:

  • Pursuant to Article 489 of the Civil Act: The debtor may retrieve the deposited property as long as the creditor has not accepted the deposit, has not notified the deposit office of their intent to receive it, or before a judgment confirming the validity of the deposit becomes final. However, retrieval is prohibited if pledges or mortgages have been extinguished by the deposit.
  • Deposit Made by Mistake (착오 공탁): When a deposit was executed erroneously without any underlying legal duty or statutory ground.
  • Extinction of the Cause of Deposit (공탁원인의 소멸): Where, following the deposit, the debt has been fully settled through separate compromise or where a court ruling confirms the non-existence of the underlying liability.

Applicants must also note Article 9-2 of the Deposit Act (Restrictions on Retrieval of Deposits), newly established on October 16, 2024.

Article 9-2, Paragraph 1 provides that a depositor who makes a performance deposit for a crime victim cannot retrieve it on the grounds in Article 9(2)(1) and (3), namely retrieval under Civil Act Article 489 or extinction of the cause of deposit. However, retrieval is possible upon proof of the fact (proviso of the same paragraph) where (1) the person designated as recipient consents to retrieval or notifies the deposit office of refusal to receive the deposit, or (2) an acquittal becomes final or a non-indictment disposition (excluding a suspension of indictment, Kiso-yuye) is made in the criminal case. Retrieval of a deposit made by mistake (Article 9(2)(2)) is not restricted by this provision.

6. Statutory Interest on Deposited Funds and 10-Year Limitation Rules

During the period that funds remain in court custody, statutory interest accrues pursuant to judicial regulations, and rights to these funds are governed by an unyielding statutory period of extinctive prescription.

6.1 Statutory Interest Rate and Payment Mechanism

Under Article 6 of the Deposit Act, Article 51 of the Deposit Rules, and Article 2 of the Rules on Interest on Deposited Money, interest may be affixed to monetary deposits. According to official public legal guidelines (current as of August 15, 2026), the prevailing interest rate applied to court deposits is 35/10,000 per annum (0.35% per annum) (note: the original text of the specific interest rule was not directly cross-checked). Pursuant to Article 52 of the Deposit Rules, accrued interest is paid out concurrently with the principal when the deposit is disbursed.

6.2 The 10-Year Statute of Limitations and Reversion to National Treasury

Court deposits cannot be left unclaimed indefinitely. Article 9, Paragraph 3 of the Deposit Act provides that where the deposited property is money, the right to receive or retrieve the principal or interest is extinguished by prescription if not exercised for 10 years from the time it becomes exercisable.

According to the official FAQ of the Supreme Court Electronic Deposit System, the statutory 10-year period commences as follows:

  • Payee's Withdrawal Right: In performance deposits, the 10-year extinctive prescription begins to run from the date on which the payee receives formal delivery of the deposit notice.
  • Depositor's Retrieval Right: Where a depositor holds a legitimate ground for recovery, the 10-year prescription commences from the date of the deposit.

Once this 10-year limitation period expires without formal claim, the underlying rights are permanently extinguished, and the entire principal and accrued interest irrevocably forfeit to the national treasury of the Republic of Korea. Parties receiving deposit notices must therefore act diligently to secure their funds.

7. Objection and Appeal Procedures Against Deposit Officer Dispositions

Because court deposit officers (persons designated by the chief judge of the district court or branch court from among court clerks, Deposit Act Article 2, Paragraph 1) exercise formal examination authority based strictly on submitted documentary records, applications that contain omissions, lack essential certificates, or fail jurisdictional criteria are met with a formal administrative refusal (non-acceptance disposition, Bul-suri, 불수리 처분).

7.1 Filing Objections Under the Deposit Act

A party aggrieved by a refusal or disposition issued by a deposit officer may lodge a statutory objection under Article 12 of the Deposit Act through the following procedural steps:

  • Submission of Written Objection: The objecting party does not initiate an immediate direct lawsuit in court; rather, they must submit a written objection (이의신청서) directly to the deposit office where the officer is stationed (Deposit Act Article 12, Paragraphs 1 and 2).
  • Internal Correction by the Officer: If the deposit officer, upon re-evaluating the objection, determines that the objection is well-founded, they must immediately alter the disposition and accept the application or pay the deposit (Deposit Act Article 13, Paragraph 1).
  • Mandatory 5-Day Transmission to District Court: If the deposit officer concludes that the objection is groundless, they are statutorily required under Article 13, Paragraph 2 to forward the written objection together with their written opinion to the competent district court within 5 days of receipt.

7.2 Judicial Adjudication and Appellate Relief

Upon receiving the forwarded objection, the competent district court decides by a reasoned decision and, if it finds the objection well-founded, orders the deposit officer to take an appropriate disposition (Deposit Act Article 14, Paragraph 1). The objecting party may appeal against this decision under the Non-Contentious Case Procedure Act (same article, Paragraph 2).
