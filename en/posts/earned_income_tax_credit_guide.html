1. What Is Korea's EITC? — Purpose and Background

Korea's Earned Income Tax Credit (근로장려금, pronounced geunno jangryeogeum) and Child Tax Credit (자녀장려금) are refundable tax credit programs administered by the National Tax Service (NTS). They are designed to supplement the income of low-income working households, encourage labor-force participation, and ease the financial burden of child-rearing.

The EITC was first introduced in 2009 and the Child Tax Credit was added in 2015. Both programs use the prior calendar year's income as the basis for each annual determination. Eligibility criteria and payment amounts may change year to year as government policy evolves. This article provides general informational guidance as of June 2026. Actual eligibility and payment amounts vary by individual circumstances and must be verified directly with the NTS or Hometax.

Importantly, a household that qualifies for both programs can receive the EITC and Child Tax Credit simultaneously, as the two are independent benefits.

2. Eligibility — Household Type, Income, and Asset Requirements

2.1 Household Types

Both credits classify applicants into one of three household types, which determine the applicable income threshold.

  • Single-person household (단독가구): No spouse, no dependent children under 18, and no direct lineal ascendants aged 70 or older
  • Single-earner household (홑벌이 가구): Has a spouse, dependent children under 18, or direct lineal ascendants aged 70+. If a spouse is present, either the applicant or the spouse must have total wages below KRW 3 million per year
  • Dual-earner household (맞벌이 가구): Both the applicant and the spouse each have total wages of KRW 3 million or more per year

2.2 EITC Income Requirements

The combined household income (총소득) from all sources — wages, business income, religious worker income, interest, dividends, pensions, and miscellaneous income — must fall below the following annual thresholds:

Household TypeAnnual Income Limit
Single-personBelow KRW 22 million
Single-earnerBelow KRW 32 million
Dual-earnerBelow KRW 44 million

Note that specific rules differ slightly between wage earners and self-employed individuals. Always check the latest NTS guidelines for precise thresholds.

2.3 Child Tax Credit Income Requirements

The Child Tax Credit is available to single-earner and dual-earner households (not single-person) with at least one dependent child under age 18 (no age restriction for severely disabled children). The combined household income must be below KRW 70 million per year.

2.4 Asset Requirements

For both credits, the total value of assets held by all household members as of June 1 of the prior year must be below KRW 240 million. Included assets are: real property (housing, land, buildings), lease/jeonse deposits, financial assets (bank accounts, stocks, bonds), private passenger vehicles (excluding commercial vehicles), and other assets. Debts and liabilities are generally not deducted.

If total assets fall between KRW 170 million and KRW 240 million, only 50% of the calculated credit is paid.

2.5 Other Disqualifying Conditions

  • No Korean citizenship (exceptions apply for foreign nationals married to Korean citizens)
  • Claimed as a dependent child of another Korean resident
  • Income from specified professional businesses (lawyers, doctors, pharmacists, tax accountants, etc.)
  • Receiving National Basic Livelihood Benefits for one or more months during the application year (some restrictions)

3. Payment Amounts — How Much Can You Receive?

3.1 EITC Payment Structure

The EITC uses a three-stage income-based structure: a phase-in range (credit rises with income), a flat range (maximum credit maintained), and a phase-out range (credit decreases as income rises). Below are general maximum amounts for the 2026 filing year (based on 2025 income); exact figures are set by annual NTS notice.

Household TypeGeneral Maximum Annual Credit
Single-personUp to approx. KRW 1.65 million (~USD 1,200)
Single-earnerUp to approx. KRW 2.85 million (~USD 2,100)
Dual-earnerUp to approx. KRW 3.30 million (~USD 2,400)

Use the Hometax simulation tool (모의계산) to estimate your expected credit before applying. Actual amounts are finalized by NTS after review.

3.2 Child Tax Credit Payment

The Child Tax Credit pays between KRW 500,000 and KRW 1 million per dependent child. Lower-income households receive amounts closer to the maximum. For example, a household with two qualifying children could receive up to KRW 2 million. The 50% reduction rule for assets between KRW 170–240 million also applies.

4. How and When to Apply

4.1 Regular Application (정기신청)

The regular application period runs from May 1 to May 31 each year, covering the full prior year's income. All income types are eligible. Payment is typically issued in late September.

4.2 Semi-Annual Application (반기신청) — Wage Earners Only

Wage earners only can receive the credit in two installments:

  • First half of year: Apply September 1–15 → paid in late December
  • Second half of year: Apply March 1–15 → paid in late June

A final reconciliation is conducted during the next year's regular application. If more was paid in the semi-annual installments than the final annual calculation, the overpayment may be recovered. Self-employed individuals and religious workers must use the regular application only.

4.3 Application Channels

  • Hometax (hometax.go.kr): PC web — most common method
  • Sontax (mobile app): Smartphone application
  • ARS: Call 1544-9944 and follow voice prompts
  • Local Tax Office: In-person at your jurisdictional tax office

The NTS sends a notice letter with an individual authentication number (개별인증번호) to eligible households before the application period. Even without receiving such a letter, applicants who meet the requirements may apply directly.

4.4 Late Applications

Applications submitted after the regular deadline (May 31) but before November 30 of the same year are accepted as late applications. However, only 95% of the calculated credit is paid (a 5% penalty reduction).

5. Common Disqualifications and Mistakes

5.1 Income Exceeds the Threshold

Applicants are sometimes surprised to find their NTS-registered income is higher than expected. Part-time work after leaving a job, freelance income, and miscellaneous earnings all add up. Combined spouse income is also included. Before applying, check your income summary on Hometax to avoid surprises.

5.2 Asset Value Exceeds KRW 240 Million

Because debts are not deducted, a mortgaged property is counted at full value. Jeonse deposits, savings accounts, stocks, and vehicles are all included. The asset total can be higher than expected, resulting in a 50% reduction or full disqualification.

5.3 Household Composition Errors

If the household registry (주민등록) does not match the actual dependency relationship, or if a claimed dependent child does not meet the income limit (generally below KRW 1 million annual income), the application may be rejected. Verify household registration records before applying.

5.4 Professional Business Income

Individuals running a specified professional business (law, medicine, pharmacy, tax advisory, etc.) are ineligible for the EITC regardless of income level.

5.5 Filing an Objection

If you believe a disqualification or reduced payment is incorrect, you can file an objection within 90 days of receiving the notification. Submit via Hometax (online) or at your local tax office.

6. Comparison with US EITC — A Shared Philosophy

Korea's EITC was modeled on the US Earned Income Tax Credit, one of the most studied anti-poverty programs in the world.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Can platform workers (delivery drivers, gig workers) apply?

Yes, if they meet the income and asset requirements. Platform income may be classified as wage income or business income depending on the arrangement. The income type affects whether semi-annual or only regular application is available. Individual verification with NTS is advised.

Q. Is the credit taxable income?

No. The EITC and Child Tax Credit payments are not subject to income tax.

Q. What happens if I received a semi-annual payment but don't meet the annual requirements?

The final annual reconciliation through the regular application will determine the correct amount. If the semi-annual payments exceed the final calculated credit, the difference will be recovered (typically via offset against future tax obligations or direct recoupment).

Q. How do I simulate my expected credit?

Hometax offers a dedicated simulation tool under the Credit/Year-End Settlement menu. It provides an estimate based on income and household data but is for reference only — the final amount is determined by NTS review.
