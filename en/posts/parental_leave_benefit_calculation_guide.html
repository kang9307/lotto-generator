Korea Parental Leave Benefit - Calculating the Amount and the Both-Parents Bonus

How much lands, when it lands, and why 3+3 became 6+6 - as of August 2026

2026-08-07 Finance & Economy

Read this first - scope, date and limits

This is a Korea-only benefit, available to people enrolled in Korean employment insurance, and the figures below are current as of August 2026. Nothing here replaces the eligibility review or benefit decision made by a Korean employment center. The payment rates, the monthly caps and floor, and the requirements and amounts of the both-parents special provision have been revised frequently. Whether a particular person qualifies, and how much they actually receive, cannot be settled by this article and depends on the review by the employment center with jurisdiction. Before filing, check the current guidance directly at Employment Insurance (ei.go.kr) and Work24 (work24.go.kr). This article only organises the material you need in order to decide.

If you searched for 3+3, the scheme has a different name now

The phrase 3+3 still shows up in search results for the scheme that raises the benefit when both parents take leave. That label was attached when the enhanced window covered only the first three months, and it is not the current rule.

In statute the scheme is the special provision on parental leave benefit for a child within 18 months of birth, Article 95-3 of the Enforcement Decree of the Employment Insurance Act. Government guidance and press releases usually call it the 6+6 both-parents parental leave scheme. The rename happened for straightforward reasons.

  • The enhanced window grew from the first three months to the first six months
  • The child age test was settled at within 18 months of birth
  • The monthly cap now climbs in steps to a maximum of 4.5 million won
  • When the general parental leave cap rose in January 2025, the cap for the first two months of the special provision was lifted to 2.5 million won to match

So the numbers that belong in your calculation today are not the 3+3 numbers. Carrying over the old 2 million and 3 million won caps throws a six-month total off by millions of won. On this topic in particular, checking the date stamp before trusting a figure is not optional.

Working out your own amount - three tiers, not one rate

Parental leave benefit does not pay a single flat rate. The payment rate and the cap both change twice as the months accumulate. As of August 2026 the structure looks like this.

TierPayment rateMonthly capMonthly floor
Months 1-3100% of monthly ordinary wage2.5 million won700,000 won
Months 4-6100% of monthly ordinary wage2 million won700,000 won
Month 7 to the end80% of monthly ordinary wage1.6 million won700,000 won

Several points in this table are easy to misread.

  • A cap is a ceiling, not a payment. If your ordinary wage falls below the cap, your wage is the reference. A 2.5 million won cap in months 1 to 3 does not mean everyone receives 2.5 million won
  • The floor works the other way. Even where the ordinary wage falls short of 700,000 won, 700,000 won per month operates as the baseline
  • From month 7 the rate itself drops to 80%. It is not only the cap that falls; the multiplier in the calculation changes
  • Tiers are counted by actual months of leave used. Returning to work and taking leave again later does not reset the count; it accumulates
  • A month that starts or ends mid-month is prorated by day. If a partial month sits at either end, the figure that lands in your account will not match the table

Numbers make it concrete. Take someone whose ordinary wage is 3 million won per month and who uses 12 consecutive months. In months 1 to 3 the 100% figure of 3 million is cut to the 2.5 million cap, so 2.5 million per month. In months 4 to 6 the cap of 2 million binds, so 2 million per month. From month 7 to 12 the 80% figure of 2.4 million is cut to the 1.6 million cap, so 1.6 million per month. The total is 23.1 million won.

Worth noticing: an ordinary wage of 3 million won and one of 5 million won produce the same total, because the cap binds in every tier. The replacement rate falls steeply as pay rises, and that is precisely why the both-parents provision below matters so much for higher earners.

Why nobody waits for the 25% deferred payment any more

The older version of this benefit carried a mechanism called deferred payment. Each month, 25% of the calculated amount was held back, and it was paid as a lump sum only once the employee had returned to work and stayed for at least six months. During the leave itself, only 75% arrived.

That mechanism was abolished in the reform that took effect on 1 January 2025. The full calculated amount is now paid monthly during the leave. Two practical things change.

  • Cash flow moves forward. The money arriving during the income gap is 25% larger. That alone reshapes the household budget for the early months of leave
  • The benefit is decoupled from returning to work. Under the old rule, an employee who could not return never saw the withheld portion. The 25% no longer sits hostage to circumstances that may be outside anyone's control

What still needs checking is the boundary date, meaning which leave start dates the change applies to. If you have leave already in progress, or a withheld balance from an earlier period, that part belongs to your employment center rather than to a table. The figures in this article assume a newly starting leave.

Is the both-parents route better for us, or one parent taking a long stretch?

This is where the largest sums move. If both parents take leave before the child reaches 18 months, the first six months for each of them are paid at 100% of ordinary wage and the cap climbs in steps.

Now compare two arrangements that consume the same twelve months of household leave: one parent taking 12 consecutive months, versus both parents taking six months each under the special provision.

2 million won21.6 million won24 million won+2.4 million won

5 million won23.1 million won40 million won+16.9 million won

The lesson in this table is that the size of the gap depends entirely on pay level.

  • The lower the ordinary wage, the smaller the gain. A cap that climbs to 4.5 million won is irrelevant if the wage is 2 million; the wage is the limit and the staircase does nothing
  • The provision does its heaviest work when the wage sits at or above the cap line. Actually collecting the 4.5 million won of month 6 requires an ordinary wage of at least 4.5 million
  • When the two parents earn very differently, the choice between both using the enhanced window and the lower earner taking a longer stretch is genuinely open. That calculation has to include the household income gap, not only the benefit
  • One parent taking 12 months means one income missing for 12 months; six months each means two incomes missing for six months each. Equal totals, different shapes of pressure on the household
  • After the enhanced window, the remaining months revert to the general rule. If both parents use 12 months each, months 1 to 6 sit in the special provision and months 7 to 12 fall in the 1.6 million won tier
The amounts above are arithmetic illustrations using only the rates and caps. Real payments shift with proration, employer payments and work history. For your own case, the reliable route is a consultation with the employment center.

Three gates the both-parents provision has to clear

The table figures assume every condition is satisfied. In practice, three checkpoints do the filtering.

  1. Child age - at the point the leave starts, the child must be within 18 months of birth. This splits on a single day, so if the month count is ambiguous, confirm it in advance
  2. Both parents must use leave - both of them, though the periods do not have to overlap. Sequential use works, and periods that never coincide still satisfy the requirement
  3. Months used by each - the cap staircase is set by how many months each parent actually used. If one of them uses only three months, both are capped at the month-3 step

The third gate causes the most disappointment. The 4.5 million won cap is a month-6 figure that appears only when both parents complete six months. If one parent uses a single month and stops, both settle at the month-1 cap of 2.5 million won. Put plainly, how long your spouse takes changes what you receive.

Sequence matters too. The requirement is only complete once the second parent begins leave, so the enhanced portion for the parent who went first may be settled retroactively. Budgeting on the assumption that 4.5 million won lands in month one will not survive contact with reality. The actual payment timing and settlement method are questions for the employment center.

Single parents sit on a different cap line

Single-parent households have their own cap structure, separate from the both-parents track.

  • Months 1-3 - cap of 3 million won (higher than the general 2.5 million)
  • Months 4-6 - cap of 2 million won
  • Month 7 onward - cap of 1.6 million won

The early three months carry a cap 500,000 won higher. On top of that, in the 18-month extension covered below, single parents qualify without the condition that both parents use at least three months. Single-parent status has to be evidenced with documents, so if this applies, confirm what evidence is expected before filing.

What has to be true before you can use 18 months

Since 23 February 2025 the leave entitlement has been 18 months per parent, up from 12, for a household maximum of three years. Those extra six months are not automatic.

  • Both parents must have used at least three months of parental leave, or
  • the household must be a single-parent household, or
  • the parent must be the parent of a child with a severe disability

In other words, if a spouse takes less than three months, the extension never opens. It points the same direction as the special provision. The 2025 reform is designed around both parents participating, and that is the single idea running through all of it.

The number of separate stretches also rose, from two splits to three, which means the leave can be broken into four blocks. Note that benefit for the extended six months falls in the month 7 onward tier, so the 1.6 million won cap applies. A longer entitlement does not extend the higher early caps.

When you can file, and what you have to hand in

This is not money an employer arranges on your behalf. You have to claim it. Two lines bound the timing.

  • Opening line - you can file once one month has passed since the leave began. Day one of the leave is too early
  • Closing line - you must file within 12 months of the day the leave ends. Past that point, meeting every requirement changes nothing

Claims go through Work24 (work24.go.kr) online or the employment center covering your residence or workplace. The older employment insurance address (ei.go.kr) now routes to Work24. The usual paperwork looks like this.

  • Parental leave benefit application form
  • Parental leave confirmation form - completed by the employer, required only once at the start
  • Evidence of ordinary wage - payroll ledger, a copy of the employment contract and similar
  • Evidence of any money received from the employer during the leave

The step that stalls most often is the confirmation form. Without it the review does not move. Naming the person responsible for that document before the leave starts removes most of this risk.

If the parental leave starts within 18 months of the birth, there is a route to file maternity leave and parental leave together in a single combined application. If a maternity leave is coming up, it is worth checking whether that route applies to your case before filing twice.

There is also an entry threshold. The leave must be granted for 30 days or more (excluding any period overlapping with maternity leave), and before the leave begins the insured unit period under employment insurance must total 180 days or more. Those 180 days do not have to be with a single employer; they are counted cumulatively.

When the amount that lands is smaller than the calculation

Even after the arithmetic works out, payment can be restricted or reduced. Three grounds do most of the work.

  • Moving to a new employer - if you change employer during the leave, payment stops from that date
  • Taking up work - payment is restricted if you take up work during the leave. Work here means contracted hours of 15 hours or more per week, or income of 1.5 million won or more per month including self-employment income
  • Employer payments pushing past the wage - if money received from the employer plus the benefit exceeds the monthly ordinary wage, the excess is deducted from the benefit

The third applies where a company pays its own allowance or support during the leave. Company support is not itself a problem; only the portion above the ordinary wage gets adjusted. If your employer runs such a scheme, disclosing it at the filing stage is the cleaner path.

The work threshold is also widely misunderstood. The 1.5 million won line and the 15 hours line are separate tests. Low pay does not save you if the hours are 15 or more, and short hours do not save you if the income crosses the line. Freelance work and small self-employment income both count.

Stitching this together with paternity leave and reduced hours

Planning around parental leave alone leaves gaps at both ends. Two adjacent schemes fill them.

The entries that actually change a plan are these.

  • The benefit base caps for reduced working hours rose on 1 January 2026. The first 10 hours of weekly reduction moved from 2.2 million to 2.5 million won, and the remaining reduction from 1.5 million to 1.6 million won, aligning with the parental leave caps
  • Parental leave you do not use is doubled when converted into reduced working hours. That is the lever for households that want a long runway with a smaller income gap
  • The child age limit for reduced hours now reaches grade 6 of elementary school. Plans get abandoned on the assumption that it ends in the early grades, which is no longer the rule
  • Spouse maternity leave is 20 days, usable in up to four blocks within 120 days of the birth. That allows the days to be timed around the other parent's return to work rather than burned immediately
  • The three schemes are governed by separate requirements. Qualifying for one does not automatically qualify you for another

What happens if the money was wrongly received

This benefit comes out of employment insurance, and receiving it improperly does not end with paying it back. The sanctions come in three layers.

  • Repayment order - all or part of the benefit received can be ordered returned
  • Additional collection - up to twice the improperly received amount where the person acted alone, and up to five times where there was collusion with the employer
  • Criminal penalty - under the Employment Insurance Act, up to five years of imprisonment or a fine of up to 50 million won may also apply

The recurring patterns are familiar: leave that exists only on paper while the person keeps working, income earned during leave and never reported, and employers processing nominal leave to shave payroll costs. Claiming not to have known does not help once the facts are established.

Voluntary disclosure, however, opens a route to exemption from the additional collection. If you realise a payment was wrong, reporting it before it is discovered carries a far lighter burden. The default rule is to tell the employment center at the point income appears or the working arrangement changes.

Filing first and sorting it out later is a bad plan here. The correct order is to check the restriction grounds before filing. When the answer is not obvious, describing the facts to the Ministry of Employment and Labor customer center on 1350 is the reliable move.

What this article could not pin down

Some figures are absent because official guidance did not confirm them directly. If any of these apply to you, ask the employment center.

  • The reference point for calculating ordinary wage - which date's wage is used, and how far bonuses and allowances are included, varies with the individual pay structure
  • The boundary date for the deferred payment abolition - which leave start dates fall under full monthly payment, and how an older withheld balance is handled
  • Public officials and private school teaching staff and others outside employment insurance - separate schemes apply, so these figures do not transfer
  • Whether artists and labour providers covered by employment insurance special provisions qualify for this particular benefit
  • How long payment actually takes after filing, which depends on processing conditions
  • Timing and mechanics of settling the both-parents portion once the second parent begins leave
  • Additional local government subsidies, which exist in some regions on entirely different terms
  • How the extension requirement is evidenced - what documents establish that both parents used three months or more

These bear directly on amount and eligibility, so filling them with guesses breaks the plan. Confirm your own case through Work24 or on 1350.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Do both parents have to be on leave at the same time to get the bonus?

No. The requirement is that both parents use parental leave before the child turns 18 months, and whether the periods overlap is not tested. Sequential use qualifies, and periods that never coincide qualify. What the timing does affect is the staircase: the cap step is set by how many months each parent used, so if one takes a short stretch, both are capped at that step.

Q. If my ordinary wage is far above the cap, do I collect the full cap?

Where the rate-adjusted figure exceeds the cap, the cap becomes the reference. On an ordinary wage of 5 million won, 100% in months 1 to 3 is 5 million, but it is cut to the 2.5 million cap. The replacement rate falls as pay rises. The reverse also holds: where the wage is below the cap, the wage is the reference, so writing the cap into your budget as expected income will mislead you.

Q. Does splitting the leave reset the tier count?

Tiers count cumulative months used. Take three months, return to work, then take three more, and the second stretch lands in the months 4 to 6 tier. Splitting does not bring back the month-1 cap. The value of splitting lies in controlling return dates and care gaps, not in the benefit tiers.

Q. What if my employer will not complete the confirmation form?

Without it the review does not proceed. Parental leave itself is an entitlement an employer cannot refuse once the requirements are met, so where discussion fails, the next step is to raise the situation with the regional labor office or the Ministry of Employment and Labor customer center on 1350 and confirm the procedure. Assigning a document owner before the leave begins avoids most of these cases.

Q. Can we claim again on the same terms for a second child?

Parental leave is granted per child, so it can be used again for another child, and the benefit is available again where the insured unit period and other requirements are met afresh. How the tier count is reckoned for that child, and how the requirements are assessed, can differ case by case, so confirm with the employment center before filing. The both-parents provision likewise applies the 18-month line separately for each child.

Five things to settle before you file

The calculation itself is simple, but how a household divides the leave moves the total by tens of millions of won. In order:

  1. Which tier your ordinary wage lands in - whether the cap binds first or the wage does changes the whole calculation
  2. Whether the child is inside 18 months - this decides whether the both-parents route opens at all
  3. How many months your spouse can take - their months set your cap step, and the 18-month extension hangs on the same fact
  4. Whether income might appear during the leave - check the 15-hour and 1.5 million won lines in advance so repayment never becomes a question
  5. Filing window and paperwork - from one month after the start, within 12 months of the end, with a named owner for the confirmation form

The hardest two to undo are the third and the fifth. A spouse's months cannot be revisited once their leave has ended, and a missed filing deadline has no remedy. Fixing both at the planning stage is the safe order.
