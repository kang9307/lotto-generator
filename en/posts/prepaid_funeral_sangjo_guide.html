Korean Prepaid Funeral (Sangjo) Plans - Complete Guide to Sign-up, Cancellation, and Safeguards

What to check before you sign, how escrow protection works, and what you actually get back if you cancel

Jul 3, 2026 Practical

Introduction: Why You Should Scrutinize a Sangjo Plan Before Signing

A death in the family usually arrives without warning. In that moment, a prepaid funeral (sangjo) plan that promises to handle the entire process and cost sounds appealing. But a sangjo plan is a prepaid installment contract with a specific legal structure - the money you pay in monthly is not fully protected, and cancelling partway through often returns far less than expected. This guide, current as of July 2026, walks through how sangjo plans work, what to verify before signing, how refunds are calculated, bankruptcy risk, and alternatives, based on information published by Korea's Fair Trade Commission (FTC) and Korea Consumer Agency (KCA). It does not recommend any specific provider or product; if you are considering signing up, treat this as a starting point and re-verify against the latest official sources.

1. What Is a Sangjo (Prepaid Funeral) Plan?

1.1 Contract Structure and Purpose

A sangjo plan bundles funeral-related staff, a hearse and other vehicles, supplies (shroud, casket), and venue-related services into a fixed total price. You pay that price off in dozens of installments and receive the service when it is actually needed. Legally, this is not a financial product like a bank deposit or insurance policy - it is classified as a "prepaid installment transaction" under Korea's Installment Transactions Act, and it is regulated by the Fair Trade Commission, not financial supervisors. That distinction matters in practice: sangjo providers are not subject to the same continuous soundness supervision as banks or insurers.

1.2 Minimum Requirements for Providers

To register as a prepaid installment provider, a company must maintain at least KRW 1.5 billion in capital and escrow a set percentage of the prepayments it collects from consumers with an outside institution. This escrow obligation is the core safeguard behind sangjo plans, covered in detail below.

2. Before You Sign: Verify Registration and Escrow Status

2.1 Confirm the Provider Is Officially Registered

The first thing to do before signing up is confirm the provider is officially registered with the FTC as a prepaid installment business. Naesangjo Chajajwo (mysangjo.or.kr), run jointly by the FTC and Korea's sangjo mutual-aid associations, lets you search a company name to check registration status and operating condition. If you already hold a plan, identity verification on the site also shows how much you have paid in and which institution holds your escrowed funds.

2.2 Escrow Ratio and Escrow Institutions

Under Korea's installment transaction rules, a prepaid installment provider must escrow at least 50% of the prepayments it receives with a consumer-protection insurance body such as a bank or a mutual-aid association (Korea Sangjo Mutual Aid Association, Sangjo Guarantee Mutual Aid Association, and others). Reports indicate that by the first half of 2026, total prepayments across the sangjo industry had passed KRW 11 trillion with over 10 million subscribers, and the average escrow ratio was reported in the low-50% range. In other words, if a provider suddenly shuts down, you are likely to recover only the escrowed portion (roughly half) rather than everything you paid in - so checking the escrow ratio and institution before signing should be routine.

2.3 A Note on Capital and Financial Health

Sangjo providers are not required to continuously disclose financial soundness the way regulated financial firms are. Still, basic financial disclosures published periodically by the FTC and available through Naesangjo Chajajwo are worth checking before you contract.

3. Service Scope and Add-On Costs

3.1 What a Standard Contract Includes

A typical sangjo plan includes staff support such as funeral directors, a hearse, funeral supplies (shroud, casket), and services related to setting up the memorial hall. However, the included items and grade (for example, the material of the shroud or casket) vary widely by product, so you should confirm in writing exactly which items and services are included.

3.2 Where Extra Costs Show Up

In practice, extra costs often arise from items not covered by the contract - upgrading to a premium shroud or casket, extra staff, catering, or venue rental fees. There have also been reported cases of consumers being lured into signing with promises of free high-value gifts, only to be billed millions of won in additional charges later. Before contracting, ask the sales representative specific questions about total cost and where add-on charges can occur, and keep the answers in writing if possible.

4. Cancellation and Refunds: When and How Much You Get Back

4.1 Cooling-Off Period - Within 14 Days

Within 14 days of receiving the contract or terms, you can withdraw your application without any specific reason. Cancelling within this window means no penalty and a full refund of what you have paid, and the company must refund it within a set period after receiving your withdrawal notice. Rather than rushing to sign, using this cooling-off window to compare other products is a reasonable approach.

4.2 How Mid-Contract Cancellation Refunds Are Calculated

Once the cooling-off period ends, cancellation works differently. A large share of early payments is often already spent on sales commissions and other operating costs, so cancelling early in the contract typically returns much less than the total paid in. The FTC sets minimum refund standards through its official notice on calculating refunds for terminated prepaid installment contracts, and your contract will also specify a penalty clause. Because the exact refund amount varies by product and how long you have paid, the most accurate approach if you are considering cancellation is to use the estimated refund calculator on Naesangjo Chajajwo and cross-check it against the penalty clause in your original contract.

4.3 Common Downsides of Cancelling

Beyond a smaller refund, cancelling after paying for a long time also means losing your accumulated payment record - if you sign up for a similar plan later, you typically start from scratch. Before deciding to cancel, it is also worth comparing whether a membership transfer or succession program to another provider might be more favorable than cancelling outright.

5. Bankruptcy and M&A Risk - What to Do

5.1 If Your Sangjo Company Shuts Down

If a provider that properly escrowed its prepayments goes bankrupt or has its registration revoked, consumers can claim the escrowed amount (at least 50% of prepayments) from the escrow institution (a bank or mutual-aid association). Because the escrow ratio is not 100%, full recovery is not guaranteed, and the process and timeline can vary by case.

5.2 What to Check During a Merger or Transfer

Sangjo providers are sometimes acquired by another company, or member contracts are transferred. In that case, you must confirm whether your existing payments and service terms are honored identically by the new provider, and whether the contract terms have changed unfavorably. Official succession programs can sometimes let you carry over your existing payments to a reputable participating provider's service, so the first step is confirming whether the process is official.

5.3 Watch for Illegal Solicitation Exploiting Bankruptcy News

The Korea Consumer Agency and FTC have repeatedly warned about illegal solicitation that improperly obtains member lists from defunct sangjo companies and misrepresents itself as an official successor to push re-enrollment. Contracts signed this way often lack proper escrow protection, creating a high risk of secondary harm. If you receive an unfamiliar solicitation call, verify through Naesangjo Chajajwo or the FTC/KCA whether it is a legitimate official process before responding.

6. Comparing Pay-As-You-Go Plans and Booking a Funeral Home Directly

6.1 Pay-As-You-Go (Postpaid) Funeral Services

Postpaid funeral services require no advance monthly payments; you pay only for the services actually used when a funeral occurs. Because no prepayment accumulates, there is no risk of losing prepaid money if the company fails - often cited as the biggest advantage. However, because the contract is simpler upfront, consumers may be more exposed to upselling for premium items or extra staff at the time of service. Getting quotes from two or three providers under identical conditions and comparing line-item pricing is a practical way to guard against this.

6.2 Booking a Funeral Home Directly

Another option is skipping a sangjo plan entirely and booking a funeral home directly. Much of the staff, vehicles, and supplies a sangjo provider offers can also be sourced individually through a funeral home. That said, funeral homes are fundamentally in the venue-rental business, so you may not get the same attentive guidance and hand-holding through the process that a sangjo provider offers.

6.3 Which Should You Choose?

There is no single right answer. If you want to avoid the burden of long-term installment payments and the risk of a provider's bankruptcy, postpaid plans or booking a funeral home directly can be good alternatives. If you prefer to lock in a defined product and service level in advance, a prepaid sangjo plan may suit you better. What matters most is comparing total cost, service scope, and risk across all three options on the same basis and choosing what fits your situation.

7. Consumer Protection Checklist

  • Before signing, check registration status, operating condition, and the escrow institution on Naesangjo Chajajwo (mysangjo.or.kr).
  • Get included items and grades specified in writing, and be especially cautious of sign-up offers built around free gifts or bonuses.
  • Remember you can withdraw penalty-free within 14 days of receiving the contract or terms.
  • Before cancelling, check the estimated refund amount using the official refund calculator.
  • If you receive an unfamiliar call urging re-enrollment, verify it is an official succession process through Naesangjo Chajajwo or the FTC/KCA first.
  • Where possible, compare postpaid plans and direct funeral-home booking alongside cost and terms.

Conclusion: Verify First, Don't Rush

Used well, a sangjo plan can ease the burden of an unexpected moment. But because of its prepaid installment structure, prepayments are not fully escrowed, mid-contract cancellation often returns less than what you paid in, and provider bankruptcy remains a real risk. If you are considering signing up, start by checking registration and escrow status, read the included items and penalty clauses in the contract carefully, and compare against alternatives like postpaid plans or direct funeral-home booking. The information in this guide reflects general conditions as of July 2026; rules and figures can change over time, so re-verify against the latest official sources immediately before signing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is a Korean sangjo (prepaid funeral) plan?

A prepaid installment contract for funeral-related staff, vehicles, and supplies: you agree to a total price, pay it off in installments, and receive the service when needed. It is not a financial product but a transaction regulated by the Fair Trade Commission.

Q2. What should I check first before signing up?

Search the provider on Naesangjo Chajajwo (mysangjo.or.kr) to confirm official registration and which institution holds its escrowed prepayments.

Q3. If I cancel partway through, do I get all my money back?

Usually not - a large share of early payments is often already spent on operating costs, so early cancellation typically returns less. Within 14 days of the contract, you can withdraw penalty-free.

Q4. What happens to my money if the sangjo company goes bankrupt?

You can claim the escrowed amount (at least 50% of prepayments) from the escrow institution, though full recovery is not guaranteed. Also watch for illegal solicitation exploiting defunct companies' member data.

Q5. How does this compare to postpaid plans or booking a funeral home directly?

Postpaid plans carry no prepayment loss risk but watch for on-site upselling; direct funeral-home booking lets you source similar items individually. Compare upfront burden, total cost, and service quality.
