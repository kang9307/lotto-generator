How to Use Korea's National Pension Service (NPS)

From estimated pension lookups to certificates and back-payment applications - what works without a branch visit, as of August 2026

2026-08-09 IT & Technology

Read This First - Scope and Limits of This Guide

This article describes the situation as of August 2026 and is not a substitute for official NPS guidance or its case-by-case decisions. It is written for people enrolled in Korea's National Pension system - Korean residents and foreign workers covered by it - not for readers with no connection to the Korean scheme. The menus and features of the NPS website and mobile app are reorganized from time to time, so names given here may not match exactly what you see. Also, any estimated pension figure is a projection; the amount actually paid is calculated by the NPS when you claim. Individual eligibility and amounts can only be confirmed through the NPS (nps.or.kr) and its call center at 1355 (from within Korea).

Almost everyone earning income in Korea is connected to the national pension in some way, yet the moment you actually need something from the National Pension Service - an enrollment certificate for a job change or a loan, a look at how much pension you can expect, a decision about paying back-contributions - the first hurdle is figuring out which counter handles it. The short answer: most lookups, certificates, and applications no longer require a branch visit. This article is not about the pension system itself - eligibility rules, contribution rates, claiming ages - but about how to use the agency. For the system itself, see the companion piece Korea National Pension Complete Guide - Enrollment, Contributions, and Claiming.

There Are Four Channels - What Each One Does, in One Table

There are four main ways to interact with the NPS, and they have distinctly different jobs. Choosing the right first channel for your task is what saves you queues and repeat trips.

One principle covers most of it: looking at your own record and printing documents is online work by default; anything involving another person or original paperwork is counter work by default. With that principle in mind, the detailed lists below are mostly for confirmation.

What You Can Finish on the Website Without Visiting a Branch

The e-civil service > individual section of the website is home base for personal users. As of August 2026 it lists roughly twenty lookup services, over ten certificate types, and some thirty report-and-application services. Listing them all would bury the useful ones, so here is the shortlist that covers most real-life needs.

  • Enrollment history lookup - since when and how much you have paid, with total months and amounts on one screen
  • Estimated pension amount lookup - a projection of your old-age pension based on your actual contribution record
  • Unclaimed pension check - a menu for finding benefits you are entitled to but have not claimed
  • Certificate issuance - enrollment certificates, benefit payment certificates, payment confirmations, issued on the spot
  • Back-payment (chunap) and re-payment applications - from the application itself through to getting a virtual account for payment
  • Voluntary enrollment and voluntarily-continued enrollment - joining without income, or extending enrollment past age 60, including withdrawal
  • Recipient bank account changes and work-income reports - the routine notifications that arise while receiving a pension
  • Consultation reservation - if a branch visit is unavoidable, book it before you go
The e-civil service menu also carries items such as unemployment credit applications, contribution exemption requests and resumption reports, and overpayment lookups. Because the list itself gets reorganized, the fastest habit when you wonder whether something can be done online is to search the full e-civil service menu first - it beats waiting at a counter.

Estimated Pension - The Logged-In Lookup and the Simulator Are Different Things

The single most searched-for NPS service is the estimated pension. The catch is that the website has several similarly named menus, and the meaning of the number you get depends on which one you used.

  • Estimated pension amount lookup (login required) - calculated from your actual enrollment record after identity verification, on the assumption that your current income continues to age 60
  • Pension simulation and quick calculators (no login) - you type in income and enrollment years yourself; instantly usable, but disconnected from your real record
  • Deferred pension monthly amount lookup - a separate menu showing what deferring your claim would pay per month
  • Disability and survivor pension estimates - looked up on a separate track from the old-age pension
  • The same lookups exist in the mobile app, so if phone-based verification is easier for you, app login and then the pension menu is the fastest route

The one thing not to do with these numbers is read them as a promise. An estimated pension rests on the assumption that future income, prices, and the law stay as they are today, and the actual amount is determined by the NPS at claiming time. It is a planning compass, not a guaranteed deposit.

Certificates - Pick the Right Type First and You Will Not Get Lost

When people get stuck on certificate issuance, it is rarely the process - it is the type. Whoever is asking for the document (an employer, a bank, a government office, a foreign institution) has a specific certificate in mind; match their wording to the NPS menu name first, and the rest is a few clicks. Here are the frequently used ones by purpose.

A few practical points worth knowing before you start clicking.

  • The enrollment certificate and the detailed history statement support English issuance - if the document is for a visa or a foreign pension authority, issue the English version from the start
  • Depending on the certificate, you may be able to choose between printing, an electronic document, or fax delivery - no printer does not mean no certificate
  • If the receiving party doubts a document, the e-civil service section has a certificate authenticity check menu for verification
  • The Gov24 portal (gov.kr) also connects to NPS enrollment certificate issuance - a useful detour when the NPS site is under maintenance
  • Proxy issuance may not work online because delegation has to be verified - in that case confirm the required documents with a branch first

The Mobile App - Where It Differs From the Website

The official NPS app (its Korean name translates roughly as NPS by My Side) is available on Google Play and the App Store, and it carries a large share of the website's individual services. By the agency's own description it handles around a hundred civil services, around the clock, without a visit.

  • Lookups - enrollment history, estimated pension, and paid or unpaid contribution records, right after login
  • Certificates - the main certificates, including the enrollment certificate and benefit payment certificate, can be issued in the app
  • Reports and applications - frequently used items such as bank account changes and consultation reservations are included
  • Login - besides simple authentication and certificates, you can register fingerprint or face recognition to make later access quick
  • Asked which is better, the honest answer is: the app is faster for lookups and certificates; a PC is more comfortable for long application forms
  • Install the app only by searching the official app stores. Installation via a link received by text message is not a path that exists in any legitimate scenario

Authentication Is Where Most People Get Stuck

Because these services expose a pile of personal data, every personal lookup, certificate, and application sits behind identity verification. In practice, failed attempts almost always fail here, not at the menus. As of August 2026 the supported methods and the common sticking points look like this.

  • Simple authentication - login through private certificate providers such as Naver, Kakao, and PASS; if you have a phone in your own name, this is the fastest route for most people
  • Joint certificate and financial certificate - the traditional methods; the fallback when simple authentication is unavailable and the default for some tasks
  • If your phone is not under your own name - corporate phones, family-registered phones - simple authentication cannot even be issued; prepare a joint or financial certificate instead
  • Right after a legal name change or resident registration correction, certificate data and NPS records can disagree and verification fails; reissue the certificate so the details match, then retry
  • When verification keeps failing, switching to a different authentication method is faster than hammering the same one; if nothing works, call 1355
  • The roster of simple-authentication providers can change - the list shown on the login screen is the authoritative one

Back Payments, Re-Payments, Voluntary Enrollment - Applications That Go Through Online

Even the heavyweight applications that feel like they must need a branch consultation are, in many cases, accepted through the e-civil service section. Whether these moves are financially worthwhile is a question for the system guide; here we only cover where the procedure happens.

  • Back-payment (chunap) application - if you have exempted or excluded periods and are currently paying contributions, the application can be filed online; the same service is also linked from the Gov24 portal
  • Re-payment (repayment of a past refund) application - for people who once took a lump-sum refund and want to restore that period; also in the e-civil service menu
  • Payment runs through the internet payment service (anypay.nps.or.kr) - virtual account issuance, electronic billing, and automatic transfer enrollment are all handled online
  • Voluntary enrollment and withdrawal, voluntarily-continued enrollment after 60 - the applications, including standard monthly income changes, have online menus
  • Unemployment credits and regional subscriber contribution support - the applications that lighten the contribution burden are also accepted online
That an application can be filed online and that the choice is good for you are two different statements. Back payments in particular cut both ways depending on the period and amount, so before pressing the submit button, look at how your estimated pension would change, or run the decision past 1355 or a branch consultation.

1355 and the Branch Counter - What Online Cannot Do

Phone consultation is at 1355, no area code, from within Korea. It is not toll-free - normal call charges apply - and from abroad you dial +82-63-713-6900. Hours are weekdays 09:00 to 18:00, and beyond voice there are chat consultations, a chatbot, and sign-language video consultation channels.

  • What 1355 handles - general guidance on the system, how procedures apply to your situation, which forms and documents you need, and which branch is nearest
  • Verifying suspected impersonation - 1355 is also the default channel for checking whether a text or call in the NPS name is genuine
  • Branch-by-default tasks - proxy applications requiring original delegation documents, and disability or survivor pension claims with tangled paperwork
  • Lump-sum refund claims by foreign members - for members leaving Korea, the claim is made at a branch before departure as the standard route
  • When online verification is impossible - if you cannot obtain any authentication method, walking into a branch with ID is ultimately the fastest fix
  • Reserve before visiting - book via the consultation reservation menu or 1355 and confirm the documents to bring; it reliably prevents a second trip

How to Tell Real NPS Messages From Impersonation Scams

Pensions attract impersonation fraud like few other topics. Smishing texts with titles like benefit eligibility notice or refund payment guidance, urging you to tap a link, have been repeatedly reported as NPS impersonation patterns. Based on the agency's official guidance, the dividing lines are clear.

  • The NPS does not send refund-collection links by text. Any message dangling a refund or payout behind a link is suspect on its face
  • The NPS does not distribute its app through text-message links. Searching the official app store yourself is the only legitimate installation path
  • The NPS does not ask you to read out your resident registration number or bank account number over the phone. Demands for transfers or messenger-app payments mean hang up immediately
  • If in doubt, do not use any number the message gave you - dial 1355 yourself, and type nps.or.kr into the address bar yourself
  • If you already tapped the link or gave out information, contact the police (112 in Korea) and your bank at once and run a malware scan on the phone
  • The e-civil service menu includes a block-remote-account-change safety service, which shuts down the non-face-to-face route for changing the receiving account - worth considering for elderly recipients in the family

For Foreign Members - Lump-Sum Refunds and Social Security Agreements

Foreign workers in Korea are enrolled in the national pension when the requirements are met, and they use the same channels described above. What is different is the variable of leaving the country, which adds items worth tracking separately.

  • Whether a foreign national is covered depends on visa status, reciprocity with the home country, and social security agreements - two people with the same nationality can land on different answers if their visa types differ
  • Lump-sum refund - members from eligible countries can reclaim their paid contributions as a lump sum when returning home; the claim is filed at a branch before departure as the standard route
  • An airport payment service at Incheon Airport, paying the refund in foreign currency cash on the day of departure, has been operated; confirm current conditions with the NPS when you file
  • If your country has a social security agreement with Korea, totalization of enrollment periods and other terms can change which route is favorable; the terms differ by country, so rely on the NPS country-specific guidance
  • The website's e-civil service section includes a foreign pension eligibility self-check menu - a reasonable starting point for mapping how Korean and home-country pensions might connect

What This Article Could Not Confirm

These are the points deliberately left open rather than asserted - either the current official value could not be confirmed, or the item changes too often to pin down.

  • The complete roster of simple-authentication providers - Naver, Kakao, and PASS are confirmed; anything beyond that, and changes to the lineup, must be checked on the login screen
  • A complete feature-difference list between the website and the app - the number and scope of services shifts with each reorganization
  • Details of branch visit reservations - the reservation menu exists, but bookable time slots and the range of covered tasks need to be checked in the official guidance
  • Operating hours of the chat and chatbot consultations - whether they match phone hours was not confirmed
  • The country-by-country requirements for foreign members' lump-sum refunds and the current operating conditions of the airport payment service
  • Which certificates allow proxy issuance and with what documents - delegation requirements differ by task and could not be generalized

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Where do I check my estimated pension?

Use the estimated pension amount lookup in the website's e-civil service section after identity verification, or log in to the official app. The no-login simulators take income and years you type in yourself, so they are disconnected from your actual record. Either way the figure is an estimate; the actual amount is determined by the NPS at claiming time.

Q. I need an enrollment certificate right now - do I have to go to a branch?

No. The enrollment certificate is issued instantly on the website and in the app, in both Korean and English. Payment confirmations and benefit payment certificates are online too. Some certificates offer electronic-document or fax delivery when you have no printer. Only proxy issuance, which requires delegation checks, points you to a branch and its document list.

Q. Can I use the website without a joint certificate?

Yes. Simple authentication via providers such as Naver, Kakao, and PASS, plus the financial certificate, are supported, so a phone in your own name covers most tasks. If the phone is not in your name, simple authentication is blocked - prepare a joint or financial certificate. The provider lineup can change; the login screen's list is authoritative.

Q. I got a text in the NPS name - how do I check whether it is real?

Do not tap the link first. The NPS does not send refund links or app-installation links by text, and does not ask for resident registration or account numbers by phone. Verify through 1355 dialed yourself - never a number from the message - or type nps.or.kr into the browser directly.

Q. Which tasks truly require a branch visit?

Tasks needing original delegation documents such as proxy applications, document-heavy disability and survivor pension claims, lump-sum refund claims by departing foreign members, and any application where online verification is impossible for you. Book a consultation via the e-civil service reservation menu or 1355 and confirm the documents beforehand to avoid a second trip.

Wrapping Up - Which Channel First, by Situation

Using the NPS well comes down to the first channel you pick. Ordered by how often the situations come up.

  1. You wonder how much you have paid and will receive - log in to the app or website, open enrollment history and the estimated pension lookup; five minutes, done
  2. An employer or bank demands a document - match their wording to the NPS certificate name, then issue it on the website or in the app
  3. You are weighing back payments or voluntary enrollment - settle the cost-benefit question first, and once decided, carry the application and payment through the e-civil service section
  4. You are unsure whether a rule applies to you - ask 1355 first (weekdays 09:00-18:00, paid call) and confirm the documents before moving
  5. Paperwork is tangled or a proxy is involved - book a consultation reservation and go to the branch; it is reliably faster than walking in cold
  6. You received a text or call in the NPS name - touch nothing, and verify through 1355 first
The menu names, supported authentication methods, and operating hours in this article are as of August 2026 and may be reorganized later. Estimated pension results are projections only; actual amounts and eligibility are decided by the NPS through its own calculation and review. Decisions with real financial consequences - back payments, voluntary enrollment - should be finalized through NPS consultation (1355 within Korea), not this article. Impersonation scams using the NPS name cause real losses: never tap links of unclear origin, and verify through official channels only.
