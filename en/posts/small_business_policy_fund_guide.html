One Phrase, Two Completely Different Things

Separate the money you receive from the money you repay, and the order in which to read an announcement settles itself

August 31, 2026 Finance & Economy
Scope and standing
Everything below describes South Korean government programs and applies to businesses registered in Korea. It is general information about how those programs are structured, not financial or legal advice, and it does not recommend any particular product or lender. The structure described is as of 2026. Individual eligibility and terms are decided by each announcement and by the administering agency, and the figures change every year and from one announcement to the next.

Start by splitting the phrase

Among people running small shops in Korea, the phrase a policy fund has opened up circulates in almost the same tone every time. There is money from the government, the terms are good, somebody down the street already got theirs. One thing is reliably missing from that conversation: whether the money has to be paid back.

A policy fund is not one program. It is dozens of programs run by different ministries and agencies under different statutes, gathered by ordinary speech under a single label. Inside that label sit grants that carry no repayment obligation, interest subsidies that cover part of the interest on a commercial loan, and, forming the bulk of it, loans. A loan is debt. The rate is lower and the term longer by design, but the obligation to repay principal and interest is no different from a bank product.

That is why sequence matters. Before choosing which program to pursue, establish whether the one in front of you gives money or lends it. That single split changes how the announcement reads. For a grant you look for what must be proven to receive it; for a loan you look first at when and how it comes back out. What follows lays out that sorting process in the order the system itself is built.

Money you repay and money you keep

The names carry the answer. The service registered on the Gov.kr portal is called small business policy fund loan support, with Articles 9 and 21 of the Act on the Protection of and Support for Micro Enterprises listed as its legal basis. The annual document from the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency is likewise titled a policy fund loan plan announcement. Both names contain the word loan.

Programs with no repayment obligation use different words: grant, subsidy, voucher. Those generally come with restrictions on how the money is spent and an obligation to reconcile it afterward, and spending it outside the stated purpose makes it recoverable. Receiving the money is not the end of the process but the start of an evidence and settlement obligation.

DimensionPolicy loanGrant or subsidy
Nature of the moneyBorrowed. Principal and interest are repaidDisbursed. Not repaid as a rule
Clue in the titleLoan plan, loan program, lending, refinancingSupport program, subsidy program, voucher
Obligation that remainsA repayment schedule, credit consequences if missedSpending evidence, settlement, clawback for misuse
What the review weighsCapacity to repay and credit standingSoundness of the plan and eligibility
When it goes wrongDelinquency, acceleration of the debtRecovery, penalty surcharge, exclusion from future rounds

A third form is worth recognizing. In an interest subsidy arrangement you borrow from a financial institution while the government covers part of the interest. The money is still borrowed; only the interest burden falls. When an announcement lists both lending and interest subsidy amounts, its budget is split across those two mechanisms.

Which agency handles what

The same label covers programs with different administrators, different target groups and different terms. This is where following somebody else's account goes wrong. The fund the shop next door received may not apply to yours at all.

  • Ministry of SMEs and Startups — the policy owner. It publishes the small business policy fund loan announcement under its own name. The 2026 program was published as Ministry Announcement No. 2025-656 on December 29, 2025, handled by the division responsible for small business management stabilization policy.
  • Korea Small Business and Market Promotion Agency — the executing body for small business funds. Online intake runs through its policy fund site, and in-person intake runs through regional centers nationwide.
  • Korea SMEs and Startups Agency — handles policy funds aimed at small and medium enterprises. Its target is an SME under Article 2 of the Framework Act on Small and Medium Enterprises, which is a different size tier.
  • Local governments and regional credit guarantee foundations — run region-level interest subsidy programs and guarantees. Separate announcements appear region by region even in the same period.
  • Bizinfo — the integrated support program portal operated by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups. It groups announcements into fields such as finance, technology, workforce, exports, domestic sales, startups and management. Lending, guarantees and interest subsidies all sit inside the finance field.

In practice it saves time to settle first whether you fall in the small business tier or the SME tier, then read only that agency's announcements. Reading across both tiers at once blurs conditions that are not comparable.

Eligibility turns on industry, size and years in business

The Korean term for a small business owner has a statutory definition. Article 2 of the Framework Act on Small and Micro Enterprises sets it as a small enterprise under Article 2(2) of the Framework Act on Small and Medium Enterprises that has fewer than 10 regular employees and meets an industry threshold. That threshold appears in Article 3(1) of the Enforcement Decree.

  • Mining, manufacturing, construction and transportation — fewer than 10 regular employees engaged in the main business
  • All other industries — fewer than 5 regular employees
  • The revenue condition follows the industry table in Schedule 3 of the Enforcement Decree of the Framework Act on Small and Medium Enterprises
  • When counting regular employees, executives and daily workers, people hired for a fixed term of three months or less, dedicated researchers in a corporate research institute, and part-time workers under 60 hours a month are excluded (Article 3(3) of the same Decree)
  • A business that grows past the threshold is still treated as a small business for three years starting the year after that occurs

The condition most often overlooked is the excluded industry list. Meeting every size requirement does not help if the industry itself is off the table. The exclusion notice on the small business policy fund site is maintained against Korean Standard Industrial Classification codes and broadly covers these categories.

  • Gambling and speculative amusement equipment manufacturing, wholesale, retail and rental, and the operation of gambling facilities
  • General and dance entertainment drinking establishments and similar adult entertainment venues
  • Financial services, insurance and pension services, and services auxiliary to finance and insurance
  • A substantial part of real estate services
  • Professional services such as legal, accounting and tax services, veterinary practice, customs brokerage and appraisal
  • Tobacco-related wholesale, and fortune telling and similar services

The same notice also carries exceptions. Golf practice ranges and screen golf ranges are listed as eligible, and property management, some real estate brokerage, shared office operations and massage facilities operated by visually impaired proprietors are handled as exceptions. Disaster-related funds add their own carve-outs, such as entertainment venues located in designated tourism zones. Falling inside a broad excluded category is not automatically the end, and sitting outside one is not automatic safety. The thing to check against is the industry code on your business registration, not the category name.

Requirements on years in business vary by fund. Some carry none, others require under three years or under seven, and some add an age condition. Meeting a requirement means you become eligible to apply, not that support is settled. A review follows.

Applications run on announcements, not open counters

There is rarely a permanently open window. A budget is allocated, an announcement is published, an intake period opens, and when the money runs out it closes. The intake labels on the small business policy fund application screen show this directly. As of an August 2026 check, several funds carried a closing condition reading until the budget is exhausted.

The meaning is unambiguous. Even with calendar dates remaining, the round ends the day the budget does. A few funds, such as the refinancing loan and the specialized fund for micro manufacturers, were marked as continuously open, but that is a per-fund exception rather than a property of policy funds generally. Either way, it has to be re-checked in the current announcement.

Three practical consequences follow.

  1. Whoever is already prepared has the advantage. Starting to collect documents after seeing the announcement can be too late, because some of them take days to issue.
  2. Miss it and you wait. The next announcement may carry a different budget and different terms. Fund names change and target groups narrow.
  3. Things cluster early in the year. Budgets follow the fiscal year, so many intakes open in January. Applications for the 2026 small business policy fund program began on January 5.

Which makes starting to look into this only after money gets tight the worst possible sequence. Meeting the requirements while there is still slack, then applying when the need arrives, is the order the system is shaped for.

What the paperwork is actually proving

Memorizing a document list is pointless. It differs by fund and changes by year. Knowing what the documents are trying to establish, on the other hand, tells you what to prepare for any announcement. The proof requested falls into four groups.

  • That the business exists — that it is a registered business actually trading right now. Registration details and evidence of a place of business sit here.
  • Size and industry — whether the size thresholds are met. Employee count, industry code and revenue scale carry this.
  • Tax and public charge standing — whether national and local taxes are clear of arrears. Here the state itself is the requirement, before any document.
  • Use of the money — what facility spending covers, or what operations working capital funds. Depending on the fund type, a plan or quotations attach here.

Individual funds layer their own proofs on top. The fund for businesses run by people with disabilities asks for a welfare card or a certification, disaster-related emergency funds ask for a disaster certificate issued by a local government, and the fund for credit-constrained proprietors requires completing designated training beforehand. The eligibility sentence often contains something you have to obtain in advance, so reading the eligibility clause before the document list is the more useful order.

Dressing up the paperwork is not on the table. Making revenue or tax records say something other than what happened is not a way to pass a review, it is grounds for sanction. The lending restrictions published by the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency explicitly list businesses that applied by false or improper means, or used funds outside their stated purpose, within the past three years. A false application can lead to recovery of the money, exclusion from future programs and criminal exposure.

What is already decided before the review

A policy fund review has a part where people exercise judgment and a part that screens you out before judgment begins. The second part is not something a well-written application gets past. There is one piece of good news attached: you can check that part yourself before applying.

The restricted-borrower list published by the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency shows the axes clearly. As of an August 2026 check, its guidance enumerated the following.

  • Businesses suspended or closed
  • Businesses in arrears on taxes
  • Businesses with adverse credit information registered — delinquency, subrogation or substitute payment, default, related-party status, disturbance of financial order, rehabilitation or bankruptcy
  • Businesses whose main industry falls in the excluded list
  • Businesses that applied falsely or improperly, or used funds outside their purpose, within the past three years
  • Distressed and marginal businesses — full impairment of equity after two consecutive loss years, an interest coverage ratio below 1.0 for three consecutive years, and similar tests
  • Businesses exceeding the industry-specific debt ratio ceiling, with stated exceptions
  • Businesses involved in serious social harm, such as serious accident law violations, habitual wage arrears or embezzlement by officers
  • Sound businesses with ready access to private finance — listed companies, holders of a rating above a threshold, and those exceeding equity or asset thresholds

That last item captures the nature of the program well. It exists to fill a gap for those who cannot readily reach private finance, not to hand favorable terms to anyone who wants them. Being too solid also puts you outside it.

Announcements for small business funds carry exclusions of the same character. The exact wording differs by program, so the attachment to the relevant announcement is what governs, but arrears, delinquency and dormancy are the three axes worth checking first in any program. Whether national and local taxes are settled, and whether anything is registered against you in credit information, are best established before writing the application. Caught here, no amount of document preparation moves that round forward.

Interest and repayment structure

A policy fund rate is not a fixed number but a formula. The Korea SMEs and Startups Agency states that it applies a policy fund base rate, adjusted by fund type, credit risk grade, collateral type and preferential conditions. The base rate moves quarterly. The same fund therefore prices differently in a different quarter, and differently for different applicants in the same quarter.

Small business funds work the same way. As of an August 2026 check, the guidance screen from the Korea Small Business and Market Promotion Agency showed each fund's rate as a base rate of 3.85% plus a fund-specific spread. The table below reproduces what was shown at that moment, and it does not mean those are the current values. When the base rate moves, every line moves with it.

What to take from the table is not the values but three facts. First, rates inside the same program span more than a factor of two. Second, some funds are fixed and others move every quarter. Third, weaker credit prices higher. A policy fund is not uniformly cheap.

What shapes the burden more than the rate is the repayment structure. These loans generally include a deferment period during which only interest is paid. The monthly figure looks small, but the principal does not shrink by a single won. The moment deferment ends, the entire principal is compressed into the remaining term and the payment jumps. This is the classic case where a low rate does not mean a low burden.

  • A longer deferment lowers the early burden and raises both the post-deferment monthly payment and the total interest
  • For floating-rate funds, the test is the monthly payment if rates rise, not the payment at today's rate
  • Refinancing lowers a rate; it does not remove a debt. Look at how the total repaid and the term change
  • Lending routed through a financial institution can bring separate collateral or guarantee requirements. The procedure differs from direct lending
  • The basis for a decision is the monthly figure on an amortization schedule, not a single rate

Anyone who promises approval

You can file the application yourself. Online intake is open, regional centers accept walk-ins, and inquiries are free through published call centers. Even so, parties offering to handle the filing for a fee exist, and some of them become a real problem.

What separates them is not the company name but what they say.

  • They guarantee approval — the agency decides on eligibility and submitted evidence. No third party is positioned to promise an outcome.
  • They imply influence over the review — hints about being connected on the inside are themselves the signal.
  • They ask for fees up front — money leaves before any result exists.
  • They offer to prepare the documents for you — once the records say something untrue, the liability stays with the applicant.
  • They operate through personal phone numbers and private bank accounts rather than official channels.

The agencies are aware of this. The Korea Small Business and Market Promotion Agency posts on its application screen that it never requests deliveries of goods or similar under any pretext without an official contract procedure. In February 2025 the Ministry of SMEs and Startups held a council with its policy finance institutions where the main item was a plan to eliminate improper third-party intervention, including creating a legal definition of that conduct and building a cooperation framework with the National Police Agency. In June 2026 the ministry was reported to be pursuing an amendment to the Small and Medium Enterprises Promotion Act that would define falsified paperwork and its brokerage, false or exaggerated advertising suggesting influence over a review, and fees above a statutory ceiling as improper intervention, punishable by up to five years imprisonment or a fine of up to 50 million won.

To put it plainly: paid advisory work is not itself unlawful. What is a problem is promising approval, dressing up records, and demanding compensation above the ceiling. And if any of the three occurs, the person left holding the consequence is the applicant. When in doubt, asking the same question at an official call center is the fastest way to settle it.

After a rejection, and when not to borrow

When the answer is no

Rejection is common, and it usually leaves information for the next round. The order is this.

  1. Establish the reason. Whether it was an unmet requirement, an exclusion, or an exhausted budget changes the response completely. The intake channel or call center can address this.
  2. Separate what can be fixed from what cannot. Arrears and delinquency change once resolved. Something arising from the shape of the business, such as an excluded industry, does not change for that program.
  3. Check whether and when reapplication is possible. Some programs impose a waiting period. The relevant clause is in the announcement.
  4. Appeal procedures vary. Some programs provide a route to contest an evaluation result, while reasons such as an unmet requirement or an exhausted budget are not the kind of thing that is contested. Whether a procedure exists has to be read in the announcement.

When a policy fund is not the answer

Here is the part that has to be said plainly. A policy fund does not generate revenue. Repayment comes out of trading, and if trading cannot currently cover the payment, a well-priced loan becomes a device for postponing the problem. The deferment period hides that postponement particularly well.

  • If several loans already exist and monthly payments exceed operating profit, restructuring existing debt is the branch to examine before adding more
  • If a revenue decline is structural rather than temporary, borrowed money does not change that structure
  • If the stated use is a vague cushion rather than a concrete plan, that is not a reason to borrow
  • If winding down is on the table, separate programs exist for closure and restarting, and those announcements are the ones to read
  • If the picture is unclear, the sequence is to sort out the situation at a neutral advisory channel rather than a lending desk

Deciding not to borrow is also a conclusion. The sense that skipping an application means losing out usually comes from having read a loan as a grant. Restore the word debt and not borrowing often stops looking like a loss.

Where to look and what to check against

Where the information comes from decides the outcome. Summary articles are usually written against last year's announcement, and the numbers have moved since. The order for reading primary sources is as follows.

StepWhere to lookWhat to check
1Ministry of SMEs and Startups announcement boardWhether this year's announcement exists, with its number and date
2Bizinfo portalAnnouncements in the field that fits your situation, splitting lending from guarantees and interest subsidies
3The announcement text and attachmentsEligibility, excluded industries, limits, rates, terms, intake schedule
4The executing agency's siteCurrent rate per fund and intake status, including whether it has closed
5Local government and regional guarantee foundationSeparate announcements and interest subsidy programs for your location
6Official call centersIndividual points the announcement does not settle

The thing to watch for when checking is the year label. Establish which year's announcement a figure belongs to and whether that announcement is still live. Fund names persist across years while targets and terms shift underneath them. And for values that move quarterly, such as rates, the executing agency's current screen can be more up to date than the announcement text.

What could not be confirmed

These are the items left without figures, because the basis could not be pinned down in a primary document.

  • Total annual budget — the ministry board serves the announcement body as an attached file only, so the total could not be established from the source itself. That figure lives in the attachment.
  • Lending limits per small business fund — the executing agency's guidance screen shows a rate for each fund but not a limit. Limits are in the announcement.
  • Loan terms and deferment periods for small business funds — omitted for the same reason.
  • Full text of the exclusion clauses in the small business announcement — the industry list is available from the executing agency, but the clauses about applicant standing require the announcement attachment.
  • Whether an appeal procedure exists — it varies by program and no uniform rule was established.

The short version

What to remember is not the numbers but the order.

  1. Split giving from lending first. If the title says loan, it is debt.
  2. Settle whether you are in the small business tier or the SME tier, then read only that agency's announcements.
  3. Check your industry code against the exclusion list, and against its exceptions.
  4. Check arrears, delinquency and dormancy first. Caught here, no document gets you past it.
  5. Intake runs per announcement and closes when the budget does. Have documents ready in advance.
  6. The rate is a formula. Look at fixed versus floating, and the payment after deferment ends.
  7. Anyone guaranteeing approval is promising something they cannot promise. You can file it yourself.
  8. If the repayment source is not visible, not borrowing is also a conclusion.

Used well, a policy fund buys time. That time is borrowed and it accrues interest. Read announcements with that in mind and what needs checking arranges itself.

Please note
What is written here is general information about South Korean government programs and does not recommend any financial product. Every figure quoted is stated with its source and the moment it was observed, which does not mean it still holds. Individual eligibility, terms, limits and rates are determined by each program's announcement and by the administering agency, so confirm them in the current announcement and through official channels before applying. Readers outside Korea should note that these programs apply to businesses registered in Korea, and equivalent schemes elsewhere follow entirely different rules.
