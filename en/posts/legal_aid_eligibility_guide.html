Legal Aid Eligibility Guide — Income Test, Covered Cases, and What Free Really Means

A scheme built so that money is not the reason a dispute goes unfought — Korean system, 2026 figures

Aug 31, 2026 Law & Legal
Read this first. This article describes how the legal aid system in South Korea is structured. It is general information, not legal advice, and it does not assess the merits or strategy of any individual case. Eligibility figures are set annually by government notice, so check the year stamped on each number below and confirm the current position with the relevant institution before applying.

1. When the case is winnable but you cannot start it

You were not paid. You have the evidence. You know exactly who owes you. And you stop at the door of a law office, because you are afraid of the answer to the question about the retainer. When the cost looks larger than the benefit, people simply drop it. That is the exact gap this scheme was built to close.

Attorney fees in Korea usually split into a retainer and a success fee, and on top of that sit the filing fee and the service fee paid to the court. The first pair goes to a lawyer, the second to the court. Different recipients mean different bodies can reduce them, and missing that distinction is what produces the complaint that starts with the words they said it was free. More on that below.

The statute behind all of this is the Legal Aid Act. Legal aid means providing representation by an attorney or a public-service legal officer, and other support in legal affairs, to people who cannot get adequate protection of the law because of economic hardship or lack of legal knowledge (Legal Aid Act, Articles 1 and 2). The institution at the centre of day-to-day practice is the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, reachable on 132.

Two beliefs distort expectations. One is that the agency solves everything; the other is that only low earners may use it. Neither holds. What follows is a map of how to reach the scheme: how far the help goes, how eligibility is actually constructed, where free stops, and what exists after a refusal.

2. What legal aid actually does for you

The most common mismatch of expectations lives here. Legal aid is not one service but a bundle of supports at different depths. Advice, document drafting and full representation sit behind different gates. Advice is wide open; the closer you get to representation, the more screening appears.

The range of matters is not narrow. Article 6 of the Enforcement Rule of the Legal Aid Act sets the covered case types as civil, family, administrative, constitutional complaint and criminal matters, whether by way of litigation, representation in adjudication, or criminal defence. So it is not just landlord and tenant disputes: divorce and custody, challenges to administrative dispositions, and criminal defence all sit inside the frame. The agency does, however, state that filing a criminal complaint on a client behalf is not included. Writing someone criminal complaint for them and defending an accused person are different layers of work.

Compressed into one line: almost anyone may ask; fighting on your behalf goes through screening. Which means an ambiguous eligibility position is not a reason to give up in advance.

3. Eligibility runs on two axes

Whether aid is granted is decided where two axes cross. One is whether you are an eligible person; the other is whether the matter is an eligible case type. Satisfying one is not enough.

3.1 First axis — 125 percent of standard median income

For the general public and foreign residents, the income line is 125 percent of the standard median income or below. The standard median income is published each year by the Ministry of Health and Welfare, so this line moves annually. Using last year table produces a wrong answer.

For 2026 the standard median income for a four-person household is 6,494,738 won per month, a 6.51 percent rise on the 2025 figure of 6,097,773 won. The one-person figure is 2,564,238 won. Multiplying by 1.25 gives the left-hand column below.

How income is measured matters as much as the line itself. The agency states that it applies the assessment table of standard median income and health insurance contributions produced by the Ministry of Health and Welfare. In other words the primary indicator is the health insurance contribution, not a bank balance or a felt sense of hardship. The method varies with household composition: for households of employee subscribers only, the contribution is compared against the figure for that household size, and where several members are employee subscribers the combined contribution is used. Households of regional subscribers only are assessed on the annual income shown in the regional contribution statement, and mixed households on combined annual or monthly income against the size-based figure.

What about assets? The published 125 percent table carries income indicators only; no asset threshold appears on it. By contrast the court-administered scheme described later applies to a person who lacks the means to bear litigation costs, which draws assets into what must be substantiated. Different schemes measure different things, and knowing that saves a wasted trip.

3.2 Second axis — is this an eligible case?

Clearing the income line does not help if the matter falls outside the scheme. Support is defined case type by case type, and some types carry their own tests. Small business owners, for example, qualify for free advice and aid on civil matters arising from commercial activity, including personal rehabilitation and bankruptcy but excluding employment relations, where they are at or below 125 percent of median income or have annual turnover of 200 million won or less. The basis is Article 15 of the Act on the Protection and Support of Small Enterprisers together with Article 3 and Annexed Table 1 of the free legal aid programme guidelines.

4. The doors that open on something other than income

This is what people miss most often. Many stop at the 125 percent figure, but the agency eligibility guidance also runs two further lists: one by status and one by type of harm suffered. Landing on either changes the test that applies.

4.1 Eligibility by status

The status list runs to nearly forty entries of quite different character, so scanning it for your own situation is faster than reasoning about it.

  • Basic livelihood security recipients, the near-poor, long-term small-sum delinquent debtors, and children and young people at risk of inheriting debt (under 24)
  • Persons of national merit, compensation-eligible veterans, Korean War and other war veterans, independence patriots, May 18 Democratic Movement merit holders, special mission merit holders, defoliant aftereffect patients
  • Single-parent families, unmarried mothers and fathers, child heads of household, children under protection
  • People with disabilities, marriage migrants and naturalised citizens, foreign residents, North Korean defectors, persons without registered family relations
  • Farmers and fishers, seafarers (at or below 125 percent of median income), commercial freight drivers, platform-adjacent workers, artists
  • Police and firefighters, mid- and long-term service veterans, unemployed young people and university students

Two things are worth noting. Status, not income, opens the door in some cases, and the list grows over time — entries added later, such as victims of particular disasters or workers in specific industries, sit alongside long-standing ones. A refusal heard several years ago does not settle the position today.

4.2 Eligibility by type of harm

The harm-based route is not confined to civil disputes. It reaches victim support tied to criminal proceedings, violence within the family sphere, and administrative or public-interest group disputes.

  • Workers owed wages, and seafarers affected by unpaid wages or accident compensation
  • Victims of domestic and sexual violence, victims of sexual offences against children and adolescents, children harmed by abuse
  • Crime victims and students harmed by school violence
  • Jeonse fraud victims, and small-deposit tenants under the Housing Lease Protection Act at or below 125 percent of median income
  • Victims of illegal private lending, virtual asset investment fraud, medical accidents, humidifier disinfectant harm, and serious industrial accidents together with bereaved families
  • Applicants for personal rehabilitation, bankruptcy and discharge, platform workers, and public-interest litigation by groups of affected people

For crime victims the agency concentrates on two things: damages claims against the offender and applications for state crime victim compensation. That pairing makes a distinction visible — punishing the accused and recovering money for the harm run on separate tracks.

5. What free actually covers

The section people most often get wrong. The short version: some cases are free and some carry a partial charge, and even in a free case a chargeable stretch remains.

5.1 The fully free side

The agency lists workers owed wages, farmers and fishers, and basic livelihood security recipients among those served free of charge. A ceiling applies even there: high-value matters where the amount won exceeds 300 million won are excluded from free legal aid. The design follows the purpose. Free service exists to stop disputes dying for lack of money, so a claim large enough to fund itself is treated as able to bear its own cost.

5.2 The partial-charge side

Members of the general public and foreign residents who come in under the 125 percent line pay two things in principle. One is court disbursements such as filing and service fees; the other is an attorney fee paid to the agency, which the agency describes as roughly 30 percent of the level set by Supreme Court rules.

The agency also notes that of some 150,000 supported cases, only 9.8 percent involved an applicant bearing the whole of the attorney fee and filing fee, and that even then the total averaged 170,000 won. That figure is labelled as being on a 2016 basis in the guidance itself. It is old enough that it should not be read as today cost level; the safer reading is directional — full self-funding is not the common outcome. Where a separate body provides support, costs may be waived in whole or in part.

5.3 The stretch that free does not reach

Here is the crux. Filing fees and service fees are paid to the court, so they are not the agency to forgive. The agency states that even after an aid decision, filing and service fees must be paid in advance. A separate scheme handles that stretch: court-ordered litigation aid under Article 128 and following of the Civil Procedure Act, covered in section 8.

So costs come in three blocks. Attorney fees the agency removes or sharply reduces. Court costs require a separate application for deferral or waiver. The other side costs, payable on a loss, remain a distinct exposure. Treating the three as one number is where the arithmetic goes wrong.

6. How to apply, and why advice comes first

Understanding why step one is a consultation makes the rest legible. A case enters the system when staff, during the consultation, assess eligibility, prospects of success and the appropriateness of aid, and open a file on that basis. Intake is the output of the consultation, not something that precedes it.

6.1 Where to go

There are four entrances. Phone advice runs on 132, weekdays from 9am to 6pm. In-person advice is offered at branch and field offices, with booking in advance. Video and online advice is booked through the website or mobile. And a separate electronic intake system lets an application be filed without visiting an office at all. Whichever entrance you use, the next step is the same.

6.2 What to bring

The density of a consultation is decided by what you carry into it. Arriving empty-handed usually buys you an instruction to come back with documents, and one visit is gone.

  • Proof of who you are — identification, plus any certificate of status such as livelihood recipient, veteran or single-parent household
  • Proof of income — health insurance payment certificate, or the contribution statement if you are a regional subscriber
  • The skeleton of the dispute — contract, loan note, transfer records, messages, medical certificate, notice of an administrative disposition
  • Enough to identify the other side, and a one-page timeline — name, contact details and address (for a business, trade name and registration number), plus what happened when and when you demanded what

That last item pulls more weight than it looks. Consultations are short, and where the facts are already ordered the whole slot goes into judgement rather than reconstruction. If putting a demand into documented form is the missing piece, the piece on certified content-proof mail in Korea covers that ground.

6.3 What happens after intake

  1. Once the application is filed, the agency begins fact-finding
  2. At a certain point it will encourage settlement. Agreement here ends the matter before any suit is filed
  3. If settlement fails, eligibility, prospects of success, prospects of enforcement and appropriateness are assessed to decide on aid
  4. Where aid is granted, the case is run, and a win can be carried through to enforcement
  5. On a loss, an appeal is considered and the file is closed

Step two is easy to read as a delay, but weighed against the cost and time of a full judgment, quite a few disputes are better ended there. For smaller money claims in particular it is worth measuring this route against the small claims procedure to see which moves faster.

7. Refusals, and who makes that call

Applications are not always accepted. The agency names three grounds.

  • Not an eligible person, or not an eligible case type — the block is status or subject matter
  • Low prospect of success — fact-finding suggests the claim is hard to sustain in law
  • No prospect of enforcement — there is nothing to recover from even on a win

Being clear about who decides matters. This call is made by the agency, not by a court, through internal assessment following the consultation and fact-finding. So a court saying no and the agency declining aid are entirely different events. The first is a loss; the second is a refusal of support, not a denial of the right itself.

A rejection can be met with an internal objection, after which the matter is reconsidered. The exception is stated: grounds of ineligible person or ineligible case type are excluded from that route. Read the other way, that means the prospect-of-success and prospect-of-enforcement calls are contestable. New material is what gives an objection substance.

One line is drawn here deliberately. Whether a particular case is winnable is not something this article can say. That judgement needs the whole file and the weight of the evidence, and the people who make it are already appointed.

8. The agency is not the only route

A block at the agency is not the end of the road. Several schemes exist with different purposes, covering stretches that do not overlap. Court costs and criminal defence in particular belong to other schemes from the outset.

8.1 Court litigation aid — the scheme for filing and service fees

Article 128 of the Civil Procedure Act lets a court grant litigation aid, on application or of its own motion, to a person who lacks the means to bear litigation costs. Cases that will clearly lose are excluded, and the applicant must substantiate the grounds. The objective scope sits in Article 129, the subjective scope in Article 130, and revocation in Article 131.

  • Scope — deferral of court costs, deferral of attorney and enforcement officer remuneration and advanced expenses, exemption from security for costs, and other deferrals or waivers
  • Application — made in writing, with a statement of the means of the applicant and their family, a 1,000 won stamp and two rounds of service fees attached
  • Filed with the court where the suit will be brought, or, once brought, the court holding the case record
  • It applies to civil litigation, and foreign nationals and corporations may apply

One word carries a lot here. Deferral is not waiver. It means you need not pay now, not that the liability disappears, so a later settlement of accounts is possible. It is also worth knowing that filing the application itself costs a stamp and service fees.

8.2 Court-appointed counsel in criminal proceedings

Criminal matters run differently. Article 33 of the Criminal Procedure Act requires a court to appoint defence counsel at state expense where the accused has none. Paragraph 1 makes appointment mandatory in defined situations such as detention or minority. Paragraph 2 provides for appointment on request where poverty or another reason prevents retaining counsel. Paragraph 3 lets the court appoint of its own motion where age, mental capacity or education make it necessary for the protection of rights. A request under paragraph 2 is supported by evidence, unless the record already establishes the ground.

So if you are an accused person, checking the appointment of defence counsel comes before working through income tables. That track operates independently of the agency assessment.

8.3 Other doors in family and consumer matters

Family problems have their own well-developed advice channels, and family matters are within the agency covered types in any event. If the whole shape of a divorce is the question, the piece on the divorce process step by step gives the map. For disputes over goods and services, the consumer dispute resolution route is frequently faster than litigation. Choosing the channel that matches the nature of the dispute is the most reliable way to hold costs down.

9. What preparation changes — limitation, evidence, the other side

Knowing about a scheme and being in a state to use it are different things. Some things disappear quietly while you wait.

9.1 Limitation periods — the thing that cannot be restored

Rights come with windows. Once a limitation or exclusion period has run, the clearest facts in the world will not let you litigate. No aid scheme reverses that. Periods differ by type of right, so how long do I have belongs at the front of the consultation.

9.2 Evidence — what time eats

  • Messages vanish with a new handset or an app clear-out. Export the original conversation rather than relying on screenshots
  • Transfer records take extra steps to obtain once the bank retrieval window has passed, and a medical certificate written straight after an incident carries more weight than a later one
  • Where the site changes, as with construction or repairs, photograph it so the date metadata survives, and confirm any verbal promise by message afterwards so a trace exists

9.3 Information about the other side — tied directly to enforcement

This is why prospect of enforcement sits among the refusal grounds. If who the other party is, where they live and what they hold are all vague, a win recovers nothing and the assessment can turn against you. Secure a name and registered address for an individual, or trade name, registration number and representative for a business. Establish whether the counterparty is a company or a natural person, because the responsible party differs. Note the account used in the transaction, the business location, vehicles and any other clue to assets, along with anything suggesting the other side is already subject to attachment for other debts.

In one line: limitation costs you the right, lost evidence costs you the proof, and an empty counterparty costs you the recovery. All three only get worse with time.

10. What this scheme cannot do

Setting expectations precisely is the honest move. Legal aid lowers a threshold; it does not change outcomes.

  • Having representation is not the same as winning. Facts and evidence produce the result. Support does not mean success
  • An impecunious opponent means no recovery even on a win. A judgment is a title to enforce, not a machine for making money appear
  • Free has an edge. Filing and service fees belong to the court, and even in a free category high-value matters are excluded
  • Not every criminal step is covered, since filing a criminal complaint on your behalf is outside the guidance, and how a case is run sits within the support criteria and the judgement of the staff handling it
  • Criteria change annually and speed is not guaranteed. Someone eligible last year may not be this year, and fact-finding and assessment take time. Where a limitation deadline is near, say so at the consultation

What it does do is clear enough. It lets cases begin that would otherwise never have started because of cost. For some disputes, that alone changes the outcome.

11. What could not be confirmed

Collected in one place, the points this article could not pin down.

  • Specific asset thresholds. The published 125 percent assessment table shows no asset line. Whether separate asset criteria apply to particular categories was not established here
  • Two turnover figures for small businesses. Aid for civil matters arising from commercial activity is described with a 200 million won turnover ceiling, while the status list carries a separate entry for small enterprisers with turnover of 300 million won or less. These are probably distinct programmes, but the relationship was not confirmed
  • The full contents of Annexed Table 1 of the free legal aid guidelines. That table is the final basis for which categories are served free of charge, and the full text was not cross-checked
  • Current cost statistics and processing times. The 9.8 percent full self-funding figure and the 170,000 won average are labelled 2016 in the guidance, and no published figure was found for average time from intake to decision

12. Frequently asked questions

Q. If my income is slightly over the line, is that the end of it?

The general line is 125 percent of median income, but the status and harm-based routes open separately. Scan the lists in section 4 first. Advice itself is available regardless of the income test, so when the position is unclear, asking is the next step.

Q. How is household size counted?

Assessment follows health insurance subscription structure. The calculation differs for households of employee subscribers only, regional subscribers only, several employee subscribers, and mixed households. Family relationships and insurance household composition can diverge, so bringing the contribution certificate to the consultation is the accurate route.

Q. Litigation has already started. Can I still apply?

Court litigation aid is filed with the court where the suit will be brought, or, once brought, the court holding the case record. Proceedings being under way does not close the door. Agency aid likewise starts with disclosing the current stage of the matter at the consultation and receiving an assessment.

Q. Criminal case: agency, or court-appointed counsel?

They are separate tracks. If you are the accused, appointment under Article 33 of the Criminal Procedure Act is the first thing to check; in defined situations such as detention or minority it happens without a request, and poverty or a similar reason supports a request. On the victim side, damages claims and applications for state crime victim compensation are where the agency concentrates.

Q. I was refused. Can I apply again?

A rejection can be met with an internal objection and reconsidered, except where the ground is that the applicant or case type falls outside the scheme. If the refusal rested on prospects of success or enforcement, newly obtained evidence or information about the other side assets is the material that gives an objection weight. Routes outside the agency are set out in section 8.

13. Summary — one thing you can do today

Legal aid is not a scheme that makes you win. It is a scheme that lets you start. That door does not open on income alone: there are three keyholes — the income line, status, and type of harm — and matching any one of them changes the answer you get.

Costs are not one thing either. Attorney fees sit with the agency, court costs with the court litigation aid scheme, and criminal defence with court-appointed counsel. Knowing that no single counter resolves all three saves wasted journeys.

The one thing to do today is simple. Write the timeline of your dispute on a single page. What happened when, when you demanded what, and what is outstanding now. That page turns consultation time into judgement time, and it separates the refusal grounds worth contesting from the ones that are not. The rest starts with a call to 132.

To repeat. This article is general information about the legal aid system in South Korea and is not legal advice. It does not assess the prospects or handling of any individual case, and it does not recommend any particular attorney or law firm. Amounts and eligibility criteria are stated on a 2026 basis and change each year through notices and guideline revisions. Before applying, confirm the current position directly with the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, the courts or the Ministry of Justice, and seek qualified assistance where you need it.
