Scope and reference dates

Yeonggwang County in Jeollanam-do is a rural and coastal county in southwestern Korea whose population has been falling for a long time. This article was first published in June 2025 and was re-checked against official statistics on 29 September 2026. Every figure below carries its reference date (year and month) and its source.

A population figure depends on which statistic it comes from. The Statistics Korea census counts residents at the census date, while the resident-registration figures of the Ministry of the Interior and Safety and the county office count by registration. Joining figures with different bases into one series can make changes look larger or smaller than they were, so the table below has a source column.

What has happened since (checked 2026-09-29)

The county office reports a resident-registration population of 53,713 at the end of January 2026 (26,913 men, 26,800 women) in 28,639 households. A secondary source (Namuwiki) gives 53,752 for July 2026, but this could not be confirmed against the county table. The county population board lists an end-of-August 2026 release dated 2026-09-01, but it is an attached file and its figure could not be read.

So since 2020 the county population has stayed in the low 53,000s. Because the census and the registration figures use different bases, the roughly 600-person difference between 2020 and 2026 cannot be read as proof that the decline has stopped.

The first version of this article gave 49,812 for 2025 and described a continuing fall. No source supporting that value was found in this check and it does not match the published figures, so it was removed.

The birth rate was not a one-way decline

The first version said the total fertility rate had fallen to 0.9 in 2025, but Yeonggwang has been reported very differently. News reports citing Statistics Korea gave the following.

  • 2.54 in 2019, 2.46 in 2020, 1.87 in 2021 and 1.81 in 2022
  • First among all Korean municipalities for four straight years, 2019 to 2022
  • The values have been coming down since 2020, so a high rate cannot be assumed to persist.

These figures were confirmed through news reports (Newsis, 2020-08-26, Namdo Ilbo), not directly from the Statistics Korea tables (KOSIS birth statistics). Values for 2023 and later were not confirmed this time.

How to read causes and policy effects

Population change is natural change (births minus deaths) plus migration (moves in minus moves out). The first version gave detailed figures such as migration rates, the farm-population share and inflows per policy. No sources could be found this time, so all of them were removed.

It is confirmed that the county has run birth-support and settlement-support programs and that its fertility rate ranked first in the country. There is no data here that would establish how much any single policy contributed, and judging particular policies or politicians is outside this article.

To check for yourself, see the county-level tables of KOSIS "Internal Migration" (moves in and out) and "Vital Statistics" (births and deaths).

Outlook: a straight-line extension is not evidence

The first version used a regression to project the county at around 33,000 in 2040 and gave a local extinction index. This time the input data for that regression and the basis of the index could not be confirmed, and the real 2020 to 2026 path (staying in the low 53,000s) does not match it, so both were removed. Extending the past rate of decline in a straight line is weak evidence for this reason.

Official projections should be read from the Statistics Korea population projections. Yeonggwang values were not confirmed this time, so this article carries no numerical projection.

What could not be confirmed this time

  • Age-structure figures such as the share aged 65 and over, the aging index and the old-age dependency ratio (the first-version values were removed because no source was found)
  • Annual births and deaths, and the net migration rate
  • Values for intermediate years such as 2005 and 2010 where sources disagree
  • The registered population at the end of August 2026 (attached file on the county board)
  • County-level population projection values for Yeonggwang
