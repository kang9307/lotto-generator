The Complete Moving Guide: From a 60-Day Timeline to Damage Claims and Deposit Settlement

From 60 days out through moving day and after - choosing a mover, packing, paperwork, and move-out cleaning and deposit settlement, all in one practical guide

Jul 24, 2026 Life & Practical

Introduction: A Timeline Is Half the Battle

Signing a lease or closing on a home isn't the finish line - it's the starting gun. You still have to pick a mover, pack everything up, handle paperwork like address changes, and on the way out, clean the old place and settle the deposit. When everything lands at once, something inevitably slips through the cracks. This guide, current as of July 2026, breaks moving preparation into a timeline from 60 days out through moving day and after, and covers choosing a mover and filing damage claims, packing technique, paperwork, and the cleaning and deposit issues that come up on your way out.

One caveat up front: exact procedures, costs, and rental norms vary by location, home type, and country, so this article covers the general flow and isn't a substitute for your local authority's official guidance, your specific contract, or legal advice. Always cross-check the latest rules for where you live.

1. The Moving Timeline: 60 Days Out to After the Move

Moving prep feels overwhelming as one big blob of tasks, but breaking it into phases makes everything clearer. If you have a lot to move, or overlapping life events like a school transfer, start around 60 days out; for a light, small move, 30 days out is often enough.

1.1 60-30 Days Out: Nail Down the Big Picture

This is when you lock in your moving date and rough out a budget. Getting quotes from at least three moving companies is standard practice, and moving on a weekday during the off-peak season tends to be cheaper. If you have kids, start the school-transfer paperwork; if you have a pet, begin carrier-crate training early.

1.2 14 Days Out: Lock In Quotes and Start Packing

Confirm your mover and get a written contract in place. Start packing non-essentials first to cut down on last-minute chaos, sketch out a rough furniture layout for the new place, and book any installation services, like internet setup, that need scheduling.

1.3 7 Days Out: Focus on Paperwork

Utility hookups often can't be disconnected and reconnected the same day, so book at least a week ahead. This is also the time to prepare your change-of-address filing, set up mail forwarding, and knock out address updates for banks, insurance, and subscriptions in one batch. Defrost the refrigerator, and set aside cleaning supplies and trash bags you'll need on moving day itself.

1.4 Moving Day and After

Moving day has its own sequence and checkpoints, and after the move there's a wrap-up list to finish - both covered in detail in sections 6 and 7 below.

2. Choosing a Mover: Quotes, Contracts, and Damage Claims

2.1 Full-Service vs. Partial vs. Self-Pack

With a self-pack move, you pack everything yourself and the company only handles transport - cheapest, but the most time and labor on you. A partial-service move has the company pack large items like the fridge and washer while you handle the rest - a reasonable middle ground. A full-service move has the company handle everything from packing to arranging furniture to final cleanup - fastest and most convenient, but the most expensive.

2.2 Comparing Quotes and What to Check in the Contract

Getting quotes from at least three companies is standard, and an in-home estimate is more accurate than a phone quote. Keep the written estimate - it carries the same weight as the contract. Confirm upfront whether the quote includes items like a ladder truck, AC reinstallation, packing materials, and any elevator- or parking-related surcharge, since these are the most common sources of surprise charges on moving day.

2.3 Damage Claims: Know Your Filing Deadline

Most movers operate under a standard contract that lets you file a claim when loss or damage is the mover's responsibility - but every contract has a filing deadline, and missing it can forfeit your right to compensation. In the United States, federal rules require interstate movers to accept written damage claims for up to nine months from the delivery date. In South Korea, the standard moving contract sets a much tighter window - a written notice of damage is required within 30 days of delivery, or the right to claim compensation lapses. Whatever your situation, check the specific deadline in your contract, inspect items as they come off the truck, and document any damage in writing and with photos immediately rather than waiting.

2.4 Avoiding Scams and Hidden Fees

Treat a quote that's dramatically lower than every other bid with suspicion rather than relief. Avoid any company that wants to skip a written contract or asks you to sign paperwork with blank price or date fields. Be cautious of large upfront cash deposits, and check the company's reviews and business registration before booking.

3. Packing Technique: Labeling, Fragile Items, and an Essentials Box

3.1 Label Boxes to Speed Up Unpacking

Writing the destination room and a summary of contents in large letters on each box lets you decide where it goes the instant it arrives, cutting down on unpacking time dramatically. Color-coding boxes by room with stickers or tape also helps the moving crew sort quickly without asking.

3.2 Packing Fragile Items

Wrap breakables like dishes, glasses, and framed items individually in bubble wrap or packing paper, line the bottom of each box with a thick cushioning layer, and fill gaps with clothing or paper to minimize shifting in transit. Marking boxes clearly as fragile also encourages more careful handling.

3.3 Pack a Separate Essentials Box

Toiletries, a change of clothes, medication, phone chargers, and other items you'll need on moving day and the day after belong in a dedicated essentials box - load it last and unload it first. Mixing these items in with everything else means wasting time hunting for them once you've arrived.

4. Sorting Your Belongings and Decluttering

Since moving cost scales with volume, cutting down what you own is the single most reliable way to save money. Anything you haven't used in the last 90 days and don't expect to use again is a reasonable candidate to let go of. Donating, selling secondhand, or giving items away lightens your load and can put a little cash back in your pocket. Tell your mover about anything you're getting rid of before the quote so it isn't factored into the estimate.

5. Address Changes, Utilities, and Subscriptions

5.1 Change of Address and Mail Forwarding

In the United States, filing a change of address with USPS - online or at your local post office - is the standard first step, and pairing it with updates to your driver's license and vehicle registration is worth doing at the same time. In South Korea, residents are expected to file a change-of-residence report within 14 days of moving, and mail forwarding can be requested at the same time; missing the window without good reason can carry a fine.

5.2 Utilities, Internet, and Subscription Addresses

Gas service in particular often can't be shut off and turned back on the same day, so schedule it at least a week ahead; electricity, water, and internet transfers should be scheduled early too. Bank, insurance, and credit card address updates are easy to forget, so track them on a dedicated checklist, and don't forget billing and shipping addresses on subscriptions and online accounts.

5.3 Address Rules Vary by Country

Address-registration systems differ meaningfully from country to country, so if you're moving internationally, confirm the local procedure separately. Beyond South Korea's 14-day filing window, systems like Japan's paired moving-out/moving-in notices, or the US's per-state DMV updates, each have their own quirks - treat this as general orientation and check the current official procedure for your specific location.

6. Move-Out Cleaning, Repairs, and Security Deposit Settlement

6.1 Normal Wear and Tear vs. Actual Damage

Under the normal wear-and-tear principle recognized in many jurisdictions, deterioration from ordinary, non-negligent use of the property is typically the landlord's responsibility, not the tenant's. Sun-faded paint or wallpaper, small nail holes from hanging pictures, and light indentations from furniture are common examples of normal wear and tear. Damage that goes beyond that - large holes in the wall, an unauthorized paint job, broken fixtures, or ruined flooring - generally falls on the tenant to repair or pay for.

6.2 Cleaning and Documenting Condition

Once everything's out, a basic clean - floors, window tracks, the bathroom, kitchen grease - followed by a walkthrough with the landlord or property manager is the safest way to close things out. Photos or video of the unit's condition, ideally both when you moved in and again as you move out, are useful evidence for separating normal wear and tear from actual damage if a dispute comes up.

6.3 Settling the Deposit

Settle any outstanding utility bills or association fees before you leave, and if the landlord claims damage beyond normal wear and tear, ask for a documented cost breakdown. It also helps to know that in some jurisdictions, if the full deposit isn't returned as agreed, tenants can decline to fully vacate or hand over keys until it's settled - though the specific leverage available depends entirely on your local tenancy law. If a dispute doesn't resolve directly, a local consumer protection body or small-claims court may be the next step.

7. Moving Day Itself

Keep ID, contracts, medications, and valuables with you, separate from the moving truck. Before you leave the old place, do one last check for leaks, locked windows, and the gas valve, and confirm the mover's crew count and inventory list. At the new place, check furniture and appliances for damage on the spot, and if you find any, document it with the mover's crew right away so you stay within your contract's damage-claim window.

8. After the Move

Finish your change-of-address filing within the required window, and settle the final utility bills and deposit at your old address. Double-check that your address updated with banks, card issuers, and subscriptions, and take a moment to introduce yourself to the building manager or new neighbors.

9. Cost-Saving Tips and Common Mistakes

9.1 Ways to Cut Costs

Moving on a weekday during the off-peak season tends to bring the price down. Comparing quotes from at least three companies often reveals a meaningful spread for the same job. Reducing what you own, and choosing a partial-service move over full-service when it fits, are both practical ways to lower the bill.

9.2 Mistakes People Commonly Make

Failing to confirm whether a quote includes a ladder truck, AC reinstallation, or packing materials is one of the most common ways people get hit with surprise fees on moving day. Missing your contract's damage-claim filing deadline is another common and entirely avoidable mistake - put it on your checklist the moment you sign.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Should I start planning 60 days out?

If you have a lot to move or overlapping life events, yes; for a small, light move, 30 days out is often enough.

Q2. What do I do if my belongings are damaged in the move?

Check your contract's filing deadline (commonly 9 months in the US, 30 days in South Korea), inspect items immediately, and document damage in writing with photos.

Q3. How fast do I need to update my address?

Deadlines vary by country - South Korea requires filing within 14 days, with fines for missing it without cause.

Q4. How much cleaning or repair am I responsible for when moving out?

Normal wear and tear from ordinary use is typically not the tenant's responsibility; damage from negligence or misuse generally is.

Q5. What should I check to get my deposit back safely?

Document the unit's condition with photos before handing back the keys, distinguishing normal wear and tear from actual damage.

Conclusion: Timeline, Contract, and Settlement - Don't Skip Any of the Three

Moving prep comes down to three things: a timeline from 60 days out through after the move, a mover contract with clear damage-claim terms, and paperwork that runs from change-of-address filing through deposit settlement. Work through the phases in order, get written quotes before signing anything, know your damage-claim deadline, and separate normal wear and tear from actual damage when it's time to settle the deposit.

Exact procedures, costs, and rental norms vary by region and country, so treat this guide as a general roadmap and confirm the latest official guidance for where you live, along with your specific mover's and landlord's terms.
