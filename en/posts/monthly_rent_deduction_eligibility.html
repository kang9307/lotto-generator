Korea Monthly Rent Tax Credit: Who Qualifies and How Much Comes Back

Four eligibility gates, three numbers, and the five-year window that reopens a year you missed - for the 2025 tax year

Aug 11, 2026 Law & Legal

First - what this article is based on, and what it is not

This is a Korean tax rule, described as it stood for the 2025 tax year, the settlement filed in early 2026. It is not tax advice. The income test, credit rates, and cap for Korea monthly rent credit are set by the Restriction of Special Taxation Act and have been amended, with two upward revisions in recent years. Older articles still floating online carry the retired numbers. Nothing here fixes any individual eligibility or amount, and the final determination rests with the National Tax Service. Before filing, check the guidance for your tax year on Hometax (hometax.go.kr) and take anything ambiguous to the national tax counselling centre at 126 or your district tax office.

A tax credit comes off the tax, not off the income

If you rent a place at 600,000 won a month, 7.2 million won leaves your account over a year. Under this rule, 17 percent of that, roughly 1.224 million won, is struck directly off the tax you owe. The distinction people miss is the one between a deduction and a credit, and missing it makes the perceived benefit wrong by several times.

  • A deduction shrinks the income the tax is calculated on. Deduct one million won and the real saving is one million multiplied by your marginal rate, so around 60,000 won in the lowest bracket.
  • A credit is subtracted from the tax after it has been calculated. One million won of credit removes one million won of tax regardless of your bracket.
  • Monthly rent relief in Korea is the second kind, which is why it lands harder than card-spending deductions of similar size.

One limit is written into the statute: the amount is deducted from the calculated global income tax. If there is no tax to begin with, there is nothing to give back. In a year where your income was small enough that the calculated tax is near zero, you can satisfy every requirement and still feel almost nothing.

This article covers only how someone already paying monthly rent recovers part of it through tax. Choosing between a lump-sum jeonse deposit and monthly rent is a different question, handled in Jeonse vs Monthly Rent: Structure, Cost Comparison, and How to Decide. If you want the map of every year-end settlement item first, Korea Year-End Tax Settlement: A Practical Saving Guide for Salaried Workers is the better starting point.

The four gates that decide eligibility

National Tax Service guidance groups the conditions into four blocks. One broken block cancels the other three, however perfect they are, so it pays to walk them in order.

GateRequirement for the 2025 tax yearWhere you check it
1. Income An employee with total salary of 80 million won or less, excluded if comprehensive income exceeds 70 million won Withholding tax receipt, income data on Hometax
2. Household Head of a household owning no house as of the last day of the tax period, 31 December. A household member also qualifies if the head claimed neither the housing fund deduction nor this credit Resident registration abstract, home ownership of everyone in the household
3. The home Within national housing size, 85 square meters, or an officially assessed price of 400 million won or less. Residential officetels and gosiwon are included. Households with three or more children use a 100 square meter floor-area test Floor area on the lease, the public housing price portal
4. Name and address The lease is in the name of the taxpayer or a basic-deduction dependant, and the lease address matches the resident registration address Address on the lease, address on the abstract

The third gate joins its two conditions with or, and people read it as and. A home larger than 85 square meters still qualifies if its assessed price is 400 million won or less, and a home above that price still qualifies if it is within national housing size. Both do not have to be true.

Officetel tenants should look once more at the form of the contract. The guidance puts residential officetels and gosiwon inside the eligible category, but a unit leased on office terms and merely lived in creates a separate mismatch between the contract and the actual use.

Rates and the cap - where the 1.7 million ceiling comes from

Three numbers carry the whole rule. 80 million won is the doorway, 55 million won is where the rate splits, and 10 million won is the ceiling on the rent that counts.

Total salaryComprehensive income provisoCredit rateRent counted, per yearTheoretical maximum credit
55 million won or less Dropped from this band if comprehensive income exceeds 45 million won 17% 10 million won 1.7 million won
Above 55 million up to 80 million won Excluded entirely if comprehensive income exceeds 70 million won 15% 10 million won 1.5 million won
Above 80 million won - Not eligible - 0 won

The trap is that total salary and comprehensive income are tested side by side. With business or rental income on top of a salary, you can sit under 55 million won on salary while comprehensive income passes 45 million, which pushes you from 17 percent down to 15. The same logic knocks you out entirely if salary stays under 80 million but comprehensive income passes 70 million.

The cap sits on the rent, not on the credit. The statute treats rent above 10 million won in a year as if the excess did not exist. Worked examples make the mechanics obvious.

  • Salary 48 million won, rent 600,000 a month, so 7.2 million a year at 17 percent = 1,224,000 won
  • Salary 65 million won, rent 900,000 a month, so 10.8 million a year but only 10 million counts, at 15 percent = 1.5 million won
  • Salary 53 million won but side income lifts comprehensive income to 47 million, rent 500,000 a month, so 15 percent rather than 17 on 6 million = 900,000 won
  • Salary 83 million won means no credit at all, whatever the rent
  • Moving in during May and paying eight months means those eight payments are the base
What enters the calculation is the rent actually paid. Where the contract figure and the transferred amount differ, the evidence governs. Maintenance charges, the deposit, and utilities are not rent.

Address matching - the requirement that fails most often

The wording is that the address on the lease certificate and the address on the resident registration abstract must be the same. It is a short sentence and it disqualifies more people than anything else, for a simple reason: the registered address only changes when you file the move. Postpone that filing and the abstract still shows the old place while the lease shows the new one.

The recurring misunderstanding here involves the fixed date stamp. Tenants who obtained one often assume the tax side is handled too, but the fixed date stamp and this credit serve different purposes. The stamp secures priority for the deposit under the Housing Lease Protection Act, and it does not appear in the eligibility list the National Tax Service publishes for the rent credit. A stamp without a move filing leaves the credit unavailable.

Situations that deserve a second look:

  • The lease names building and unit number while the move filing omitted the unit
  • A multi-household building where the lot number matches but the unit is written differently on the lease and the abstract
  • Keeping your registration at a parent home while only actually living elsewhere, which does not satisfy the test
  • A renewal that changed how the address is written while the abstract was never updated
  • Company dormitories and staff housing where the contracting party is not you

How rent paid before the move filing is treated depends on the facts of the case, so this article does not settle it. If your occupancy date and registration date fall apart in some year, name that year and those payments when you ask the counselling centre or your district tax office.

Head of household or member - one household, one claim

The default subject is the head of a household that owns no house. The proviso is what makes the rule workable: a household member can claim it too, provided the head has claimed neither the housing fund deduction nor this rent credit. That is how a married couple can put the claim on the spouse who is not the registered head.

Read the proviso the other way and the constraint is just as clear. Housing-related relief cannot be split among several people in one household. If the head is already claiming a housing savings or housing lease loan item, a member of the same household cannot pivot to the rent credit. Dual-income couples should compare which housing items each of them is holding before the settlement season, not during it.

The ownership test has a date attached: the last day of the tax period. Selling a home mid-year and being houseless on 31 December reads favourably, while buying in December unsettles the whole year. If a purchase closing is scheduled near year-end, this item belongs in that calculation.

Three documents, and Hometax does the rest

Rent is one of the classic items that does not appear automatically in the simplified year-end settlement service. Unlike medical bills or card spending, nothing arrives on its own; you assemble the evidence and hand it to your employer. The guidance lists three documents.

  1. Resident registration abstract - it proves the address match and the household structure. Issued through the Government24 portal.
  2. Copy of the lease certificate - complete enough to show the tenant name, address, floor area, rent, and term.
  3. Proof of payment - bank transfer receipts, deposit slips, anything showing the money actually left.

The third is where things break in practice. Cash handed over without a receipt leaves no way to prove payment at all, and transfers routed through a family account or bundled without a memo become hard to attribute later. A few habits keep it clean.

  • Send rent from the tenant own account to the landlord account, which is the tidiest possible trail
  • Put the month covered in the transfer memo so payments can be matched one by one
  • If the rent changed from what the lease says, keep the amended agreement in writing
  • Bundling maintenance charges into one transfer makes the rent portion hard to isolate
  • After a mid-year move you need the lease and the abstract for both addresses

Housing rent cash receipts - a separate road you cannot walk twice

The credit is not the only way rent reaches your tax return. Through Hometax you can request a housing rent cash receipt, which records the payments as cash receipts and feeds them into the credit card and cash receipt spending deduction. The distinguishing feature of this road is that it does not need the landlord to cooperate. Both the National Tax Service pages and the plain-language legal information service state that issuance is possible even where the landlord is not a registered rental business, and the landlord consent is not required.

The pivotal point is that the two cannot be combined. Guidance states that rent already used for the monthly rent tax credit cannot also feed the card-and-cash-receipt spending deduction, and that the worker selects whichever single route is more favourable. That makes the decision short.

  • If you clear the eligibility gates, the credit is usually stronger, because it hits the tax directly rather than the income.
  • If salary exceeds 80 million won or a housing condition blocks you, the cash receipt road is the remaining card.
  • If your card spending already fills its deduction ceiling, the extra cash receipt amount may add little real benefit.
  • The cash receipt request needs a lease copy, resident registration abstract, and transfer records, essentially the same folder as the credit.

If a deposit loan is also in the picture

Where part of the deposit is borrowed and the rest is paid monthly, a second item enters the frame: the deduction for principal and interest repaid on a housing lease loan. It rests on Article 52 of the Income Tax Act, and the National Tax Service describes it as 40 percent of the repayment, capped together with the housing savings deduction at 4 million won a year.

Its conditions are strict and two of them decide most cases.

  • Money flow: for loans from financial institutions the funds must be paid straight into the landlord account. Guidance says a worker who received the loan into a personal account and then forwarded it is outside the deduction.
  • Timing: the borrowing must happen within three months either side of the earlier of the occupancy date on the lease and the registration date on the abstract. A loan taken out well after moving in fails here.

The rent credit and this deduction attach to different payments, one to the monthly rent and one to the loan repayment, so the underlying spending never overlaps. The household rule from earlier does resurface, though. A household member can claim the rent credit only if the head has not claimed a housing fund deduction, so splitting the loan item onto one person and the rent item onto another inside the same household runs into that constraint. If you intend to hold both, sort out whose name is on what, and who is registered as head, before the season starts.

If you missed it - the claim window is five years

Skip the paperwork at your employer and the rent credit simply passes you by. Passing by is not the same as being gone. Article 45-2 of the Framework Act on National Taxes allows a request for correction of the tax base and tax amount within five years after the statutory filing deadline, and the provision is applied to employees whose tax was handled through year-end settlement. For them the statute reads the starting point as the day after the payment deadline for the year-end settlement or withholding tax.

The procedure itself is not difficult.

  1. Log into Hometax and open the request for correction menu, then pick the tax year.
  2. Attach that year lease certificate copy, resident registration abstract, and transfer evidence.
  3. Submit the request showing the settlement recalculated with the rent credit added.
  4. The district tax office reviews it and notifies the outcome. Filing does not trigger an automatic refund.
  5. With several years in play, handle the oldest one first, since that is the one about to expire.

Plenty of tenants skip the rent credit because they would rather not show a lease to their employer. A request for correction is filed directly with the tax authority and does not pass through the company, which is exactly why some people deliberately leave it out of the settlement and claim it separately later.

Common failure points, and the line you must not cross

Turning everything above around to the failure side compresses it usefully.

  • No move filing - the lease address and registered address differ, so the test never even starts
  • Assuming a fixed date stamp is enough - it protects the deposit, it is not an eligibility condition here
  • A member claiming while the head already holds housing relief - blocked by the proviso
  • Passing on salary but failing on comprehensive income - always check when you have side or rental income
  • The lease is in a name that is neither yours nor a dependant - typically a friend or sibling signing for you
  • No payment evidence - cash with no receipt leaves the amount unverifiable
  • Counting maintenance charges as rent - only rent qualifies
  • Buying a home late in the year - the no-house test on 31 December breaks
  • Claiming both cash receipts and the credit - the same rent cannot be used twice
Manufacturing documents to obtain relief is not tax planning, it is a crime. Writing a lease showing a figure other than the real one, filing a residence move to an address you do not live at, or fabricating transfer records for rent that was never paid, breaches tax legislation and the Resident Registration Act. Where an excessive claim is established, the credit is collected back with penalty tax. If a condition is unmet, the only workable answer is to put that condition right. This article does not describe any other route.

How this compares with rental tax relief elsewhere

Readers outside Korea often ask whether this kind of relief is unusual. Several countries do something for tenants, but the machinery differs. On character alone the picture splits like this.

  • United States: the federal income tax carries no general deduction or credit for tenants as such. Several states run their own renter credit or property-tax refund style programmes, each with its own income tests and forms, administered by the state revenue department rather than the IRS.
  • Japan: the headline housing item in the income tax is built around acquiring a home with a loan, the special deduction for housing loan balances. Rent as such does not sit in that same slot, and employer housing allowances or company housing are treated as a payroll taxation question rather than a deduction.
  • China: individual income tax includes housing rent among the special additional deductions. Unlike the Korean approach of multiplying actual spending by a rate, it works as a fixed-amount subtraction, and its interaction with the housing loan interest item is restricted.
  • Korea: a rate applied to rent actually paid, then removed from the tax itself. The income, housing, and address conditions are tight, but clearing them is worth real money.

All of these change often and vary by region. The comparison above is about character only, so confirm any amount or condition with the tax authority of the country concerned.

What could not be confirmed

These points could not be pinned down from the published National Tax Service and statute pages, so no figure or firm statement about them appears above.

  • The filing deadline for housing rent cash receipts - the plain-language legal page consulted states no deadline. Read the guidance on the Hometax application screen.
  • Treatment of rent paid before the move filing - how much of it counts in a year where occupancy and registration dates diverge turns on the facts, and is an individual enquiry.
  • Values for the 2026 tax year, settled in 2027 - everything here is stated for the 2025 tax year. Later amendments must be rechecked in the Hometax guidance for that year.
  • The assessment date behind the 400 million won price test - which point in time governs, contract date or a date within the tax period, is the kind of detail that can be disputed case by case.

In short - three things to check today

The conditions look elaborate, but people fail in a handful of predictable places. Start with what you can verify right now.

  1. Pull the abstract and compare it with the lease character by character. Building and unit must match. If they do not, sorting out the move filing comes first.
  2. Check that this year rent left a transfer trail. If any of it was cash, switch to bank transfers from here on.
  3. Look back at five years of settlements for a year where rent was left out. If there is one, the oldest is the one to claim.

For someone who meets the conditions, this item is worth up to 1.7 million won a year. Break a single condition and it is zero regardless of the rent. That single condition is usually the address on the abstract.
