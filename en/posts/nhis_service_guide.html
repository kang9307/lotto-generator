This article summarizes the statutes in force as of October 2026 and only those service names visible on the NHIS website's main page, and individual eligibility rulings and refund amounts are decided by the NHIS.

Four Channels and Their Division of Roles

Contact with the National Health Insurance Service splits into four channels: the website (nhis.or.kr), The Health Insurance app, the main line 1577-1000, and branch offices with the inquiry desk. The Act names the NHIS as the insurer of national health insurance (Article 13) and lists among its duties the management of subscriber and dependent qualification, the imposition and collection of premiums and other charges, and the management of insurance benefits (Article 14, Paragraph 1).

The main page carries a “Civil Services > Service Finder” menu at the top, and the body has Personal Login, Workplace Login, and a “Frequently Used Services” block. The personal tab holds Checkup Eligibility Lookup, Refund Inquiry/Application, Qualification Acquisition/Loss Certificate, Premium Payment Certificate, Premium Payment, and Form Library. The table below gathers, channel by channel, the items whose names appear on the main page, rendered here in English.

The rule for choosing a channel is simple. If the service name is in “Frequently Used Services,” log in as an individual and enter that menu, and if the interpretation of a program or the scope of documents is unclear, ask 1577-1000 or the online inquiry desk. For a branch, find the contact details and inquire first, then visit.

The reference points are the Act as in force from January 2, 2026 (Act No. 21065), the Enforcement Rule as in force from September 28, 2026 (Ministry of Health and Welfare Ordinance No. 1202), and the Notice on Verification of Identity and Eligibility for Health Insurance, as in force from July 1, 2025 (Notice No. 2025-70). Statutes are amended from time to time, so a citation should carry the effective date of the current text.

Article numbers are often mixed up. Article 12 is the health insurance card, Article 13 the insurer (the NHIS), Article 44(2) the out-of-pocket cap, Article 47 the claim and payment of benefit costs, Article 81-4 proof of premium payment, Article 86 the offset and refund of premiums and similar charges, and Article 87 objections. Article 69 concerns premium collection and Article 79 the notice of payment, which are different provisions from the certificates.

Certificate Menus — What Is Listed and the Basis for Payment Proof

The items on the NHIS main page that read as certificates or confirmations are listed below.

  • Qualification Acquisition/Loss Certificate: It sits in the personal tab under Frequently Used Services. Managing the qualification of subscribers and dependents is a duty assigned to the NHIS by Article 14, Paragraph 1, Item 1.
  • Premium Payment Certificate: It sits in the personal tab under Frequently Used Services, in the same block as Premium Payment.
  • Health Insurance Payment Certificate: It is in the Workplace tab and carries a “moves to another site” mark.
  • Four Major Insurances Full-Payment Certificate: It is in the Workplace tab and carries a “moves to another site” mark.
  • Certificate Issuance Verification: It is in the Workplace tab and carries a “moves to another site” mark.
  • Workplace Employee Lookup, Workplace Coverage Notice, Premium Billing Statement Lookup: These are the first items in the Workplace tab.

The legal basis for payment proof is Article 81-4. A payer who receives consideration for a contract designated by presidential decree, such as construction, manufacturing, purchase, or services, from the state, a local government, or a public institution must prove payment of premiums, late charges, and delinquency disposition costs (Paragraph 1). The authority or public institution in charge of the contract may, with the payer's consent, instead query the NHIS to confirm payment (Paragraph 2).

Under Article 79, a notice of payment is a document stating the type of premium to be collected, the amount due, and the deadline and place of payment. A payment notice and a proof of payment rest on different provisions.

Refund Inquiries and Claims — Out-of-Pocket Cap Excess and Overpayments

The main page has a “Do you have refunds you haven't received?” prompt and a Refund Inquiry/Application menu. Refunds arise on three main legal bases, and a statute of limitations runs alongside them.

  • Excess over the out-of-pocket cap (Article 44, Paragraph 2): When the total of the co-payments a person bears in a year and the amounts borne for care or childbirth costs under Article 49, Paragraph 1 (less amounts already paid as care expenses) exceeds the out-of-pocket cap, the NHIS bears the excess. The NHIS must notify the person of the excess and pay it. The cap is set according to the subscriber's income level and other factors (Paragraph 3), and the calculation and payment methods are delegated to presidential decree (Paragraph 4).
  • Overpaid premiums (Article 86): Any overpayment is first applied to premiums, late charges, or delinquency disposition costs under presidential decree (Paragraph 1), the remainder is refunded to the payer (Paragraph 2), and interest set by presidential decree is added (Paragraph 3).
  • Excess co-payments paid (Article 47, Paragraph 3): When the co-payment already paid exceeds the amount notified after review, the NHIS deducts the excess from the amount owed to the medical institution and pays it to the subscriber. The NHIS may offset that amount against premiums the subscriber owes (Paragraph 5).
  • Limitation period (Article 91, Paragraph 1): The right to a refund of overpaid premiums, late charges, and surcharges (Item 2) and the right to recover excess co-payments (Item 5) expire if not exercised for 3 years. Notice or demand for premiums, and a claim for insurance benefits or benefit costs, interrupt the period (Paragraph 2).

Income reconciliation also leads to refunds. The “Health Insurance Premium Income Reconciliation System” card on the main page explains that when adjustment of premiums is requested for income (business, employment, interest, dividend, pension, and other income), the premium for the adjusted year is recalculated in November of the following year using income confirmed by the National Tax Service and others, and the difference is charged or refunded.

The “Out-of-Pocket Cap System” card carries an “Available Online” mark and lists local subscribers, employee subscribers, and dependents as eligible. The cap by income bracket is set by presidential decree, so the amount for each subscriber follows the NHIS's lookup result.

Dependent Criteria and Filing Deadlines for Employee Subscribers

Under Article 2, Paragraph 1 of the Enforcement Rule, the recognition standard for dependents is to meet both the support criteria of Annexed Table 1 and the income and property criteria of Annexed Table 1-2. What follows reflects the Rule in force as of October 2026 (effective September 28, 2026), with Annexed Table 1 last amended on March 6, 2018 and Annexed Table 1-2 on April 23, 2025, according to the text. The NHIS decides whether a given person qualifies.

Support criteria (Annexed Table 1)

  • A spouse is recognized whether living together or not.
  • A parent (including a parent's remarried spouse) is recognized if living together. If not living together, recognition requires that no sibling lives with the parent, or that any such sibling has no pay or income. A grandparent or higher ascendant is recognized if living together, and if not, only when no descendant lives with that ascendant or any such descendant has no pay or income.
  • A child who is a direct descendant is recognized if living together. If not living together, the child must be unmarried (including divorced or widowed), and a divorced or widowed child must have no children, or children without pay or income.
  • A grandchild or lower descendant is recognized if living together and the parents are absent or have no pay or income, and if not living together, if unmarried (including divorced or widowed) with no parents.
  • The spouse of a direct descendant is recognized only when living together, and is not recognized when not living together.
  • A direct descendant of the subscriber's spouse is recognized if living together and unmarried (including divorced or widowed), and is not recognized when not living together.
  • A parent or grandparent of the spouse is recognized if living together, and if not, only when no sibling of the spouse or descendant living with them exists, or any such person has no pay or income.
  • A sibling must be under 30, 65 or older, a registered disabled person under the Welfare of Persons with Disabilities Act, a person of national merit with a disability grade, or a veterans compensation recipient with a disability grade. If living together, the sibling must be unmarried (including divorced or widowed) with no parents or parents without pay or income, and if not, unmarried with no other siblings besides the subscriber and parents, or with parents and co-resident siblings who have no pay or income.

Income criteria (Annexed Table 1-2, Item 1)

  • The total of the incomes listed in Article 41, Paragraph 1 of the Enforcement Decree, calculated from the income data under Paragraph 3 of that Article, must be 20 million KRW or less a year.
  • There must be no business income. However, if the person falls under one of the following and total business income is 5 million KRW or less a year, it is treated as none: no business registration (unless there is housing rental income), registration as a disabled person, a person of national merit with a disability grade, or a veterans compensation recipient with a disability grade.
  • If the NHIS recognizes, from the relevant data, that the person meets the criteria apart from business income from a housing redevelopment project, the criteria are deemed met.
  • If the applicant is married, both spouses must meet these criteria.

Property criteria (Annexed Table 1-2, Item 2)

  • A person under Items 1 to 9 of Annexed Table 1 needs a combined property tax base of 540 million KRW or less, or above 540 million and up to 900 million KRW with total income of 10 million KRW or less a year.
  • A sibling under Item 10 of Annexed Table 1 needs a combined property tax base of 180 million KRW or less.

Acquisition timing and filing (Rule Art. 2(2) and (4), Form No. 1)

  • A newborn acquires qualification on the date of birth. Filing within 90 days of the subscriber's acquisition or change date gives qualification from that date, and filing later gives qualification from the date the form is submitted to the NHIS. If the delay beyond 90 days was due to unavoidable causes outside the person's responsibility as the NHIS determines, such as a natural disaster, illness, or accident, qualification is back-dated to the acquisition or change date.
  • The employee subscriber submits Form No. 1 to the NHIS with attachments. A family relations register certificate (1 copy) is needed only when the resident registration extract cannot confirm the criteria, and documents for disabled persons, persons of national merit, or veterans compensation recipients and for redevelopment are needed only where relevant. If the NHIS can confirm matters from data it receives under Article 96 of the Act, no form is needed.
  • There is no fee for the form, and the processing period is 3 days, or 7 days if the relationship between subscriber and dependent cannot be confirmed from public records.
  • For overseas Koreans, one resident registration extract; for foreign nationals, one copy of the alien registration card or alien registration certificate (for ethnic Koreans with foreign nationality, a copy of the domestic residence report card or its certificate). These are required only when the NHIS cannot confirm them through a data request.

Loss of qualification (Rule Art. 2(3))

  • Qualification is lost the day after death, on the day the subscriber loses qualification, on the day the person acquires qualification as another subscriber or another subscriber's dependent, and the day after the person files a loss report.
  • It is also lost the day after the NHIS confirms that the criteria are not met. If business or similar income is reported and the income criteria are found unmet, this is the last day of the month following the month the income arose, and if it was not reported and the NHIS finds it, the last day of that month. If acquisition by false or improper means is confirmed, the acquisition date applies.

Two points of eligibility are easy to state too bluntly. Even with a business registration, a disability registration or a disability grade as a person of national merit or veterans compensation recipient treats business income of 5 million KRW or less a year as none. The same exception for those without a business registration does not apply if there is housing rental income.

Clinic ID Checks and Accepted Identity Documents

Article 12 makes presentation of the health insurance card the rule (Paragraph 2), but the card need not be submitted if the institution can confirm eligibility with a resident registration card (including the mobile version), a driver's license, a passport, or another identity document set by ministerial ordinance (Paragraph 3). Paragraph 4 provides that the medical institution must verify identity and eligibility with the card or an identity document, with an exception where verification is difficult for a justifiable reason set by ministerial ordinance. The patient's presentation duty is in Paragraphs 2 and 3, and the institution's verification duty in Paragraph 4.

Paragraph 4 was added by the amendment of May 19, 2023 (Act No. 19420), and the supplementary provisions state that the amended Article 12 takes effect after one year from promulgation. Enforcement Rule Article 7-2 is marked as newly inserted on May 28, 2024. As a rule, the institution verifies eligibility through the information system the NHIS operates and, where that is unavoidable, by a method set by the NHIS president (Rule Art. 7-2(1)).

Category Accepted means Basis and notes

A stated validity period must not have lapsed

Other methods A certificate proving crisis pregnancy, and a certificate of confinement issued by the head of a facility Notice Art. 2 Items 3 and 4
Cases where the check is waived The nine exceptions in Article 3 of the Notice (list below), and emergencies where delay in care would risk life or body Rule Art. 7-2(2) Item 1 (emergencies and the like) and Item 2 (delegation to the Notice)

Article 3 of the Notice, under Rule Article 7-2, Paragraph 2, Item 2, sets the following nine exceptions to the identity and eligibility check.

  • The patient is under 19.
  • Care is given within 6 months of the day identity and eligibility were checked at the same institution (for inpatient care, during the stay and within 6 months after discharge).
  • A pharmacy or the Korea Rare and Essential Drug Center dispenses a drug on a prescription from a doctor, dentist, or oriental medicine doctor.
  • The institution receives a referral from, or a return of the patient from, another institution under Article 6 of the Rules on Standards for National Health Insurance Care Benefits.
  • Care is given to a person with a severe disability under Article 2 of the Enforcement Rule of the Welfare of Persons with Disabilities Act.
  • Care is given to a person holding a grade under Article 7, Paragraph 1, Items 1 to 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Long-Term Care Insurance Act for Older Persons.
  • Care is given to a pregnant woman under Article 2, Item 1 of the Maternal and Child Health Act.
  • Care is given under Article 44 or 50 of the Act on the Improvement of Mental Health and the Support for Welfare Services for Mental Illness.
  • At a general hospital or tertiary hospital, care is given to a person registered for the special calculation provision for a condition with a 5-year special period (including cases that reach 5 years or more by extension or re-registration; added by Notice No. 2025-70, effective July 1, 2025).

A reason outside the list is tested against Rule Article 7-2, Paragraph 2, Item 1: an emergency or similar case where delay in care risks harm to the life or body of the subscriber or dependent, or where presenting the card or an identity document is otherwise difficult. An emergency patient falls under this Rule provision, not under Article 3 of the Notice.

Article 12, Paragraph 5 of the Act bars using documents that proved qualification to receive benefits after qualification is lost, Paragraph 6 bars transferring or lending the card or an identity document so that another person receives benefits, and Paragraph 7 bars receiving such a transfer or loan or using it improperly to receive benefits.

The foot of the website carries “The Health Insurance app QR code” and “Try the app,” and the Enforcement Rule includes a form titled Mobile Health Insurance Card as Form No. 11-2.

Objections and Contact Points — The Deadlines and Format of Article 87

Article 87 of the Act governs challenges to NHIS decisions.

  • Scope: A person who objects to an NHIS decision on subscriber or dependent qualification, premiums, insurance benefits, or benefit costs may file an objection with the NHIS (Paragraph 1). Objections to decisions of the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service on care benefit costs and appropriateness evaluations go to that Service (Paragraph 2).
  • Deadline and format: The objection must be filed within 90 days of learning of the decision, by document (including an electronic document), and cannot be filed after 180 days from the date of the decision (Paragraph 3, main text). It is not limited to paper, and an electronic document is acceptable.
  • Justifiable reason: If the person shows that a justifiable reason prevented filing within that period, the time limits do not apply (Paragraph 3, proviso).
  • Special rule for medical institutions: An objection by a medical institution to a confirmation by the Review and Assessment Service under Article 48 must be filed within 30 days of the notice (Paragraph 4).
  • Details: The method, decision, and notice of decision are set by presidential decree (Paragraph 5).

The website has, under “Public Communication and Participation > Rights Relief,” the menus Objection (Review Request) Guide (Learn More, Check Eligibility, Legal Requirements), Objection (Review Request) Application, and Objection (Review Request) History. The Enforcement Rule carries objection forms as Forms No. 32, 34, and 36.

Branch and foreign-resident matters are found through “About NHIS > Organization > Branch Finder (contacts)” and the Foreign Residents Service Center menu. The attachments for filing a foreign national or overseas Korean as a dependent are the same as the Form No. 1 standard in the preceding section.

The Enforcement Rule also carries as forms an application for installment payment of delinquent health insurance premiums (Form No. 31) and an application for extension of the premium due date (Form No. 28-2). The approval requirements and procedure follow the relevant provisions and branch guidance.

Telling Phishing and Smishing Scams Apart and Staying Safe

NHIS matters such as health checkups, refunds, and identity checks make easy material for impersonation. What follows are general precautions.

  • Check the link address: Before tapping a link in a text, look at whether the address is nhis.or.kr. Do not open shortened URLs or unfamiliar domains, and type the website address in directly.
  • Refuse installation files: Install apps from official markets such as Google Play or the Apple App Store. Do not open installation files (.apk) that arrive by text or messenger.
  • Refuse requests for financial details: Refuse any call or text asking for your account password, security card number, or one-time code.
  • ATM and remote-control requests: A demand, on the pretext of a refund, to go to an ATM or install remote-control software is a textbook fraud.
  • Call back directly: Caller IDs can be spoofed, so do not reply to the number in a message and instead contact the main line 1577-1000 (paid call, weekdays 09:00 to 18:00) or the website's online inquiry desk.

If you tapped a link or installed an app, cut the network with airplane mode and tell the police (112) and your financial institution at once.

What Could Not Be Verified

  • Certificate issuance fees (whether free) and delivery methods (PDF saving, printing, fax, electronic document wallet) could not be verified in the statutes or on the website's main page.
  • A menu named Minwon Yeogiyo and a path of Personal Civil Services > Certificate Issuance and Verification could not be verified on the main page. What appears there is “Civil Services > Service Finder.”
  • Fax issuance requests through 1577-1000, unmanned civil service kiosks, fingerprint-based issuance, and issuance through Government24 could not be verified. Only that 1577-1000 is a paid line open weekdays 09:00 to 18:00 is confirmed on the main page.
  • Individual-use certificate menus named a Qualification Confirmation Certificate, a Near-Poor Co-Payment Reduction Certificate, and a Health Insurance Premium Full-Payment Certificate could not be verified. The main page shows only the Four Major Insurances Full-Payment Certificate in the Workplace tab (moves to another site).
  • Whether the Mobile Health Insurance Card is a separate app from The Health Insurance app, and how the menus inside the app are organized, could not be verified.
  • Settlement after the fact when a patient has no ID and no exception applies (settlement within 14 days of the visit, a 100% co-payment equivalent to non-covered care, and so on) could not be verified in the Act, the Rule, or the Notice.
  • The timing of the out-of-pocket cap notice (around August each year), advance payout account registration with automatic deposit, and an “Appointment Request” menu could not be verified.
  • The scope of work handled at branch counters (new applications for near-poor co-payment reduction, handling for foreign nationals and overseas Koreans, installment payment counseling for arrears) and the documents needed for proxy applications could not be verified.
  • The cap amounts by income bracket, and the extent to which NHIS texts or notices contain links, could not be verified.
