1. Challenges in Comparing AI Music Detectors and the Need for Lineage-Aware Evaluation

In general, rapid advancements across audio signal processing and deep learning have dramatically improved the acoustic quality and production fidelity of synthetic musical compositions. Neural audio generators can now synthesize natural vocal expressions, harmonic balances, and multi-track mixing structures that rival human-produced recordings. In response to these developments, streaming distributors, record labels, and copyright licensing bodies have sought automated detection technologies capable of verifying track origins and identifying artificial acoustics. Consequently, research institutions and engineering teams have proposed diverse classifier architectures designed to flag synthetic acoustic artifacts.

However, comparing the accuracy metrics published across separate detection systems has historically presented severe methodological difficulties. Individual models are trained on distinct, non-standardized audio corpora, and their published performance figures often rely on test cohorts that differ fundamentally in difficulty, genre distribution, and generation engine lineage. A detector optimized exclusively against older diffusion models may exhibit brittle failure modes when confronted with novel transformer architectures. Conversely, benchmarks claiming near-perfect accuracy often achieve those numbers simply by evaluating easily separable acoustic samples under artificial conditions.

The primary source describes ArtifactBench v2 as a "lineage-aware, metadata-first evaluation suite for AI-generated music detectors." Crucially, the project is not a single classifier intended to detect deepfake songs in isolation. Instead, it serves as an open-source benchmarking framework designed to evaluate and verify whether multiple distinct detection models are measured fairly, reproducibly, and robustly. The repository emphasizes that standard aggregate detector scores fail to provide meaningful comparisons when underlying experimental protocols remain unstandardized.

According to the project documentation, aggregate accuracy scores become uninterpretable due to three pervasive procedural flaws. First, models frequently evaluate differing subsets of test files in silence, dropping unsupported codecs or difficult lengths without transparent logging. Second, evaluators often tune their decision thresholds directly on test data, introducing severe data leakage that artificially inflates reported precision. Third, detectors frequently suffer from unrecorded upstream training overlap, where evaluation tracks unintentionally duplicate audio seen during pre-training.

In the interpretation of this article, methodological rigor in audio classification benchmarks is vastly more consequential than minor differences in neural network depth. If an evaluation harness quietly ignores files that encounter decoding exceptions, an unstable model that crashes on complex polyphonic audio will appear superior to a robust model that evaluates every difficult clip. ArtifactBench v2 addresses these systemic distortions by freezing an ordered evaluation cohort and enforcing strict metadata-driven protocols.

2. Cohort Composition of 828 Tracks and the Three-Way Split Structure

The frozen primary protocol of ArtifactBench v2 consists of an ordered cohort containing exactly 828 audio tracks. The original documentation states that this curated set comprises 605 AI-generated tracks alongside 223 authentic real-world tracks. Rather than relying on an unverified, scraping-based audio dump, the benchmark utilizes a tightly controlled cohort specifically engineered to challenge detectors across diverse synthesis paradigms while maintaining a grounded baseline of human musicianship.

To ensure evaluation integrity and prevent information leakage across operational phases, the 828 tracks are strictly partitioned into three distinct subsets prior to running model comparisons: a calibration set, a validation set, and a sealed test set. The authors explicitly confirm that the public v2 release does not introduce new arbitrary audio bundles, ensuring that all participating researchers evaluate the exact same frozen cohort.

This three-way partitioning scheme establishes rigorous experimental guarantees through the following structural rules:

  • All 828 tracks are tracked through ordered metadata entries and indexed via cryptographically secure SHA-256 digests.
  • The ratio of 605 synthetic tracks to 223 authentic tracks reflects real-world operational distributions in catalog intake queues.
  • The public distribution avoids distributing large raw audio bundles directly, relying instead on digest-based runtime manifest bindings.
  • Historical v1.1 and v1.2 development files remain preserved in dedicated repository directories but are separated from the frozen v2 protocol.

In the interpretation of this article, including a clearly defined cohort of 223 genuine human recordings is vital for industrial deployment viability. In production streaming platforms, the most catastrophic failure mode of an AI detector is a false positive—flagging a human composer's legitimate work as synthetic. Such errors jeopardize distributor contracts, stall royalties, and create legal liabilities. By isolating authentic tracks within a sealed test set, the benchmark ensures that detectors cannot sacrifice genuine music to boost synthetic catch rates.

3. Threshold Calibration and Inference Failure Isolation Protocols

A foundational innovation within ArtifactBench v2 is its uncompromising threshold selection protocol. The primary documentation specifies that "thresholds are selected on calibration only, maximizing TPR at FPR <= 5%." This rule eliminates the widespread practice of tuning operating thresholds on test splits after inspecting classification outputs.

In machine learning evaluation, altering decision thresholds based on test set distributions invalidates benchmark integrity, transforming an evaluation of generalizability into a measure of test set over-fitting. ArtifactBench forces every model to establish its decision boundary strictly within the calibration subset. Furthermore, by constraining the false positive rate to 5% or below, the benchmark mirrors commercial requirements where false alarms must remain tightly bounded while maximizing the true positive detection rate.

Equally critical is the protocol's handling of computational exceptions. The original documentation mandates that "inference failures remain outside classification metrics and are reported as bounds." If a detector encounters a broken audio header, memory exhaustion, or signal dimension mismatch, the benchmark does not discard the track or silently adjust the metric denominator.

  • Operating threshold discovery is strictly confined to the calibration partition, ensuring zero contamination of the sealed test set.
  • The 5% false positive rate ceiling acts as an industrial safeguard against false accusations against human musical creations.
  • Inference failures are isolated from core accuracy metrics and exported as structured failure counts and analytical ranges.
  • Runtime exceptions, decoding errors, and model crashes are prevented from distorting true classification metrics.

In the interpretation of this article, isolating inference failures into distinct operational bounds represents honest systems engineering. When an audio classifier fails to process noisy codecs or irregular sample rates, removing those failures from the test set artificially inflates the model's reported accuracy. By reporting failure rates as explicit ranges outside classification scores, ArtifactBench enables production teams to assess architectural robustness alongside raw predictive precision.

4. Evaluation of Four Baseline Models and Common Intersection Comparison

To demonstrate standardized comparative evaluation, ArtifactBench v2 incorporates four baseline detection models spanning diverse machine learning architectures: ArtifactNet (ONNX), SpecTTTra, Deezer ISMIR, and MoM-CLAM (CLAM). These models represent distinct paradigms in audio representations, from convolutional and spectrogram transformers to metric-learning embeddings.

To maintain absolute statistical validity, the original documentation enforces that "paired metrics use the common successfully scored test-ID intersection." If Model A successfully evaluates 800 files while Model B only parses 750, calculating raw averages across differing denominators introduces severe evaluation bias. The benchmark dynamically identifies the exact subset of audio IDs successfully processed by all evaluated candidates before generating paired head-to-head metrics.

The paired intersection comparison protocol ensures statistical parity through several procedural guarantees:

  • Direct 1-to-1 comparative scores are calculated exclusively on the set of test audio IDs successfully scored by all models.
  • Any file triggering a decode failure or memory error in any single candidate is omitted from the paired intersection subset.
  • Uniform signal preprocessing standards, including a center crop policy (--crop-policy center), are applied across all models.
  • Run outputs are saved in structured directories (results/four_model/) with individual model telemetry and unified manifests.

In the interpretation of this article, this intersection-based comparison is the single most important design element separating ArtifactBench v2 from conventional machine learning leaderboards. Standard benchmarks frequently reward fragile models that drop difficult audio tracks by calculating averages only over their surviving subsets. By enforcing evaluation across the exact intersection of successful tracks, ArtifactBench guarantees genuine parity across all competing algorithms.

5. Environment Setup and Custom Detector Registration via BenchModel

ArtifactBench provides a streamlined, Python-based installation and execution workflow designed for immediate experimental reproducibility. Researchers clone the public repository and install dependencies in editable mode inside a standard virtual environment. To support audio codec invariance testing across diverse formats, the system requires the ffmpeg binary to be available on the operating system PATH.

To eliminate version discrepancies and prevent upstream drift, the project explicitly pins its SONICS framework dependency to commit 9156ffad151f797c71556923c4a02fa01fa8fc91. When running CLAM with its dedicated feature extractors, users install supplementary dependencies via the clam extra flag.

# Clone the repository and navigate into directory
git clone https://github.com/Intrect-io/artifactbench
cd artifactbench

# Create and activate Python virtual environment
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate

# Install dependencies in editable mode
pip install -e .

Following environment setup, users execute audio binding scripts to connect the evaluation manifest with upstream audio files verified against SHA-256 digests. ArtifactNet's ONNX runtime weights are automatically fetched from HuggingFace (intrect/artifactnet), simplifying deployment. Executing a four-model comparative evaluation is accomplished via the following benchmark command:

# Execute benchmark comparison across four models simultaneously
python -m artifactbench.bench \
    --model artifactnet --model spectttra --model deezer_ismir --model clam \
    --clam-repo ./MoM-CLAM \
    --clam-ckpt ./MoM-CLAM/model_wts/best_model_triplet_loss_margin_0.2.pth \
    --manifest artifactbench_v2_runtime_manifest.json \
    --split all --n-per-source 0 --n-codec-pair 0 --crop-policy center \
    --output results/four_model

The framework also supports the integration of external detection models. The documentation indicates that third-party developers can register custom detectors by implementing the abstract `BenchModel` interface. Once wrapped in this standard interface, any custom classifier receives full access to ArtifactBench's automated calibration routines, failure logging, and paired intersection comparisons against the four default baselines.

6. Licensing Architecture and Separation of Weights and Datasets

ArtifactBench establishes a modular legal and architectural separation between its execution code, model weights, and underlying audio datasets. According to project metadata, the benchmark runner codebase and evaluation harnesses are released under the permissive open-source MIT license.

In contrast, model checkpoints and audio evaluation cohorts remain subject to separate licensing terms. For instance, ArtifactNet model weights are hosted independently within HuggingFace's model hub (intrect/artifactnet), while evaluation metadata is published on the HuggingFace dataset hub (datasets/intrect/artifactbench). Third-party models such as CLAM require users to clone upstream repositories and acquire checkpoints under their respective licenses.

  • Benchmark runner and evaluation harness codebase: Distributed under the open-source MIT license.
  • Individual detector weights and checkpoints: Governed by original author and upstream project licenses.
  • Audio cohort metadata and cryptographic digests: Maintained independently on the HuggingFace dataset hub.

In the interpretation of this article, this decoupling is an essential structural strategy for audio research. Distributing recorded music involves complex copyright, neighboring rights, and publicity protections. Bundling full audio binaries inside code repositories creates immediate legal vulnerabilities. By adopting a metadata-first architecture based on cryptographic hashes and runtime manifest bindings, ArtifactBench protects open-source contributors while establishing a clean, sustainable evaluation standard.
