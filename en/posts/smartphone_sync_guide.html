The Complete Guide to Syncing Your Smartphone and PC

From Windows Phone Link to Apple Continuity and KDE Connect - a task-by-task guide to connecting your devices

Jul 9, 2026 Tech & IT

Introduction: Why Sync Your Smartphone and PC Now

Smartphones and PCs are good at different things. A phone goes everywhere with you, catching notifications and snapping photos, but its screen is small and long typing is awkward. A PC has a big screen and comfortable input devices, but you can't carry it around. Use them separately and you end up digging your phone out of your pocket to check one text message, or hunting for a cable just to move a photo onto your computer. Syncing the two removes that friction.

As of July 2026, the tools for doing this are already mature. Windows users can handle Android notifications, messages, photos, calls, and even some phones' app screens right from the PC using Microsoft's Phone Link. Inside Apple's ecosystem, an iPhone and a Mac feel like a single device thanks to Continuity - the umbrella name for Handoff, Universal Clipboard, and AirDrop. And when the ecosystems don't overlap, as with Android and Mac, Google's services and open-source tools like KDE Connect fill the gap.

This guide walks through what's available for each OS combination, then breaks it down by task - file transfer, notification mirroring, clipboard sharing, photo and contact backup, and tethering - so you can pick the most practical method for what you actually need. It also covers security and privacy considerations, a beginner setup checklist, and frequently asked questions.

1. Windows Phone Link - Android and iPhone

1.1 Installing and pairing

Phone Link is a Microsoft app built into Windows 11 (and some versions of Windows 10); it was previously called "Your Phone." Setup is straightforward: open the Phone Link app from the Windows Start menu, install the "Link to Windows" companion app on Android or the Phone Link companion app on iPhone, then pair by scanning the on-screen QR code or signing in with your Microsoft account.

1.2 What you can do with Android

Connecting an Android device gives you the broadest feature set. From the PC screen you can read and reply to text messages, view recent photos, make and receive calls, and manage notifications. On select phones where the manufacturer has pre-installed the "Link to Windows" service, you can also use app streaming (mirroring), which pops an Android app's own screen into a PC window you control with mouse and keyboard. A contact-sync feature that syncs contacts to Teams for Life has also been added.

1.3 Limits when connecting an iPhone

An iPhone can also connect through Phone Link, but the scope is narrower. Reading and replying to texts, making and receiving calls, and managing notifications all work just as they do on Android. However, app streaming and screen mirroring are not available due to iOS policy. This is a structural difference, not a Microsoft setting - Apple simply does not allow third-party apps to remotely control the iOS screen. If you want to control your phone's screen from a PC, an iPhone owner is better served by looking at Apple's own Mac integration instead.

2. Apple's Ecosystem - iPhone and Mac Continuity

2.1 Check the requirements first

Apple's Continuity features share a common set of requirements. Every device must be signed in with the same Apple Account, and that account must have two-factor authentication turned on. Bluetooth and Wi-Fi must be enabled on each device, and devices generally need to be within about 10 meters (30 feet) of each other. Each device also needs to be running a supported, up-to-date OS version. If even one of these is missing, Handoff or Universal Clipboard can suddenly stop working, so these four items are the first thing to check when something breaks.

2.2 Handoff

Handoff lets you pick up on one Apple device exactly where you left off on another. Start writing an email on your iPhone, sit down at your Mac, and an icon appears in the Dock that lets you continue the same email there. It works with many of Apple's own apps - Calendar, Clock, Contacts, FaceTime, Freeform, Keynote, Mail, Maps, News, Notes, Numbers, Pages, Reminders, Safari, and Stocks - as well as third-party apps built to support it.

2.3 Universal Clipboard

Universal Clipboard lets you copy text, images, photos, or video on one Apple device and paste it on another - copy a line from a webpage on your iPhone and paste it straight into a document on your Mac. For it to work, the devices need to be near each other, signed in with the same Apple Account, and have Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and Handoff all turned on.

2.4 AirDrop

AirDrop wirelessly sends documents, photos, videos, website links, map locations, and more to a nearby Apple device. Unlike Handoff or Universal Clipboard, AirDrop does not require the two devices to share the same Apple Account. Any nearby Mac, iPhone, or iPad can exchange files even under different accounts, which makes it especially convenient for quickly sharing a photo or two with family or coworkers.

3. Connecting Android and Mac

3.1 Back up photos with Google Photos and view them on a Mac

Because Android and Mac come from different companies, there's no deep integration comparable to Apple's Continuity. The most practical bridge is Google's ecosystem. Turn on Backup & Sync in the Google Photos app on your Android phone, and you can then sign in to photos.google.com on your Mac - no cable required - to view the same photos. Note that this is not two-way sync: deleting a photo in Google Photos leaves the local copy on the Android device untouched, and changes made in the Android app aren't automatically reflected on the Mac either.

3.2 Google Drive for desktop and a 2026 change to know about

The Google Drive desktop app also runs on Mac and can watch a chosen folder in the background to automatically upload photos and videos. As of July 2026, though, there's a change worth knowing. Google stopped allowing new photo backup folder setups through Drive for desktop starting June 15, 2026, and said existing setups would only continue to be supported through August 10, 2026. If you haven't already configured this, you can't start fresh through this route - you'll need to move to the Google Photos app, the web, or another method going forward.

3.3 Third-party apps and direct transfer

If you'd rather drag and drop files directly, third-party Android-to-Mac apps are another option. These tools mount an Android phone on a Mac like an external drive over USB or Wi-Fi, letting you copy whole folders of photos, videos, and documents at once. They're especially useful when you want to move files locally without a cloud account, or move a large batch of files in one go.

4. Cross-Platform Tools That Don't Care About Your OS

4.1 KDE Connect

KDE Connect is an open-source, cross-platform tool that runs on Linux, Windows, macOS, Android, and even iOS through the App Store. Its defining feature is that it communicates directly between devices on the same local network (the same router) rather than routing through a cloud server. It offers clipboard sync, file sharing, notification sync, sending and receiving text messages from a PC, remote input that turns your phone into a mouse, keyboard, or media remote, and a presentation-remote mode. In 2026 it picked up security improvements such as an OpenSSL upgrade and a pairing-protocol version check, along with clipboard sync tweaks that reduce accidental overrides caused by timestamp differences. Keeping data off external servers is a clear advantage for privacy-conscious users.

4.2 Browser-based nearby transfer tools

There are also web-based file transfer tools, like Snapdrop, that need nothing more than a browser. Devices on the same Wi-Fi network discover each other on a webpage and exchange files with no app install required, offering an AirDrop-like experience for OS combinations AirDrop doesn't cover, such as Windows-Android or Linux-iPhone. The exact behavior and security settings vary by tool, so it's worth checking each service's own documentation before use.

4.3 A cross-platform workflow built on your Google account

Another OS-agnostic axis is your Google account itself. Google Photos, Google Drive, Gmail, and the web version of Google Messages are all reachable from a browser regardless of whether you're on Android, iPhone, Windows, or Mac. If you'd rather not lock yourself into one company's ecosystem, treating an account-based cloud service as the shared hub is a practical choice.

5. A Task-by-Task Practical Guide

5.1 File transfer

If both devices are in Apple's ecosystem, AirDrop is the fastest option. For a Windows-Android combination, use Phone Link's photo send/receive feature or KDE Connect's file sharing. For large files or whole folders, a cable-based approach such as uploading to Google Drive or using a third-party transfer app is more reliable.

5.2 Notification mirroring

To avoid missing phone notifications while working on a PC, Windows users can rely on Phone Link's notification feature, while Linux or Mac users can use KDE Connect's notification sync. You can view and even reply to notifications right on the PC screen, cutting down on how often you need to check your phone during meetings or focused work.

5.3 Clipboard sharing

Between an iPhone and a Mac, Universal Clipboard works automatically with no extra setup. For Android with Windows or Linux, turn on the clipboard sync feature in Phone Link or KDE Connect. Text copied on one device can be pasted directly on the other, saving you from retyping links or verification codes.

5.4 Photo and contact backup

For photos, iCloud Photos is the default in Apple's ecosystem and Google Photos is the default on Android. For contacts, syncing to iCloud on iPhone or your Google account on Android means they carry over automatically when you switch devices. It's safer to treat Phone Link's recent-photo view or Teams contact sync as a supplement to these cloud backups rather than a replacement.

5.5 Tethering (hotspot)

When your PC loses internet access, your phone's personal hotspot can bridge the gap. On iPhone, a Mac signed in with the same Apple Account often sees the personal hotspot appear in its Wi-Fi list with no extra setup, while on Android you turn on Wi-Fi hotspot or USB tethering from the tethering settings. Turning it off after use is a good habit to avoid burning through your data plan.

6. Security and Privacy Checklist

The more convenient a feature, the more attention it deserves at the permissions level. First, for apps like Phone Link or KDE Connect that touch texts, notifications, and contacts, review the permission scope requested at install and decline anything unnecessary. Second, it's safer to temporarily turn off clipboard and file sync on public Wi-Fi - even a local-network tool can't fully rule out an unfamiliar device on the same network attempting to pair. Third, always enable two-factor authentication for account-based features like Apple Continuity. Fourth, unpair devices you no longer use from your connected-accounts list. Fifth, if you're using a company-issued device, check your company's policy before mixing personal cloud accounts with work accounts in any sync setup.

7. A Beginner's Setup Checklist

If you're setting this up for the first time, following this order will keep you from getting lost.

  1. Identify your device combination first - Android-Windows, iPhone-Mac, Android-Mac, iPhone-Windows, and so on.
  2. Install the official tool that matches your combination - Phone Link for Windows plus a smartphone, or simply confirm your Apple Account sign-in in Settings for iPhone plus Mac.
  3. Sign both devices into the same account (Microsoft or Apple), and turn on Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.
  4. Complete pairing by scanning the on-screen QR code or signing in as prompted.
  5. Grant only the permissions you actually need - notifications, clipboard, photo access, and so on.
  6. Test one frequently used feature first, such as file transfer or notification mirroring, confirm it works, then turn on the rest one at a time.

Following this order avoids the situation where you grant every permission at once and then can't figure out which one caused a problem later.

Conclusion

There are already several solid ways to connect a smartphone and a PC. If you're inside a single company's ecosystem - Windows plus Android, or iPhone plus Mac - the official tools, Phone Link or Continuity, are all you need. For an Android-Mac combination that spans ecosystems, using your Google account as a shared bridge or a third-party tool works well, and if you want an option that doesn't care about the OS at all, an open-source tool like KDE Connect is the alternative. What matters most is choosing the tool that fits your task and paying as much attention to permissions and account security as you do to convenience. Use this guide, current as of July 2026, as a starting point and turn on the combination that fits your own devices one step at a time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Can Phone Link mirror an iPhone screen the way it does Android?

No. Phone Link supports messages, calls, and notifications on iPhone, but app streaming and screen mirroring are not available due to iOS policy. This is a structural limit, not a Microsoft setting - Apple does not allow third-party apps to remotely control the iOS screen.

Q2. What should I check first when Universal Clipboard suddenly stops working?

Check that both devices are signed in with the same Apple Account, that two-factor authentication is enabled, that Bluetooth and Wi-Fi are both on, and that Handoff is enabled. The connection can also drop if the devices are more than about 10 meters apart.

Q3. There's no official Apple tool for syncing Android with a Mac. What should I do?

The simplest path is to turn on Backup & Sync in Google Photos on your Android phone and sign in to photos.google.com on your Mac. This is not two-way sync, so deletions or edits aren't automatically reflected on the other side. A third-party transfer app is worth considering for frequent large file moves.

Q4. Is KDE Connect safe, and how is it different?

KDE Connect communicates directly between devices on the same local network without a cloud server, which is a clear privacy advantage, and its security protocol and pairing checks have been strengthened recently. Still, stay aware of the possibility of unfamiliar devices attempting to pair on public Wi-Fi.

Q5. Will automatic photo backup through Google Drive keep working?

As of July 2026, there's a change to know about. Google stopped allowing new photo backup folder setups through Drive for desktop from June 15, 2026, with existing setups supported only through August 10, 2026. If you haven't set this up yet, switching to the Google Photos app or web is the safer path.
