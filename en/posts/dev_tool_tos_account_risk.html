When One Sentence in the Terms Can Stop Your Account - Six Clause Types to Read Before Adopting a Dev Tool

What each clause type decides, what is hanging on a single account, and what to secure before you adopt anything

2026-09-05 Dev & Infra

Developers usually weigh three things when adopting a new tool. How well does it work, how much does it cost, and how fast can it be wired in. When the tool ships for free, the second question disappears entirely and the decision speeds up. Download it, sign in with an existing account, and drop it into the pipeline the same afternoon.

Something else gets decided quietly in that single sign-in, though. It decides which account you started using the tool under, and therefore what is already hanging on that account. You attached one tool, and the terms governing that tool now sit alongside your mail, your documents, your authentication, and your cloud storage, all reachable through the same identity.

This article is not about whether a particular company behaved well or badly. It is about what ends up depending on one account when a free developer tool enters a working pipeline, and about the clause types in an agreement that determine that answer. Once you know the clause types, every new tool can be read in the same order, and the parts you skipped announce themselves as specific open risks rather than a vague unease.

This article is not legal advice. What follows is an informational summary of what each type of contract clause decides, grounded in publicly published wording. The validity of any particular agreement, the enforceability of any particular clause, and the outcome of any actual dispute depend on jurisdiction, contract form, and the standing of the parties, and those judgments belong to competent authorities and qualified professionals. Every quoted passage carries the date it was read.

The trigger - two stories that split out of one sentence

In early September 2026 a post appeared on a Korean developer community titled, in translation, the controversy over Google Antigravity banning third-party use and the account suspension terms. The link it points to is a post on X, the platform formerly called Twitter.

The community summary carries four threads. First, that the service terms prohibit reaching the service through third-party tools and permit suspension of both Antigravity and Gemini CLI access. Second, that earlier wording used the ambiguous phrase your account, which spread a worry that an entire Google account could be permanently suspended. Third, that a lead on the Antigravity side clarified the suspension scope means the Antigravity account, and that account-wide terminations had not occurred. Fourth, that the umbrella terms nevertheless contain provisions capable of reaching a whole account for serious or repeated violations of service-specific terms.

Three different layers are tangled together there. There is what the community summarized, what a company representative is reported to have said, and what is actually written on the terms page. All three point at the same event, but they carry very different weight. A statement tells you about practice, and practice does not amend a contract. A summary tells you the direction of a story, and summaries routinely shed the conditions attached to a sentence. What is contractually possible is fixed by the wording alone.

So the rest of this article does not relay the controversy. It relays the wording, and follows only the structural consequences that the wording creates.

Separating the clause from the claim

The Google Antigravity Additional Terms of Service, read on 2026-09-05, run to eight numbered clauses. Clause 6, the one at the center of the story, is four sentences, and each sentence does a different job.

You must not abuse, harm, interfere with, or disrupt the Service. This includes, but is not limited to, using the Service in connection with products not provided by us. Using third party software, tools, or services to access the Service (e.g. using OpenClaw with Antigravity OAuth) is a breach of this Agreement. Such actions may be grounds for suspension or termination of your Antigravity and/or Gemini CLI accounts.
  • The first sentence establishes a parent category: abuse, harm, interference, disruption. Those are four distinct behaviors, and each stands as an independent prohibition on its own terms
  • The second sentence places using the service in connection with products not provided by the operator inside that category, and attaches the phrase but is not limited to. The list is a floor, not a ceiling
  • The third sentence is the most categorical of the four. It states flatly that using third party software, tools, or services to access the Service is a breach of this Agreement, and puts a concrete example in parentheses
  • The fourth sentence states the consequence, and it is not written as a certainty. Such actions may be grounds for suspension or termination, and the object is bounded: your Antigravity and/or Gemini CLI accounts

The headline phrase account suspension and the fourth sentence differ in two places. One is certainty. Saying something may be grounds describes a discretion, not an automatic enforcement pipeline. The other is scope. The object written into the sentence is two service accounts.

Stopping there, however, means having read half of it. Clause 1 of the same document pulls in the umbrella terms. The scope limitation inside an additional-terms document therefore does not become the scope limitation of the entire agreement. That overlap is exactly why layering appears below as the sixth clause type rather than a footnote.

Put plainly: the wording agrees with the summary on the point that third-party access is defined as a breach. The claim that suspension is automatic, and the claim that a whole consumer account is the object, do not come out of this clause. Those two are separate matters that would have to be established in different documents and different provisions.

Type one - permitted use and prohibited use

The first clause type to read is the one that decides what you may and may not do. What it settles is simple: whether the thing you are about to do sits inside or outside the contract.

The distinction to draw while reading is the nature of the list. An enumerated list means behavior outside the list is, in principle, outside the prohibition. An illustrative list means the list is merely examples, and the operative test is the abstract category sitting above it. The marker that separates the two forms is the phrase but is not limited to, and clause 6 above is precisely that form.

Placing other documents alongside it makes the difference in form visible. The Gemini API Additional Terms of Service, effective March 23, 2026 with a page-level last-updated stamp of 2026-04-28, state that you may not use the Services to develop models that compete with the Services, prohibit attempts to reverse engineer, extract or replicate any component of the Services, and add that you may only access the Services, or make API clients available to users, within an available region. The Anthropic Commercial Terms of Service, effective June 17, 2025, prohibit accessing the Services to build a competing product or service, including training competing models or reselling the Services except as expressly approved, prohibit reverse engineering or duplicating the Services, and extend the same prohibition to supporting any third party attempting that conduct.

  • Abstract-category form — broad words such as abuse, interfere, and disrupt establish a category, and examples hang beneath it. Low predictability, wide reach
  • Enumerated-behavior form — concrete acts such as reverse engineering, reselling, and training competing models are listed by name. Anything outside the list moves to a different provision
  • Attached-condition form — a regional restriction constrains not the act but the condition under which it happens. The same usage becomes a breach when the condition slips

When choosing a developer tool, what matters in this type is not the length of the prohibition list but whether your usage pattern reads as falling inside the parent category. Whether an internal batch job counts as automation, whether a call from CI counts as a third-party client, and whether routing through a corporate proxy counts as altering the access method are all decided right here.

Type two - automation and third-party clients

The second type carries unusual weight for developer tools, because a developer default reflex is to wire things together. If there is a CLI, it gets wrapped in a script. If a token comes out, it gets handed to another agent. If calls get frequent, a cache or a proxy appears in front. All three of those are ways of reaching the same resource through a path that is not the official client.

That is exactly why clause 6 of the Antigravity additional terms puts an example in parentheses. It names a specific third-party agent used together with the service OAuth credential. Naming a combination rather than a single product suggests the test being applied is where the authentication credential ends up flowing.

There are three questions worth separating inside this clause type.

  • Is the prohibited thing automation, or an unofficial client? If automation itself is prohibited, an internal scheduler is caught too. If unofficial clients are the target, a script wrapping the official CLI leaves room for a different reading
  • Is there an official API opened under separate terms? Even within one company, a conversational product and a programmatic API are commonly governed by different documents. Where an API path exists, moving automation onto it is the contractually safer option
  • Are free and paid tiers treated differently? Some documents split data handling, quota, and suspension discretion along tier lines

The third question is visibly answered in the documents. The Gemini API additional terms state that for unpaid offerings the provider uses the content you submit and the responses generated to provide, improve, and develop its products and services, while for paid services it states that prompts and responses are not used to improve products. The Antigravity additional terms have the same shape. Clause 3 states that user data, interaction data pertaining to usage, related metadata, and any feedback provided are recorded and stored, collectively called Interactions. Clause 5 states those Interactions are used to evaluate, develop, and improve research, products, services, and machine learning technologies, and that employees and contractors may access, view, review and use them. The same clause notes that a setting exists for changing your preference on how such data is used.

How per-token pricing reshapes tool selection is a separate subject, covered in The Dollars Vanished From Your AI Coding Tool. The concern here is not cost but how an access path gets classified in the contract.

Type three - suspension powers and advance notice

The third type is the spine of this article. A suspension clause settles three things: on what grounds access can be cut, through what procedure, and whether you are told in advance.

Clause 6 of the Antigravity additional terms describes grounds but not procedure. It ends with a single sentence about being grounds for suspension or termination. The procedure lives upstream.

The Google Terms of Service, in the wording effective July 30, 2026, break the grounds apart. There is material or repeated breach of the terms or of service-specific policies. There is action required by law or court order. And there is conduct that causes harm or liability to users, third parties, or the provider, an item that carries examples: hacking, phishing, harassing, spamming, misleading others, or scraping content.

The sentence on notice reads that before taking the described action, the provider will give advance notice when reasonably possible. Exceptions follow, for cases where notice would cause harm, would violate the law, would compromise an investigation, or would compromise the security or integrity of the service.

The practical weight of that sentence sits entirely in the conditional. Advance notice is a principle, not a guarantee. Among the listed exceptions, compromising an investigation and compromising security are both items with wide judgment room. When planning around this, treating the no-notice case as the default is the safer posture.

Setting the Anthropic commercial terms beside it shows a difference in form. That document enumerates suspension grounds with letters and numerals. It covers cases where the provider reasonably believes there is a risk to or attack on any of the Services, where the customer is using the Services in violation of the named compliance, policy, and use-restriction sections, where provision is prohibited by applicable law or would result in a material increase in the cost of providing the Services, and where a vendor suspends or terminates the third-party services required to enable access. That last item is a written acknowledgment that suspension can arrive through no fault of the user at all.

Google Antigravity Additional TermsNo effective date or last-updated line in the documentOne-sentence discretionary form: may be grounds for suspension or terminationNone; deferred to the upstream document
Google Terms of ServiceEffective July 30, 2026Three families of grounds, with examples attachedAdvance notice when reasonably possible, with four stated exceptions

What the table shows is not a ranking but a difference in predictability. An enumerated form lets you calculate in advance whether you are caught; a discretionary form makes that calculation harder while leaving room to respond to abuse patterns nobody had listed yet. Either way, the adopting side has the same job: deciding in advance what survives a suspension.

Type four - export routes and access after suspension

The fourth type determines the actual size of the damage when the first three become real. It settles when and in what format data can be taken out, and whether that route survives after the account stops.

The Antigravity additional terms contain only a deletion route. Clause 3 states you will have the option to delete your Interactions, and directs deletion requests to a named support mailbox. There is no sentence about export in that document.

Where export does appear in the Google Terms of Service, it comes with a condition attached. If the provider stops offering a service, users are given an opportunity to export their content from the account using Google Takeout, subject to applicable law and policies. That sentence hangs off the service-discontinuation scenario, not the account-suspension scenario.

The suspension scenario lives in the support documentation instead. When an account is disabled, you cannot sign in to the provider services and cannot use the sign-in-with-provider feature. On data the phrasing is conditional: if you cannot get in to your account, you may be able to download and save account data from some services. For certain violation types, such as account hijacking and child sexual abuse and exploitation, even that is blocked. Appeals are filed by signing in and starting the process, and for specific violations the provider reviews up to two appeals, after which further submissions are closed automatically. If no action follows, the account is permanently disabled and considered for deletion. The documentation notes that residents of the EU may have additional resolution options available.

The specification of the export tool itself also shapes the plan.

ItemWhat the support documentation statesEffect on planning
CoverageEmail, documents, calendar, photos, videos, registration and account activity, among othersEverything turns on whether tool output falls inside or outside that list
Format and deliveryZip or tgz, delivered by email link or to Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or BoxIf the delivery destination is tied to the same account, the route loses much of its value

The last row is the core of this clause type. Export presupposes a signed-in session. A plan that arranges export after a suspension has the order inverted. Securing an export route is adoption-time work, not incident-time work.

Type five - governing law and dispute resolution

The fifth type settles whose law applies and which court hears the matter if a dispute arises. It does nothing at all on an ordinary day, and then determines the entire realistic range of responses the moment something goes wrong.

The Antigravity additional terms carry no governing-law or dispute clause. The upstream document fills that seat. The Google Terms of Service state that California law will govern the terms and all disputes arising out of or relating to them, and that such disputes resolve exclusively in the federal or state courts of Santa Clara County, California, USA. Two softeners follow. If local law prevents filing in those courts, disputes may be filed locally, and where California law cannot be applied, local law may apply instead.

How a clause like that is treated in Korea is a question of jurisdiction. Article 6 of the Act on the Regulation of Terms and Conditions voids clauses that lose fairness by violating the principle of good faith, and presumes unfairness for clauses that are unreasonably disadvantageous to the customer, that are hard to anticipate in light of the whole circumstances of the transaction type, or that restrict essential contractual rights to the point where the purpose of the contract cannot be achieved. Among the recognized grounds for voidness is content that unreasonably excludes or limits the customer rights of rescission and termination while granting unreasonably broad rights to the provider. Article 3 places on the provider a duty to disclose the terms, furnish a copy, and explain important content, and provides that a provider in breach of that duty cannot assert those terms as part of the contract.

  • A general rule and an individual holding sit on different layers. Whether a given clause is actually void in a real case turns on the contract form, on whether the party is a consumer or a business, and on whether there was room to negotiate
  • Different jurisdictions reach different conclusions. The same wording is treated differently depending on which consumer-protection regime the dispute runs under
  • A choice-of-law agreement does not displace every mandatory local rule. Where that boundary falls is itself set jurisdiction by jurisdiction

General doctrine on unfair standard terms and the routes for asserting voidness are collected separately in Unfair Contract Terms: Complete Guide to Voidance and Dispute Resolution. The concern here is narrower and more practical. In day-to-day terms, whether the work is stopped while the argument runs arrives long before the question of whether the argument can be won.

Type six - how many layers the agreement has

The sixth type decides where to go to read the other five. Most tool agreements are not standalone documents; they are thin documents that pull other documents in by reference.

Clause 1 of the Antigravity additional terms states that three documents govern use of the Service: the Google Terms of Service, these additional terms, and the Privacy Policy. Clause 2 bundles the three into a single Agreement and directs the reader to start with the upstream terms. Clause 8 adds a further layer, stating that if you select a third party or open source model as your main agent model you become subject to that model terms, and naming the Anthropic commercial terms address explicitly for that case. At the head of the document sits the mirror-image exception: reaching the service through certain enterprise product lines, through a Workspace subscription under specific pre-GA offering terms, or with an enterprise agent platform API key means the terms accepted or signed by your administrator apply instead, and that the document does not apply to you.

  • Three at minimum — the upstream terms, the additional terms, and the privacy policy
  • Four if you change models — the terms of whichever model provider you selected are added
  • A different bundle on the organizational path — the contract the administrator signed displaces the document wholesale
  • Service-specific policies exist on top of that — the upstream terms list breach of service-specific policies among the grounds for suspension

The structural consequence is blunt. Saying you read the terms only means something if you can count how many documents that was. Since one document holds the suspension procedure and a different one holds the export route, a conclusion drawn from a single document is usually half a conclusion.

The six types in one table

Translated into something usable at adoption time, the six types look like this. The right-hand column states what stays open when that item is left unconfirmed.

Clause typeQuestion to answerRisk left open when it stays unconfirmed
Permitted and prohibited use Is the prohibition list enumerated or illustrative? Is there room for your usage pattern to be read into the parent category? Work you believed was ordinary use gets reclassified as a breach after the fact. Under an illustrative clause, absence from the list is not a defense
Automation and third-party clients Are unofficial clients prohibited, or is automation itself? Is there a separate official API path? Where does the authentication credential flow? The wrappers and proxies already attached to the pipeline become the breach route. Any design that handed a token to another agent has to be rebuilt from scratch
Suspension powers and advance notice Are the grounds enumerated or discretionary? Is advance notice promised unconditionally or conditionally? What are the exceptions? Schedules and release plans that never assumed an unannounced stop simply stop. Without an alternative, you do not control when work resumes
Export and post-suspension access Is the export route in the agreement or only in support documentation? Does it presuppose a signed-in session? Is the tool output inside the export coverage? Data locks at the same moment access does. Where a document offers only a deletion route, no contractual guarantee covers getting anything back out
Governing law and dispute resolution Which law and which forum? Is there a local-law softener? Does the document distinguish consumers from businesses? Even where a claim exists, cost and elapsed time block any realistic response. The duration of the outage stops being yours to control
Layering of documents How many documents govern in total? Which document holds the answer to each type? What contract replaces them on the organizational path? A conclusion from one document gets reversed by the upstream one. The classic case is a scope limitation that exists only in the additional terms and not above it

Blast radius - what is hanging on one account

Everything so far has been about documents. From here it is about the size of the damage. The same clause produces wildly different outcomes depending on what is hanging off the account in question.

The sentence that shows the scope has already appeared. When an account is disabled, you cannot sign in to the provider services and cannot use the sign-in-with-provider feature. The second half is the decisive part, because it means services run by entirely different companies that you logged into with that identity are blocked at the same moment.

Where authentication is hanging

  • Every external service attached through social sign-in — deployment dashboards, billing consoles, error tracking, documentation tools
  • Two-factor authentication apps and recovery codes, when they live in that account mail or cloud storage
  • Any other account for which this one was designated as the recovery email, where a suspension severs the recovery path of a second account as well
  • Domain registration, certificate renewal notices, and billing destinations, which are the terminal points of automated flows

Where output is hanging

  • Code, logs, and conversation history produced by the tool that exists only on the service side
  • Mailboxes, documents, and calendars, which is where the working context of a project actually lives
  • Design material and credential notes that exist only on the cloud drive
  • Any storage designated as the delivery target for export archives, if it is tied to the same account

How far a single leaked OAuth token can propagate is covered in One-Click Token Theft on github.dev. The blast radius of a suspension has the same shape, with one inversion. A token leak is an event where an attacker gets in; an account suspension is an event where you cannot get in. The countermeasures point in opposite directions.

Where personal and organizational accounts diverge

The exception at the head of the Antigravity additional terms creates this divergence at the document level. On the organizational path, the terms accepted or signed by the administrator apply and the consumer-facing document does not. In other words, the counterparty changes.

ItemUsing a personal account for workUsing an account issued by the organization
Contracting partiesThe individual and the service providerThe organization and the provider, with the individual as a user
Applicable documentsThe published consumer-facing bundleTerms accepted or signed by an administrator, sometimes with negotiated additions
Who responds to a suspensionThe individual appeals directly and burns the limited review count personallyAdministrator channels and contractual support routes may exist separately
Where output residesInside the personal account, with no path for the organization to reach itInside the organizational tenant, where an administrator can transfer and export
On departure or absenceHandover depends on the cooperation of one individualHandled through account transfer procedures
Personal useFew constraintsSubject to organizational policy

The allocation of responsibility hangs here too. Clause 4 of the Antigravity additional terms states that you are solely responsible for agents you create, orchestrate, or initiate within the Service. The list covers the actions and tasks the agent performs, determining whether the use is fit for its use case, authorizing the agent access and connection to data, applications, and systems, and exercising judgment and supervision when it is used in production environments. Clause 7 repeats the same shape for auxiliary skill files, disclaiming warranties and placing fitness-for-purpose and supervision on the user.

On a personal account, that solely responsible points at one person. On an organizational account, who it points at is settled by the contract the administrator signed. Starting a tool on a personal account with the intention of migrating it to the organization later leaves the responsibility for the pre-migration window ambiguous precisely because of that sentence.

How to cite terms that carry no date

Terms get amended, so a citation needs a point in time attached. The way that point is supplied differs from document to document.

  • Google Terms of Service — an effective date sits at the top of the document. The wording read here is the one effective July 30, 2026
  • Gemini API Additional Terms — an effective date of March 23, 2026, with a separate last-updated stamp of 2026-04-28 at the foot of the page. The two dates mark different things
  • Anthropic Commercial Terms — effective June 17, 2025, with a link to the previous version, which means earlier wording can be compared directly
  • Google Antigravity Additional Terms — neither an effective date nor a last-updated line appears in the document

For the last case there is exactly one remaining method: the reader supplies the timestamp. Record the moment of reading, keep a copy of the wording as it stood then, and record a hash of that copy. The point is to be able to reconstruct later what the adoption decision was actually based on.

# keep a copy of the wording as it stood at adoption time
mkdir -p terms
D=$(date +%F)
curl -sSL https://antigravity.google/terms/ -o terms/antigravity_$D.html
curl -sSL https://policies.google.com/terms -o terms/google_universal_$D.html
sha256sum terms/*_$D.html | tee -a terms/HASHES.txt

The habit matters here because the triggering controversy itself contains a claim that the wording changed. The community summary reports that earlier wording did not bound the scope, and that the object was tightened afterwards. The wording as of 2026-09-05 names the object as your Antigravity and/or Gemini CLI accounts. Which of those two sentences existed at which moment is something only the party holding a dated copy can state.

The other side of this work, preparing terms and policy documents as the party publishing a service, is laid out in Five Questions to Answer Before You Launch. What follows here is the opposite vantage point: reading somebody else terms and deciding whether to adopt.

What free means inside a contract

The route by which a free tool ends up contractually weaker than a paid one is structural rather than emotional. Three things compound.

  • Data handling differs. The Gemini API additional terms state that for unpaid offerings the content submitted and the responses generated are used to provide, improve, and develop products and services, while for paid services prompts and responses are not used to improve products
  • The cost of cutting you off is low. With no fee relationship, there is little contractual consideration to weigh against a suspension decision. Paid arrangements, by contrast, leave room for refunds, service levels, and notice mechanics to attach
  • Support routes differ. The channel for free users is usually a public forum or a designated mailbox, with no contractual response deadline attached

The Antigravity additional terms end by pointing questions and suggestions to a public discussion board, with only deletion requests routed to a support mailbox. That is not a design flaw; it is the ordinary shape of a free offering. What changes is that the moment it enters a working pipeline, that shape becomes the ceiling on your recovery time.

The decision therefore belongs to substitutability rather than tool quality. If the same job can be done through another route, a suspension is an inconvenience. If it cannot, a suspension is a stoppage.

What to finish before you adopt the tool

Translated into an adoption procedure, the material above splits into two groups, divided by whether the item can be deferred past adoption.

Finished before adoption

  • Account separation — keep the tool account apart from the primary account carrying mail, documents, and authentication. If separation is impossible for a given tool, that fact is itself a risk item
  • Recovery-path separation — keep the recovery email and second factor for the tool account outside that account. Inside it, a suspension severs the recovery path along with everything else
  • Export-route confirmation — establish whether the output falls inside the export coverage and whether export presupposes a session. Where scheduled export exists, enable it at adoption
  • A dated copy of the terms — count all governing documents, and record each one version marking together with the moment it was read
  • A named alternative — write down, by name, what performs the same job if this tool stops tomorrow. If it cannot be named, the tool is still an experiment rather than a working path

Maintained after adoption

  • Local copies of output — replicate the code and logs the tool produces to somewhere outside the service on a schedule. The age of the copy is the worst-case loss
  • Credentials kept out of the copies — maintain an exclusion list so tokens and keys do not ride along into the backup
  • An inventory of access paths — keep a list of every wrapper and script that touches the service. When the terms change, that list is the blast radius
  • Amendment watching — compare copy hashes periodically. For documents without a last-updated stamp, there is no other way to detect a change at all
# replicate tool output and logs locally every day
printf '%s\n' '.env' '*.token' 'credentials.json' | tee /backup/EXCLUDE
rsync -a --delete --exclude-from=/backup/EXCLUDE ~/work/agent-out/ /backup/agent-out/
git -C ~/work/repo bundle create /backup/repo-$(date +%F).bundle --all

The item skipped most often is the first one in the second group. While the tool is working, the copy looks useless, and by the moment it becomes useful it can no longer be made. The recovery ordering set out in Account Hacked? Complete Recovery Guide rests on the same premise. What you can do after the lock is a function of what you did before it.

What could not be confirmed

  • The original post on X that triggered this — the link the community post points to is a post on that platform, and the body is not reachable in a form that opens without a session. The wording of the post itself is therefore not quoted anywhere above, and what is relayed carries an explicit marker that it comes from the community summary
  • The wording and timing of the representative statement — the statement that suspension scope is limited to service accounts reaches this article through the community summary. Its original phrasing and timing remain unestablished, so it is not treated as wording with contractual effect
  • The amendment history of the Antigravity additional terms — the document carries no effective date, no last-updated line, and no previous-version link. Nothing is asserted here about what the earlier wording said or when it changed
  • Whether a Gemini CLI specific terms document exists — the additional terms name Gemini CLI accounts as a suspension object, but whether a separate document governs that service alone falls outside the scope of this article
  • The facts of individual suspension cases — public forums carry appeal threads, but what conduct actually occurred in each case and what procedure was applied is known only to the parties and the provider. No generalization about how the clauses operate is drawn from those cases here
Stated again: none of the above substitutes for legal advice on a specific matter. Every quoted passage reflects the wording as of the stated reading date, and terms are amended without notice. Whether a clause is enforceable, whether a given act constitutes a breach, and which remedies are open all turn on jurisdiction, contract form, and the standing of the parties. Where contractual position or actual loss is at stake, the right move is to obtain a determination from a competent authority or a qualified professional.
