The 2025 South Korean Presidential Election: Key Candidates, Platforms, and Outlook

As the 2025 South Korean presidential election (Daeseon) approached, the contours of the major candidates came into focus and each party's policy direction became clear. This election was a pivotal event with far-reaching implications for South Korea's political, economic, and social landscape. This post provides a comprehensive look at the key candidates, their core platforms, and the election schedule based on information available at the time.

2025 Presidential Election Schedule

The 21st presidential election (Je 21-dae Daetongryeong Seongeo) was held on June 3, 2025.

Major Presidential Candidates and Parties

The major presidential candidates either confirmed or widely discussed were as follows. (Candidate information was subject to change.)

  • Deobureo Minjudang (Democratic Party of Korea): Lee Jae-myung
  • Gungminui Him (People Power Party): Kim Moon-soo
  • Gaehyeoksindang (Reform Party): Lee Jun-seok
  • Minju Nodongdang (Democratic Labor Party): Kwon Young-guk
  • Independent: Hwang Kyo-ahn, Song Jin-ho

Core Platforms by Candidate

Each candidate put forward a distinct vision and set of policies for the direction South Korea should take.

1. Lee Jae-myung (Deobureo Minjudang / Democratic Party of Korea)

  • Key Slogan/Vision: Restoring people's livelihoods, building a fair society
  • Core Platforms:
    • Pursuing a two-consecutive-term presidential system (Daetongryeong 4-nyeon yeonimje)
    • Continued reform of the prosecution and judiciary
    • Expanding "basic series" policies including basic income and basic housing
    • Securing new engines of economic growth through digital and green transformation

2. Kim Moon-soo (Gungminui Him / People Power Party)

  • Key Slogan/Vision: The economy president (Gyeongje Daetongryeong); making South Korea great for business
  • Core Platforms:
    • Stimulating corporate investment and job creation through deregulation
    • Labor market flexibility
    • Reducing presidential authority and implementing accountable politics
    • Pursuing simultaneous National Assembly (Gukhoe) and presidential elections in 2028

3. Lee Jun-seok (Gaehyeoksindang / Reform Party)

  • Key Slogan/Vision: A president who yields power (Him ppae-neun Daetongryeong); political reform for future generations
  • Core Platforms:
    • Drastically reducing presidential authority and exploring a parliamentary cabinet system (Uiwon Naegakje)
    • Slimming down and streamlining government organizations
    • Creating a fair competitive environment and strengthening meritocracy
    • Expanding investment in science and technology and nurturing future industries

4. Kwon Young-guk (Minju Nodongdang / Democratic Labor Party)

  • Key Slogan/Vision: An equal South Korea free from discrimination
  • Core Platforms:
    • Reducing inequality through aggressive tax increases including a wealth tax
    • Strengthening labor rights and eliminating discrimination against non-regular workers
    • Responding to the climate crisis and transitioning to an ecological society
    • Significantly expanding the social safety net

5. Hwang Kyo-ahn (Independent)

  • Key Slogan/Vision: Defending liberal democracy; rooting out election fraud
  • Core Platforms:
    • Strengthening the fairness and transparency of the election system
    • Bolstering national security and solidifying the South Korea-US alliance
    • Restoring traditional values and normalizing education

6. Song Jin-ho (Independent)

  • Key Slogan/Vision: Making South Korea a Northeast Asian digital finance hub
  • Core Platforms:
    • Fostering the digital asset market and establishing the relevant legal framework
    • Strengthening protections for virtual asset investors
    • Building a new industrial ecosystem based on blockchain technology

Outlook and Key Points to Watch

The 2025 presidential election was held amid a complex mix of domestic and international circumstances. The key points to watch included:

  • Economic Recovery and Livelihood Issues: In the face of prolonged recession fears, each candidate's proposals for economic recovery and livelihood stabilization were a major point of contention.
  • Political Reform and Restoring Trust: With public distrust of politicians running high, each candidate's vision and feasibility for political reform and national unity drew close attention.
  • Intergenerational Conflict and Social Cohesion: The growing political influence of younger generations highlighted the importance of bridging intergenerational interests and fostering social cohesion.
  • Solving the Housing Problem: Proposals for stabilizing the still-heated real estate market and addressing housing affordability had the potential to significantly sway voters.
  • International Affairs and Diplomatic Security: Each candidate's stance on South Korea's diplomatic and security strategy and its relationships with neighboring countries amid a rapidly shifting international landscape was another critical variable.

Voters were encouraged to carefully examine each candidate's policies and vision and make a wise choice for the future of South Korea.

This post was written based on information publicly available at the time, and may differ from actual developments. Please refer to official announcements from each candidate and political party for the latest information.
