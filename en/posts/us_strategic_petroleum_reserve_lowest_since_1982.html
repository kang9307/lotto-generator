What Was Reported and Where It Can Be Opened - The Indicator Table

The sentence "the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest level since 1982" compares one stock figure with past figures. The shorter the sentence, the more it drops: which row of which statistical table the figure comes from, and what its unit and reference date are. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) is the emergency crude oil stock held by the U.S. government, and its volume is published by the EIA in weekly and monthly statistics. This article lists what can be read from those tables, using the values consulted.

The EIA Weekly Series - Name, Unit and Release Cycle

Knowing the exact name of the figure is the first step. The EIA series concerned is "Weekly U.S. Ending Stocks of Crude Oil in SPR (Thousand Barrels)", that is, the weekly end-of-period stock of crude oil in the U.S. SPR, and its unit is thousand barrels. It is listed on a row separate from commercial stocks, so the SPR is not part of the crude oil stocks held by private companies.

  • The unit is thousand barrels. 284,552 means 284,552 thousand barrels, which is about 284.55 million barrels. The "284.6 million barrels" in the press is a rounding of that.
  • The basis is the end-of-week stock. "Ending Stocks" is in the name of the series. It is the stock at the last point of the week, not the average over the week.
  • Release comes after the week ends. The release date in the table consulted is 23 September 2026 and the value is for the week of 18 September. The next release is announced for 30 September. A value an article calls the latest is as old as the release lag.
  • The SPR row is separate from the commercial stock row. The table footnote says that from January 2005 total crude oil and petroleum product stocks exclude lease stocks. Whether the phrase "U.S. crude oil stocks" includes the SPR depends on which row it points to.

With these four checks one can work backwards from a stock figure in an article to the table and row it came from. When a value differs from the table, look for the difference first in three places: rounding, reference date and series (weekly or monthly).

The Basis of "Since 1982" - Barrel Stock, Weekly Versus Monthly

"The lowest since 1982" can rest on one of three bases: the stock level in barrels, days of supply relative to consumption, or the share of total storage capacity. What can be confirmed in the sources consulted is the first. For the other two, the denominators are not in the tables.

To compare by barrel level, one must decide which series and which point in time are being compared. In the monthly series the end-of-October 1982 stock is 284,592 thousand barrels. The weekly value of 284,552 thousand barrels for 18 September 2026 is 40 thousand barrels lower (back-calculated). In the weekly series the 1982 values start at 270,455 for the week of 20 August and rise, and the 284,906 of the week of 5 November is the first weekly value above the 18 September value. An article writing "since October 1982" is consistent with this relationship.

  • The margin of comparison is narrow. 40 thousand barrels is only 0.014% of the stock (back-calculated). "Lowest" rests on a slight difference, so when the next weekly value appears, the sentence itself may change.
  • Weekly and monthly have different timing. A monthly value is the stock at the end of the month and a weekly value is the stock at the end of the week. Mixing the two series brings the timing difference into the value. This article places side by side only weekly-to-weekly and monthly-to-monthly comparisons.
  • In 1982 the stock was rising. In the monthly series it grew from 235,271 in January 1982 to 293,827 in December. The values of that time belong to a process of filling the reserve, while the 2026 values belong to a process of drawing it down. The same level has the opposite direction.
  • Days and share are not used in this article. Consumption and storage capacity are not in the tables consulted, so whether it is the lowest on those bases cannot be judged.

Stock and Flow Are Different Quantities

Reports on the strategic reserve mix numbers of different kinds. A stock is the quantity stored at a point in time, and a flow is the quantity that moved over a period. The two use the same unit, the barrel, but cannot substitute for each other. Sorting the values quoted in this article by kind gives the following.

  • Stock. The values of the EIA weekly and monthly series (for example 284,552 thousand barrels). The quantity stored at the end of a particular week.
  • Authorised volume. The 172 million barrels DOE announced on 11 March 2026. The total permitted for release, not the quantity actually delivered.
  • Awarded volume. DOE's statement of 29 September 2026: more than 133 million barrels across four completed exchanges from five earlier solicitations. It is a quantity under contract, and the time of delivery is a separate matter.
  • Volume to be solicited. Up to 40 million barrels in the same announcement. Bids are due on 6 October and delivery is scheduled for November and December.
  • Returned volume. An exchange is a structure in which borrowed barrels come back together with premium barrels. DOE said the premium in returned barrels was 25% in earlier exchanges. OilPrice.com reports 1.25 barrels returned per barrel taken, with deliveries starting early next year.

These five values cannot be added or subtracted to explain the change in stock. The fact that the authorised 172 million and the stock decline of 130.51 million (back-calculated) differ does not tell why they differ. The authorised volume is a ceiling, the awarded volume is a contract, the stock is the quantity actually stored, and the three are offset in time. This article does not fill in the relationship between them in a way the sources do not.

What the Sources Consulted Say About Cause

On the cause of the fall in stock, what the sources consulted state directly is all listed below. Building further causal claims is outside the scope of this article.

  • DOE's announcement of 11 March. It states that the President authorised the release of 172 million barrels from the SPR, as part of a coordinated release of 400 million barrels unanimously agreed by 32 International Energy Agency member countries. It said delivery would take about 120 days at the planned discharge rate.
  • DOE's description of the exchange method. It is an exchange, not a sale, and the companies return the borrowed barrels with premium barrels. DOE said this increases the SPR's long-term inventory at no cost to taxpayers.
  • DOE's replenishment plan. The March statement says it plans to more than replace these reserves with about 200 million barrels within the next year.
  • OilPrice.com's account. It writes that the decline stems from the March authorisation to release 172 million barrels and names three companies as counterparties to the exchange contracts. This article does not repeat the company names. The counterparties are not named in the DOE announcements.

Placing these four together, it can be read that the period of decline in stock overlaps in time with the release and exchange announcements. But which delivery under which contract produced each weekly decline, and how the stock will move when returns begin, cannot be read from the tables consulted. A general treatment of oil prices and the structure of crude oil ETFs is given by the Crude Oil ETF Complete Guide and is not repeated here.

How to Read the "Operating Minimum" Sentence

The OilPrice.com article mentions two lower limits. One is 252.4 million barrels, introduced as the minimum under federal law; the other is the customary operating minimum, that 250 to 300 million barrels are needed to pump and process oil efficiently. Below is what to keep in mind when reading such a sentence.

  • These values are not values in the EIA table. No lower limit appears in the EIA table consulted. They are carried over only as the article's description.
  • The statutory minimum could not be checked against the text of the law. The 252.4 million figure is used only by OilPrice.com and the text of the provision could not be opened.
  • The range is wide. 250 to 300 million barrels spans 50 million barrels. The current stock of 284.55 million lies inside this range (a back-calculated comparison). Being inside follows from how the range is defined in this sentence, and it is not a verdict that the stock is sufficient or insufficient.
  • The remark "self-defeating" is a quoted opinion. The article quotes an expert, but that is the speaker's judgment and not a statistic. This article does not use the opinion as evidence.
  • A lower limit is not a test; it is a scale on which the reader places the value. How many barrels the stock holds is a statistic; whether that stock is acceptable is a judgment. This article deals only with the former.

The order for reading a stock figure can be reduced to one line: check the name, unit and reference date of the table; decide which series are being compared; do not mix stock and flow; and carry over a limit or a cause only as far as that source states it directly.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • 283.8 million barrels. OilPrice.com cites it as the value for the week after 18 September, but the EIA table consulted goes only as far as the week of 18 September, so it could not be checked. It will be confirmed, or not, in the next EIA release (scheduled for 30 September).
  • Conversion of stock into days or share. Consumption and storage capacity are not in the material consulted, so it was not calculated.
  • The federal statutory minimum of 252.4 million barrels. It is OilPrice.com's account and could not be checked against the provision.
  • The counterparties to the exchange contracts and the 1.25 return ratio. These are OilPrice.com's account. DOE's announcement of 29 September names no companies and gives the return premium as 25%.
  • All past values of the weekly series. Because the table was consulted through a summarising lookup, the full weekly values for 1982 could not be listed directly. This article uses only the values that came back (20 August, 1 October, 5 November 1982 and so on).
  • The detailed cause of the fall in stock. Which weekly declines are deliveries under which contract is not in the material consulted.
  • Revision history of the EIA statistics. Whether past values were later revised could not be checked.
