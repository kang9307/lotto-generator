Why consumer protection does not carry over to a private resale

In Korea the protection of the e-commerce act attaches to dealings with a business. A peer-to-peer sale sits closer to a Civil Code sale, which changes what to check and what to keep on record

Aug 26, 2026 Life & Practical

This article explains the framework in general terms and is not legal advice on any individual matter. Everything below describes Korean law and Korean institutions and was compiled from official material published as of August 2026; provisions and operating standards can be amended. In a live dispute, the accurate source is the relevant authority guidance and the statutory text itself.

You open the parcel and it does not match the photo. You send a message and nothing comes back. On a retail site you would have pressed the return button and been done, but here there is no button to press. That button was never attached to this kind of transaction.

As neighbourhood resale has become routine, people carry over the reflexes they learned from online shopping. Seven days to change your mind, a platform that sorts things out when they go wrong, and a consumer helpline behind that. Those reflexes hold when the other party is a business. In a sale between two private individuals, all three work differently.

Separate the part the law guarantees from the part the two people have to settle themselves, and the rest follows on its own: which trading method to use, and what to keep on record. Procedures for disputes with a business are covered separately in the consumer dispute resolution guide.

1. It is not a transaction with a business, and everything follows from that

Online retail in Korea rests on the Act on Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, whose signature protection is the right of withdrawal. Article 17(1) allows a consumer who has concluded a purchase contract with a mail-order business to withdraw within seven days of receiving the written contract terms or of receiving the goods. What matters in that sentence is not the period but the subject. The right belongs to a consumer, and the duty falls on a mail-order business.

The plain-language legal service run by Korea Ministry of Government Legislation defines online direct trading as trading goods or services between users through an online platform, and then fixes its position in a single line. A peer-to-peer sale is carried out by electronic means, but because the selling party does not hold the status of a business it falls outside the e-commerce act. A Korea Fair Trade Commission explanatory note from February 2022 likewise proceeded on the premise that withdrawal rights and similar provisions do not apply to private transactions.

That does not leave nothing behind. The Civil Code rules on sale still apply. Article 580 makes the seller liable where the subject matter has a defect, subject to a proviso: not where the buyer knew of it or failed to know of it through negligence. Article 582 requires that right to be exercised within six months from the day the buyer became aware of the fact.

Read the two together and the character of a private sale emerges. Protection did not vanish; it changed shape. Against a business, the law grants a window in which a change of mind alone undoes the deal. Between individuals there is no such window, and the argument shifts to proving that an undisclosed fault existed.

2. The platform is not a party to the trade

Using an intermediary service makes it feel as though the company stands behind the deal, because the listing, the payment and the conversation all happen inside it. The position the law assigns is different. Article 20(1) requires a mail-order intermediary to notify consumers in advance, by a method prescribed in Ordinance of the Prime Minister, that it is not a party to the sale. That line tucked into the listing page is a statutory notice, not a formality.

Nor does that let the intermediary walk away. Article 20(3) requires prompt action, including identifying causes and harm, to resolve complaints or disputes arising from use of the cyber mall. And peer-to-peer trading has its own provision in Article 20-4, which places four things on the intermediary.

  • Verify private seller identity data — a phone number and other identity data prescribed by Presidential Decree
  • Cooperate in a dispute — on request, provide the verified identity data and transaction records to help resolve a dispute between a private seller and the other side
  • Flag payment escrow — where escrow is available, inform both the private seller and the consumer and recommend that they use it
  • Distinguish sellers — where private and business sellers are mixed on one screen, mark each by an appropriate method

The third and fourth carry the practical weight. Escrow guidance was written into the statute because in private trades the risk of money and goods passing out of step is structurally high. And the labelling duty exposes the fact that, on the very same screen, buying from an individual and buying from a business are legally different acts. Buy from a seller marked as a business and the left column of the table above applies; buy from a private seller and the right column does.

An enforcement decree revision that took effect in 2026 narrowed the identity data an intermediary must verify in private trades. Collecting less personal data and being able to identify a counterparty in a dispute pull against each other. The implication for a user is single: what the platform vouches for is narrower than it feels, and that scope shifts over time.

3. Each trading method secures something different

The same item at the same price carries a different risk profile depending on how it changes hands. Meeting in person, courier with a bank transfer, and platform escrow are each safe at different points and exposed at different points. Ranking them best to worst blurs the judgement, so it is better to split them by what each one blocks and what it leaves open.

MethodWhat it securesWhat it does notFits when
In person You see it work before money moves, so goods and payment rarely pass out of step Internal faults that surface later; the safety of the meeting and the location it reveals The item can be tested and the trip is short
Courier plus bank transfer No distance limit; the waybill and the transfer record fix timestamps Sending money first does not guarantee anything arrives; damage in transit is contested Terms are fully checked and the sum is modest
Platform escrow A third party holds the money and releases it after delivery, cutting the prepayment risk structurally It does not vouch for quality or authenticity; fees and cover vary by service The sum is large or the counterparty is new to you

On the Fair Trade Commission description, payment escrow is a safeguard in which a trusted third party such as a bank holds the payment and releases it to the seller once delivery is complete. What it blocks is prepayment risk and nothing else; whether the item is genuine and matches the description remains between the two people.

The test is simple. The larger the sum, the newer the counterparty, and the harder the condition is to inspect, the more the method matters. Applying the same ceremony to a few thousand won of household goods and to a several hundred thousand won device is either excessive or insufficient. Card and wallet security is a different subject, covered in the online payment security guide.

4. Signals that should stop the trade

Memorising scam mechanics achieves little. The mechanics keep changing, and the more detail you know the easier it becomes to convince yourself that this one is different. Keeping a handful of stop signals works better. None of the items below is evidence of a crime; each is a reason to look once more before money leaves.

  • Priced far below the market — sitting alone below the band of comparable listings usually has a reason
  • Moving to an outside messenger — the moment the thread leaves the platform, the record you would show in a dispute disappears
  • Refusing escrow — the fee may be the stated reason, but the risk that fee was absorbing now transfers to you
  • Money before goods — full prepayment set as a condition with no exception entertained
  • Account in a different name — whatever the explanation, the thread that identifies the counterparty is cut
  • Pressure to decide now — repeated warnings that someone else will take it
  • Avoiding fresh photographs — you ask for a specific angle and the old images come back instead
  • Payment steered to an outside link — details entered on a screen the platform does not serve

One signal alone settles nothing. People do sell in a hurry, and accounts do get created. The axis is how many overlap. When two or more appear together, the price no longer matters and passing is the better call.

5. What to put on record before trading

A private dispute turns on proving condition rather than on following a procedure. That fixes the preparation too: capture the condition and the agreement at the time of the trade in a form you can produce later. It applies to buyer and seller alike.

  • Record the condition with a timestamp — scuffs, wear and missing parts included, not only the flattering angle
  • Note the identifiers — serial number, model name, engraving, anything that pins down this individual unit
  • Video beats a still for function — the power coming on, the sound, the screen responding
  • Turn the agreement into text in the chat — summarise anything settled by phone and send it back for confirmation
  • Keep the conversation on the platform — the record you could submit stays in one place
  • Decide payment and method together — agree the method that suits the sum and the item before money moves
  • Film packing and unboxing unbroken — sometimes the only basis when courier damage is contested

It looks fussy but costs a few minutes. With a record of the condition at handover you can argue an Article 580 point with material rather than assertion; without one, only two memories remain.

6. When something has already gone wrong

There is an order to it. Fix the record first, then find the channel that fits. Capturing the chat, the payment and transfer records, the waybill number and the listing page is step one. Once the other side pulls the post or tidies the account, none of it survives.

ChannelWhenBasis and character
Platform report and dispute process Contact breaks off, or terms are performed differently Backed by the dispute measures in Article 20(3) and the duty in Article 20-4 to supply identity data and transaction records
Korean police cyber crime reporting system Money appears to have been taken by deception Covers money obtained under the pretext of a trade, and offers a lookup for recent fraud complaints tied to an account or phone number
Electronic transaction dispute mediation You want the amount and responsibility settled as a civil matter The committee sits under Article 32 of the Framework Act on Electronic Documents and Transactions; applications run under Article 33(1), and Article 33(4) sets a mediation proposal within forty-five days
Civil proceedings Mediation fails or the other side does not engage Where mediation succeeds, Article 35(3) of the same Act gives the record the effect of a judicial settlement under the Civil Procedure Act

Which channel fits depends on the case. Nothing arriving with contact cut off is a different animal from an item that arrived in the wrong condition. Push both into the same process and neither resolves well. The elements of criminal fraud are set out separately in the fraud complaint guide.

7. The seller carries a different set of risks

Safety advice about resale is usually written from the buyer seat. But in a private trade both sides are private individuals, so risk sits on both.

  • Defect claims after handover — a fault raised days later. Without a record of the condition at handover there is nothing to answer with
  • Return demanded after use — used as much as wanted, then a change of mind. Here the absence of a statutory withdrawal window protects the seller
  • Personal data exposure — the waybill carries a real name, address and phone number together, as do return addresses and account numbers
  • Payment trouble — a reversed transfer or a delayed confirmation lands after the item has gone. Hence ship only after payment clears
  • The cost of overselling — describing only the good parts weakens your position under Article 580. Writing the faults down protects the seller

What sellers most often overlook is personal data. The item may be worth tens of thousands of won, but the name, address and phone number handed over to complete it cannot be recalled. Use a masked number or an alternative dispatch option where one exists; where none does, at least know the exposure before starting.

8. Where repeated selling becomes a business

Here the circle closes back on the first section. Private trades sit outside the e-commerce act because the seller is not a business. The moment the seller becomes one, the trade comes back inside the Act, and that threshold is set by the system rather than declared by the person.

Korean National Tax Service guidance states that selling or brokering through online channels on a continuous and repeated basis, rather than as a one-off, requires business registration. The filing deadline is within twenty days of the business start date, and failure to register carries an additional tax of one percent of supply value for a sole proprietor along with loss of input tax deduction. A separate mail-order business report to the local district office may also apply.

  • The axis is continuity, not amount — less about what one sale was worth than whether the activity keeps going
  • Intent to profit — clearing out things you used is not the same as buying in order to sell
  • Repetition of the same category — the same class of item appearing again and again is hard to explain as personal clearing
  • Duties arrive with the status — from that point the disclosure, notice and withdrawal rules of the e-commerce act apply to you
  • Do not decide it alone — once the scale feels different, check with the tax office or the online tax portal

What circulates in this area are numbers, an annual turnover figure or a count of sales per year. Rather than taking comfort or alarm from such figures, it is more useful to know that a test exists and a body decides it. If the purpose and shape of your buying and selling has changed, your status may have changed with it, and settling that status is the tax office job.

9. Meeting locally to trade

The appeal of neighbourhood trading is seeing the item; the flip side is actually meeting a stranger. That introduces considerations online trading never had.

  • Meet in an open place with people around — avoid underground car parks and quiet side streets
  • Somewhere with recording is better — the front of a shopping centre or a station where footage exists to check later
  • Do not hand over your address as a pin — setting the meeting at your door discloses where you live
  • For a valuable item, keep the option of not going alone — along with avoiding late hours, company alone changes the situation
  • Decide in advance what to inspect on the spot — power, accessories, serial number, things visible only there
  • Leave a record whether cash or transfer — with cash, state receipt in the chat thread
  • Trading with a minor is a different matter — parental consent questions arise, so high-value deals call for caution

The step people skip most often in person is inspection, because examining an item closely in front of the seller feels rude. Yet a minute spent there beats weeks spent later. For categories with their own history checks and paperwork, such as cars, follow that category process, as set out in the used car buying guide.

10. What could not be confirmed

Some items were left without figures or firm statements, either because no basis was found in published official material or because they vary by operator and by date.

  • Escrow fee rates and cover — these differ by service and change with terms revisions
  • A concrete amount or count threshold for business status — tax authority guidance frames the test as continuity and repetition, and the figures that circulate publicly have no official basis that could be located
  • Annual volume of private-trade disputes — figures appear in press coverage, but the primary statistic could not be pinned down, so none is quoted here
  • The current list of identity items an intermediary verifies — delegated to Presidential Decree and therefore subject to revision, as is the interface between platform mediation and committee mediation

11. Frequently asked questions

Can I return an item bought from a private seller within seven days?

The right of withdrawal under Article 17(1) of the Korean e-commerce act attaches to a consumer who contracted with a mail-order business. Ministry of Government Legislation guidance states that a peer-to-peer sale, though carried out electronically, falls outside that Act because the seller does not hold business status. No statutory window exists for returning something simply because you changed your mind. Whether a return happens rests on the agreement and on any separate platform procedure.

Is a private seller liable for defects?

The Civil Code rules on sale apply. Article 580 establishes seller liability where the subject matter has a defect, with a proviso excluding cases where the buyer knew of it or failed to know through negligence. Faults disclosed in advance, or that a brief inspection would have revealed, are hard to litigate. Article 582 requires the right to be exercised within six months from the day the buyer became aware.

Does the platform pay if something goes wrong?

An intermediary is not a party. Article 20(1) requires advance notice, readily visible to consumers, that it is not a party to the sale. It is not free of duties, though. Article 20(3) requires prompt measures to resolve disputes, and Article 20-4 addresses private trades separately: verifying identity data, cooperating on request with identity data and transaction records, informing and recommending payment escrow, and distinguishing private from business sellers.

Where do I take a problem?

Start with the platform reporting and dispute process. Where money appears to have been taken by deception, the Korean police cyber crime reporting system accepts reports and offers a lookup for recent fraud complaints tied to an account or phone number. To settle amounts and responsibility civilly, mediation can be requested from the committee established under Article 32 of the Framework Act on Electronic Documents and Transactions.

Do I need to register as a business if I keep selling?

Korean National Tax Service guidance requires business registration for selling through online channels on a continuous and repeated basis rather than as a one-off. The deadline is within twenty days of the business start date, and non-registration carries an additional tax of one percent of supply value for a sole proprietor. A separate mail-order business report may also apply. The axis is continuity and repetition, not the amount.

12. In short

Most of the confusion in resale comes from mistaking which kind of trade it is. Buying from a business on a retail site and buying from an individual on a platform look alike on screen and differ in law. Withdrawal attaches to a consumer contracting with a business; a private trade keeps the Civil Code rules on sale.

Which makes the practice simple. First, check on screen whether the seller is an individual or a business. That labelling is a statutory requirement. Second, pick the method that matches the sum and the item. Escrow blocks prepayment risk; condition still has to be seen. Third, put condition and agreement on record, because the argument runs on proof rather than procedure.

Walking away is also an option. Two overlapping signals and the price stops mattering. And if selling has become repetitive, the story changes — business status is set by the system, and from that point the first section applies from the other side.
