This is a guide to the South Korean passport for Korean nationals. It explains how a citizen of the Republic of Korea gets a Korean passport, based on the Ministry of Foreign Affairs passport portal, Gov24 (the Korean government's online civil service portal), and Ministry of Foreign Affairs press releases. Fees follow the official fee table reflecting the increase that took effect on March 1, 2026 (as of September 29, 2026), and the military-service information follows the Ministry of Foreign Affairs announcement that applies to applications received from May 1, 2025. Rules and amounts can change, so check the official sites again before applying. Other travel preparations such as flights, travel insurance, international driving permits, and travel cards are covered in our airfare guide, travel insurance guide, international driving permit guide, and travel card guide.

1. The Overall Application Process and Basic Principles

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs passport portal describes a passport as a type of official document that certifies the identity, including the nationality, of the holder and allows the holder's country of nationality to exercise diplomatic protection over the holder, and states that citizens who wish to travel abroad must carry one (Article 2 of the Passport Act). In Korea, applications are handled by passport agencies such as city, county, and district offices; overseas, they are handled by Korean diplomatic missions.

The basic principle is that the applicant must apply in person, under Article 9 of the Passport Act. Applying through a representative is allowed only in exceptional cases: minors under 18, people who cannot apply in person because of illness, disability, or accident, and cases of protocol necessity. A first-time passport can only be applied for in person; online applications are not accepted.

The steps to getting a passport in your hands are as follows.

  • Choose the passport type: choose among a multiple-entry passport (10 years for those 18 and over, 5 years for those under 18), a single-entry passport, or an emergency passport according to your age and situation, and choose between 58 and 26 pages.
  • Prepare documents and a photo: prepare your ID and one passport photo taken within the last six months, and if you are under 18, a legal guardian's consent form and related papers.
  • Choose how to apply: if it is your first application or a reason that requires a visit, such as correcting your information, go to the counter; if you have been issued an electronic passport at least once before, you can also renew online through Gov24 and other channels.
  • Pay the fee and wait for processing: pay the fee for the type and page count, and wait for the processing time listed on Gov24 (8 days in total for an ordinary passport, excluding Saturdays and public holidays).
  • Collect it: an online application must be collected in person, and if you have an old passport that is still valid, bring it with you.

2. Which Passport Type You Need and How to Choose

The first things you decide on the application form are the type of passport and the number of pages. The validity period, how many times it can be used, and the fee all depend on the type.

2.1 How Multiple-Entry and Single-Entry Passports Differ

The Passport Act divides passports into two groups by how many times they can be used. In the passport portal's description, a single-entry passport is a passport that allows foreign travel limited to one round trip from the place of issue (one departure and one entry), and a multiple-entry passport is a passport that allows foreign travel without limit on the number of trips until its expiry date. A single-entry passport is valid for up to one year.

The validity of a multiple-entry passport depends on age. The official fee page states that under Article 6 of the Enforcement Decree of the Passport Act, a person aged 18 or over who applies for a multiple-entry passport is issued a passport valid for 10 years, and the period cannot be chosen. Those under 18 are issued a 5-year multiple-entry passport, and the fee differs for those aged 8 and over and those under 8. The fee table also lists a 26-page passport valid for "less than 5 years," but the official pages covered in this article do not explain when it is issued.

2.2 What Non-Electronic Emergency Passports and Travel Certificates Are For

An emergency passport is a non-electronic passport issued for urgent reasons. The passport portal defines eligibility as cases where there is no time to be issued (or reissued) an electronic passport and urgent issuance of a passport is recognized as necessary, and it is issued as a single-entry passport valid for one year. You also need a written statement of reasons for the emergency passport, and if you apply at the passport service centers at Incheon or Gimhae Airport, a copy of your air ticket as well. Gov24 lists the processing time for an emergency passport as "1 day in total."

There are two points to watch. First, the official guidance requires you to check in advance whether the country you plan to visit recognizes non-electronic (emergency) passports and what entry restrictions apply, and it provides a separate country-by-country list. You should not assume an emergency passport works everywhere the same way as an electronic one. Second, people whose identity cannot be verified and people who have lost a passport three or more times within five years cannot be issued an emergency passport.

The travel certificate appears in the fee table as a "sticker-attached" document costing 25,000 won, and Gov24 lists the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and diplomatic missions as the receiving and processing offices, with a processing time of "1 day in total." The requirements for who receives a travel certificate do not appear in the official pages covered in this article.

3. 2026 Passport Fees - How They Are Built and What Each Type Costs

Fees differ by type, validity, page count, and age. On February 23, 2026, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a press release titled Passport issuance fee raised by 2,000 won, and a notice from a Korean diplomatic mission announced an increase of 2 US dollars from March 1, 2026. The reason the notice gave was that with the introduction of the next-generation electronic passport in 2021, manufacturing and issuance costs rose significantly, but the passport issuance fee had not been raised for the past 20 years.

That is why guidance written before the increase shows different numbers from today's. Even within the passport portal, the page for men subject to military service lists a 10-year multiple-entry passport at 50,000 won and a single-entry passport at 15,000 won, which does not match the current fee table. It is safer to go by the fee table on the passport portal's fee page.

3.2 The Fee Breakdown and Free Passport Certificates

The International Exchange Contribution is included in the fee under the Korea Foundation Act, and a note to the fee table explains that it funds the Korea Foundation's international exchange and public diplomacy projects and operations. It is 12,000 won on multiple-entry passports for those 18 and over and 9,000 won on 5-year passports for those aged 8 to under 18, while the contribution column is blank for passports for children under 8 and for single-entry passports, emergency passports, travel certificates, and passports with remaining validity.

An emergency passport costs 50,000 won, but it is 17,000 won if you submit documents proving a relative's death or critical condition. The official guidance states that 33,000 won can be refunded if supporting documents are submitted within six months after applying for an emergency passport. In other words, even if you could not provide the proof when applying, you can get the difference back by submitting it later.

The five kinds of passport certificates, such as a certificate of passport issuance record and a certified passport copy, cost 1,000 won each, and the fee is waived if you use an unmanned civil-service kiosk or print them from Gov24.

4. Common Documents and Photo Rules - Requirements That Prevent Rejection

Gov24 warns that if the passport photo does not meet the standard, the application may be rejected or issuance of the passport may be refused. Fewer papers are needed now because many can be checked through the government's shared administrative information network, but your ID and photo still have to meet the standards.

4.1 Basic Documents and Accepted Forms of ID

The basic documents for a person aged 18 or over applying for a first passport are as follows. For a reissue, you also submit your old passport if it is still valid.

  • Passport application form: filled in at the counter; the passport portal has a completed example.
  • ID: a valid resident registration card, driver's license, passport, or similar document issued by a state agency, with a photo attached and built-in security features. A disability registration card (welfare card) showing a photo and resident registration number is also accepted. Documents that show only a date of birth are not accepted.
  • One passport photo: it must have been taken within the last six months. For online renewal you upload a photo file, and if it does not meet the standard, the application may not proceed or may be rejected.
  • Certificate of family relationship records: not required if it can be checked through the shared administrative information network. Gov24 notes that if you do not consent to that information sharing, you must submit it yourself.

4.2 Photo Rules and the Ban on AI-Retouched Photos

Gov24 lists three points about photos. First, among photos you have used on ID such as a resident registration card, driver's license, or passport, any photo taken more than six months before the application date cannot be reused. Second, photos taken with personal devices such as phones or digital cameras can be used only if they meet the passport photo standard. Third, photos arbitrarily retouched using photo editing programs, photo filter functions, and the like (including AI-assisted editing, processing, compositing, and generated creations) cannot be used.

The six-month rule matters even after issuance. Gov24 states that if a photo is found not to have been taken within six months, the issued passport may be canceled and the fee will not be refunded. For detailed specifications such as photo size and background, it is best to check the photo rules on the passport portal separately.

5. Extra Documents by Applicant and the Change for Military-Service Men

Depending on your age and military-service status, you may need more than the common documents. The rules for men subject to military service in particular changed in 2025.

5.1 Guardian Consent and Proxy Applications for Minors Under 18

A passport application for a person under 18 requires the consent of a legal guardian (Article 9(4) of the Passport Act). Even when the legal guardian applies in person, the consent form must still be completed. The additional documents are as follows.

  • Legal guardian consent form: completed by a person with parental authority (father or mother) or a guardian. If parental authority is shared, the details of all legal guardians are filled in and a representative signs (or seals) it.
  • Legal guardian's certificate of seal impression (or a certificate of personal signature or an electronic certificate of personal signature): not required if the legal guardian who signed (or sealed) the consent form visits in person.
  • Basic certificate and family relationship certificate (detailed or specific): not required if they can be checked through the shared administrative information network.

The people who can apply on a minor's behalf are legal guardians (persons with parental authority, guardians, and so on) and relatives within the second degree who are 18 or older. When a relative within the second degree applies, a power of attorney, the delegating legal guardian's ID (a copy is fine), and the visiting representative's ID are also required.

5.2 10-Year Passports for Men Who Have Not Completed Service, and the Separate Travel Permit

A man subject to military service is defined as a male from January 1 of the year he turns 18 to December 31 of the year he turns 37. Under the previous rules, men in reserve-for-service status and those serving as supplementary, alternative, or shipboard reserve service personnel were issued 5-year multiple-entry passports and submitted military-service documents. The passport portal's page for men subject to military service still shows these old rules and the pre-increase fees, so you should not rely on that page's validity periods and fees.

According to a Ministry of Foreign Affairs press release (April 11, 2025), the lifting of the passport validity limit applies to passport applications received on or after Thursday, May 1, Korea time. It covers all men who have not completed military service, including those in reserve-for-service status and those serving as supplementary, alternative, or shipboard reserve service personnel, and they can apply for a passport without the military-service information check, such as submitting military-service documents. This means that those aged 18 and over are eligible for a 10-year multiple-entry passport like any other adult.

There is one point that is easy to confuse. The same press release stated that the overseas travel permit system for men who have not completed military service (under the Military Manpower Administration) and passport administrative sanctions against those staying abroad without a permit (under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs) remain in place regardless of this revision. The passport portal also states that passport issuance does not mean an overseas travel permit under the Military Service Act. The official guidance says that men who have not completed military service cannot leave the country without an overseas travel permit even if they have a passport, and that if they stay abroad without a permit or beyond the permitted period, passport sanctions such as an order to return the passport or invalidation are possible in addition to sanctions under the Military Service Act.

6. Online Renewal vs. In-Person Application - When Each Is Possible

With online renewal, you do not go to a counter to apply and only visit to collect the passport. But not every case can be handled online.

6.1 Who Can Renew Online and Where

Eligibility is Korean nationals who have been issued an electronic passport at least once before. Gov24 notes that electronic passports were introduced on August 25, 2008. The application channels are as follows.

  • In Korea: Gov24 (enter "passport reissuance" in the search box) and KB Star Banking (select "passport reissuance application").
  • Overseas: the Overseas Koreans 365 civil service portal. Applications are accepted only at diplomatic missions that handle passport work.
  • Under 18: a legal guardian verifies their own identity and applies on the minor's behalf through Gov24 or Overseas Koreans 365. KB Star Banking is listed as still being prepared.

6.2 Cases That Require an In-Person Application, and Collection in Person

The official guidance lists the following cases as not eligible for online application.

  • First-time electronic passport applicants
  • Diplomatic and official passport applicants
  • Emergency passport applicants
  • Repeat losers of passports: two or more losses within five years, or one loss within five years while a valid passport that has not expired exists
  • Those wishing to change their romanized name (including a spouse's romanized surname), those under administrative sanctions, and those wishing to be reissued a passport with the remaining validity
  • Those whose legal guardian status must be verified: including minors under 18 whose case needs checking by staff because of parental authority or guardian appointment.

The reissue page adds that reissuance due to correction or change of recorded information, loss or damage of a passport, or an administrative agency's error can only be applied for in person. However, the page on reissue after loss or damage lists the method as "in-person or online application" and names only repeat losses as ineligible for online application, so the wording differs between pages. If your passport was lost or damaged, it is better to ask the receiving office first.

Collection is also fixed. The official guidance states that the applicant must visit in person to collect the passport, and says to bring your old passport if it is still valid, or your ID if not. If a legal guardian applied online for a minor under 18, the guardian can collect it on the minor's behalf. You choose the collection office when applying, and it cannot be changed once the application is complete. Gov24 says an issued passport can be collected in person or through a paid individual postal delivery service, yet it also states that online applications must be collected in person.

7. Processing Time and What to Watch When Collecting

The first question most people have is how many days it takes. Plenty of anecdotal turnaround times circulate online, but the figure with an official basis is a different one.

7.1 How the Official Processing Time Is Counted, and Why Anecdotal Times Differ

The Gov24 service guide lists the processing time as "8 days in total" both for ordinary passports for applicants aged 18 and over and for ordinary passports for minors under 18. When the processing time is six days or more, days are counted including the day of receipt, excluding Saturdays and public holidays. In Gov24's own example, for a matter with an 8-day processing time received at 2 p.m. on Tuesday, September 20, 2022, the deadline is 11:59 p.m. on Thursday, September 29.

The issuance, reissue, and online pages of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs passport portal do not list a number of processing days. Anecdotal claims like "it usually comes in a few days" have no official basis, so our view is that if your departure date is fixed, you should apply with a margin based on the 8 days (excluding Saturdays and public holidays). If you have an urgent situation, besides the emergency passport there is also a 48-hour electronic passport that requires review by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. However, the official guidance says that running out of pages or validity through the applicant's simple carelessness does not qualify.

7.2 Bringing Your Old Passport and the Warning About Damaged Passports

Progress updates are provided through the issuance status check on Gov24 or KB Star Banking, or by KakaoTalk or text message. According to the online renewal guidance, if you apply for online renewal while your passport is still valid, you can keep using the old passport until you receive the new one, and you must bring that old passport when you go to collect the new one. The official guidance adds that the remaining validity required for entry can differ by country, so check in advance.

Damage also needs care. The passport portal says that if a passport is damaged, including arbitrary scribbles or commemorative stamps unrelated to official entry and exit procedures, there may be restrictions on foreign entry and exit and on issuing air tickets, so you should get it reissued. In particular, it warns that if damage occurs, such as stains on the personal data page or pages being torn out, it may be grounds for refusal at immigration abroad, or in serious cases you may be investigated and face disadvantages such as criminal punishment, and asks holders to take special care at all times.

8. What We Could Not Confirm

The following could not be pinned down in official source text, so this article does not state them as figures or firm claims.

  • The body of the February 23, 2026 Ministry of Foreign Affairs press release: only the title (Passport issuance fee raised by 2,000 won) is posted, and the body is in an attachment. The size of the increase was available only from a diplomatic mission's notice (2 US dollars) and the current amounts only from the fee table; we did not see the original comparison table with the pre-increase amounts.
  • Processing days on the passport portal: not given on the issuance, reissue, or online pages. The "8 days in total" in this article is the Gov24 figure.
  • Payment system fees for online renewal overseas: the same guidance page gives different percentages, so they are not included.
  • Detailed passport photo specifications and eligibility for travel certificates: not given on the official pages covered in this article.
  • Whether reissue after loss or damage can be applied for online: the reissue page and the loss-and-damage page are worded differently.
