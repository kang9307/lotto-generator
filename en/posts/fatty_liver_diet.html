What Is Fatty Liver? The Name Changed from NAFLD to MASLD

Fatty liver is a condition in which too much fat builds up in liver cells. The 2021 Korean Association for the Study of the Liver (KASL) guideline defines simple fatty liver (NAFL) as 5% or more of hepatocytes containing fat, with no evidence of hepatocyte injury. It usually causes few symptoms and is often found by chance on blood tests or imaging during a health checkup.

In 2023 an international multisociety consensus (Rinella et al., 2023) renamed non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). A person is classed as MASLD when the liver has fat, at least one cardiometabolic risk factor is present, and no other cause explains the fat. The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) health portal now uses the MASLD name too. The KASL guideline cited here was written with the older term NAFLD, so the sections below keep the guideline's own wording.

Main Risk Factors

  • Excess calorie intake: overeating carbohydrates and fat
  • Excess sugar intake: especially sugar-sweetened drinks
  • Lack of exercise: low energy expenditure
  • Insulin resistance: prediabetes or diabetes
  • Abdominal obesity: linked to more visceral fat

The Big Picture in the Guidelines: Weight, Diet and Exercise

What the guidelines put first is not a particular food but weight loss, diet control and exercise. The table below summarizes what was checked against the source texts this time. The weight-loss figures apply to people who are overweight or obese.

The KASL guideline explains that a Mediterranean diet improves liver fat and insulin resistance more than a low-fat diet. Even so, the right pace and calorie level depend on age, weight, medications and other conditions, so the target should be set with a clinician or dietitian.

Dietary Principles

Carbohydrates and sugars

  • Cut back on: sugar-sweetened drinks, snacks and refined carbohydrates such as white bread
  • Choose: whole grains such as brown rice, oats and whole wheat, plus legumes and vegetables

The European guideline strongly recommends avoiding sugar-sweetened beverages. The World Health Organization recommends keeping free sugars below 10% of total energy intake, and ideally below 5%.

Protein

It is enough to include fish, chicken breast, beans and tofu, and low-fat dairy evenly in meals. The guidelines checked this time give no fatty-liver-specific figure for grams per day, so none is stated here. If you have kidney disease or take medication, discuss protein amounts with your clinician first.

Fats

  • Choose: foods rich in unsaturated fat such as olive oil, nuts and fish
  • Cut back on: trans fats (margarine, shortening, some processed foods), red and processed meat, and fried food

The Mediterranean-style diet in the European guideline means reducing sugars and refined carbohydrates, saturated fat, ultra-processed food, and red and processed meat.

Foods Often Mentioned and the Limits of the Evidence

Many foods are said to be "good for the liver," but the guideline texts checked this time contain no graded recommendation for individual foods. Read the foods below as examples of how to build meals, not as foods that treat fatty liver.

Foods and Habits to Avoid

Alcohol

The guideline says even moderate or lower drinking needs attention in people with NAFLD. The KDCA health portal also explains that alcohol causes fat buildup and cell damage in the liver and can lead to alcoholic liver disease. How much, if any, is acceptable depends on the state of the liver, so check with a clinician. This article does not set a period of abstinence such as "a few months."

Sugar-sweetened drinks and ultra-processed food

  • Soda, fruit juice and other sweetened drinks are items the European guideline recommends avoiding.
  • Cutting back on ultra-processed food, red meat and processed meat fits the Mediterranean-style direction.

Meal timing and speed

The guidelines checked this time contain no statement recommending late-night eating rules or fasting windows (intermittent fasting) as a fatty liver treatment. The guideline standard is reducing total daily energy intake, so consider timing rules only as a means to that end, and always consult a clinician if you use diabetes medication.

A Practical Meal Plan Example

The plan below is not a diet set by the guidelines but an example that translates the Mediterranean direction into Korean-style meals. Portions and calories differ from person to person, so do not follow it as written.

Sample daily menu

Breakfast

- Oatmeal + berries + a small amount of nuts
- Unsweetened Greek yogurt
- Water or barley tea

Lunch

- Brown rice + grilled fish
- Broccoli and spinach salad + olive oil dressing
- Miyeokguk (seaweed soup)

Dinner

- Quinoa or mixed-grain rice + chicken breast
- Vegetable salad
- Tofu or bean side dish

Snack (if needed)

- Up to a handful of nuts, or carrot sticks + hummus

Meal-prep tips

  1. Shopping: buy mostly fresh ingredients and check the labels on processed food
  2. Side dishes: wash and cut namul and salad ingredients ahead of time
  3. Protein: pre-cook chicken breast and fish and refrigerate
  4. Grains: cook brown rice and mixed grains ahead and store in portions

Tracking Progress and Tests

How often to have blood tests (liver enzymes ALT and AST, lipids, blood glucose), an abdominal ultrasound or a fibrosis assessment such as elastography depends on your risk level and is decided by your clinician. This article does not set a testing schedule.

Indicators you can watch at home

  • Weight change: check that loss is gradual, no more than about 1 kg per week
  • Waist circumference: the Korean criterion for abdominal obesity is 90 cm or more for men and 85 cm or more for women (KDCA)
  • Diet and exercise log: a food diary and whether you exercise at least 3 times a week

When to See a Professional

Signs that call for a medical visit

  • Jaundice: yellowing of the whites of the eyes or the skin
  • Severe fatigue or abdominal bloating: if it disrupts daily life or persists
  • Raised liver enzymes or fatty liver on a checkup: bring the result to a consultation whatever the value

Conditions to manage together

  • Diabetes: adjust the diet alongside endocrinology care
  • Hypertension and dyslipidemia: manage with the relevant specialty
  • Obesity: build the weight-loss plan with a clinician and dietitian

Benefits of seeing a dietitian

You can get a personalized meal plan that accounts for age, sex, activity level and existing conditions, and it is easier to adjust as things progress.

Closing

The core of the guidelines is weight loss, better diet quality, exercise and caution about alcohol. This time we did not find evidence that any particular food clears fatty liver. Fatty liver is known to be a condition that can improve with lifestyle changes, but the course differs by person, so manage it together with a clinician, bringing your checkup results. This article does not replace diagnosis or treatment.

# Fatty Liver Diet Checklist

As of: 2026-09-29
Sources: KASL 2021 guideline, EASL-EASD-EASO 2024, KDCA National Health Information Portal. This does not replace diagnosis or treatment; set personal targets with a clinician or dietitian.

## 1. Guideline figures at a glance

| Item | Guideline summary | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Weight loss (overweight or obese) | 5-7% or more: less liver fat / 7-10% or more: better liver inflammation and fibrosis | KASL 2021 |
| Weight loss (Europe) | 5% or more / 7-10% / 10% or more (fat, inflammation, fibrosis in that order) | EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 |
| Pace of loss | Up to 1 kg per week. Loss faster than 1.6 kg per week was linked to worsening | KASL 2021 |
| Calories | Cut at least 500 kcal a day | KASL 2021 |
| Exercise | Moderate intensity or higher, 30 minutes or more, at least 3 times a week | KASL 2021 |
| Diet | Mediterranean-style pattern, limit ultra-processed food, avoid sugar-sweetened beverages | EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 |

## 2. Weekly check

- [ ] Did I avoid sugar-sweetened drinks (soda, sweetened juice)?
- [ ] Did I cut back on ultra-processed food, red meat and processed meat?
- [ ] Did I include whole grains, legumes, vegetables and fish in meals?
- [ ] Is my weight falling gradually, at no more than 1 kg per week?
- [ ] Did I do moderate-intensity exercise for 30 minutes or more, at least 3 times?
- [ ] Have I checked with a clinician whether I may drink alcohol and how much?

## 4. Signs that call for a medical visit

- Yellowing of the whites of the eyes or skin (jaundice)

## 5. Terminology

The older name NAFLD became MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) through a 2023 international consensus. The KASL 2021 guideline is written with the older name.
