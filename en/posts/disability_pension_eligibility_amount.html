Korea's Disability Pension - Sorting Out Four Look-Alike Benefits First

The basic and supplementary benefits, the three eligibility axes and recognized income, plus the 2026 thresholds and reductions

Aug 26, 2026 Finance & Economy

Disability pension, disability allowance, National Pension disability benefit, basic pension. In Korea these four names look alike, and their application counters overlap, so they rarely get sorted out in one sitting. Yet they rest on different statutes, different funding and different target groups. Look up an amount or a requirement without first fixing which program you mean and the answers keep failing to line up. Half the reason search results contradict each other traces back to exactly this.

So this piece starts with the split. It separates the four, then works through the disability pension alone: what the basic and supplementary benefits are each meant to cover, what each of the three eligibility axes actually looks at, and what recognized income is, since that is the concept applicants stumble over most.

Read this first. This article is general information about a Korean welfare program, not a determination of anyone's entitlement. Actual eligibility and payment amounts are settled by the reviewing authority against the standards in force on the date of application. The figures here were confirmed in August 2026 and are re-notified every year. Check your own case with your local community service center, the Bokjiro portal, or the Health and Welfare Call Center at 129.

1. Four look-alike programs - the statute and the funding differ first

The first thing to know is that these four fall into two camps. One weighs a contribution record; the other three look at present circumstances regardless of contributions.

Put into sentences, the table splits along these lines.

  • Only the National Pension disability benefit is contributory. Without a payment record there is nothing to draw. The other three can reach people who never paid in, because they are funded from taxes.
  • Each program grades disability on its own scale. The disability pension and the disability allowance use the severe and mild categories of the disability registration system; the National Pension uses grades one through four; industrial accident insurance uses a further scale of one through fourteen.
  • The disability pension and the disability allowance are mutually exclusive. Fall in the severe category and you are on the pension track; fall outside it and you are on the allowance track. They are not drawn side by side.

The basic pension has nothing to do with disability at all, looking only at age and recognized income, though as we will see it connects to the disability pension at the age of 65. What this article covers is the first row of the table. Korea's basic pension and the National Pension's enrollment and benefit structure are each written up separately, so if the table places you on another row, that is the faster read.

2. Two parts of the disability pension - basic and supplementary

The name suggests a single payment, but it is the sum of two benefits with different characters. The Disability Pension Act splits it into a basic benefit and a supplementary benefit, and each is meant to fill a different hole.

  • The basic benefit covers income lost through the loss or marked reduction of work capacity. It fills the earnings that stopped, which is why its payment band runs from 18 to 64.
  • The supplementary benefit covers the extra spending that disability brings, separate from lost earnings. That is why its amount varies by income bracket and why it keeps being calculated past 65.
  • The basic benefit for 2026 is 349,700 KRW a month, up 7,190 KRW from 342,510 KRW in 2025, reflecting the 2.1 percent consumer price change recorded for 2025. The widely quoted ceiling of 439,700 KRW is that figure plus a supplementary benefit of 90,000 KRW.
  • The raised amounts applied from the payment of 20 January 2026. Regular payment falls on the 20th of each month.

The supplementary benefit is not one number but a table split by bracket and age. The Ministry of Health and Welfare publishes the following for January 2026.

What the table alone does not explain is the conditions under which the special case applies, so which cell a given household sits in has to be confirmed at the local office.

3. Three eligibility axes - age, disability grade, recognized income

Eligibility is tested on three things at once. Miss one and the others carry no weight; clear one and nothing is settled either. What matters is that each axis looks at something different.

  • Age - 18 and over. Article 4 of the Disability Pension Act defines the beneficiary as a severely disabled person aged 18 or over. Ministry guidance treats an applicant as in scope if they turn 18 by the last day of the month of application. People aged 20 or under who are enrolled in a school under Article 2 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act are excluded, because that band is handled by the disability child allowance.
  • Disability grade - severe under the Disability Pension Act. Registration is required, and in the former grading this was grade 1, grade 2, and grade 3 with multiple impairments. This is not settled on paper alone: it goes through a disability grade review by the National Pension Service.
  • Recognized income - at or below the selection threshold. The figure built from the applicant's and spouse's income and assets must sit at or below that year's threshold. For 2026 that is 1.4 million KRW a month without a spouse and 2.24 million KRW with one.
  • Of the three, recognized income is the only one you can estimate in advance. Age is plain and the disability grade comes out of a review, but knowing how recognized income is built lets you place yourself roughly. That is why the next section is the long one.

Whether all three axes are cleared is the reviewing city, county or district office's call. No guide, this one included, stands in for that decision.

4. Recognized income - not the balance in an account

This is where application conversations most often go wrong. Read recognized income as monthly earnings and the arithmetic fails; read it as total assets and it fails too. The figure is an administrative value that presses income and assets into a single monthly number.

Recognized income = monthly assessed income + income-converted value of assets

Monthly assessed income = (regular earned income − earned income deduction) + other monthly income

Income-converted value of assets = [{(general assets − general asset deduction) + (financial assets − 20 million KRW) − debt} × 4% per year ÷ 12 months] + value of luxury vehicles and high-value memberships

Source: Ministry of Health and Welfare, disability pension overview - recognized income calculation (confirmed August 2026).

Start with why such a contraption exists and the rest follows. Look only at monthly income and someone with substantial property passes; look only at property and someone earning every month passes. Weighing both means putting them in the same unit, and the way to do that is to convert assets into monthly income. As a result, a sizeable gap opens between an account balance and recognized income.

  • The earned income deduction runs in two layers. A basic deduction of 950,000 KRW per person per month comes off regular earned income first, then a further 30 percent comes off what remains. It exists so that working is not itself a reason to fall out.
  • Other monthly income takes in business income, asset income, public transfer income and private transfer income. Imputed rent on rent-free housing lands in the private transfer column.
  • The general asset deduction varies by region. 135 million KRW in large cities, 85 million in mid-sized cities and 72.5 million in rural areas comes off general assets first, which is why the same house yields different results in different places. The 20 million KRW financial asset deduction is per household, so it does not scale with the number of people.
  • Luxury vehicles get no general asset deduction and their value is added straight in. The bar is a vehicle valued at 40 million KRW or more and under ten years old. But one vehicle owned by the disabled person is excluded from this treatment. This is one of the points where the calculation parts ways with the basic pension, so carrying figures over from another program's guidance produces the wrong answer.

The same machinery, explained in more depth on the older-age side, sits in Korea's basic pension eligibility and amounts. Deduction values and exceptions differ between programs, so the numbers do not transfer, but the reasoning behind why it is calculated this way carries over intact.

5. The 2026 threshold and two reductions

This is the part that changes yearly. Keeping two numbers apart prevents most of the confusion. The selection threshold is the gate that decides whether you get in; the basic benefit amount is the reference figure once you are in.

How the threshold is set is worth knowing too. It is not a ranking of individuals but a single line notified each year, worked backwards so that the lower 70 percent of severely disabled people by income fall inside it. The review simply compares that line against an individual's recognized income.

Nor does clearing the gate mean the full basic benefit is calculated. Two reductions of different character apply for different reasons.

  • Couple reduction. Where both spouses draw the basic benefit, each calculated amount is cut by 20 percent. Having a spouse does not trigger it on its own; both have to be recipients.
  • Excess reduction. Where recognized income plus the basic benefit reaches or passes the selection threshold, the amount is reduced in steps. It exists so that no inversion opens up between someone just inside the gate and someone just outside it.
  • Read the other way, the excess reduction means the closer recognized income sits to the threshold, the further the calculated amount drifts from the headline maximum. The 439,700 KRW figure is a ceiling, not an average.

6. Application and review - the process forks

After filing, the process does not run in a single line; it forks. The check on money and the review of disability are handled by different bodies.

  • Filing. In person at any eup, myeon or dong community service center, or online through the Bokjiro portal.
  • Means investigation. The applicant's and spouse's income and assets are examined to build the recognized income figure. Where there is a spouse, their income and assets are examined too even if only one person files.
  • Disability grade review. Handled by the National Pension Service. It runs through a medical advisory panel with two or more specialist physicians, and where needed the Service can request supplementary records or have the applicant examined directly at a designated institution.
  • Decision and notice. The city, county or district office combines both strands, decides and notifies. Once decided, payment is regular on the 20th of each month.

What can be done when a result does not sit right is also written into the statute. Article 18 of the Disability Pension Act lets a person who objects to a payment decision or any other disposition file an objection with the provincial governor, mayor, county head or district head, in writing, within 90 days of learning that the disposition was made. The existence of an objection route does not mean a result will be overturned, but it does mean a notice is not the end of the road.

Nor does a negative outcome close the file. Article 10-2 of the same Act sets up applicant history management, under which income and assets are re-examined annually for five years. That is a channel that does not require filing afresh each year.

7. What changes at 65

This is the point where the disability pension and the basic pension meet. The Ministry of Health and Welfare states that the basic benefit runs until the month before the recipient turns 65, after which it is replaced by the basic pension. The basic benefit exists to cover income lost to reduced work capacity, and from 65 the basic pension occupies the same slot. The design deliberately avoids stacking the two.

  • The supplementary benefit does not stop. As the earlier table shows, a separate 65-and-over band is set, with amounts varying by bracket.
  • The line being compared against changes. For 2026 the disability pension threshold is 1.4 million KRW a month with no spouse, while the basic pension's single-household threshold is 2.47 million KRW. The same person crosses into a different comparison simply by turning 65.
  • The basic pension is applied for, not granted automatically. Even with a transfer described in the guidance, the state of processing and the resulting amount are worth confirming at the community service center or the National Pension Service.

8. Where benefits overlap

Overlap and adjustment rules sit in separate provisions for each program, so they do not reduce to one sentence. What follows is only what official guidance confirms; other combinations are not asserted here.

  • The disability allowance addresses a different group. It covers registered adults 18 and over who are not severely disabled under the Disability Pension Act, so the definitions exclude each other.
  • The disability child allowance excludes disability pension recipients. Guidance does say that severely disabled people aged 18 to 20 who are still enrolled in school may choose between the severe disability child allowance and the disability pension.
  • The basic pension changes places with it at 65, as described above.
  • National Pension benefits feed into the income side. Public transfer income sits in the other monthly income column of assessed income, so a regular public benefit affects the recognized income calculation. Whether the disability pension and the National Pension disability benefit can be drawn at the same time is a separate question this article could not confirm against the statute.

9. What changes yearly and what does not

Welfare guidance goes stale fast because the shifting parts and the fixed parts are mixed together. Separating them tells you how much of an older article to trust.

  • What changes. The basic benefit is adjusted for the prior year's consumer price change. The selection threshold is re-notified to track movements in the income and assets of severely disabled people. Deduction values such as the earned income deduction and the general asset deduction are adjusted too.
  • What does not. The device of merging income and assets into recognized income, the split into basic and supplementary benefits, the three axes of age, disability grade and recognized income, and the principle that nothing starts until an application is filed.
  • That is why every figure here carries a year. The 2026 threshold of 1.4 million KRW was 1.38 million in 2025. Pulling an amount from guidance with no date attached produces a mismatch, and in a year when the threshold rises, the outcome can differ even when nothing about the applicant has.

10. Where applications commonly stall

More often than the procedure itself, it is the paperwork and the post-award duties that trip people up. These are the items worth knowing before the first trip.

  • Spouse paperwork. Recognized income covers both spouses, so the consent form for disclosure of financial information is needed for two people. Turning up with one person's form is a common reason for a second trip.
  • Proof of debt. Debt is deductible in the asset conversion, but without documentation it may not be picked up automatically. If there is a mortgage or a deposit liability, the paperwork is worth assembling.
  • Disability re-review. The grade review is part of the application process, and guidance states that where a registered person reaches a scheduled re-diagnosis date, the previously registered grade governs while the review runs. The interval depends on the individual registration history and has to be checked with the Service.
  • Reporting changes in income and assets. A reporting duty continues after payment starts. Amounts wrongly paid because a change went unreported are recoverable under Article 17 of the Disability Pension Act, and if not returned within the period they may be collected in the manner of delinquent national or local taxes.
Moving assets around to fit under a threshold is not covered here. Benefits obtained by false or otherwise improper means are recoverable, and unreturned amounts may be collected in the manner of delinquent taxes. Where an asset position is ambiguous, the right direction is not concealment but assembling documentation for legitimate deductions such as debt, and any item you are unsure about is safer raised with the community service center or the Health and Welfare Call Center at 129 before filing.

What could not be confirmed

These are the items that official sources did not settle. None of them is asserted in the body above, and none is filled in with guesswork here.

  • The special case conditions and the differing supplementary ranges. The Ministry table carries separate special case cells but not the conditions that trigger them. The press release describes the supplementary benefit as 30,000 to 90,000 KRW while the policy table rises to 439,700 KRW in the 65-and-over band; which range each figure describes could not be separated out from the sources.
  • Whether the disability pension and the National Pension disability benefit can be drawn together. The income-side treatment is confirmed; a provision governing concurrent receipt is not.
  • The step formula for the excess reduction. That reduction proceeds in steps is confirmed; the band-by-band arithmetic is not.
  • The interval for disability re-review. How cases are handled during a re-review is confirmed; the rule setting the interval is not.

Wrapping up

The first move when looking into the disability pension is not to find an amount but to work out which of the four programs your situation belongs to. Severe category or not, contribution record or none, past 65 or not. Settle those three and the field narrows to one program, at which point the numbers start agreeing with each other.

  • Sorting comes first. The disability pension is non-contributory welfare, the National Pension disability benefit is contributory insurance, the disability allowance covers those outside the severe category, and the basic pension runs on age.
  • Eligibility is tested on three axes together. Age 18 and over, severe under the Disability Pension Act, recognized income at or below the threshold. Only recognized income can be estimated in advance.
  • Read every figure with its year attached. For 2026 the threshold is 1.4 million KRW a month without a spouse and 2.24 million with one, and the basic benefit is 349,700 KRW a month. All of it is re-set annually.
