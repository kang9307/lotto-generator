When a Breach Notice Arrives - The Window That Closes and the Rights That Do Not

One clock runs out on a date and the other does not. Separate them, then start with what today allows

2026-09-07 Life & Practical

A breach notice contains two kinds of time. One kind ends on a date. The other does not. The compensation desk an operator sets up belongs to the first kind: it opens on a day, it shuts on a day, and if nothing happens in between, that particular process closes without you. Statutory claims and mediation belong to the second kind. They remain after the desk is gone.

Blurring the two costs you in both directions. Treat the claim desk as if it were a legal right and you miss the deadline while believing you are still early. Treat it as if it were the whole of your position and you file once and consider the matter finished. Pulled apart, each half tells you plainly what it wants from you.

This article is anchored in a Korean case. The dates, the claim path and the benefit list below apply to users of one Korean streaming service, and the statute cited is Korean law. If you are one of those users, the calendar in the first section is yours. If you are not, the structure still transfers: every breach notice anywhere splits into a company programme with a deadline and a legal position without one, and the order in which you handle them is the same. The statutes and the agencies, however, do not transfer. Where this article names a Korean provision or a Korean public service, the equivalent in your own jurisdiction will differ in scope, in timing and sometimes in existence.

What follows is a summary of an operator notice and of provisions in the Korean Personal Information Protection Act, offered as general information. Whether compensation is owed in an individual case, in what amount, and what legal effect any particular consent wording carries, are matters for the competent authority and the courts. Dates and figures are as of 7 September 2026.

First - what, where, and by when

The occasion is a cyber intrusion at TVING, a Korean online video service. The operator published an apology and compensation notice dated 3 September 2026, and that notice carries the claim period and the procedure. Before the story of how it happened, look at the calendar.

ItemAs stated in the notice
Claims openMonday 7 September 2026, 10:00
Claims closeWednesday 30 September 2026, 23:59
Delivery beginsTuesday 6 October 2026 from 10:00, in sequence
Who is eligibleMembers informed that they are affected
How to check eligibilityThe claim page under the MY menu, after logging in
Where claims are filedThe official website or the official mobile app. The notice states that claims through the call centre are not supported
How many timesOnce only. The notice states that the selected items cannot be changed after filing
EnquiriesA dedicated support line, 1551-2391, weekdays and weekends 09:00 to 18:00, closed on public holidays

There is not one deadline but two. The claim deadline and the benefit usage deadline run separately. The notice states that benefits unused within their validity period lapse automatically and that extensions are not available. Filing a claim is therefore not the moment you stop watching the calendar.

The claim procedure in four steps

The notice sets out four steps. The number of steps matters less than where each one happens. All four occur inside the operator's own surfaces, with no point at which the process hands you off to another site.

1. Log in to the official website or the official mobile app
2. Go to MY menu > compensation claim page
3. Complete identity verification
4. Select the benefit and submit

That shape is itself a filter against impersonation. Because the process starts at login, any screen that asks for personal details before a login is not this process. If a page you reached by tapping a link in a message asks for your ID and password first, close it and type the address yourself instead.

What to look at once the claim is filed

The notice puts the claim status and the delivery record in the MY menu, and says a text message goes to the mobile number registered on the account once delivery is complete. Two things follow. The first is worth doing in advance: check that the registered number is the one you actually use. The second is a caution rather than a task, because messages imitating that very notification rise during the same window.

The benefits and their separate validity periods

The notice describes four benefit lines. Reading the conditions before the amounts is the more practical order, because the conditions decide what you can receive and the periods decide when you can use it.

ItemContentPeriod and conditions
Hacking and phishing insuranceOne year of cover through an insurer, at no cost, with coverage up to 3 million won6 October 2026 to 5 October 2027. The policy identifier appears in the MY menu from 6 October, and claims are made to the insurer using that identifier
Premium viewing featuresThree months of highest-quality playback, 400 downloads, and four simultaneous devices6 October 2026 10:00 to 31 December 2026 23:59. Applied to accounts subscribed on the ad-supported, basic or standard tiers, with some partner content excluded
Points50 points, stated as worth 5,000 wonSame period, usable against individually purchased content. Accounts already on the premium tier as of 30 September receive these points once instead of the item above
Entertainment benefitOne of two: a one-month pass to another service, or a cinema discount couponSame period. Accounts that already hold a pass for that other service receive coins of equivalent value instead

In the insurance line, up to 3 million won is a coverage ceiling, not an amount scheduled for payment. A ceiling describes the most the policy would pay if a covered incident occurred; whether anything is paid, and how much, depends on the incident types the policy defines and on assessment. The scope of cover and the exclusions live in the insurer's documents rather than in the notice, so reading those documents once the identifier is issued is the sequence that matches how the product works.

How to read the numbers

The figures circulating around this case are large, and large figures change units easily. Based on reporting of the investigation findings, the picture sorts out as follows. Once the units are aligned, the character of each number becomes more interesting than its size.

The figure as usually seenWhat it actually counts
About 39.54 millionNot people but accounts, and duplicates are included. It is reported as the sum of active, dormant, closed and test accounts
About 19.53 million peopleAn estimate the operator produced by removing duplicates on its own. Those who announced the findings are reported to have described it as a figure that has not been verified. Duplicates could be identified among accounts carrying a linking identifier, but accounts without one could not be resolved to individuals, which is why the total account count was announced instead
20 data fieldsNot twenty fields per account but the maximum per account. The reported averages diverge to 11.1 and 4.6 depending on whether the account carried a linking identifier
24GBThe volume on the user-data side. A separate volume for the development-project side appeared in the same announcement, so merging or swapping the two distorts the scale
Up to 3 million wonAn insurance coverage ceiling. Neither a scheduled payment nor an award of damages

Two practical uses come out of these distinctions. The first is that checking whether your own account is affected is independent of the total. A large total does not include you and a small one does not exclude you; only the eligibility check answers that. The second is that impersonation messages quote large figures as if the figure were the credential. A message can carry an accurate number and still be false.

Why notification is not automatic payment

Notification and compensation rest on different foundations. Notification is a statutory duty. Article 34 paragraph 1 of the Personal Information Protection Act requires a controller who becomes aware that personal data has been leaked to inform the affected data subjects without delay. Paragraph 4 of the same article requires a report to the Protection Commission or to a specialised institution designated by Presidential Decree.

No provision there requires payment of money or the granting of service benefits. Compensation therefore emerges as a programme the operator designs. Because the operator designs it, the operator also sets who qualifies, by when, and through what channel, and the result is a claim-based system. Determining who receives what requires matching an account to a person, and here that matching is performed by the person, through a login.

Knowing this difference sorts the sentences you are reading. Whatever appears in a breach notification is there because the law demands it. Whatever appears in a compensation notice is there because the operator promised it. The first is contested against the statute; the second is contested against the published terms.

What remains regardless of the claim

Whether or not you file, the routes the statute lays down stay where they are. At the level of the provisions themselves, they look like this. Which route suits which case, and how any of them would end, belongs to individual judgment, so what follows describes only the shape of each mechanism.

ProvisionMechanismShapeWhat this route does not do
Art. 39(1)Damages claimA data subject who suffers loss from a violation of the Act may claim damages. The controller cannot escape liability unless it proves the absence of intent or negligence, which places that burden on the operatorThe fact that loss occurred, and its extent, still have to be asserted by the person claiming
Art. 39(3)Multiplied awardWhere a leak arises from intent or gross negligence and causes loss, the court may set damages within a range not exceeding five times the amount of lossIt is not automatically five times. The court weighs the eight circumstances listed in paragraph 4 of the same article
Art. 39-2Statutory damagesWhere personal data is leaked through intent or negligence, a data subject may claim a reasonable sum as damages within a limit of 3 million won, without separately proving the actual amount of loss3 million won is not a standard payment. It is a ceiling, and the court fixes the sum considering the whole tenor of argument and the evidence
Art. 43Dispute mediationAn application may be filed with the Dispute Mediation Committee. Paragraph 3 provides that a controller notified of an application shall respond to the mediation absent special reasonsMediation is not a judgment. Its requirements and procedure differ from litigation
Arts. 44 and 47Timing and effect of mediationA proposal is drawn up within 60 days of the application. A party who does not indicate acceptance within 15 days of receiving the proposal is deemed to have accepted it, and an accepted mediation has the same effect as a judicial settlement60 days is not the day a conclusion arrives; it is the deadline for drafting the proposal. And if either party files suit, mediation processing is suspended
Art. 49Collective mediationWhere the same or similar harm affects many data subjects, collective mediation may be referred or applied for. If the controller accepts the outcome, it may be recommended to draw up a compensation plan covering affected people who were not partiesNon-parties are not included automatically. Commencement is publicly announced and additional participation is filed for
Art. 51Group actionA qualifying organisation may sue to prohibit or halt the infringing conduct where collective mediation was refused or its outcome not acceptedThis action is not a route to monetary compensation. What it asks for is prohibition and cessation

Two cells in that table attract more misreading than the rest: five times and 3 million won. Both are ceilings rather than benchmarks. The provisions say only that a court may set an amount within that range, and what is actually recognised varies with the case.

One more point. Article 39-2 paragraph 3 allows someone who has claimed damages under Article 39 to convert that claim into a statutory damages claim at any time before oral argument on the facts concludes. The two routes, in other words, do not exclude each other.

The seven things a notice must contain

Judging whether the notice you received was adequate requires knowing what the law asks for. Article 34 paragraph 1 enumerates seven items.

  • The categories of personal data leaked
  • When the leak occurred and the circumstances of it
  • Specific information on what the data subject can do to minimise the harm that may result
  • The controller's response measures and the remedy procedures available
  • The department and contact details for receiving reports where harm has occurred
  • Information on legal rights and how to exercise them, including damages under Article 39, statutory damages under Article 39-2 and dispute mediation under Article 43
  • Other matters prescribed by Presidential Decree

The sixth item overlaps exactly with the subject of this article. The law requires the notice itself to explain the claim routes and the mediation system. If your notice contains that paragraph, that paragraph is where the next action begins.

Article 34 was amended on 10 March 2026, and the amended provision takes effect on 11 September 2026. The amendment adds a new paragraph 2 requiring notification not only when a leak has occurred but also when the controller becomes aware of a possibility of a leak as prescribed by Presidential Decree, in which case information for minimising harm goes to every data subject potentially affected. Article 29 on security safeguards, along with Articles 39 and 39-2, take effect on the same date.

Different fields carry different risk

Treating leaked data as one undifferentiated block produces an equally undifferentiated response. Each field generates a different downstream risk, and therefore a different countermeasure.

FieldWhat it enables on its ownWhat it enables in combination
NameWeak identifying power in isolationIt lends credibility to everything else. An approach that opens by using your name reads as though a real relationship exists
Mobile numberIt creates a channel: messages and callsPaired with a name it supports approaches disguised as identity-verification messages. It also serves as a hint inside account-recovery flows
Email addressOn many services it is the login identifier itselfWhere the same password was reused, it becomes the raw material for replaying a known pair against other services
Date of birth and genderValues that never change, so they cannot be retiredName, date of birth and phone number together approach the verification set used at many counters
Payment historyIt produces sentences only someone who knows the date and the amount could sayAn approach framed around a refund or a payment error acquires specificity, and specificity lowers suspicion
Cross-service linking identifierNot information a person can readA value usable to tie records scattered across services back to one individual

A dividing line runs through that table. Passwords can be changed and cards can be reissued. Names and dates of birth cannot. When an unchangeable value has left, the response is not recovery but reducing the number of procedures for which that value serves as the pass. If an account still verifies you using nothing but fixed values like a date of birth, that account is where turning on an additional factor pays the most.

What it means that a key left with the data

According to reporting of the investigation findings, part of the stored mobile numbers and email addresses had been encrypted, but the encryption key was exfiltrated alongside them, and the result was assessed as equivalent to plaintext. The phrasing looks technical; the meaning is not.

Encryption is a measure that stops a stored value from being read as it stands. The key is the means of reversing it. So when ciphertext and key leave together by the same path, encryption is not operating for that data. Structurally it is the safe and its key carried out in one trip.

The same reporting carries the other half. Passwords were stored in a form that cannot be reversed and are not recoverable as plaintext, and refund account numbers were assessed as remaining encrypted and not decryptable. Encryption was not nullified across the board but for particular fields, and that distinction is what sets the order of response. Fields whose key travelled with them are handled on the same footing as fields that left in plaintext; the rest are handled with ordinary measures.

A reading rule for notices comes out of this too. Where a notice says only that data was stored in encrypted form and says nothing about whether the key left, that sentence alone does not establish that the field is safe. On the statutory side the adjoining provision is Article 29 on the duty of safety measures, and whether measures existed and whether they were sufficient is assessed by investigation and review.

Today - what each measure blocks and what it does not

Articles that list countermeasures are common. A bare list makes everything look mandatory, and trying to do everything usually ends in doing nothing. Attaching what each measure blocks and what it leaves untouched restores an order. The general sequence for responding to any breach is set out separately in the data breach response guide, so what follows is only what fits this particular shape of incident.

MeasureWhat it blocksWhat it does not block
Clearing password reuse elsewhereReplaying a known ID and password pair against other services. Without reuse that path is cutCirculation of the name, number and date of birth already gone. And approaches that persuade you to type things in yourself
Enabling two-factor authenticationLogins where only the password is known. It closes the cheapest attack pathApproaches that relay the verification code in real time, and reuse of sessions already issued
Moving the second factor from SMS to an app or a security keyAttacks that intercept the message itselfThe recovery problem when a device is lost. Without backup codes stored separately, you lock yourself out too
Reviewing payment methods and recurring chargesIt surfaces subscriptions running unnoticed. The earlier they are found, the more room there is to dispute themIt does not stop payments in advance. It is closer to a detection measure
Telecom identity-theft prevention service (M-Safer)Run free of charge by the Korea Association for ICT Promotion. A subscription-status lookup sweeps telecom services opened in your name, and a subscription block prevents new mobile line activation, number portability and name transfers in advance. Budget mobile operators are coveredFraud using a line already opened, and identity theft outside telecoms. Per the official guidance, the preventive block covers mobile lines
Credit transaction safety blockA scheme introduced in August 2024. Once registered, new credit is blocked across the board: personal loans, secured loans, card loans and new credit card issuanceLoans already drawn down and existing contracts. Account opening and transfers are outside its scope. Release requires a branch visit
Integrated account information service (Accountinfo)Operated by the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute. It cannot prevent an improper filing or issuance, only tell you it happened

If an order has to be picked, clearing password reuse comes first, because the cheapest and most common follow-on attempt in incidents of this shape is replaying an already-known pair against other services. Whether account credentials are already circulating can be checked free and without registration through a government-run leaked-credentials lookup service, which was expanded in January 2026 so that an email address can be queried alongside an ID and password. Password managers and two-factor authentication are each covered separately.

What is less urgent, and what is unnecessary

Changing a resident registration number is a separate scheme under Article 7-4 of the Resident Registration Act and carries conditions. The provision lists cases such as suffering or risking harm to life or body, or suffering or risking property damage, as a result of the leaked number, and the responsible ministry's guidance states that it is not mere leakage but harm or the risk of harm arising from leakage, supported by evidence. Leakage alone does not make it automatic.

Paid credit-monitoring products can be judged against the observation that in this shape of incident the dominant risk is the increase in contact channels rather than payment data itself. Most of the public measures listed above are free or handled through official counters, so looking at what remains uncovered after using them, and only then considering paid options, wastes the least.

Dormant and closed accounts

The disclosed figure in this case is reported to cover not only active accounts but long-dormant ones and accounts already closed. That carries a structural problem.

People who already left sit in the worst position for receiving a notice. They deleted the app, so no push arrives. They do not log in, so a message displayed only after login is never displayed. If the email address on file is an old one, mail does not land either. Yet they are inside the count.

The path to checking therefore forks. If the account still exists, eligibility appears on the claim page under the MY menu after login. If it does not, that path is closed, and contacting the dedicated support line named in the notice is faster. The notice contains no sentence describing how closed accounts are handled.

Where even recalling which services you once used and abandoned is the difficulty, searching an old mailbox for signup confirmations and payment receipts is the practical method. A mailbox is more accurate than memory. Which sites you registered with using an identity-verification service can also be swept through the government-run verification-history lookup and membership withdrawal service.

The stretch where impersonation rises

The stretch in which a breach becomes widely known and a compensation process opens holds every condition impersonation needs. Many people know about the same event, many are actively waiting for a message, and the shape of the real procedure is public. Impersonation copies that shape exactly.

Trying to tell them apart by wording therefore works poorly. Sentences copied from a real procedure read like a real procedure. The reliable test is not the wording but the path.

Tap the link you received   ->  a human has to judge whether the address is genuine
Open the official app/URL   ->  there is nothing to judge
  • Do not tap the link in the message. Type the official address into the address bar, or start from the official app already installed
  • The procedure in the notice begins at login. A screen that asks for a name, a date of birth or a card number before any login is not that procedure
  • The notice states that claims through the call centre are not supported. An offer to file on your behalf by phone does not match the published procedure
  • Benefits are delivered to the account. A request for a bank account number or a verification code in order to receive them does not match the published delivery method
  • Instructions to install a file or a remote-support tool are not part of this procedure

Responses by type of approach are covered separately in the phishing and scam message guide and the voice phishing guide. In Korea, intrusion incidents and personal data complaints reach a counselling centre by dialling 118, where urgent hacking-related consultation runs year round.

What is left after the deadline

Once 23:59 on 30 September passes, what closes and what remains sorts out like this.

ItemAfter the deadline
The operator's compensation claimAccording to the notice, it can no longer be filed
Benefits already claimedEach item carries its own validity period, and the notice states that anything unused within it lapses
Damages claim under Art. 39Remains. It follows the limitation rules for civil claims
Statutory damages under Art. 39-2Remains
Dispute mediation under Art. 43Remains. Application and procedure carry no cost
Reporting an infringement under Art. 62Remains. That article lets a person whose rights or interests were infringed report to the Protection Commission, and provides for a reporting centre handling intake and counselling

Missing the deadline does not, however, put you in an easier position. The routes that remain generally cost more time and more material. The claim desk shuts within days but takes a few clicks; the remaining routes are open indefinitely but require preparation. Handling the shorter clock first is simply the correct order.

What every route needs in common is a record. Keep the notice itself, the eligibility screen, the confirmation screen, and the dates and content of any unusual contact that follows, and whichever procedure you eventually take, the material exists. Screens are not recoverable once they have gone by.

This is a general description of a legal framework, not legal advice on a specific matter. The current wording of each provision and its effective date can be re-checked in the official statute database, and the compensation conditions and deadlines are governed by the operator notice as the authoritative text. Which route fits an individual case, and what outcome it produces, rests with the competent authority and the courts. Readers outside Korea should treat the statutory citations here as an illustration of structure rather than as their own law.

What could not be confirmed

The following items could not be grounded in a primary document at the time of writing. Where a value is known through another route but does not appear in the primary text, it is placed here.

  • The body of the investigation announcement. A press release dated 3 September 2026 exists, but its body is distributed as an attached document, so the original wording could not be read. The account counts, field counts and the encryption-key description are written here on the basis of reporting about that announcement and may differ from the announcement's own phrasing
  • The definition behind the expression 70 types. This figure appears alongside the 20 fields, but no source examined explains what unit it counts, which is why the value is not used in the body of this article
  • The original text of the breach notification notice dated 3 June. Its existence on the notice board was verified, but the document itself could not be opened. The list of leaked fields it contains is known only through quotation in reporting
  • The exact scope of the fields whose encryption key was exfiltrated. Reporting extends as far as saying part of the mobile numbers and email addresses, and how large that part is could not be established
  • The insurance policy terms. The 3 million won ceiling is stated in the notice, but the covered incident types, the exclusions and the documents required for a claim are not in the notice text
  • How closed accounts file a claim. The notice contains no sentence on the treatment of withdrawn members. Reporting indicates that re-registration is required, but that could not be confirmed against the notice itself
  • The cause and the allocation of responsibility. Part of the initial intrusion path is reported as not yet established, and the precise number of affected individuals and the outcome of administrative proceedings are reported as still pending. Being unsettled, they are not treated in this article
  • The consent wording on the claim screen. What consent is collected during the process can only be seen by entering the screen, and the wording itself is not reproduced in the notice
