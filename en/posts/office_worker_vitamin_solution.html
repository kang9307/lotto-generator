You wake up at 7 a.m. and don't get home until after 10 p.m. Even on weekends, work is on your mind. You find yourself anxiously checking your email on your phone. Sound familiar?

73% of modern office workers report chronic fatigue, and research shows that the vast majority of cases stem from nutritional imbalances. In our hectic daily lives, it's easy to overlook essential nutrients - and the result is a broken-down energy system.

The 3 Vitamins Every Office Worker Must Have

1. Vitamin B Complex - The Engine of Energy Metabolism

Why you need it:

  • Converts carbohydrates, protein, and fat into usable energy
  • Maintains normal nervous system function
  • Regulates stress hormones

Deficiency symptoms: severe fatigue, poor concentration, depressed mood, tingling in hands and feet

Recommended daily intake: B1 (1.2 mg), B2 (1.4 mg), B6 (1.5 mg), B12 (2.4 mcg)

2. Vitamin C - The Stress Fighter

Why you need it:

  • Regulates the stress hormone cortisol
  • Protects cells with powerful antioxidant action
  • Strengthens the immune system
  • Supports skin health through collagen synthesis

Deficiency symptoms: frequent colds, slow wound healing, bleeding gums, skin breakouts

Recommended daily intake: men 90 mg, women 75 mg (200-500 mg during periods of high stress)

3. Magnesium - Nature's Tranquilizer

Why you need it:

  • Relaxes muscles and improves sleep quality
  • Involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions
  • Relieves stress-related anxiety
  • Regulates blood pressure and maintains heart health

Deficiency symptoms: muscle cramps, insomnia, headaches, anxiety, irregular heartbeat

Recommended daily intake: men 420 mg, women 320 mg

Office Worker Vitamin Checklist

Morning Routine (7-9 a.m.)

  • Take one B-complex tablet (on an empty stomach or after a light meal)
  • Wash it down with a glass of warm water
  • Avoid taking it with caffeinated drinks

Lunchtime (12-1 p.m.)

  • Take 500 mg of vitamin C (within 30 minutes after your meal)
  • Pairing it with citrus fruits or vegetables boosts absorption
  • Drink plenty of water

Evening (8-9 p.m.)

  • Take 200-400 mg of magnesium (after dinner)
  • Having it with warm herbal tea can promote better sleep
  • Taking it alongside calcium improves absorption

Precautions

- If you are already taking medication, consult your doctor before adding supplements.
- Pregnant or nursing women and those with specific health conditions should seek medical advice first.
- Do not exceed recommended dosages; adjust to your individual constitution.
- Supplements are an aid, not a replacement for a balanced diet.
