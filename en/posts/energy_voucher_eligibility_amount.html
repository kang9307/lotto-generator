Korea's Energy Voucher - Who Gets It and How Much

Two gates, an amount set only by household size, and the point where summer and winter part ways - as of August 2026

Aug 10, 2026 Law & Legal

Read this first - the reference date and the limits of this article

This article describes a South Korean government program as of August 2026, and it does not substitute for an eligibility determination or an administrative decision. The Energy Voucher's amounts, application window and usage period are re-set by official notification every program year. Whether a particular household qualifies cannot be settled by this page. The final determination rests with the eup, myeon or dong community service center for your registered address and with the Korea Energy Agency, which implements the program. Before applying, check the current-year announcement at the official site (www.energyv.or.kr) or through the integrated call center (1600-3190).

What the program actually gives you - not cash, but a right against energy bills

Some households in Korea leave the air conditioner off in August and ration the boiler in January. The Energy Voucher is aimed squarely at that. Its formal name is the energy usage voucher. The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy oversees the program and the Korea Energy Agency runs it, while the intake counters are the local community service centers that handle welfare administration and the Bokjiro online portal.

Despite the word voucher, nothing lands in a bank account. The support arrives in one of two forms. The first is bill deduction, delivered through a virtual card, which subtracts the amount automatically from an electricity, city gas or district heating bill. The second is the National Happiness Card, a physical card, used to pay for fuels you buy and load yourself, such as kerosene, LPG or briquettes. Which of the two you choose has more effect on what you actually feel than the headline amount does.

The core idea: this is not a cash benefit but a right to spend a fixed sum on specific energy sources within a fixed window. Miss the window, or aim it at the wrong energy source, and it simply does not land.

Two gates - benefit status and a household-member characteristic

This is where most misunderstandings begin. Being a basic livelihood benefit recipient does not by itself make a household eligible. The income gate and the household-member characteristic gate must be cleared at the same time.

Gate one: benefit recipient status

You must be a recipient of livelihood, medical, housing or education benefit under the National Basic Living Security Act. Receiving any one of the four clears this gate. Note the design: the program does not run a fresh income test of its own, it simply borrows a status that has already been determined elsewhere.

Gate two: household-member characteristic

At least one member of the registered household must fall into one of the categories below. It does not have to be the applicant, and one qualifying member is enough for the whole household.

  • Older adult: born on or before 31 December 1961
  • Infant or young child: born on or after 1 January 2019, aged 7 or under and not yet in school
  • Person with disabilities: registered under the Act on Welfare of Persons with Disabilities
  • Pregnant or postpartum woman: pregnant, or within six months of giving birth
  • Patient with a severe disease, a rare disease, or a severe intractable disease
  • Single-parent family: a beneficiary under the Single-Parent Family Support Act
  • Child-headed household
  • Multi-child household: parents with two or more children under 19

The trap here is the categories defined by birth date. Older adult and young child status are not counted in years but drawn against a cutoff date fixed for each program year. A household rejected last year is not automatically rejected this year, and one that qualified last year is not automatically safe this year. The young-child category is the sharper edge, because a child entering school drops out of it, which can knock a household out of the program in the year that happens.

Who is excluded

A household is excluded where every member receives benefits at a care facility under Article 32 of the National Basic Living Security Act. The logic is structural: heating and cooling are provided by the facility, so no household energy bill arises.

Clearing both gates means a household is described as eligible in the official guidance, not that payment is confirmed. Household composition and benefit status are re-checked against administrative records, and the determination is made by the community service center and the Korea Energy Agency.

How much in 2026 - household size is the only variable

One thing sets the amount: the number of people in the registered household. How low the income is, which of the four benefits is received, and which characteristic category opened the door make no difference to the figure. That simplicity is both the design and the limitation of the program.

Household size2026 amountNote
1 person295,200 KRWA total for the entire usage period, not a monthly payment
2 people407,500 KRW
3 people532,700 KRW
4 or more701,300 KRW

Two things in that table deserve attention. First, the ladder stops at four. A household of five and a household of eight receive the same figure, so support per person thins out as the family grows. Second, this is not a monthly deposit. It is a single total covering the whole usage period, so a household that burns through it in two cold months has nothing left for the rest.

To judge whether that figure covers your winter, it helps to compare it against your own bills. Summer and winter - what changes is the energy source, not the money

One misconception comes up constantly: that the summer voucher and the winter voucher are two separate applications. You apply once. What differs is the usage window and which energy sources you may spend it on during that window.

ItemSummerWinter
Bill deduction period1 July - 30 September 20261 October 2026 - 31 May 2027
National Happiness Card periodNot applicable3 October 2026 - 31 May 2027
Energy covered by bill deductionElectricity onlyElectricity, city gas or district heating - choose one
Energy covered by the physical cardNot applicableElectricity, city gas, kerosene, LPG, briquettes

What this means in practice is straightforward. In summer there is only electricity. Fans, air conditioning and refrigeration dominate summer consumption, so the design follows the load, but the city gas a household burns for hot water is not deducted during the summer window. In winter the options widen. Fuels that arrive without a bill, such as kerosene, LPG and briquettes, can only be paid for through the physical card in the winter window.

The winter rule that you pick one of electricity, city gas or district heating for bill deduction also matters. A home heated by city gas but with heavy electricity use should aim the deduction at whichever winter bill runs larger. Households in district-heating apartments who reflexively pick electricity are the classic case of leaving money on the table.

Bill deduction carries one more condition. Support applies only where the deduction has been registered and the bill has been issued by 31 May 2027. If your May consumption is billed in June, the final month can fall outside the window.

Bill deduction or the physical card - how to choose

This is not a matter of preference. It is decided by what your home burns for heat.

Many households already hold a National Happiness Card, since the same card system is used for other Korean welfare payments such as pregnancy and childbirth medical expense support. It is issued through card companies including BC, Lotte, Samsung, KB Kookmin, Shinhan and Hyundai. If you already hold one, you are usually guided to link the voucher to the existing card rather than open a new one.

If the choice is still unclear, one rule settles most cases. Heat that arrives with a bill means bill deduction; heat you buy and load means the physical card. A household running a kerosene boiler that registers for bill deduction will never see a single won of it reach the heating cost.

Applying - window, counters, documents, and the caseworker route

The 2026 application window runs from 15 June to 31 December 2026. Because the usage period extends to 31 May 2027, a household that only hears about the program in the middle of winter still has room to apply if it is before the end of December.

Who may apply

  • The person concerned: someone whose benefit recipient status is confirmed as of the application date and who may qualify under the household-member characteristic
  • A proxy: a household member listed on the resident registration abstract, or a relative, holding a letter of delegation
  • A caseworker acting ex officio: the official obtains consent by phone or in person and files on the household's behalf

Where to apply

  • In person: the eup, myeon or dong community service center for your registered address
  • Online: Bokjiro (www.bokjiro.go.kr)
  • Ex officio: the caseworker files after obtaining consent

Documents to prepare

  • The energy usage voucher application form
  • For a proxy application: a letter of delegation and the proxy's identification
  • For bill deduction: the most recently paid electricity, city gas or district heating bill

Being turned away at the counter for want of a bill is common. If your household no longer receives paper bills, download the latest statement from the supplier's app or website beforehand. For older residents with limited mobility, the ex officio route carries real weight: telling the welfare officer at the community service center about the situation often leads to the case being handled through individual contact.

Where households fall out

The patterns that recur at the counter are fairly fixed. More households lose the benefit to procedure than to ineligibility.

Briquette coupons and the kerosene voucher - winter support is choose-one

The Energy Voucher is not the only public support for winter heating in Korea. But they do not stack. The winter Energy Voucher is not provided where the household falls into any of the following.

  • A recipient of winter fuel costs under the Emergency Aid and Support Act
  • A person or household issued a briquette coupon for that year by the Korea Mine Rehabilitation and Mineral Resources Corporation
  • A person or household issued a briquette-conversion energy voucher for that year

The kerosene voucher follows the same logic. It targets basic livelihood benefit households running an oil boiler, specifically those that are single-parent families or child-headed households, and it does not stack with the winter Energy Voucher, the briquette coupon, or emergency-aid winter fuel costs. It is issued as a card and used to buy heating kerosene at filling stations and kerosene retailers.

The deciding question is simple. Which support actually reaches the fuel your home burns? A household heating with briquettes that picks the Energy Voucher and takes it off the electricity bill still faces the same winter heating cost.

How this sits beside schemes in other countries

Two points are worth separating for readers outside Korea. First, other Korean supports run in parallel with this one and are applied for separately. Second, most countries with cold winters or hot summers run something structurally similar, though the design differs.

  • Korea, alongside the voucher: the electricity utility operates a welfare discount for benefit recipients, near-poor households and people with disabilities, applied for separately from the Energy Voucher
  • Korea, alongside the voucher: city gas suppliers and local governments run their own bill reduction schemes, and some municipalities add local heating support of their own
  • United States: the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, known as LIHEAP, is a federally funded program administered through states and territories to help low-income households with home energy costs. Eligibility rules and payment amounts are set at state level, so they are not comparable line by line with the Korean figures above
  • United Kingdom and elsewhere: schemes such as the Warm Home Discount exist to reduce winter energy costs for lower-income households, again with their own national rules. Treat the existence and the purpose as the comparison point, not the amounts

The practical sequencing inside Korea is worth stating plainly. Secure the standing tariff discounts first, because they reduce the bill every month, then let the Energy Voucher absorb the seasonal spike. Discounts lower the rate while the voucher spends down a balance, so the two waste less when they overlap. If you are already a benefit recipient, the eligibility pools overlap heavily with other Korean welfare cards, and we have laid out one of them the same way in the culture voucher card guide.

What could not be confirmed

These items could not be settled from the official guidance, so no figures for them appear above. Check them directly with the call center (1600-3190) or your community service center.

  • How the total splits between summer and winter: the official amount guidance presents a total by household size, and the internal allocation between the two seasons could not be confirmed. This article therefore stays with the total, the usage windows and the eligible energy sources
  • Whether an unused balance carries over or is refunded: the official usage guidance does not state whether a remaining balance lapses at the end of the usage period or rolls into the next year
  • How an unused summer balance is treated in winter: whether a summer remainder can be spent in the winter window, and whether that requires a separate request, could not be confirmed
  • The current-year amount and application window for the kerosene voucher: the eligibility and the overlap rules are confirmed, but the amount and schedule are set annually, so no figure has been carried over here
  • The current-year scale of the briquette coupon: this is announced separately by the Korea Mine Rehabilitation and Mineral Resources Corporation
No unconfirmed amount has been filled in by estimate. Welfare figures change from one year to the next, so leaving a blank and pointing at the counter that can answer it beats printing a wrong number.

Frequently asked questions

Q. I am a benefit recipient, so why am I told I do not qualify?

Most likely because no household member falls into a characteristic category. Benefit status alone does not clear both gates. Going through every member one by one, checking birth year, disability registration, disease recognition and single-parent support status, often turns up a category that was missed.

Q. I live alone and the amount feels small.

The figure is set by household size alone, so a one-person household sits in the lowest bracket. That said, a single-person household also consumes less, so coverage as a share of the actual bill can run higher. Layering a utility welfare discount on top changes what you feel more than the headline number does.

Q. I did not use any of it over the summer. What happens to that portion?

Summer and winter are designed with different windows and different eligible energy sources, and the treatment of an unused summer portion is one of the items this article could not confirm. Describing your household situation to the call center is the accurate route.

Q. I moved. Do I have to apply again?

The program runs off your registered address, so after filing a move-in report it is safest to tell the community service center at the new address. If you registered for bill deduction, a change of supplier or account holder can break the deduction.

Q. I already have a National Happiness Card. Do I need a new one?

Usually not. Households are commonly guided to link the voucher to the card they already hold, so tell the card company or the community service center that you have one before starting a new application.

In short - three things to check now

The Energy Voucher is not a complicated program. What it does have is a fixed set of places where the money leaks. Three checks cover most of it.

  1. The household-member characteristic: do not give up after looking only at benefit status. Lay out every member and test them against older adult, young child, disability, pregnancy, disease, single-parent, child-headed and multi-child categories. One match is enough
  2. The delivery method: heat that arrives with a bill means bill deduction, heat you buy and load means the physical card. Get this wrong and the size of the amount stops mattering
  3. The deadlines: apply by 31 December 2026, spend by 31 May 2027, and for bill deduction make sure the bill is issued within that window

And remember that winter support in Korea is a choose-one structure. If your household already receives a briquette coupon or the kerosene voucher, the winter Energy Voucher will not be added on top, so start from which support actually touches the fuel you burn.

The amounts, requirements and dates in this article are as of August 2026 and change with each program year. Do not treat this page as confirmation of your household's eligibility or entitlement. Before applying, check the current-year announcement at the official Energy Voucher site (www.energyv.or.kr), and confirm eligibility and amounts with the community service center for your registered address or the integrated call center (1600-3190).
