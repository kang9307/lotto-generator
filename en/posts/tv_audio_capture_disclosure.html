What Does Turning the TV Off Actually Turn Off - What a Set Keeps Doing in Standby

The definition of standby, the difference between a fingerprint, a sound and a transcript, and how far the settings reach

2026-09-07 Tech & IT

Press the power button on the remote and the screen goes dark. The picture that forms in most people's heads at that moment is a simple one: the machine has stopped. Open the product documentation for the same set, though, and the word is not being used in that sense at all.

Off is not one condition. There is a state in which only the panel has gone dark, one in which panel and speakers are silent while a circuit waits for an infrared pulse, and one in which part of the system is deliberately kept running so the next start-up feels instant. Manufacturer documentation gives those three separate names, and almost every argument about televisions and microphones turns out to be an argument about which state someone meant.

Everything stated here as fact comes from manufacturer support pages and manuals, broadcast standards, published patents and papers, a regulator's complaint and consent order, and Korean statutes. A claim that appears only in reporting is attributed rather than asserted, and unsettled items are collected near the end with the reason attached. The settings paths and statutes below are Korean; menu names, model availability and applicable law differ by country.

The trigger - what was published, and how open the evidence is

In the first days of September 2026 a hardware publication released an investigation into one manufacturer's smart televisions: a video running roughly 135 minutes, produced with other hardware channels and a group of independent researchers. By the time the story reached Korean-language outlets the headline had compressed into one sentence — the sets were caught recording audio while the screen was off and scanning the other devices on the home network.

The nature of the evidence has to be pinned down first. There is no written article version of the same investigation; the primary output is that one video, and a video is an awkward object to quote precisely. One of the collaborators, however, posted a technical write-up on a community forum the day before the video went live, and that document fixes the test target in a single line. The set is an OLED65G5WUA running webOS 10.2.1, with an explicit caveat attached: the work was carried out on exactly one unit.

Two sentences in that document change the direction of the story rather than reinforcing the headline. The first says the content recognition daemon on the test set was inactive, the library empty and the database filled with zeroes. The second says the voice processing chain runs on the device itself, and that what travels to a remote endpoint is natural language processing and the search backend, with the wake phrase named outright. Neither disproves anything shown in the video; both narrow the range of things the video can be about.

The manufacturer has said nothing about this investigation. As of the reporting there was no response, which is a different state from having declined to respond. A line from the same company stating that its televisions do not collect or record ambient conversations circulates alongside the story as though it were a rebuttal, but that line belongs to a separate matter from July 2026. Carrying it across as an answer to this investigation produces a false sentence.

The single-unit caveat deserves a moment of its own. Television firmware is not one artefact; it varies by model year, panel line, sales region and the consent state a set happens to carry, and any of those can change what a packet capture shows. A finding on one set becomes a finding about a product line only when someone reproduces it on more sets, and nothing published so far claims that step has been taken.

Four different things arrived bundled as one story

This investigation reads as a single indivisible lump because four claims of quite different character were published in one package. They rest on different evidence and call for different responses from anyone who owns one of these sets. Left bundled, the loudest word covers the other three, and the loudest word is almost always the least well supported one.

Quoting it beside a description of an investigation makes it read as a measurement, and it is not one.

Off does not point at a single state

A television with its plug in the wall never dies completely. Something has to stay awake to receive the infrared pulse from the remote, and something has to keep time and hold a network connection if a scheduled recording or an overnight update is expected. Product documentation therefore splits the single word into layers, and the layers do not behave alike.

That collapse is harmless in daily life and corrosive in a technical account: the moment a report says the screen was off without saying which row it means, the same sentence can describe an unremarkable standby or a set fully awake with the panel blanked. Any conclusion drawn before the row is identified is a conclusion about an unknown.

What the manufacturer documentation already says

What makes this distinction tractable is that most of it is already written down, in public, by the company that built the sets. The Korean-market support page for the quick start family describes them like this: rather than shutting down completely when the set is switched off, the television moves into a standby state so the picture comes up faster next time. The same page adds that because the feature has to hold a standby state, energy consumption in standby may be higher than usual.

That second sentence is more informative than it looks. Standby power is a measurable quantity, and a documented increase in it is a documented statement that something is running. The page is describing a set that has been asked to stay partly awake, in the plainest possible terms.

The same document records, in a table, that the name and menu path of this feature have moved with almost every firmware generation. The labels below are Korean-market menu names in translation; wording, depth and availability differ by region and firmware version.

The 2022 model generation carries a separate feature called standby mode, whose description says wallpaper and Bluetooth music playback continue after the set has been switched off. The sentence that matters more than the wallpaper is the next one: wake phrase recognition keeps working even when the wallpaper itself is dark. A blank screen is not evidence that this mode is inactive.

The decisive sentence sits on the voice recognition support page. It states that when the television recognises the phrase Hi LG while the set is off, a chime sounds. The same page then lists the conditions under which the feature does not work: voice recognition switched off, no internet connection, or, depending on model generation, standby mode or the quick start features switched off.

Read that list in reverse and the causal chain falls out without inference. Leaving a quick start feature enabled is the condition that keeps a powered-down set listening for its wake phrase. That is not a guess about hidden behaviour; it is what two pages of the manufacturer's own documentation say laid side by side, and it means the convenience feature and the always-listening behaviour share one switch.

ACR - working out what is on the screen

The technique by which a set works out for itself what it is currently displaying is called automatic content recognition, usually shortened to ACR. The commercial reason for it is openly stated: if a viewing history can be assembled regardless of whether the content arrived over the air, through HDMI or from an app, that history becomes raw material for advertising and recommendation. It is a business model with published documentation.

The first thing to separate is what the standards specify and what they leave alone. The relevant document in the next-generation broadcast standard describes a receiver extracting a fingerprint from video or audio frames and sending it to a remote server, which matches it against a database. Attached to the same section is a qualification: extraction and transmission may be performed by proprietary technology agreed among the fingerprint provider, the broadcaster and the receiver manufacturer, and it is not normatively specified by the standard. Writing that the standard defines the fingerprinting method is therefore wrong.

The second thing that drifts is which path the data takes. ACR in a television is not a technique for listening to a room through a microphone. The scope section of the same standard describes what it is written for: a set-top box over HDMI, delivering uncompressed video and audio and nothing else. That is extraction from the display and decode path. A patent from one operator is more concrete: a wake every 0.1 seconds, a read of the colour values of 13 pixels, and one-second batches sent upstream. A 1080p frame holds more than 2.07 million pixels, so 13 of them is not a screen capture but a minute sample, distinctive enough for a database lookup and no more.

The cases where content recognition through a microphone genuinely became a controversy involved smartphone applications, not televisions. Summaries blend the two, and the blend produces claims neither source supports. Keeping the display path and the microphone path apart is the most useful discipline available when reading about ACR.

What survives in a fingerprint and what is thrown away

Audio fingerprinting does not send the original sound either, and the clearest way to see why is to walk the processing chain of the algorithm most of this field cites, published in 2002. Each step throws something away permanently.

  • The incoming audio is downmixed to a single channel at 5 kHz. A large part of what makes a human voice recognisable is already gone at this point
  • Only the range between 300 and 2000 Hz is examined. Everything above that band effectively ceases to exist
  • That range is divided into 33 bands
  • Across time and across frequency, only the sign of the energy difference is retained. Magnitude is discarded in its entirety
  • The result is 32 bits every 11.6 milliseconds

Turning that into numbers gives the scale. CD-quality audio runs at roughly 1,411 kilobits per second; this fingerprint runs at roughly 2.8. That is on the order of five hundred times smaller, and what remains is a stream of sign bits describing a narrow band at the low end. There is no room in that stream for speech.

A second well-known algorithm from a different lineage points the same way. Its paper describes reducing the spectrogram to a sparse set of coordinates and states that the amplitude component has been eliminated at that step, adding that each hash fits into a single 32-bit integer. Different design, same outcome: the parts of the signal that would carry speech are removed early and are not recoverable afterwards.

The advertising arm's public documentation describes its own pipeline in matching terms. A sample of roughly 10 milliseconds — one hundredth of a second — is taken from the playing audio and hashed on the device to produce a signature, and that signature is associated with an IP address and device identifiers but not with a name, postal address or email address.

Cannot be reversed is not the same as contains nothing

Two sentences have to be held together here, because quoting either alone tips the account immediately.

The first is that reconstructing the original audio from a fingerprint does not work. At a compression ratio of several hundred to one, with magnitude discarded and the band limited to the low end, the information needed to rebuild speech is not in the stream. The operator's own documentation says its software neither transmits nor stores human speech or original audio, that a signature cannot be turned back into the original audio, and that it does not analyse speech, words or language.

The second is that this does not make a fingerprint empty. A paper published in 2024 addressed the assumption of privacy safety directly and reported that its authors were the first to identify speakers and words from fingerprints alone: 40 speakers at up to 90 percent accuracy, 10 spoken digits at up to 82 percent. The same paper states that its attack does not reverse the heavy compression: it reads the statistical structure that remains rather than rebuilding the signal.

The summary has two clauses. Reconstruction fails. Inference succeeds, at least partially, under laboratory conditions. Quote only the first and a fingerprint sounds inert; quote only the second and it sounds like a recording.

The moment the microphone opens - wake phrase and session

Fingerprints and transcripts travel different paths, and the third row of the table above is much closer to the real question here than the first two. A transcript is not a compressed statistical summary. It is words.

The manufacturer's privacy policy carries collection items named voice information and voice-converted text information, gathered when a user invokes voice recognition on a device that has it. Retention is up to one year from collection. One detail in the same entry is worth noting: the example devices listed are refrigerators, water purifiers, air conditioners and robot vacuums. Televisions are not inside that parenthesis. The absence is neither denial nor confirmation. It is a gap in the document.

The technical write-up says what was described earlier: the voice chain runs on the device, and what goes out is natural language processing and the search backend. Korean-market support material adds that the wake phrase can be chosen between two options. That is the documented design of a voice assistant, not hidden behaviour.

Among people who watched the video, interpretation split. One reading, argued with timestamps attached, holds that every recorded utterance was processed after voice input had been explicitly initiated — either by the wake phrase, which was enabled, or by a microphone button pressed on a search screen. That reading summarises the genuine anomaly in one sentence: when silence arrived the session did not close promptly, it went on transcribing what was said afterwards, and the input box stayed visible along the bottom of the screen.

Another reading reported a scene in which a near-silent stream was amplified and conversation became audible in it. The two accounts do not contradict each other: if a session stays open past the silence that should have closed it, whatever is said in the room goes into it by definition, and the anomaly and the audible conversation are one event described from two ends. Separately, the claim that voice commands are logged in plain text traces back to a single anonymous forum comment, rebutted in the same thread almost immediately.

Which is why the question as usually posed — recording or fingerprinting — omits an option. The most precise description the material supports is the third: the transcript of a voice assistant session that stayed open longer than it should have. That is narrower than the headline, more consistent with the documents, and checkable on another set.

Device discovery - what is visible on the same network

Network discovery belongs to a different layer than anything above it. A viewing record is information about what was watched. A list of the devices on the same network is information about what kind of household this is. Those two are not degrees of the same thing.

Underneath a domestic router, devices announce themselves so they can find one another, and those announcements routinely carry the device type, the manufacturer and whatever name the owner typed in. A single list of them is readable in ways that have nothing to do with networking.

  • Household composition — the number and type of personal devices narrows down how many people live there
  • Equipment owned — how deeply the home is committed to one manufacturer's ecosystem becomes visible
  • Daily rhythm — which devices appear and disappear at which hours indicates whether anyone is home
  • Age clues — the presence of school devices or toy-adjacent hardware is a signal of its own

This is not a defect in any particular device. It is the specified behaviour of the protocols. The service discovery specification tells implementers to give devices human-readable, friendly names, explains why in an appendix headed with the idea that what you see is what you get, and states that the name shown to the user is the primary identifier of the service. A companion specification on device descriptions makes manufacturer and model name required fields, serial number recommended, and requires a device's unique name to persist across reboots.

Privacy was not a design input when these documents were written. The security considerations section of the service discovery specification says only that nothing beyond the existing naming system's requirements is needed. A document from the same standards body seven years later states that the mechanism exposes device information, visible not only to devices taking part in discovery but to anything quietly listening on the network.

One asymmetry in the screen-sharing family of specifications is hard to unsee. To launch an application remotely, a user confirmation screen is required. To query which applications are installed and which is running, there is no corresponding requirement. Turning the set on asks permission; looking inside it does not. The same specification requires the response to carry the MAC address when remote wake is enabled.

What these have in common is that they accumulate without anyone doing anything on the television. A viewing record requires viewing; a device list requires only power and a network connection. What observation of that traffic can and cannot answer is covered in Seeing Network Traffic, and configuring devices in a home is the subject of the Smart Home & IoT Security Complete Guide — Threats, Settings & Management.

How a list of nearby access points becomes a location

There is one more layer. When a device also collects the list of wireless access points it can see around it, that list becomes material for narrowing down where the device is. Access points stay in one place for years, so the set of identifiers visible from a given spot behaves like a coordinate even though no coordinate was transmitted.

How easy that conversion is can be read off the public documentation of positioning services. One service's official reference instructs the caller to include at least two physically distinct access points in the request body, with the MAC address the required field of each entry. The response is a latitude, a longitude and an accuracy expressed as a radius in metres. Another service implementing the same interface calls the two-access-point minimum a mandatory privacy restriction. Stated plainly: with no satellite signal and no IP address, two nearby access point identifiers are enough to produce coordinates.

That this is not hypothetical is established by a complaint the United States competition authority filed against a different manufacturer in 2017. One paragraph enumerates what the content recognition software gathered alongside the viewing data itself.

Defendants' ACR software also periodically collects other
information about the television, including IP address,
wired and wireless MAC addresses, WiFi signal strength,
nearby WiFi access points, and other items.

  IP address · wired and wireless MAC addresses ·
  wireless signal strength · nearby access points

The same complaint states that the software gathered up to 100 billion data points a day from more than 10 million televisions and retained them indefinitely, and lists demographic attributes joined to the viewing data: sex, age, income, marital status, household size, education, home ownership and home value. The consent order that followed defined viewing data as its own term, required notice separately from the privacy policy and the terms of service, and required that on an interactive medium such as a software update it be unavoidable. Location signals in general are covered in the Complete Guide to Blocking Smartphone Location Tracking, and the family of signals that recognise a device without storing anything on it is the subject of Why You Are Still the Same Person After Clearing Cookies.

Academic measurement records the same behaviour on television-class devices. A 2019 study crawled 1,000 channels on each of two streaming platforms and found 69 percent and 89 percent respectively contacting known trackers. It also found device identifiers, serial numbers, wireless MAC addresses and the connected network name sometimes sent over unencrypted connections, and noted one platform's external control interface returning the network name with cross-origin restrictions left open, so any web page could read it. Matched against a public wireless network database, that value narrows the user's location. One conclusion carries forward on its own: the countermeasures offered, such as limiting ad tracking, had no practical effect.

What stands out is where the official guidance stops. The Korean instructions for the terms screen give the path and then finish by saying to review the service details and cautions, tick select all, and press agree. How many individual consent items sit inside that screen, and what each is called, is not written anywhere on the page. The English-language version has the identical shape: review the terms, then press select all.

The Korean-language privacy policy is similarly thin. The collection items associated with televisions are described at about the level of a recommended content list, and no wording corresponding to viewing information or content recognition appears. The advertising arm's public documentation says consent can be withdrawn from the settings menu of a television in which the service is integrated, without specifying a menu path.

So what remains after everything reachable has been switched off falls into two categories. The first is items whose existence is hard to learn about because the settings themselves are not documented. The second is behaviour that follows from the mere fact of being attached to a network, and that one does not close with an individual toggle.

Cutting the network, and the price of that choice

The reliable way to remove the outbound path is to take the set off the network, and the people involved in the investigation are reported to have recommended exactly that. It works. But it carries a bill, and describing the remedy without the bill is describing half of it.

  • Security updates stop — when a software flaw is fixed, the fix never reaches that set. In a situation where a vulnerability is under discussion, this measure points the opposite way from the problem
  • Apps and broadcast extras stop — a substantial share of what was paid for in buying a smart television is precisely these functions
  • The function may simply move — attach an external streaming device and that device maker's policy applies instead. The path changes rather than disappearing
  • Some features are gated on agreement — Korean-market guidance states that voice recognition becomes active after the terms of service have been accepted

The practical decision is therefore not a binary. It depends on what the set was bought to do, and the workable answer is usually a combination of network connection, individual settings and an external device. If standby power is one of the axes being weighed, How to Choose Energy-Efficient Appliances: Ratings, Running Cost, Savings covers that dimension.

The Korean rules - at the level of the statutes

What follows is only what the statutes say. It carries no judgement about whether any product's behaviour or any company's processing conflicts with these provisions; that requires established facts and a procedure before an authority with the power to make it. These are Korean statutes, and a set sold in another country is governed by that country's law.

A few notes belong with the table. The version of the data protection statute currently in force took effect on 2 October 2025, and an amended version is scheduled for 11 September 2026. Separately, the special provisions for information and communications service providers that used to sit in the old Chapter 6 were deleted by the 2023 amendment. The current Article 39-3 concerns litigation procedure, so citations resting on the old special provisions no longer hold — and they still circulate.

Standby power is worth a line as well. Televisions are not on the designated product list of the Korean standby power reduction programme. Standby consumption is handled instead under the energy efficiency management notices, as a measurement item on the test report, and it is not among the items printed on the label a shopper reads in a store. Measured, but not surfaced.

The settings paths and statutes above are Korean, and screen layouts differ by firmware version, model and sales region. In other countries the menu names will not match, some features are not offered at all, and the applicable law is that country's own. This article carries no judgement as to whether any product's behaviour or any company's processing conflicts with any law, and it is not a substitute for legal advice or a product diagnosis. For the actual settings on a given set, the manufacturer's guidance for that firmware version is the place to look.

What I could not confirm

The items below were kept out of the body as statements of fact because their wording could not be matched against a primary source. The reason is recorded alongside each one.

  • Whether raw audio was genuinely obtained from the wire or from storage — the primary output is a single 135-minute video, so a textual comparison is not possible, and no available material states the format of the payload. The body attributes this to the reporting rather than asserting it
  • Which state the screen-off condition actually was — the reporting itself hedges in parentheses, saying the set at least appeared to be off. Whether a quick start feature was enabled is not settled
  • How the consent items were configured during the test — the circumstantial evidence points the other way, in that the wake phrase appears to have been enabled. Whether the recognition daemon was inactive because consent had not been granted is not stated anywhere
  • The claim that data was buffered while the network was cut and uploaded on reconnection — this appears only in secondary reporting, with no corresponding statement in the technical write-up. Even that reporting says voice input rather than ambient sound
  • Plain-text logging of voice commands — the source is a single anonymous comment, rebutted in the same thread. It is not a primary source
  • Whether an item named viewing information exists on sets sold in Korea — the Korean manual's table of contents has no such entry. Whether the feature is absent there or carries a different name is unresolved, so no Korean menu path is asserted for it
  • The names of the individual consent items on the terms screen — no published manufacturer document enumerates them, and without a physical screen this cannot be settled
  • Whether recent models carry a built-in microphone — the published list of supported models runs only through the 2022 generation, and beyond that the guidance says to consult the specifications for the individual model
  • The details and identifier of the vulnerability — disclosure is in progress and no assigned identifier is retrievable. Previously published flaws of the same family are separate matters, already fixed in 2024
  • The manufacturer's position on this investigation — there is no related posting on the official channels, and the widely quoted line belongs to a different matter from July 2026
  • How thoroughly the claim that fingerprinting sends no original audio has been verified — it rests on algorithm design, standard text and the operator's public documentation. No study has confirmed it independently by decrypting actual traffic; a 2024 measurement study examined traffic patterns only and left content decoding as future work
  • Whether content recognition actually operates on models sold in Korea — Korea does not appear on the country list the advertising arm publishes for the service. Whether the feature is not deployed there or the list is out of date is unresolved, so Korean operation is nowhere assumed
  • What values a television sold in Korea actually emits during discovery — what the specifications permit is documented, but which fields a given product populates is outside the specifications and would require direct measurement
