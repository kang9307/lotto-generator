The Occasion — An Article Saying Barcodes Are Giving Way to QR Codes, and Its Distance from the Primary Sources

On October 1, 2026, the business newsletter The Hustle published an article saying that the product barcode is being replaced by two-dimensional codes such as the QR Code, and the article was summarized on GeekNews. The thread of the article runs like this. In 1992 an engineer at the Japanese company Denso extended the barcode into two dimensions and created the QR Code, the product barcode first scanned in 1974 (the UPC) became a system administered by GS1, and Sunrise 2027 is described as a shift that GS1 is introducing "next year", tied to large retailers changing over to new scanners and tracking databases.

Some sentences in the article cannot be confirmed from a primary source. These include the statement that 79% of consumers prefer QR Codes and the statement that GS1 announced the plan two years ago. The statement that GS1 requires both codes within 50 mm of each other has a basis in a GS1 guidance page, but the wording is of a different kind (section 7). This explainer re-checks them against documents GS1 itself has published as far as possible, and separates whatever cannot be matched, with its source named. The documents consulted are GS1 Korea's guide to the 2D barcode transition plan, GS1 global's 2D barcode standards page and retail 2D page, and GS1 US's Sunrise 2027 page and a 2022 press release, and GS1's retail 2D implementation guideline page (ref.gs1.org).

The questions a reader needs answered fall into three. What exactly changes by 2027, is it a goal or an obligation, and what changes for retailers, manufacturers and shoppers. Safety practice for consumers scanning a QR Code is covered separately in the QR code scam prevention guide, so this explainer stays with the change in the product barcode system.

What Sunrise 2027 Is — The Original Wording of the Target Year

The GS1 Korea page (the Distribution and Logistics Promotion Institute of the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry) titled the 2D barcode transition plan describes the plan this way. GS1 is preparing a next generation barcode to replace the 1D barcode used for nearly 50 years, and is running the move to a 2D barcode as a global project that goes beyond simply reading the product identification code (GTIN) to check product or price information, so that retailers, manufacturers and consumers can obtain and use the information they want. The wording of the item headed business goal is as follows, translated here from the Korean page.

  • It aims to make it possible, by the end of 2027, to use 2D barcodes as well as 1D barcodes in all retail stores in the world.
  • 1D barcodes will continue to be usable, but from 2027 onward, if a manufacturer wishes, it can freely use a 2D barcode that also works at the point-of-sale checkout of every retailer.
  • The 2D barcode that GS1 intends to move to for retail purposes is the GS1 QR Code.

What this wording conveys is a state in which the checkout can read a 2D barcode. The subject of the first sentence is the retail store and the verb is making it possible. It does not mean manufacturers must put a QR Code on every product by 2027. The second sentence in fact states that 1D barcodes continue in use and describes 2D as an option for manufacturers who want it. Here the year 2027 is the target point for readiness on the store side.

GS1 US presents the same plan as a transition for retail. It explains Sunrise 2027 as the retail transition from 1D UPC barcodes to 2D barcodes such as QR Codes, an initiative that supports retailers in making sure their point-of-sale systems can read and process these data-rich codes and lets brands share more information with consumers. A January 2022 GS1 US press release described the 2027 date as a timeline set by the retail industry in collaboration with GS1 US to equip consumers with more information about the products they buy. What matters is that the body that set the date is the industry and not a government.

The phrase by 2027 sounds like a deadline, but its nature differs from document to document. The table below sets the wording of the documents consulted side by side.

In summary, the GS1 documents consulted contain no legal obligation or penalty clause. On whether it is mandatory, the GS1 retail 2D guideline page states the document's purpose as helping a "smooth, voluntary transition" from linear to 2D barcodes, and The Hustle writes that it is not mandatory or government mandated. The former is a statement of the guideline's purpose and does not separately address legal force; the latter is the article's statement. The page calls the 2027 target "Ambition 2027" and describes it as the initial goal. Conversely, the possibility that trading partners may demand it is not something the GS1 documents prescribe; it is the article's interpretation about market power. The parts that are not rules can be learned only by checking each retailer's listing conditions.

The GS1 US wording in the third row of the table also has a way of being read. The statement that it is a milestone but not all capabilities are needed means 2027 is not a day on which everything is complete. The first step is being able to read 2D, and using additional data such as product variants or production and expiration dates is placed in a later step. This division into steps is taken up again in section 6.

1D and 2D — What Differs and Which 2D Codes Are in Scope

GS1 Korea describes the limit of the existing barcode this way: it stays at reading the product identification code (GTIN) to check product or price information. A 2D barcode can carry only the number, can add supplementary information using GS1 Application Identifiers (AI), and can instead carry a GS1 Digital Link to widen how it can be used. The GS1 global page states that GS1 has standards for five 2D barcodes currently in use, and that the two most widely recognised are the two in the table below.

The 2D code GS1 intends to move to for retail is the QR Code (GS1 Digital Link URI). DataMatrix is introduced as a code used mainly in healthcare, and in GS1 Korea's retail transition plan the target is the GS1 QR Code. The capacity figures are the values written on the GS1 global page, and a figure comparing them with the capacity of a 1D barcode is not in the documents consulted.

The GS1 global retail 2D page explains two kinds of codes separately. Traditional barcodes are typically scanned by companies and organisations to get supply chain information, and traditional QR Codes are typically scanned by consumers to reach a web page with product information. The slogan on the GS1 retail page is One scan. Infinite possibilities., and The Hustle, in the passage presenting Hardcastle's remarks, writes that the same code works at checkout, in the stock room and for the customer. The remark of a chief executive of a global consumer goods company, quoted on the GS1 US page, points the same way: the information consumers want, traceability and scanning at checkout can be handled by a single barcode. From this distinction one can see that a QR Code with GS1 standards inside is a different thing from a QR Code that merely holds a web address.

GS1 Digital Link — How a Product Number Becomes a Web Address

The GS1 global page labels the QR Code for retail as QR Code [GS1 Digital Link URI] and says it supports GS1 Digital Link URI syntax. The GS1 Korea page explains that encoding GS1 Digital Link instead of GS1 Application Identifiers widens how the barcode can be used, and shows an example shaped like a web address (URL) rather than a string of numbers. Below is the example string for a 2D barcode that appears on that page.

https://GS1_company.com/01/08801062866076/10/A001?17=220531

The page explains this example as a GTIN, lot number A001 and an expiry date of May 31, 2022. The structure can be read this way. After the domain, what follows /01/ is the GTIN, what follows /10/ is the lot number, and what follows ?17= is the expiry date (six digits in YYMMDD order). These numbers are GS1 Application Identifiers, and their definitions can be looked up on GS1's Application Identifier browser page. The same page also shows a form like https://GS1_company.com/01/08801234560016 as an example that encodes only the number.

In the original example the GTIN in the URL and the GTIN in the explanatory text are written with different values, so only the structure is quoted here and not the meaning of the digits. The practical facts that can be learned from this structure are as follows.

  • Product-level information is extended by appending a lot number or expiry date on top of the same GTIN of the same product.
  • The front part of the address (the domain) is an area the brand decides, and the rear part follows GS1 syntax.
  • What the scanner needs is not to open the whole address but to extract the GTIN. GS1 US describes the retailer's first step as installing scanners that can read 2D barcodes and extract the GTIN at the point of sale.
  • When a consumer scans with a smartphone, they can be taken to the web page the brand has linked. The GS1 global page gives ingredients, allergens, nutrition, sustainability, recipes and usage instructions as examples.

In the end the number rules inside the address are the standard, while the domain and the linked page are run by the brand. Keeping that page accurate and current becomes the manufacturer's job. The Hustle points out the same thing. Printing a new square on a box is the easy part, and because the code is a link, the real task is making sure the information behind the link is accurate and contemporary.

GS1 US calls these three steps Crawl-Walk-Run and opens with the following. Sunrise 2027 is a milestone, but it is not necessary to implement all the capabilities of 2D by that date, and starting the transition now is a chance to get ahead of the curve. It stands out that the first step is limited to retailers reading and manufacturers adding the code.

The consumer figures are easy to misread. The Hustle writes, in the words of someone who helps GS1 conduct research, that 79% of consumers preferred QR Codes. The GS1 US page has three figures from a 2025 GS1 US consumer survey, worded as follows.

  • 79%: said they are more likely to purchase products with a scannable QR Code (via smartphone) that provides additional product information.
  • 77%: said product information is important when making a purchase.
  • 62%: said they are willing to spend more money on a product that offers detailed product information.

The 79% is a response about being more likely to purchase, and it is not the same sentence as preferring QR Codes. The survey's respondents and sample are not on the page consulted. The Hustle also adds that this does not mean consumers will scan every item. The explanation is that they want information ready when they actually need it.

There is also evidence that retailer readiness is not simple. According to a 2022 GS1 US press release, in a test with three national retailers and their solution providers, the retailers had image scanners installed and yet several readiness challenges appeared in scanning, processing and storing data, and the result was that hardware and software infrastructure across all POS formats needs to evolve. A retail representative quoted in the same press release said the test taught that scanning a 2D barcode and ingesting its data are two very different capabilities.

The Transition Period — The Problem of Printing Two Codes Together

Until there is confidence that the 2D code reads at every checkout, the question remains whether packaging must carry both codes. The 2022 GS1 US press release says GS1 US will continue to work with industry to create requirements, conduct testing and provide recommendations to optimize 2D barcode placement, read priority and scanning performance in both the dual (U.P.C. plus 2D) and single (2D) marking scenarios. In other words both scenarios are under consideration.

The Hustle writes this part more assertively. Its statement is that until retail catches up, GS1 requires products to carry both codes within 50 mm of each other. This statement was not found in the GS1 Korea, global or US documents, but it has a basis in GS1's retail 2D implementation guideline page (ref.gs1.org/guidelines/2d-in-retail). That page says that until 90% of POS scanning solutions can use GS1-compliant 2D barcodes and at minimum capture the GTIN, products carrying a retail 2D barcode on-pack need to be accompanied by a POS linear barcode. So dual marking is a transition-period statement tied to that condition. The 50 mm figure appears on the same page in the sentence that the 2D barcode needs to be within 50 mm (1.97 inches) of the linear barcode's centre; this is reported as a result of extensive independent testing to achieve the target retail IPM, and the page refers readers to section 6 of the GS1 General Specifications for barcode placement rules. The sentence carries no SHOULD or MUST marking. So 50 mm is a figure from testing, the normative wording that requires points to must be checked directly in section 6, and it should be checked with GS1 Korea or the GS1 General Specifications before being applied to package design.

Other statements in The Hustle are separated by the same standard.

  • The statement that nearly half of the retailers polled together with GS1 have already started to introduce QR capabilities is a researcher's remark, and the survey method could not be confirmed.
  • The statement that last year a large brand unveiled a packaging upgrade of 45 thousand SKUs is a case the article cites and is not a GS1 document.
  • The statement that a QR Code holds 200 times more data than the barcodes of today is the article's comparison, while the GS1 global page instead states capacity figures directly (QR 7,089 numeric and 4,296 alphanumeric).

The Situation in Korea — The Plan GS1 Korea Describes

The body in charge of this plan in Korea is the Distribution and Logistics Promotion Institute of the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry (GS1 Korea). The transition plan page says the Institute plans to communicate with relevant technology and industry experts and discuss several agenda items to bring about the 2D barcode transition of the domestic distribution industry by 2027. There is also a notice recruiting experts from retailer IT and system departments, POS device and solution companies, retail IT companies, and marketing and consulting.

The same page has link titles pointing to a case of using GS1 2D barcodes on Jeju Samdasoo and a Chinese case of GS1 2D food traceability. The bodies of those cases could not be confirmed and only the titles are relied upon. The footer of the page carries a 2022 copyright notice, so whether the time the page was written matches its present state cannot be known.

Within the range confirmed, this is as far as can be said about Korea. The share of domestic POS terminals ready to scan 2D, whether any obligation applies, schedules by sector, and the relation to government policy are not in the material consulted. Continuing with a domestic forecast has no basis, so it is not done. Requirements from domestic trading partners are best checked directly with the retailer concerned and with GS1 Korea's guidance. For a manufacturer or supplier, the items to check can be listed as follows. They turn the step guidance of the documents above into practical questions, and the answer to each item is not in the documents.

  • Whether the retailer you supply can read 2D barcodes at checkout, and if so which code (GS1 QR Code and so on) it targets.
  • The retailer's requirement on whether to keep the existing 1D barcode on the package and add 2D, or to use 2D alone.
  • Whether a QR Code already in use carries GS1 Digital Link syntax and the GTIN.
  • Who will update the information on the web page the code links to, and how often.

What Could Not Be Confirmed

  • The normative wording that both codes must be shown within 50 mm of each other. The 50 mm figure was confirmed as a test result on GS1's retail 2D guideline page, but the wording of GS1 General Specifications section 6, the normative placement rule (including whether it uses SHOULD), was not opened. Whether The Hustle's requires points to that rule could not be confirmed.
  • GS1 global's official Sunrise 2027 declaration and the industry endorsement statement. Those documents are PDFs and were not opened. A statement to the effect that all retailers can scan 2D by the end of 2027 as a minimum standard appears in secondary material, but the original wording was not read.
  • The readiness of Korean POS, its schedule, and whether any obligation applies. The GS1 Korea page goes only as far as the plan level. The 2025 GS1 standard QR guide and the smart food QR notice, which are announced separately, were also not opened.
  • The respondents and sample size of the 2025 GS1 US consumer survey. The page has only the figures.
  • The relation between The Hustle's statement that GS1 announced it two years ago and the actual start of the plan. GS1 US already mentions Sunrise 2027 in its January 2022 press release.
  • Why, in the GS1 Korea guide example, the GTIN in the URL and the GTIN in the explanatory text differ. Only the structure was quoted.
  • No official statement was found on the possibility that some retailers will stop accepting 1D barcodes after 2027. The GS1 Korea wording in fact states that 1D barcodes remain in use.
