Korea Industrial Accident Insurance Guide - Coverage, Benefits and How to Apply

Everything workers in Korea need to know about 산재보험: who is covered, what injuries qualify, and how to claim benefits from KCOMWEL

Introduction: What to Do When You Are Injured at Work in Korea

A machine catches your hand on the factory floor. You slip on a wet corridor in the office and fracture your wrist. After years of working with hazardous chemicals, you are diagnosed with an occupational disease. If you work in Korea and experience any of these situations, Korea's Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance — known as 산재보험 (san-jae boh-heom) — is the primary social safety net designed to protect you.

Unlike private insurance, this system is funded entirely by employer contributions. As a worker, you pay nothing into it. Coverage is automatic: any business that employs at least one worker is enrolled by law, and the worker does not need to apply for enrollment. This guide explains the system's coverage, the criteria for recognizing a work injury, the types of benefits available, and how to file a claim — all in the context of Korean law and the Korean government's official procedures. Foreign workers and expatriates employed in Korea are also covered by the same system.

1. Overview of Korea's Industrial Accident Compensation System

Korea's industrial accident insurance system is governed by the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act (산업재해보상보험법). The supervising ministry is the Ministry of Employment and Labor (고용노동부), while KCOMWEL (근로복지공단, Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service) handles day-to-day operations including benefit decisions and payments.

Three principles define the system:

  • Employer-funded: Employers pay all premiums. Premium rates vary by industry and injury history.
  • No-fault coverage: Workers receive benefits regardless of who was at fault, as long as the injury is work-related. The only major exceptions are intentional self-harm or injuries caused purely by the worker's intoxication against explicit rules.
  • Fast compensation: The administrative process is designed to be faster and simpler than civil litigation against an employer.

2. Who Is Covered

2.1 Automatically Covered

All workers employed by businesses subject to the Act are covered automatically. This includes:

  • Full-time, part-time, fixed-term, and daily-wage workers
  • Workers in manufacturing, construction, services, IT, and most other industries
  • Foreign workers legally employed in Korea
  • Undocumented foreign workers, if an actual employment relationship can be established (prevailing interpretation)

2.2 Special-Category Workers (Platform and Independent Contractors)

Korea has progressively extended coverage to workers in non-traditional employment relationships. Delivery riders, insurance agents, tutors, golf caddies, chauffeurs, and certain others classified as nomu-jegongja (노무제공자) may be covered under special provisions. The list of covered occupations has expanded in recent years. Check with KCOMWEL to confirm your specific occupation's status.

2.3 Who Is Not Covered

  • Civil servants (covered under the Public Officials Accident Compensation Act)
  • Military personnel (covered under the Military Personnel Accident Compensation Act)
  • Seafarers (covered under the Seafarers Act)
  • Some categories of household employment and small-scale agricultural and fishing businesses
Key point: Even if your employer failed to register with KCOMWEL, you may still be entitled to benefits if your workplace is legally required to be enrolled. Do not assume you are unprotected simply because your employer did not complete registration.

3. What Counts as a Work-Related Injury in Korea

To receive benefits, the injury, illness, disability, or death must be recognized as a work-related (업무상) event. Korean law classifies qualifying events into three categories.

3.1 Work Accidents (업무상 사고)

Injuries or deaths that occur while performing duties required by the employment contract, or while following an employer's instructions. Key examples:

  • Injuries sustained while directly performing assigned work tasks
  • Accidents during company-organized events, training, or business travel
  • Injuries caused by defects in employer-provided facilities or equipment
  • Accidents while using employer-provided transportation

3.2 Occupational Diseases (업무상 질병)

Diseases that develop because of exposure to hazardous working conditions over time. A significant causal relationship between the work and the disease must be established. Common examples include:

  • Pneumoconiosis (lung disease from dust exposure)
  • Occupational hearing loss from prolonged noise exposure
  • Occupation-related cancers (e.g., from chemical exposure)
  • Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases linked to overwork or severe stress (karoshi-type cases)
  • Musculoskeletal disorders from repetitive motion

3.3 Commuting Accidents (출퇴근 재해)

Since January 2018, injuries sustained while commuting to or from work via the ordinary route and method are recognized as work injuries. This covers both employer-provided transportation and ordinary private commutes. However, accidents that occur when a worker has substantially deviated from the normal commuting route for personal reasons may not qualify.

Documentation matters: Evidence such as medical records, incident reports, CCTV footage, witness statements, and work logs all help establish the causal link between your work and the injury. Collect and preserve this evidence as soon as possible.

4. Types of Benefits

Once a work-related injury or disease is recognized, the following benefits may be available. Amounts are calculated primarily based on the worker's average daily wage at the time of injury, subject to minimum and maximum compensation floors announced annually by the government.

4.1 Medical Benefit (요양급여 - Yoyanggeupyeo)

Covers the cost of treatment at KCOMWEL-approved medical institutions. Covered services include consultations, surgery, hospitalization, rehabilitation, medications, and assistive devices. There is no fixed time limit on treatment, but benefits end when the condition is deemed medically stable (healed or at maximum medical improvement).

4.2 Wage Replacement Benefit (휴업급여 - Hyueobgeupyeo)

Paid for each day the worker is unable to work due to the injury. The rate is 70% of average daily wage. The first three days of inability to work are not compensated; payment begins from the fourth day. A statutory minimum floor applies, protecting low-wage workers from receiving an extremely small amount.

4.3 Disability Benefit (장해급여 - Janghaegeubyeo)

Paid when permanent impairment remains after the condition has stabilized. Disability is graded from Grade 1 (most severe) to Grade 14. Grades 1 through 3 are paid as an annuity only; Grades 4 through 14 give the worker the choice of annuity or lump sum. The benefit amount is based on the disability grade and average daily wage.

4.4 Nursing Care Benefit (간병급여 - Ganbyeonggeupyeo)

Provided to severely disabled workers who require ongoing nursing care after their condition has stabilized. Eligibility is determined by KCOMWEL assessment and must be applied for separately.

4.5 Survivor Benefits (유족급여 - Yujokgeupyeo)

Paid to surviving family members when a worker dies from a work-related cause. Survivors can choose between an annuity and a lump sum, subject to eligibility criteria. The annuity amount depends on the deceased worker's average wage and the number of dependent survivors.

4.6 Funeral Expenses (장례비 - Jangryebi)

A fixed payment to cover funeral costs when a worker dies from a work-related cause. The amount is determined by applicable compensation standards.

4.7 Pneumoconiosis Compensation Annuity (상병보상연금)

When a worker's condition has not stabilized after two years of treatment and the severity meets a defined threshold, the wage replacement benefit is converted to a higher-rate compensation annuity.

4.8 Vocational Rehabilitation Benefit (직업재활급여)

Supports disabled workers in returning to employment through vocational training, workplace return subsidies, and rehabilitation exercise programs.

Summary of benefit types: Medical treatment costs (요양급여) → Daily wage replacement at 70% (휴업급여) → Permanent disability grades 1-14 (장해급여) → Nursing care (간병급여) → Survivor benefits and funeral expenses (유족급여·장례비) → Compensation annuity after 2 years (상병보상연금) → Vocational rehabilitation (직업재활급여)

5. How to File a Claim

Benefits are not paid automatically. The injured worker or surviving family must proactively file a claim with KCOMWEL.

5.1 Step 1 - Seek Medical Treatment and Apply for Medical Benefit

  1. Get emergency treatment first if needed. Where possible, seek treatment at a KCOMWEL-designated medical institution, which simplifies billing.
  2. Complete the Medical Benefit Application (요양급여신청서). Download it from the KCOMWEL website (comwel.or.kr) or pick it up at a KCOMWEL branch. Fill in your personal details, employer information, and a description of how the accident occurred.
  3. Attach the doctor's opinion (소견서). Ask the attending physician to complete the required medical opinion form.
  4. Obtain employer confirmation if possible. The form has a section for the employer to confirm the work injury. If the employer refuses to sign, note this on the form and submit without the signature.
  5. Submit to KCOMWEL. File in person or by mail at your nearest KCOMWEL branch, or submit online through the Total Service portal at total.comwel.or.kr.

5.2 Step 2 - KCOMWEL Review and Decision

KCOMWEL investigates whether the injury is work-related. For straightforward cases, a decision is typically issued within seven days. Complex cases involving disputed facts or medical questions may take longer due to field investigations or specialist evaluations.

5.3 Step 3 - Claiming Additional Benefits

After the medical benefit is approved, file separate claim forms as your situation develops:

  • Wage Replacement Benefit Claim (휴업급여청구서) for each period you are unable to work
  • Disability Benefit Claim (장해급여청구서) once your condition is medically stable and permanent impairment is assessed
  • Survivor Benefit Claim (유족급여청구서) in the event of a work-related death

5.4 If Your Claim Is Denied

If KCOMWEL denies your claim, you may file a Review Request (심사청구) within 90 days of being notified of the denial. If the review result is also unfavorable, you may escalate to a Re-review Request (재심사청구) before the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Review Committee, or pursue an administrative lawsuit in court.

Your employer cannot legally prevent you from filing a workers compensation claim. Even if your employer refuses to cooperate or pressures you not to file, you retain the full right to submit a claim directly to KCOMWEL. Retaliation against a worker for filing a claim is prohibited by law.

6. Coverage for Foreign Workers in Korea

Korea's industrial accident insurance system applies equally to foreign workers. This is an important protection for the large number of migrant workers employed in Korea's manufacturing, construction, and service sectors.

  • Legally resident foreign workers have exactly the same rights as Korean nationals under the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act.
  • Undocumented foreign workers are generally also protected if a real employment relationship can be established, based on prevailing court interpretations and KCOMWEL practice.
  • Language assistance is available through KCOMWEL (1588-0075) and migrant worker support organizations.

7. Relationship Between Workers Compensation and Civil Liability

Receiving workers compensation benefits does not automatically waive your right to sue the employer for civil damages. However, amounts already compensated through the insurance system are deducted from any civil damages awarded. If your employer's serious negligence or safety law violations caused the accident, consulting a legal professional about additional civil or criminal remedies may be worthwhile — but the legal analysis is complex and fact-dependent.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Does filing a claim hurt my employer?

Filing a claim does not directly result in criminal penalties for the employer. However, a high injury rate at a workplace can affect the employer's insurance premium rate. Regardless, employers are legally prohibited from discouraging, retaliating against, or penalizing workers who file claims.

Q. Can I still claim benefits after treatment is finished?

It is strongly recommended to file as soon as possible. Post-treatment claims are possible, but certain benefits such as the wage replacement benefit are subject to a three-year statute of limitations. Do not delay filing.

Q. Can the self-employed or freelancers get coverage?

Some categories of self-employed workers can voluntarily enroll in industrial accident insurance. Platform workers and others in special occupational categories may be compulsorily covered. Contact KCOMWEL to check your eligibility.

Conclusion - Know Your Rights Before You Need Them

Korea's industrial accident compensation system provides meaningful protection for workers injured on the job, covering medical costs, lost wages, permanent disability, and survivor support. Crucially, the system is funded by employers, not workers — and employer non-cooperation does not block your claim. The process involves multiple steps and specific deadlines, but support is available from KCOMWEL, labor unions, and legal professionals.
