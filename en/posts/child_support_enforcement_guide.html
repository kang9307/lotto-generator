Child Support Enforcement Guide: Performance Orders, Detention, Direct Payment

A court judgment alone doesn't collect unpaid child support - here is the step-by-step enforcement path in Korea

Jul 3, 2026 Law & Legal

Introduction: A Judgment Is Not the Same as Getting Paid

Agreeing on child support during a divorce does not mean the money automatically shows up in your account. Even with a mediation record, a judgment, or a child support obligation record from an uncontested divorce, it is common for the paying parent to delay or simply stop payments. According to Korea's Child Support Agency, operating under the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, the number of sanction cases against nonpaying parents has risen every year since the system began in July 2021, reaching 947 in 2024 and 1,389 in 2025. That growth reflects how often enforcement actually has to be pursued.

This guide walks through the enforcement tools available to a custodial parent who is not receiving child support: preparatory steps like asset disclosure and asset inquiry, court-level measures like performance orders and detention orders, the direct payment order that garnishes wages, and administrative sanctions such as license suspension, travel bans, and public disclosure. This is general procedural information, not legal advice for any specific case, and the relevant rules can change, so always confirm current requirements with the Child Support Agency or a licensed attorney before filing.

1. The Starting Point: An Enforceable Title and the Child Support Agency

1.1 You need an enforceable title first

Enforcement cannot begin without an enforceable title - a legally binding document that establishes the debt. This typically means a final divorce judgment, a court mediation or settlement record, or a child support obligation record drawn up at the family court during an uncontested divorce. If none of these exist yet, the first step is a child support claim lawsuit or a family court adjudication to establish one.

1.2 What the Child Support Agency does

The Child Support Agency (childsupport.or.kr) was established under Korea's Act on the Securing and Support of Child Support Payment. It provides free or low-cost consultation, litigation representation, and support for filing asset disclosure, asset inquiry, performance orders, direct payment orders, and detention order applications. For most custodial parents, starting with an Agency consultation is far more practical than navigating the court system alone.

2. The First Step: Asset Disclosure and Asset Inquiry

2.1 Asset disclosure

Asset disclosure is a court order requiring the debtor to submit a list of their own assets. It relies on the debtor's cooperation. If the debtor fails to appear, refuses to submit the list or take the oath, or files a false list, the creditor can ask the court to enter the debtor into the defaulter registry. Once listed, financial institutions and the general public can view the record, which effectively restricts the debtor's banking activity.

2.2 Asset inquiry

Asset inquiry lets the court search a debtor's assets directly through financial institution and public agency databases, without needing the debtor's cooperation. It is especially useful when a debtor is hiding assets. Either route serves the same purpose: locating property to seize before performance orders, direct payment orders, or seizure can actually be effective.

3. Performance Orders and Detention Orders

3.1 Performance order

A performance order is issued by the family court, directing a debtor who is withholding payment without good cause to pay within a set period. The creditor files the request, the court examines the debtor, and a reasonable deadline is set. The order itself carries no criminal penalty, but ignoring it becomes the basis for the next, harsher steps - detention or administrative sanctions.

3.2 Detention order

If the debtor still fails to pay for three or more installments without good cause after a performance order, the creditor can request a detention order. The family court can then detain the debtor for up to 30 days. Because this directly restricts personal liberty, it follows the performance order as a second, escalated step. In practice, simply being notified that a detention request has been filed is often enough to prompt payment.

4. Direct Payment Orders: Garnishing Wages Directly

4.1 Requirements

A direct payment order instructs an employer to withhold child support from wages and pay it directly to the creditor. Three conditions must be met: (1) the debtor has a regular wage or salary claim; (2) there is a periodic child support claim not yet due; and (3) the debtor has failed to pay, without good cause, two or more times. Meeting all three lets the creditor file with the family court.

4.2 Procedure and documents

Jurisdiction generally lies with the family court where the minor child's domicile is located. Required documents typically include the application, an executable copy of the title, corporate registration if the withholding employer is a company, a family relationship certificate, the debtor's resident registration, and a power of attorney if filed through a representative. Once ordered, the employer deducts the child support amount and pays it directly to the creditor's account. If the debtor changes or leaves jobs, the order loses effect, so income changes need to be tracked and re-filed if necessary.

5. Administrative Sanctions: Disclosure, License Suspension, Travel Ban

5.1 Who qualifies

For debtors who continue withholding payment without good cause for a substantial period or amount even after a court payment order, the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family can request that relevant agencies impose driver's license suspension, a travel (exit) ban, and public disclosure of the debtor's identity. Reported thresholds have referenced amounts in the tens of millions of won or three or more missed payments after a court order, but exact monetary and frequency criteria can shift with legal amendments - current figures should be confirmed with the Child Support Agency.

5.2 The process was streamlined

Previously, creditors had to go through performance order → detention order → sanctions, in that order. A legal amendment now allows sanctions to be requested directly after a performance order, without first pursuing detention. This has meaningfully increased pressure on nonpaying parents. Public disclosure typically includes the debtor's name, age, occupation, and the duration of nonpayment, creating substantial social pressure.

6. Other Support: Emergency Assistance and Advance Payment

While enforcement is underway, day-to-day survival can still be a problem. Two support programs exist for that gap. Temporary emergency child support provides KRW 200,000 per child per month, for up to nine months (with one possible three-month extension), to single-parent families who have filed for enforcement support through the Child Support Agency but are not yet receiving payment. The child support advance payment program, launched July 1, 2025, goes further: if the debtor has failed to pay entirely for three or more consecutive months (or three or more installments) and the creditor's household income is at or below 150% of the median income standard, the state pays KRW 200,000 per minor child per month until the child reaches adulthood, then seeks reimbursement from the debtor.

7. The Process at a Glance

Here is the typical sequence of steps.

Not every case follows this exact order. Depending on whether the debtor is a salaried employee or self-employed, seizure and collection procedures may be pursued before a direct payment order. The right path for your case is best confirmed through a Child Support Agency consultation.

Conclusion: You Don't Have to Fight This Alone

Unpaid child support is treated in Korean law as more than a private debt dispute - it is tied directly to a child's welfare. That is why the system layers judicial pressure (performance and detention orders), a direct recovery mechanism (direct payment orders), and administrative sanctions (license suspension, travel bans, public disclosure), alongside livelihood support like emergency assistance and the advance payment program. Together, these tools form a considerably more complete framework than what existed a decade ago.

What matters most is that none of this has to be navigated alone. The Child Support Agency supports everything from initial consultation to document preparation and litigation representation. If you are holding a judgment but still not getting paid, the next step may not be the courthouse - it may be a call to the Child Support Agency.
