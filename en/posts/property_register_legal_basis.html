What the Korean Property Register Actually Proves - Sections, Priority and the Absence of Public Credibility

The Korean register has no public credibility - how that differs from presumptive effect, and what the register never shows

Aug 18, 2026 Law & Legal
This article explains a legal framework in general terms and is not legal advice. For a specific matter, consult a qualified professional such as an attorney or a judicial scrivener. It describes the property registration system of the Republic of Korea, which differs from land registration in other jurisdictions. Provisions were checked at the Korean National Law Information Center on 18 August 2026 and statutes can be amended.

Introduction: Where a checked register still comes apart

The first document anyone pulls before buying or renting property in Korea is the certified register extract. It names the owner and shows how much debt is charged against the property, so it looks like the whole answer. Yet transactions come apart even where the register was read properly.

The reason is not that the register is unreliable. Korean real estate registration is built so that having trusted the register does not, on its own, create a right. Lawyers call this the absence of public credibility. This article sets out what is recorded where, and how far those entries reach in law. Correcting a wrong entry is covered in correcting a wrong entry in the Korean property register, and deposit protection in a lease is covered in jeonse right registration compared with a fixed date stamp, so those are linked rather than repeated.

1. The register and the certified extract - why the name changed

The Korean property register is not a paper volume. What is called the real estate register today is registration information entered and processed through an electronic data processing system and compiled as the Supreme Court Regulations prescribe. That made the word copy, which implies duplicating an original, inaccurate. The full amendment of the Real Estate Registration Act promulgated on 12 April 2011 and in force from 13 October 2011 (Act No. 10580) was the occasion on which the older wording for register copies and abstracts was reorganised into certified register extract.

Everyday speech and contracts still use the older term. The document issued at the counter, though, is the certified register extract, and the Internet Registry Office offers several forms depending on what you need.

  • Full certified extract - shows the registered matters that are in effect at the time of inspection, together with related matters
  • Full certified extract (currently effective matters) - a condensed form centred on what is live now
  • Partial certified extract (share of a specified person) - only the share held by one named party
  • Partial certified extract (current ownership status) - only the present state of ownership
  • Partial certified extract (history of share acquisition) - how shares moved over time

One fork matters here. The condensed forms are easier to read but may not show entries that once existed and were later cancelled. Whether owners changed often, or whether a provisional attachment was placed and released, is itself evidence. To see that history you have to ask for a form that carries it.

2. Three sections - description, gapgu, eulgu

Article 14 paragraph 1 of the Real Estate Registration Act divides the register into a land register and a building register, and paragraph 2 requires it to be preserved permanently. The compilation rule sits in Article 15. Paragraph 1 provides that one register record is kept for one parcel of land or one building, except that for a building divided into units one register record is used for the whole of the building. That is why pulling a single apartment unit produces the description section for the whole building alongside the one for that unit.

Article 15 paragraph 2 is the backbone of this article. A register record contains a description section recording matters concerning the description of the property, a gapgu recording matters concerning ownership, and an eulgu recording matters concerning rights other than ownership. This is a statutory structure rather than an editorial convenience, and the priority rules below run on top of it.

SectionWhat is recordedWhat the reader learns
Description section For land, the display number, receipt, location and lot number, land category, area, cause of registration and other matters. For buildings, the display number, receipt, location with building name and number, building details, cause of registration and other matters Whether the record refers to the same property named in the contract
Gapgu Matters concerning ownership. Changes of ownership together with provisional registrations, seizure, provisional attachment and decisions commencing auction Who owns it now, and whether contested entries sit on that ownership
Eulgu Matters concerning rights other than ownership. Creation, alteration, transfer and cancellation of mortgages, jeonse rights and the like How much security is charged, and whether another party holds a right of use

Why each line in gapgu and eulgu looks the way it does is explained by Article 48. Paragraph 1 requires the priority number, the purpose of registration, the date and number of receipt, the cause of registration and its date, and the right holder. Paragraph 2 requires the right holder to be recorded with a resident registration number or real estate registration number and an address alongside the name, and paragraph 4 requires the share of each holder where there are two or more, and a note where the holding is joint.

3. Gapgu - ownership and what sits on top of it

Most readers check the owner name at the bottom of gapgu and move on. The value of gapgu lies less in ownership itself than in what has been layered on top of that ownership.

  • Preservation registration of ownership - the first entry placing ownership of the property on the register. This is where a newly built building starts
  • Transfer registration of ownership - a change of owner by sale, gift, inheritance and so on, with the cause and date in the cause column
  • Provisional registration - a placeholder securing the priority of a future principal registration. Under Article 91 the principal registration takes the priority of the provisional one, so entries made in between can be pushed back
  • Provisional attachment - a preservative measure locking down disposal in anticipation of enforcing a monetary claim
  • Provisional disposition - a preservative measure holding the status quo over a disputed subject. It can signal live litigation over ownership
  • Decision commencing auction - notice that an auction has begun. Rights analysis at that stage is covered in Korean real estate court auction and the basics of rights analysis

What these entries share is that they warn the present owner may not be free to dispose of that ownership. Matching the owner name against your counterparty is only the beginning of reading gapgu.

4. Eulgu - maximum secured amount, jeonse right, easement

The entry most often met in eulgu is the revolving mortgage, and the maximum secured amount beside it is commonly misread as the actual debt.

  • Revolving mortgage - Article 357 paragraph 1 of the Civil Act allows a mortgage to be created by fixing only the maximum amount of the debt to be secured while reserving determination for the future, and provides that until determination the extinction or transfer of the debt does not affect the mortgage
  • Nature of the ceiling - paragraph 2 deems interest on the debt to be included within the maximum amount. The figure is a ceiling for the security, not today's balance
  • Jeonse right - Article 303 paragraph 1 gives the holder, on paying the jeonse deposit, possession and use of another's property according to its purpose, and a right to be paid the deposit in preference to junior right holders and other creditors out of the whole property. Paragraph 2 excludes farmland
  • Easement - Article 291 gives the holder a right to use another's land for the benefit of his own land for a defined purpose. Relationships between parcels, such as passage, surface here
  • Alteration, transfer, cancellation - eulgu records more than creation. Rights that changed, moved or were erased leave a trace

Lenders set the ceiling above the loan so that interest and delay damages stay covered. The multiple varies by institution and product, so no single figure applies, and the outstanding balance has to be confirmed with the creditor rather than read off the register. Neither the inference that a large ceiling means a large debt nor the inference that a small one means safety is guaranteed by the register. An empty eulgu does not mean an unencumbered property either, because some rights and claims never reach the register at all.

5. No public credibility - trusting the register does not create a right

Public credibility is the power of a public record to create for a person who trusted it the right the record states, even where the record diverges from the true position. Korean real estate registration does not carry that power.

In its decision of 26 February 2009 in case 2006Da72802, the Supreme Court held to the effect that real estate registration is not accorded public credibility, so where a transfer registration is a false registration, a person who trusted it, bought the property and completed a transfer registration cannot be treated as having acquired ownership. Much earlier, the decision of 10 June 1969 in case 68Da199 stated to the effect that under the current registration system, in which public credibility is not recognised, the validity of a registration is acknowledged on the premise that a substantive legal relationship matching the entry exists. Two decisions half a century apart point the same way.

  • Contrast with movables - Article 249 of the Civil Act provides that a person who peacefully and openly takes a movable and possesses it in good faith and without negligence acquires ownership immediately even where the transferor was not the true owner. Movables have a statutory protection of reliance; immovables have no counterpart
  • Contrast with other systems - some jurisdictions do accord public credibility to registration, and there an acquirer who relied on the register can be protected by that reliance alone. Korea did not take that route
  • What is left in practice - where the register diverges from the true position, the true right holder prevails as a rule. What remains for the person who paid on the strength of the register is not a real right but the possibility of a claim in damages or similar, whose availability and scope vary by case

So the idea that pulling the register makes a deal safe is not accurate. Checking it is mandatory, but it is a step that narrows risk rather than one that guarantees safety. The practice of asking whether the counterparty is really the true right holder, and on what cause the registration in that person's name was made, grows out of this structure.

6. Presumptive effect - drawing the line

The absence of public credibility does not leave registration powerless. Registration carries presumptive effect. Where a registration exists, the rights it states are presumed to exist, and the party disputing it must prove the circumstances that overturn the presumption.

In its decision of 28 February 2003 in case 2002Da46256, the Supreme Court held to the effect that once a transfer registration of ownership appears on the register, its procedure and cause are presumed proper, and that where suspicious circumstances suggesting the procedure was not lawfully carried out are proved, the presumption is broken. Presumptive effect allocates the burden of proof; it does not fix the content of the register as true.

AxisPublic credibilityPresumptive effect
In one line Creating for a person who trusted the record the right it states, even where it diverges from the truth Taking the stated rights, at first, as existing
In Korean real estate registration Settled case law does not accord it Recognised through case law
Who proves what The question does not arise. Reliance alone creates nothing The party asserting that the registration was made without cause must prove it
Can it be broken Not applicable Yes. Proof of suspicious circumstances suggesting the procedure was not lawfully carried out breaks it
For someone transacting Reliance on the register alone does not deliver ownership The register is a strong starting point, not a final determination
  • First point of confusion - reading presumptive effect as meaning the register is treated as correct blends it straight into public credibility. A presumption yields once contrary proof arrives
  • Second point of confusion - the mere fact that a registered title holder asserted a cause different from the one recorded, and that assertion was not accepted, does not by itself break the presumption, as the case law has explained
  • What it means in practice - in litigation the presumption decides who must prove what first. In a transaction it shows up as treating the register as a basis while layering other material over it

7. Priority - the priority number and the receipt number

Where several rights attach to one property, who comes first decides the outcome. Article 4 paragraph 1 provides that the priority of registered rights follows the order of registration unless another statute provides otherwise, and paragraph 2 provides that the order follows the priority number as between registrations made in the same section, and the receipt number as between registrations made in different sections. Reading gapgu and eulgu separately is how priority gets misread.

  • Within one section - two mortgages in eulgu rank by priority number. The one written above comes first
  • Across sections - where a provisional attachment in gapgu meets a mortgage in eulgu, you compare receipt numbers, not priority numbers
  • Receipt and effect - Article 6 paragraph 1 deems an application received when the application information is stored in the electronic data processing system, and paragraph 2 provides that once the registrar completes the registration it takes effect from the time of receipt. The benchmark is receipt, not completion
  • Supplementary registration - Article 5 gives a supplementary registration the priority of its principal registration, while supplementary registrations relating to the same principal registration rank among themselves by their order
  • Provisional registration - under Article 91 a principal registration made on a provisional registration takes the priority of the provisional one. It sits quietly but has already reserved its place

Article 186 of the Civil Act overlays this: a change in real rights over immovable property by juristic act takes effect only upon registration. The benchmark is the moment of registration, not the day the contract was signed. Where a gap opens between final payment and the registration application, another registration can be received in that gap, and the receipt numbers decide the result.

8. Rights that arise without registration, and what is never recorded

Some things stay invisible however carefully you read. Knowing that list is the last step.

  • Physical condition - actual area, boundaries and unlawful extensions are not settled by the description section alone. That is why ledger records are compared
  • Contractual circumstances - special terms, unpaid management fees and vacancy status are not registrable matters. They belong to the contract and the site visit
  • Circumstances of the person - the title holder's credit position, intention to sell and the scope of any agency are not answered by the register
  • So you overlay - where the extract, the ledgers, the site and the counterparty's documents disagree, the disagreement is the thing to examine. How this is arranged in a lease is covered in the jeonse fraud prevention guide

9. Inspection, issuance, and the moment of checking

Article 19 paragraph 1 provides that anyone may, on payment of a fee and as prescribed by Supreme Court Regulations, request inspection of all or part of the matters recorded and issuance of a certified extract proving them. Attached documents may be inspected only as to the portion in which the requester has an interest. Paragraph 2 allows the request to be made at a registry other than the competent one, and paragraph 3 leaves the fee amounts and exemptions to Supreme Court Regulations.

  • Inspection versus issuance - inspection means viewing the content; issuance means receiving a document certifying it. Anything to be filed has to be issued
  • Fees for issuance - on the guidance of the Korean Easy Law portal, 1,200 won at a registry office (up to 20 pages, plus 50 won per additional page), 1,000 won at an unmanned kiosk and 1,000 won through the Internet Registry Office. Checked on 18 August 2026; amounts change with the regulations
  • Fees for inspection - on the same guidance, 1,200 won at a registry office and 700 won through the Internet Registry Office
  • Validity window - the same guidance states that a record may be inspected again within one hour, and that issuance or inspection is available only within three months of the fee payment date
  • The moment of checking is the point - the extract carries the time it was issued or inspected. Registrations received after that time are not on it. Because Article 6 makes receipt the benchmark for effect, a document pulled days ago describes the position of days ago

The order to read it in

  1. Match the location, land category and area in the description section against the property named in the contract, character by character. Rule out reading the wrong property first
  2. For a unit in a divided building, read the whole-building description, the exclusive-use description and the site ownership entry together
  3. In gapgu, confirm the present owner and scan for provisional registrations, provisional attachments, provisional dispositions and auction commencement
  4. In eulgu, read the maximum secured amount and creation date of each mortgage, and check for jeonse rights and easements
  5. Lay gapgu and eulgu side by side and redraw the timeline using receipt numbers
  6. Where cancelled history matters, request a form that carries it instead of a condensed one
  7. List what the register cannot show and confirm those items through other channels

The habit of pulling the register again immediately before final payment follows from this order. The register can change between signing and completion, and the receipt numbers report the change.

Frequently asked questions

Is a deungibu deungbon different from a certified register extract?

They point to the same thing under different names. The full amendment of the Real Estate Registration Act promulgated on 12 April 2011 and in force from 13 October 2011 (Act No. 10580) was the occasion on which the older wording for register copies and abstracts was reorganised into the term certified register extract. Everyday speech still uses the older word, but the document issued at the counter is the certified register extract.

Can a buyer check the register and still fail to acquire ownership?

Yes. In its decision of 26 February 2009 in case 2006Da72802, the Supreme Court held to the effect that real estate registration is not accorded public credibility, so where a transfer registration is a false registration, someone who trusted it, bought and completed a transfer registration cannot be treated as having acquired ownership. Outcomes turn on the facts, so professional advice is needed.

How does public credibility differ from presumptive effect?

Public credibility creates a right for a person who trusted a record that diverges from the truth, and Korean real estate registration does not carry it. Presumptive effect takes the stated rights as existing at first, and in its decision of 28 February 2003 in case 2002Da46256 the Supreme Court held to the effect that proof of suspicious circumstances suggesting the procedure was not lawfully carried out breaks that presumption.

Is the maximum secured amount in eulgu the actual debt?

No. Article 357 paragraph 1 of the Civil Act allows a mortgage fixing only the maximum amount of the debt to be secured while reserving determination for the future, and paragraph 2 deems interest to be included within that maximum. The outstanding balance has to be confirmed with the creditor rather than read off the register.

What rights never appear on the register?

A tenant's position under Article 3 paragraph 1 of the Housing Lease Protection Act, the right of retention under Article 320 paragraph 1 of the Civil Act, acquisition by operation of law under Article 187 of the Civil Act, and the statutory date of tax claims under Article 35 paragraph 1 of the Framework Act on National Taxes are the main ones. Physical condition and contractual circumstances are not registrable matters either.
