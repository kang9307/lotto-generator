82% of Revenue Is Retail, 57% of Operating Income Is AWS - Taking Amazon Apart With Its Own Filings

Read the same company by revenue and it is a retailer; read it by operating income and it is a cloud company

2026-07-31 Finance & Economy

Opening: a revenue ranking will misread this company

Look up Amazon in any table of the world's largest companies by revenue and it sits near the top. Next to it, the industry column almost always says retail or e-commerce. Judged on revenue alone, that is not a wrong label.

Split the same company's operating income the same way, though, and the table flips. The channel money comes in through and the channel money is left over in are not the same place. That mismatch is the whole subject of this article.

Here is the conclusion first. Opening the segment tables Amazon itself published and doing the arithmetic gives this.

  • For fiscal 2025 (twelve months ended December 31, 2025), AWS accounted for 17.96% of revenue and produced 57.03% of operating income
  • In the same period North America and International combined were 82.04% of revenue but only 42.97% of operating income
  • So the widely quoted line, 18% of revenue and 57% of income, is accurate for fiscal 2025
  • But for the second quarter of 2026 (three months ended June 30, 2026) the same ratios had moved to 21.05% of revenue and 60.53% of operating income
  • So the sentence must always carry its period. A ratio quoted without one drifts a little further off every quarter

2. Why the structure exists

Listed as bare ratios it looks mysterious. Look at how cost attaches in each business and it becomes ordinary.

The most direct way to say it is this. I calculated how much revenue it takes to produce one dollar of operating income.

Q2 2026: revenue required per dollar of operating income

North America   116,177 / 9,123  = 12.73 dollars
International    42,197 / 1,717  = 24.58 dollars
AWS              42,232 / 16,621 =  2.54 dollars

International has to sell 24.58 dollars of goods to keep one. AWS needs 2.54. Inside one company that is not a small multiple but a gap of nearly ten times. The reasons:

  • In retail, cost grows with revenue. One more item sold brings its own purchase cost, and packaging, storage space, delivery labor, and returns handling follow behind it
  • Most of that cost is tied to people and physical movement. Automating a fulfillment center does not change the fact that the last stretch still needs a person and a vehicle
  • Cloud is built first and filled later. Data centers and servers stand before the customer arrives, and customers are layered onto them
  • So when utilization of already-installed capacity rises, the share of each additional revenue dollar that goes to cost falls. A server with ten tenants costs roughly what it cost with one in power, cooling, and building
  • But this is not a permanent law. As the table showed, AWS operating margin fell from 37.04% in fiscal 2024 to 35.43% in fiscal 2025. Build new capacity and the cost arrives before the capacity fills
  • Conversely, International operating margin improved, from 2.65% in fiscal 2024 to 2.93% in fiscal 2025. A thin-margin business is not a business that cannot improve

In short, the mismatch between revenue scale and profit contribution is a difference in cost structure, not a ranking of business quality. The segment carrying 82% of revenue is the physical base that makes the company run, and the one producing 57% of income is a different kind of business layered on top of it.

3. Why advertising does not appear in the profit table

Any discussion of Amazon eventually reaches the claim that advertising is the hidden jewel. The purpose of this section is to draw a precise line between what the filings confirm and where estimation begins.

Now the crux. Revenue is disclosed under two cuts, but operating income only under the segment cut. The operating income table has three lines: North America, International, AWS. There is no advertising line at all. Which means:

  • Advertising revenue is confirmed by the filings: 19,809 million dollars in the second quarter of 2026, 9.87% of total revenue
  • It is also confirmed that advertising is a larger revenue line than subscriptions: 19,809 million against 13,730 million dollars
  • But the advertising business has no disclosed operating income and no disclosed margin, because the cost sitting against that revenue is not reported separately
  • Advertising revenue is folded into the North America and International lines in the segment table. How exactly it is allocated I could not verify
  • Therefore any advertising margin percentage you see online is an outside estimate, not a figure derived from disclosure. This article makes no such estimate

Why the distinction matters. When an item has large visible revenue and no reported profit, people fill the blank with the number they want to believe. The inference that advertising is high-margin may well be reasonable - guessing that serving one more ad impression costs less than shipping one more box is consistent with common sense. But a reasonable inference and a verified fact are different things, and a number invented to fill an empty cell must not be allowed to borrow the authority of a filing. That is why no advertising margin appears in this article.

4. Geography, and the asymmetry of the third segment

Look at the segment names again and something odd stands out: two are places and one is a product. North America and International are geographic; AWS is a service. That asymmetry causes real confusion when reading the table.

  • North America and International are retail-centered segments divided by geography, and each also absorbs the advertising and subscription revenue of its region
  • AWS is a single line regardless of geography. Revenue from a customer in Asia using AWS lands in the AWS line, not in International
  • So reading International as the whole of Amazon's business outside the United States is wrong. The cloud part of that international business has already been removed
  • For the same reason North America is not the whole US business either. AWS revenue from US customers sits in the AWS line

International is the least profitable of the three. The direction, though, is improvement. Placing the verified values side by side:

  • International shows the 2% range annually and the 4% range quarterly. The gap between annual and quarterly may come from seasonality, but I could not confirm the cause from the filings
  • North America, at around 7%, is also in a different world from the 39.36% of AWS. The gap between retail and cloud is far larger than the gap between the two retail segments
  • Lined up, the three operating margins read 4%, 8%, and 39%. That spread is precisely what produces the distortion in section 1
  • One caution: segment operating margins should not be carried directly into comparisons between companies. The value depends on how shared corporate costs were allocated, and those allocation rules differ by company

5. Capital spending and depreciation, and the lag between them

No recent earnings discussion omits capital spending related to artificial intelligence. There is one accounting structure that must be stated here: the moment cash leaves is not the moment expense is recognized.

  • Cash spent on servers and data centers leaves in full, at that moment, through investing activities on the cash flow statement
  • On the income statement, however, that spending does not land at once. It is spread across periods as depreciation over the useful life of the asset
  • So a quarter with a large build-out reflects only part of that spending in its operating income, and the rest is booked against later periods
  • As a result operating income and cash spending tell different stories. Read only one and you will misjudge the company's cash position
  • Because of this structure, the effect of capital spending on margin arrives with a lag. How much arrives when depends on asset mix and useful life assumptions, and the company sets those assumptions

I verified two figures. But they use different definitions and must not be compared directly. The trap is common enough that I separated them into a table.

Why the net income figure needs care. Net income for the second quarter of 2026 was reported at 62,647 million dollars, more than triple the 18,164 million of the prior-year quarter. But the company also stated that this quarter's net income includes roughly 53.4 billion dollars of non-operating pre-tax other income, primarily from certain investments. In other words, a large part of net income is not operating performance. That is exactly why this article compares segments using operating income only. To see business structure, you have to cut at the operating line.

6. The layers a foreign investor tends to miss

Everything above is company data denominated in dollars. For an investor living outside the United States there are further layers sitting on top of that table. In order:

6.1 Currency

  • All results are reported in dollars. The moment you convert into a home currency, the exchange rate enters the result as a variable
  • Results can improve in dollar terms while the converted figure moves the other way, depending on the direction of the rate. The reverse is equally true
  • That is why this article never states a converted amount. A converted figure with no stated reference date for the rate is a blurred number
  • If you need a conversion, it is better to do it yourself with a reference moment you have chosen, using something like a USD/KRW Calculator

6.2 Tax

Here I separate strictly between what I verified and what I did not.

  • The general shape. Gains on foreign shares are taxed by the investor's country of residence, not by the country the company is listed in. So the rules that matter are the ones where you file
  • What varies by jurisdiction. The filing deadline, whether an interim filing is required, the basic deduction, the rate, and whether losses can be offset against gains all differ
  • One country's rule does not transfer. A deduction amount or rate that applies where someone else files tells you nothing about your own situation. Do not carry a figure out of an article written for a different jurisdiction
  • Verified for one jurisdiction. Korea's National Tax Service states that gains on foreign shares are filed at final filing in May of the following year with no interim filing, with a basic deduction of 2.5 million won per year combined across domestic and foreign shares and a rate of 20%, and that gains and losses in the same tax period can be offset
  • Not verified. Whether a separate local income tax applies on top of that rate was not confirmed from primary material here. Confirm the rule directly
  • Not verified. Exactly how currency gains enter the taxable base was likewise not confirmed. Confirm the rule directly
  • Not verified. The treatment of dividend income is also outside what I verified. Confirm the rule directly

Writing a plausible number into a blank is worse than leaving it blank and saying so. Tax rules change, and calculating under an old rule because nobody noticed it changed produces a real loss. Do not skip the step of confirming with the tax authority or a qualified adviser before filing.

6.3 Dividends and time zones

  • Whether a dividend is paid, and under what policy, was not verified in this article. Check the company's investor relations page or the dividend section of its annual report directly
  • If a holding pays no dividend, no cash arrives during the holding period. A funding plan that assumes dividend income does not hold
  • Earnings are released after the US market closes. In most other time zones that means the early hours of the next day. The second quarter 2026 material was published on July 30 US time
  • So investors abroad cannot easily reach the market in the window right after publication. There is a structural gap between when you learn something and when you can act
  • Even where extended-hours access exists, the trading conditions differ from the regular session. Those conditions have to be checked wherever you trade

7. How to read the fact that profit comes from one place

That is the end of the verified material. What remains is how to read it. The short answer: the structure is neither good nor bad; it cuts both ways.

The same factCan be read this wayAnd also this way
60.53% of operating income comes from one segment (Q2 2026)The cash engine is unambiguousIf that one place stumbles, group profit stumbles
77.94% of the increase in operating income came from one segment (year over year)The direction of growth is clearThe basis of growth rests on a single axis
78.95% of revenue sits in thin-margin businesses (Q2 2026)There is room for profit contribution to growProfit is recovered slowly relative to scale
Capital spending is rising sharplyCapacity is being built ahead of demandThe cost arrives with a lag
Advertising revenue is large but its profit is undisclosedThere may be contribution that is not visibleWhat cannot be verified cannot enter a calculation

Both columns come from the same numbers. Which way to read them is not something the numbers decide. So this is as far as the article can go:

  • Concentration is both a risk and a strength. That profit is concentrated is in itself neutral, and it works in opposite directions depending on what happens to that segment
  • A share has a numerator and a denominator, and both move. A rising AWS share can mean AWS grew or that other segments' profit shrank. The share alone does not tell you which
  • So always read the share and the absolute amount together. That is why every table here prints both
  • Do not declare a trend from one quarter. As section 1 showed, the AWS share of operating income has fallen from one year to the next
  • Do not fill in unverified cells. Put a value like an advertising margin into a calculation and the conclusion now rests on an assumption rather than on disclosure

8. How to check it yourself, and which table to open

You do not have to take these numbers on trust. Here is the path to the same tables. This may be the part of the article that stays useful longest.

  1. Go to the company's investor relations page and find the section collecting quarterly results material
  2. Open the earnings release for that quarter. Scroll past the narrative summary to the financial statements attached at the back
  3. Find the table headed segment information. Operating income by segment appears only here, never in the front summary
  4. In the same document find the supplemental net sales information table. Revenue by product and service type is here. Operating income is not
  5. If you need accounting policy and notes, read the quarterly and annual reports. They arrive later than the release but contain more detail

Once the table is open, always check these:

  • The definition of the period. Three, six, or twelve months, and the end date. Several period columns sit side by side in one document, and reading the wrong column is easy
  • The unit. Usually millions of dollars. Order-of-magnitude mistakes happen most often when converting into billions or another currency
  • The sum check. Add the segments and compare with the consolidated total. All four periods here passed it. If the sum fails, the transcription is wrong
  • Operating income versus net income. Net income mixes in non-operating items. Cut at operating income when looking at business structure
  • Net versus gross. Items like capital spending may be shown net of proceeds from sales. Do not build a change out of differently defined values
  • Trailing twelve months versus fiscal year. Supplemental metrics often use a trailing basis. Do not mix it with fiscal-year values

It also helps to know in advance what changes from quarter to quarter.

  • The segment definitions themselves can change. If reporting is reorganized, prior figures are restated on the new basis, and they will then disagree with ratios quoted in older articles
  • Product and service category names can change too. When a line is merged or split, the time series breaks
  • Seasonality exists. A quarter containing the year-end shopping season looks different in retail from one that does not. Compare a quarter with the same quarter of the prior year as the default
  • Comparative figures can be restated. If a prior period is adjusted, the growth rates change with it

9. What this article could not verify

Stated honestly. Naming what could not be verified is part of the material.

  • The fiscal 2025 breakdown by product and service category. When I summed the category values from the material I opened, they did not equal the annual total, so I judged them unreliable and left them out. The second quarter 2026 breakdown summed exactly, so only that one is used
  • How advertising revenue is allocated between the North America and International segments. No separate allocation is presented in the disclosure and I could not verify it
  • The amount of depreciation recognized per period and the useful life assumptions. This needs note-level information and went beyond what I verified
  • Local income tax, the calculation of currency gains in the taxable base, and dividend taxation. As stated in section 6, these rules must be confirmed directly
  • Whether a dividend is paid, and the policy behind it. Check the company's investor relations page directly
  • Any outlook beyond the third quarter of 2026. Even where company guidance exists, this article does not deal in future results

The method here reduces to one sentence: numbers were taken only from company-published material, ratios were calculated from those numbers, the arithmetic was printed so it can be checked, and whatever could not be verified was labeled as not verified. Getting into the habit of checking whether those four conditions hold in any piece of investment writing is worth more, and lasts longer, than memorizing any individual figure.
