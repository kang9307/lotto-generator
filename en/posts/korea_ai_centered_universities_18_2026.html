The content below rests on what news reports (AI Times, Asia Economy, IT Biz News, Money Today, Dailian and others) share in common about the press release the Ministry of Science and ICT issued on September 29, 2026. It explains a program and is not advice on admissions or school choice.

What launched — the core of the September 29, 2026 ceremony at Yonsei University

On September 29, 2026 the Ministry of Science and ICT held the "AI-Centered University and AX Graduate School Joint Launch Ceremony" at Baekyang Nuri Hall, Yonsei University in Seoul. At this event 18 AI-centered universities and 15 AX graduate schools were launched. The reports framed it as the shift of university education toward an AI focus getting under way in earnest, and the core of the program is twofold: two programs launched together on the same day, and universities in the AI-centered program making AI basic education mandatory for all their students.

The two programs differ in nature. The AI-centered university program is about reshaping undergraduate education as a whole around AI. The contents named in the announcement are strengthening AI major education, making AI basic education mandatory for all students, and creating AI convergence courses that link a field of study with AI. The AX graduate school program was introduced as support for training AI convergence talent, in other words AX talent, who combine AI technology with industry and domain knowledge. The former targets the structure of undergraduate education, the latter graduate-level talent development and industry cooperation.

The remark attributed to Vice Minister Ryu Je-myeong in the announcement points the same way. The gist is that a university in the AI era must go beyond teaching AI and become an engine of innovation that changes education itself to suit the era. The remark states the direction the policy aims at. It is not an assessment of the results or standing of any individual university.

There is one distinction worth setting up first when reading this program. "Launched" means that selection and support have begun. It does not mean that each university is already running AI basic education for all of its students. The announcement listed what the selected universities are pursuing, and the timing and method of implementation depend on each university's curriculum. The change students actually feel appears when each university revises its curriculum.

The row without a figure, that is, "Contents pursued", touches the central question of this article. Mandatory AI basic education for all students has no credits, no year of study, and no start year attached to it. That blank is what section 5 takes up.

3+15=18, 61% and 53% — recalculating the numbers

The figures in the announcement interlock, so they can be recalculated. If the calculation holds, the rest of the description reads more steadily.

  • 18 AI-centered universities. Adding the 15 converted from SW-centered universities to the 3 newly selected gives 18. The composition described in the reports and the total agree.
  • 11 non-metropolitan, 61%. 11 divided by 18 is about 61.1%. The announced 61% equals this value rounded. Working backward, the remaining 7 are located in the metropolitan area. The figure of 7 is not stated in the announcement; it is 18 minus 11.
  • 8 AX graduate schools, 53%. 8 divided by 15 is about 53.3%. It matches the announced 53%, and the remaining 7 follow from the same kind of back calculation.
  • 3 billion won a year times the maximum period. Receiving 3 billion won a year for up to 8 years would total 24 billion won, and for AX graduate schools, at up to 6 years, 18 billion won. These totals are multiplication and not figures from the announcement, and they must not be read as every university receiving the maximum period and the full amount. "Up to" is an expression of an upper limit.
  • 38 schools to be converted. The announcement names 38 SW-centered universities not yet converted as the evaluation target next year. How many of those 38 will convert is not in the announcement.

What deserves attention in this calculation is rounding. 61.1% and 53.3% were shortened to 61% and 53%. The notation is in whole percent, and the base is the small numbers 18 and 15. A change of one university moves the share by 5 to 7 percentage points, so these shares are better read as a description of composition and cannot be read as evidence of a trend or a change.

What kind of program is an AI-centered university — converting from SW-centered

What the announcement described as the main part of the AI-centered universities is the 15 converted from existing SW-centered universities. As the name suggests, the starting point of the program lies in a shift from software-centered education to AI-centered education. A plan was announced together with it: next year the SW-centered universities that have not yet converted will be evaluated and a full conversion to AI-centered pursued. The direction of the program is already out, and this launch is its first step.

The announced contents fall into three groups.

  • Stronger AI major education. Deepening the courses in which AI is studied as a major. The reports wrote that advanced AI major courses are operated.
  • Mandatory AI basic education for all students. AI basic education is made required for every student regardless of major.
  • New AI convergence courses. New courses linking each field of study with AI are opened.

The three aim at different students. The first is for students who major in AI or want to study it deeply, the second for all students regardless of major, and the third for students who want to combine AI with their own major. In this layout, "basic education for all students" covers the widest range. At the same time it is the item whose detailed criteria are least written down.

It is too early to judge the results this program will produce. The program has only just launched, and it takes time before curricula are actually revised and students take those classes. The announcement presented no performance indicators or target numbers of students. Before discussing policy effects, what can be done at this stage is to distinguish what has been fixed as a system from what is still blank. On the same current, the earlier article AI Impact on Youth Employment 2026: Analysis Report has section 5, "Status of domestic and international policy responses", and that content is not explained again here.

Reading "AI basics mandatory for all students" — what is written and what is not

The phrase in this program that touches students' daily lives most directly is "AI basic education mandatory for all students." Yet the official content as reported gives only that phrase. Nowhere in the reports are the range of years, the number of credits, the form of the course or the year of implementation given. So the phrase has to be read by separating what is written from what is not.

PhraseWhat is writtenWhat is not writtenWhere students look
All studentsA statement that selected universities target all studentsWhether all years of study, or only from a particular entry yearThe university's regulations and curriculum notices

MandatoryA direction of making it requiredWhether it is a graduation requirement, the credits, how to complete it otherwiseNotices of academic regulation amendments
AI convergence coursesNew courses linking a major with AIWhich departments and semesters, who may enrollCourse catalogs by semester
Timing of implementationA statement that it is being pursued as of the announcement dateEach university's start yearNotices from each university's academic affairs office

The "What is not written" column of the table does not point out a flaw in the program. In the usual structure, a central ministry's announcement sets the direction and the scale of support, and leaves detailed implementation to each university's curriculum revision. That the announcement does not write the details fits that structure. But if the reader rewrites "mandatory" as "a graduation requirement" or "required in every year," content that is not in the announcement is taken as fact.

Restated in one sentence: what the announcement fixed is that selected universities pursue the direction of making AI basic education for all students mandatory, and which year takes what for how many credits is decided by each university. Missing this distinction can turn the headline phrase "mandatory for all students" directly into the misunderstanding "it will be required at my school next year."

Reading "up to 8 years at 3 billion won a year"

The support terms consist of three words: per university, up to, and a year. Each word draws a different limit.

  • Per university. The unit of support is one university. There is no statement that it scales with the number of students or departments. Whether the amount is at the same level regardless of a university's size, or adjusted by university, is not written in the announcement.
  • Up to. An upper limit on the period. Eight years is the maximum, and there is no statement on whether support can end sooner. Because "up to" is attached, it cannot be read that every university receives all 8 years. Whether the period varies with evaluation or is divided into stages is likewise not written in the reports.
  • 3 billion won a year. The amount supported every year. Whether this is a ceiling or an average, or the same across the whole period, is not written. The reports rendered it as receiving 3 billion won every year.

These conditions combine into the single line "up to 8 years, 3 billion won a year." Within it, what is settled is three things: the unit of support, the scale of the amount, and the ceiling of the period. The evaluation method and whether payment differs by university remain open. The details probably lie in the program notice or agreement documents, but they are not within the range of the current reports.

What the support money is spent on is also outside the announcement. Expenditure items such as curriculum revision, hiring faculty, facilities and student support are not listed. Questions students and applicants might care about, for example whether this funding affects tuition or scholarships, cannot be answered from the announcement alone. To answer such questions one has to check the program plans or guidance that each university publishes.

AX graduate schools and industry cooperation — 26 companies and a research center

The AX graduate school program supports cultivating convergence talent that combines AI technology with industry and domain knowledge. The 15 universities selected this year are supported at 3 billion won a year for up to 6 years per university. The period differs from the up to 8 years of the AI-centered universities. The two programs share the amount and differ in the upper limit of the period.

What stands out in the AX graduate schools is the structure of industry cooperation. The reports said that 26 companies from major Korean industries, including Samsung Electronics, Hanwha Aerospace, POSCO Holdings, Doosan Robotics and LIG D&A, take part and jointly set up AX research cooperation centers with the universities. Some reports also state that a multiple-advisor system is introduced. Because companies enter as cooperating parties, the way the program is described is that research topics and the direction of talent training connect with demand at industrial sites.

  • Nature of the participating companies. The companies listed in the reports are five and serve as examples. The full list of the 26 companies is not in the reports.
  • Role of the centers. The AX research cooperation centers are set up jointly by universities and companies. The concrete operation, budget sharing and attribution of research results are not in the announcement.
  • Multiple-advisor system. A method in which a student has two or more advisors, found only in some reports. Its scope of application is not confirmed.
  • Graduate level as the target. The AX graduate school program supports graduate courses, so it is not a scheme that applies directly to undergraduates. The mandatory basic education for all students belongs to the AI-centered university side.

Separately, a program named the AI Star Fellowship was mentioned in some reports. According to them it supports each project at 2 billion won a year for up to 6 years, with 20 new projects. It is introduced as distinct from the two pillars of this ceremony, the AI-centered universities and AX graduate schools, and since it goes beyond the scope of describing those two it is not treated in detail.

What students and applicants should check — within the scope of explaining the program

This section organizes what someone encountering this program can look at next. It merely lists items to check that arise from the program, and it does not mean that some university is better or that any particular choice should be made.

  • Whether my university is a program participant. The official list has to be checked in Ministry of Science and ICT material. University names are not listed here. The division of the 18 into 3 new and 15 converted can also be seen from the official list.
  • Details of the basic education. Course titles, credits, whether it is a graduation requirement and target years appear in each university's curriculum notices and regulation amendment guidance.
  • Timing of implementation. Each university revises at a different point, so one can ask the academic affairs office which regulation applies to one's own entry year.
  • Convergence courses. Whether new courses linked with one's own major are opened, and whether there are enrollment conditions, can be seen in the course catalog each semester.
  • Graduate school matters. The AX graduate school program is for graduate courses, so look at that graduate school's admission notice and its partner company guidance.
  • Use of the funds. Whether the program funding goes to curriculum, facilities or student support is checked in the university's public materials.

What this list has in common is that the answers lie in university-level material and not in the central ministry's announcement. The announcement set the frame of the program, and what rules and courses that frame becomes inside each university differs by university. Reading the meaning of the program and confirming the rules that apply to oneself are different steps, and for the latter the university's own official notice is the most accurate.

Finally, a note on the composition of metropolitan and non-metropolitan universities. The announcement gave the non-metropolitan share as 61% for AI-centered universities and 53% for AX graduate schools, and said it plans to raise the non-metropolitan share of new selections to 70% or more from 2027. That goal is a plan about the composition of future selections and does not change the character of the universities that have just launched.

What could not be confirmed

  • The original press release of the Ministry of Science and ICT (Policy Briefing). The page would not open, so content reproduced in news reports was used for cross-checking.
  • The official list of the 18 AI-centered universities and 15 AX graduate schools. Most reports did not carry the list, and university names that appeared in some reports could not be checked against official material, so none are listed in the article.
  • The detailed scope of mandatory AI basic education for all students (years, credits, graduation requirement, start year).
  • Whether 3 billion won a year is the same amount per university, a ceiling, or varies with evaluation.
  • The conditions behind up to 8 years and up to 6 years, that is, whether there is an interim evaluation or stages.
  • The full list of the 26 participating companies and the operating details of the AX research cooperation centers.
  • The detailed terms of the AI Star Fellowship. It appeared only in some reports and was not cross-checked.
