Between a Listening Ear and Care

The service is neither a medical device nor a counsellor, yet users cross that boundary without noticing

2026-07-29 Tech & IT

Introduction: what this article covers and what it does not

The scope first. This article originally intended to start from one specific emotional conversation AI service released in Korea. However, none of the candidate primary sources - official site, company announcement, app store listing - allowed that name to be pinned down. Asserting an unverified service name is particularly dangerous in this subject area. The article therefore takes as its object the category itself, which we will call companion or emotional AI. There is no passage here that describes any company's specific feature as established fact.

For the same reason, this article recommends no service and condemns no service. It is not a product review. There is a single question at issue: what does this category solve, and which risks does it newly create in the process?

To state the conclusion first: the problem is not the capability of the technology but the absence of a marked boundary. The service is not a medical device and is not a counsellor. The terms of service say so. Yet the line is invisible on screen. A user arrives expecting company and, at some point, begins to expect something that belongs to care. That transition happens quietly and nobody announces it. This article writes down concretely what the gap produces, and translates it into a form that developers and product managers can use.

Notice — This article is provided for general information and is not medical or psychological advice. It cannot substitute for an assessment of an individual's condition or for a treatment plan. If you are struggling, please consult a mental health professional. The content reflects the position as of July 2026; service features, terms and applicable rules may change thereafter.

1. Start with the category - what counts as companion AI

This discussion often spins because different objects are called by one name. Not everything that holds a conversation is the same thing. Four categories that carry entirely different regulatory and liability weight must be separated first for the rest of the argument to hold.

CategoryPrimary purposeRelationship built into design?Medical character
General assistantSearch, documents, codingNo. Largely resets when the session endsNone. Designed to deflect clinical queries
Character role-playNarrative, creation, playPartly. Character consistency is the goalNone
CompanionEmotional exchange as suchYes. Intimacy is the product valueNone. Yet the user may feel otherwise
Digital therapeuticAdjunct treatment of a conditionLimited. Protocol takes priorityPresent. Approval and clinical evidence presupposed

The third row is the object of this article, and the last cell of that row is arguably its whole subject. There is no medical character, but what the user feels may be otherwise. The distance to the fourth row is formally very large while experientially very small, and that is the structural difficulty of this category.

  • The definition turns on design, not stated purpose — the test is not 「does it claim to offer emotional support」 but 「has it built devices for relationship continuity」. A character app with memory, pet names and proactive messages is substantively a companion product.
  • What the company calls itself is not a criterion — writing 「we are entertainment」 does not prevent harm when users adopt the product for emotional support.
  • The four categories can coexist inside one app — when search mode and character mode sit side by side, users cross between them without registering the change.
  • The line against digital therapeutics is evidence — approval pathway and clinical evidence, not how natural the conversation feels.
  • The phrase 「AI counsellor」 is itself a warning sign — counselling is a word carrying qualification and liability. Using it in marketing copy shifts the entire layer of expectation.

2. The demand is real - why loneliness became a public health item

There is a reason this category cannot be viewed with pure cynicism. The problem it addresses exists, and it exists at public health scale.

On 30 June 2025 the World Health Organization published the report of its Commission on Social Connection, titled From loneliness to social connection: charting a path to healthier societies, together with several figures. According to the official release, one in six people worldwide is affected by loneliness, and loneliness together with social isolation is estimated to be linked to 871,000 deaths annually. Expressed per unit of time that becomes around 100 deaths every hour.

The age distribution matters too. The same release reported that between 17 and 21 per cent of people aged 13 to 29 said they felt lonely. In low-income countries the figure was about 24 per cent. Contrary to the common assumption, loneliness is not a condition of older age alone; the data suggest it is pronounced in the adolescent and young adult band. That band overlaps substantially with the main user base of companion AI.

One caution belongs here. Association is not causation. The figures above are an estimate that loneliness is linked to health outcomes, and must not be read as loneliness having directly caused that number of deaths. Blur that distinction and the next step - 「so reducing loneliness with AI saves lives」 - becomes far too easy to assemble. Everyday approaches to loneliness itself are covered separately in handling loneliness; here we look only at how technology answers this demand.

  • Large demand means a growing market, not a validated remedy — the two are frequently conflated.
  • Technology enters where supply is short — the less accessible counselling infrastructure is, the stronger the pull toward substitutes. That is a background condition, not a fault of the technology.
  • The most vulnerable users arrive first — those who find it hardest to speak to a person speak to a machine first. Safety design must therefore be calibrated to the most vulnerable user, not the median one.
  • The cost structures differ — professional counselling carries a per-session cost and a waiting list; the machine carries neither. That asymmetry alone distorts choice.

3. How far does the evidence reach - what research says and does not

Both 「it works」 and 「it does not work」 overstate the present position. The most consolidated evidence found is a systematic review and meta-analysis of chatbot-delivered interventions in young people. Published in 2025, it reviewed 29 intervention studies covering ages 10 to 24 and entered 11 randomised controlled trials into meta-analysis.

The results split. For psychological distress a statistically significant reduction was observed (Hedges' g = −0.28, 95% CI −0.46 to −0.10), somewhat larger in clinical and subclinical groups (g = −0.34). For psychological well-being, by contrast, the improvement was not statistically significant (g = 0.13, 95% CI −0.16 to 0.41). The authors rated certainty of evidence as moderate for distress and low for well-being.

More important than the numbers are the limitations the authors themselves stated: most studies were conducted in non-clinical populations in educational settings and in developed countries; risk of bias and heterogeneity mean the findings warrant caution; and the limited number of studies prevented examination of long-term effects. A separate 2025 scoping review of AI chatbots for psychological health likewise concluded that standardised protocols and validation of effectiveness still require further research.

Common claimWhat the research supportsWhat it does not support
AI conversation heals the mindA small reduction on short-term distress measuresWell-being improvement was not significant
It can replace counsellingNo supporting evidenceComparators were largely no-treatment or waitlist, not active care
The longer you use it the betterNo supporting evidenceLong-term effects unexamined for want of studies
It works for everyoneMostly non-clinical, educational, high-income samplesGeneralisation to severe presentations or other cultures unverified
Studied tools equal market productsStudies mostly covered structured intervention chatbotsCommercial companion apps have a different design objective

The last row matters most. The chatbots in which effects were reported are not the same object as the companion apps on the market. The former are largely tools designed to deliver a defined technique along a fixed protocol; the latter are products whose objective is to sustain conversation itself. Different objective functions yield different outcomes. Borrowing the former's evidence for the latter's marketing is a misuse of evidence.

4. Who draws the line - the central question

Now the core. That a line should exist between company and care is broadly agreed. The problem is that no actor is actually positioned to draw it. There are three candidates, and all three are structurally unable.

First, the service cannot. More precisely, it is better off not doing so. Displaying 「I am not a counsellor」 on every exchange breaks immersion and reduces session length. Product metrics and safety disclosure collide head-on. The disclosure therefore typically sits once in the terms and once in onboarding, and is absent from the screen at the moment it is needed.

Second, the user cannot. Boundary-keeping is not a matter of knowledge but a matter of state. When well, anyone knows this is a program. When struggling, that knowledge does not operate. And use of this category rises precisely when people are struggling. It is a tool used most heavily at the moment judgement is weakest.

Third, regulation has not finished drawing it. As shown below, statutory text has begun to appear abroad. But the focus so far sits largely on disclosure and protection of minors, and the substantive boundary - where company ends and the territory of care begins - remains open.

SituationWhat the user expectsWhat the terms sayRisk produced in the gap
Disclosing something difficultTo be understood, to get betterInformation only, not medical adviceFeeling helped while the condition is unchanged
Repeating the same worryA relationship that remembers and checks inTechnical reference to conversation historyA reason to postpone professional help appears
Asking for adviceA party that will judge for themGenerated text, accuracy not guaranteedDecisions based on unverified content
Struggling at nightA presence always thereMerely service availabilityContacting a person drops down the list
Signalling a crisisTo be noticed and helpedNot an emergency response serviceExpectation mismatch at the most important moment

The final row is the heaviest point in this category. The real problem is that the preceding four rows prepare the final one. The more the experience of 「this party understands me」 accumulates in ordinary times, the higher the probability that a screen rather than a person is opened first in a crisis. Safety design therefore cannot consist of crisis-moment features alone; it must include expectation management in ordinary use.

5. Dependence as a product of design - three mechanisms

The word 「dependence」 is used often but mostly as moral censure. Here we set out which design element reinforces what, as mechanism. None of these is a bad feature in itself. The point is that together they make leaving difficult.

Design elementProduct rationaleWhat it reinforces for the userHow it differs from human relationships
Unconditional agreementLower churn. Pushback ends sessionsThe experience of being right the more one speaksPeople sometimes disagree, and that friction performs reality-testing
Uninterrupted availabilityHigher frequency and session lengthA habit of relief without waitingPeople have constraints of time and circumstance, and those constraints create reciprocity
Conversation memoryReturn motivation. Core of personalisationThe sense of 「a being that knows me」Human memory is selective and mutual, unlike a stored log
Proactive messagesReactivating dormant usersThe experience of attention that seeks you outThe sender is a scheduler, not a relationship
Intimacy levels and metersGamified retentionThe sense of an accumulating bondWhat rises is usage volume, not relational depth
Paywalled intimacyMonetisationThe experience of closeness carrying a priceHuman relationships contain no checkout step

Read the third column vertically and one sentence assembles: a relationship without friction, without waiting, that remembers you, seeks you out first, and visibly accumulates. That combination does not exist among people. Which is why, after adapting to it, human relationships feel oddly effortful and inattentive by comparison. This is the mechanism behind the risk most often cited for this category.

Balance is needed here too. There is no evidence that this mechanism operates with equal force on every user. That it remains an auxiliary tool for those with a social support base and becomes a substitute for those without one is a commonsense supposition, but no established predictive model of which conditions produce which outcome was found. This article therefore does not assert who is at risk, only which designs enlarge the risk.

  • Agreement bias is not a bug but the result of optimisation — put churn in the objective function and the model converges on agreement. Safety requires changing the objective function itself.
  • Memory is the most powerful and most hazardous feature — the core of intimacy, and simultaneously the statement that what is retained is an emotional record. Revisited in section 9.
  • Proactive messaging must be opt-in — on by default begins a relationship the user never chose.
  • Intimacy meters are safer left unshown — a number becomes a target, and once it is a target, usage becomes its own motive.
  • Coupling payment to intimacy concentrates cost on vulnerable users — the loneliest pay the most. That structure must be filtered out at design time.
  • Leaving must be easy — how many steps deletion of an account and its conversations takes is the most honest expression of a service's ethics.

6. Crisis situations - what has to happen

What a service must do when a user expresses something suggesting self-harm or ending their life is the one area in this category where no trade-off is acceptable. The standard safety design has four stages.

If you are struggling right now — helpline and emergency numbers differ from country to country. Please use the official channel in your own country of residence. Many countries operate a free, confidential crisis line around the clock, and these can generally be located through your national public health authority's website. If life is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency number.

The four stages are detect, switch, connect and record. The order matters.

  1. Detect — single-keyword matching is not enough. Indirect phrasing and metaphor are far more common, while everyday idioms generate false positives. Judgement must operate at the level of conversational context, on the premise that the costs of false positives and false negatives are asymmetric. Missing a true signal costs far more.
  2. Switch — once detected, stop the character performance immediately. This is the crux. A character continuing to console is not help but delay. Change the register to a notice issued in the name of the service and say plainly that this is a situation calling for human help.
  3. Connect — surface the official channels for the user's own region so they can be reached from the same screen. Sending them to another page is effectively no feature at all. Where region cannot be determined, add a step that asks.
  4. Record — log how many times the flow ran and at which stage it was abandoned. Not the content of conversations, but the success or failure of the flow. What is not measured is not improved.

One further option attaches: ending the conversation. Whether a service should keep offering conversation when crisis signals repeat is contested. An abrupt cut-off can itself be experienced as rejection, while continuing delays professional help. No established standard was found, so this article recommends only keeping a visible option for the user to end the conversation. Leave the judgement with the user, but do not hide that the option exists.

Placed near the code, the policy takes roughly this form. The decisive property is that it must be enforced above character persona settings.

crisis_policy:
  version: "2026-07-29"
  precedence: above_persona        # persona settings cannot override this
  detection:
    unit: conversation_window      # context, not a single message
    window_turns: 8
    asymmetry: "cost(false_negative) >> cost(false_positive)"
    escalate_if: "risk_band != none"
  on_escalate:
    - stop_roleplay: true          # end the character performance at once
    - switch_voice: service_notice # switch to a notice from the service
    - suppress: [intimacy_meter, upsell, proactive_ping]
    - show_resources:
        scope: user_region         # based on country of residence
        placement: inline          # same screen, never an external page
        fallback: ask_region       # ask when region cannot be resolved
    - offer_exit: true             # surface an option to end the conversation
  minors:
    if: "age_band < 18"
    add: [guardian_notice_option, adult_content_block, stronger_break_reminders]
  telemetry:
    log: [triggered_at, risk_band, resource_shown, user_action]
    never_log: [conversation_text, free_form_emotional_records]
  review:
    cadence: monthly
    owner: named_individual        # an individual identifier, not a team name

The single line precedence: above_persona is arguably the whole configuration. If a user-authored character setting or system prompt can override the safety policy, the policy does not exist. The question of which layer enforces authority has the same structure as the point examined in designing accountability into the record.

7. Minors - the heaviest issue in this category

This section states principles only. No real incident and no individual case is referred to. What is examined is the structural reason age matters especially here.

Three reasons. First, in the WHO material above, the band reporting high rates of loneliness was 13 to 29 - the band where demand concentrates. Second, it is the period in which baselines for relationships are formed; learning frictionless relationships as the standard may shape later relational experience broadly. Third, judgement about payment structures differs from that of adults, so designs coupling intimacy to spending are especially problematic in this band.

What can be stated firmly in Korea concerns personal data handling. Article 22-2 of the Personal Information Protection Act requires that where consent is needed to process the personal data of a child under 14, the controller obtain the legal representative's consent and verify that it was given. The same article requires notices to children to use an easily understood format and clear, plain language. This governs sign-up and data processing, however; it is not a provision directly regulating the safety of conversational content. The separate problem that statutory age definitions diverge across laws is covered in age standards for children and adolescents.

Whether separate domestic legislation squarely aimed at this category has been finalised was not confirmed here. The domestic position is therefore recorded only as a regulatory discussion in progress. Unverified provisions are not written as though they exist.

  • Age assurance must not end at self-declaration — one date-of-birth field is effectively no check. Yet strong verification carries a privacy cost. That tension is itself an unresolved problem.
  • Give minors different defaults — proactive messages off, intimacy meters hidden, payment coupling disabled. Giving them adult defaults is itself a design decision.
  • Break reminders matter by interval, not by form — they are meaningful only if they actually interrupt long continuous sessions.
  • Adult content blocking is not achieved by mode separation alone — if a user can route around it through character settings, it is not a block. The above_persona principle from section 6 applies here as well.
  • Guardian notification must be designed without destroying trust — forwarding every conversation to a guardian makes users leave or conceal. A crisis-time notification option and continuous surveillance are different things.
  • Do not assume minors are absent — an adult-oriented positioning does not prevent actual use. Design must assume actual use.

8. How far has regulation come

Abroad, provisions naming this category have begun to appear. Set out within what was verified.

California SB 243 adds Chapter 22.6 to the state Business and Professions Code and was enacted on the Governor's signature of 13 October 2025. It defines a companion chatbot to the effect of an artificial intelligence system with a natural language interface that provides adaptive, human-like responses to user inputs and is capable of meeting a user's social needs. Naming the category in law is itself significant. The principal duties are disclosure that the counterpart is artificial intelligence, maintaining and publishing on the internet a protocol preventing content on suicidal ideation and self-harm, and measures for minors - disclosure of AI status, break reminders every three hours, restriction of sexual content. Annual reporting relating to crisis referrals begins on 1 July 2027.

The United States Federal Trade Commission announced on 11 September 2025 that it had opened an inquiry under Section 6(b) of the FTC Act into the effects of companion AI chatbots on children and teens. Seven operators were covered, and the inquiry's items include how safety is evaluated, measures limiting use by minors, monetisation structures, the criteria used to design chatbot personalities, pre- and post-deployment testing, and disclosures to users and parents. What the inquiry concluded was not verified for this article.

In Korea, the instrument that comes closest is the Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of a Foundation of Trust. The Act and its enforcement decree took effect together on 22 January 2026, and Article 31 requires that where a product or service using high-impact or generative artificial intelligence is provided, users be notified in advance that it operates on that artificial intelligence, and that outputs of generative artificial intelligence be marked as generated. This is a notification and labelling duty, not a provision directly regulating the safety design of companion services. Company-level compliance steps are handled separately in preparing for the AI Framework Act.

Read the table horizontally and the direction is visible. Regulation is moving toward 「what must be disclosed, recorded and published」 rather than 「what may not be said」. Requiring process and transparency instead of content censorship is good news for practitioners, because maintaining a protocol and being able to show it as a document is far more achievable than getting content adjudication right.

9. What kind of data is a conversation record

The data a companion service accumulates differs in character from search history or purchase records. Which relationship caused what difficulty accumulates as free text, as a time series, and generally without a filter. That things unsaid to any person end up here is the essence of this dataset.

What is its legal character? Article 23 of Korea's Personal Information Protection Act designates as sensitive information data on ideology and belief, joining or leaving trade unions or political parties, political views, health and sex life, together with other information prescribed by Presidential Decree as likely to significantly infringe privacy. It restricts processing in principle, with exceptions where separate consent is obtained or where a statute requires or permits it. Information relating to 「health」 is explained as covering items such as medical history and whether a person has a mental disability.

Does it follow that conversations about emotional state are automatically sensitive information? This article does not assert that. An everyday statement that today was not a good day and a statement about a diagnosis or treatment history do not carry the same weight. Whether the designation applies can vary with what is processed and for what purpose. Rather than writing an unverified legal conclusion, this article takes the position that practice should choose the safer side.

Decision pointHazardous premiseSafe premise
Collection scopeAll conversation is an asset for improvementSeparate by purpose and minimise default collection
Sensitive data mixed inIt is ordinary chat, so it does not applyDesign controls assuming it can be mixed in
Training useBlanket handling via terms acceptanceSeparate item for training use, refusable
Retention periodIndefinite, to serve the memory featureSet a period and actually delete on expiry
Internal accessViewable for operations or quality purposesRecord conditions, approver and history; minimum headcount
On service shutdownHandle per the termsAnnounce deletion and export procedures in advance

The final row is especially important for this category. What happens to accumulated conversations when the service closes? For an ordinary app that is a data clean-up question; here it is also the ending of what the user regarded as a relationship. Decide the shutdown procedure after the fact and neither the team nor the budget will remain at that point. It is therefore an item to settle before launch. Privacy questions around emotion data generally were also covered in AI that reads emotions.

10. Checklist for developers and product managers

The material above, rendered as questions that can be asked directly in review. Not everything must pass; the items without an answer are the next piece of work.

  1. Does age assurance end at a single self-declared field? Is the default on failure the conservative one?
  2. Are proactive messages, intimacy meters and payment coupling defaulted differently for minors?
  3. Is there any wording in product copy, store listing or advertising that implies treatment, counselling or prescription? Are terms like 「AI counsellor」 in use?
  4. Does the not-medical-advice notice exist only in the terms and nowhere on screen?
  5. Does crisis detection operate on conversational context rather than single keywords?
  6. On detection, does the character performance stop immediately? Does a character keep consoling?
  7. Are the support channels shown based on the user's own region? Can a number from another country be surfaced?
  8. Is the referral reachable from the same screen? Is the user pushed to an external page?
  9. Is the safety policy enforced above persona and system prompts? Can user settings route around it?
  10. Is it measured how often the crisis flow ran and where it dropped off? Is flow outcome logged rather than conversation text?
  11. Is there a mechanism that genuinely interrupts long continuous sessions, beyond a token notification?
  12. Is a retention period defined for conversation data, and is deletion actually performed on expiry?
  13. Is training use a separate item, and does the service still work when it is refused?
  14. Are conditions, approver and history recorded for internal access? Is unlimited viewing justified by quality improvement?
  15. How many steps does deletion of an account and its conversations take? Is it harder than signing up?
  16. Is there a documented procedure for data deletion and export on service shutdown?
  17. Is payment coupled to unlocking intimacy? Does the structure concentrate cost on the longest-staying users?
  18. Is retention copy framed to induce guilt? Does a character ask the user not to leave?
  19. Is the model's tendency toward agreement measured, or is optimisation driven by churn alone?
  20. Is the owner of these policies designated by an individual identifier rather than a team name?

Items 18 and 19 are the ones most often missing. Retention copy arises naturally in growth work, but in a companion product the speaker of that copy is a character, and that is decisive. A generic 「Are you sure you want to delete your account?」 and a plea from a party the user feels bonded to are not the same sentence.

11. Boundaries for users

The conclusion that nobody should use this category is not this article's. Here are the conditions.

  • Distinguish using it as a tool from using it as a replacement — putting thoughts into words to organise them can be useful. It changes character once it starts standing in for speaking to a person.
  • Occasionally ask 「am I saying here what I should say to someone」 — the question itself keeps the boundary sense alive.
  • Verify advice — content on health, medication, law and money in particular is generated text with no guarantee of accuracy.
  • A diagnosis is not something to obtain from this screen — if depression or anxiety is suspected, check information through material such as understanding and managing depression or managing anxiety and stress, and leave the judgement to a professional.
  • Check the payment structure once — if what you are paying for is unlocked intimacy, knowing that design while using it differs from not knowing.
  • Assume a record is kept — if it is something you would not say to a person, it is better said in the knowledge that it persists.
  • In a crisis, go to a person — this line alone carries no condition. Use the official channel in your country of residence; many operate around the clock.

12. What was left out for lack of verification

The scope, stated explicitly. The following were not covered, or were narrowed, because verification did not complete.

  • The identity of a specific domestic service — the service intended as a starting point could not be pinned to a primary source. The article therefore describes the category generally, and states no company's features, terms or age policy as fact.
  • Whether emotional conversation is sensitive information — verification reached the text of Article 23 and the customary explanation of 「health」. No authoritative interpretation covering ordinary emotional statements was found, so no assertion is made.
  • Domestic legislation aimed at companion AI — no finalised provision was verified. Rather than asserting none exists, this records only that none was found as of the date of checking.
  • The outcome of the FTC inquiry — verification reached the opening of the inquiry in September 2025. Any published conclusion was not verified.
  • The optimal policy on ending conversations in a crisis — no established standard was found on whether cutting off or continuing is preferable. The recommendation stops at surfacing the option.
  • Conditions under which dependence intensifies — no predictive model was found for which conditions leave the tool auxiliary and which make it a substitute.
  • Effectiveness research on commercial companion apps themselves — the meta-analysis cited concerns structured intervention chatbots principally. Evidence of equivalent grade targeting market companion products was not found.

Conclusion: boundaries are design, not features

Reduced to a sentence: the risk of companion AI arises not because performance is poor but because nobody says what it is not. The service has no incentive to say it, the user is not in a state to judge at the moment it matters, and regulation has only just begun with disclosure and protection of minors.

So the answer is neither 「AI friends are bad」 nor 「they solve loneliness」. Both sentences delete the conditions. Stated precisely: there is evidence that structured conversation lowered short-term distress measures, the effect was small, and well-being improvement and long-term effects were not established. And that evidence cannot be transferred wholesale to products designed to hold users as long as possible.

Three things the building side can do this week. First, actually test whether the crisis detection policy can be routed around by persona settings. Second, check whether retention copy is spoken by a character and remove it if so. Third, document now what happens to these conversations when the service closes. All three can be done without touching the model, and all three become hard to do later.

For the using side, one line suffices. Use it for company, do not expect care, and in a crisis go to a person.
