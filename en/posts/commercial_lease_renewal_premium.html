Commercial Tenant Renewal Rights and Goodwill Recovery in Korea

Ten years to stay, six months to cash out - the two clocks behind every Korean storefront lease

2026-08-17 Law

Introduction: the two numbers your lease does not mention

A Korean shop lease puts three numbers on paper: the deposit, the monthly rent, and a term of two or three years. The two numbers that actually decide a small business owner's fate appear nowhere in the document. They are ten years and six months.

Ten years is the total period a tenant can hold the premises by demanding renewal. Six months is the window the statute carves out before termination so the tenant can cash out the goodwill built into the location. The two clocks are linked. A tenant who has exhausted ten years has not thereby surrendered the goodwill claim, and a tenant with renewal years left can still find the goodwill clock ringing first if the landlord blocks an incoming tenant.

Telling someone who spent 80 million won on a fit-out and five years building regulars that the term is up is not the end of a contract. It is the destruction of an asset. Korea's Commercial Building Lease Protection Act does not prevent that outcome entirely, but it does supply two devices: time to stay and a chance to hand the business on. This article sets out where each device lives in the statute and where it stops working. Everything below concerns Korean law only.

1. The converted deposit - one statute, different reach for each shop

This is where most Korean commercial lease conversations go wrong. The owner next door says rent cannot go up more than five percent, and that turns out not to be true for your lease. You are not under different statutes. You are under different provisions of the same statute, and the fork is the converted deposit.

1.1 The formula is simple

Article 2 paragraph 2 requires that, where rent is payable, an amount converted from that rent by a ratio set by Presidential Decree be added to the deposit. Article 2 paragraph 3 of the Enforcement Decree fixes that ratio at 100.

Converted deposit = deposit + (monthly rent × 100). A deposit of 100 million won with rent of 3 million won per month gives 100 million + 300 million = 400 million won. Value added tax is generally treated as outside the rent figure, but check whether the contract states VAT separately.

1.2 Regional thresholds (Enforcement Decree Article 2 paragraph 1)

Thresholds differ by region and change when the Decree is amended. The figures below were raised by the amendment effective 2 April 2019 and remain in force as of August 2026. The third column shows how much deposit is possible at a rent of 3 million won per month.

1.3 What survives above the threshold and what does not

Article 2 paragraph 1 excludes leases above the threshold from the Act in principle. Paragraph 3 of the same article immediately carves an exception back out. Above the threshold, the following still apply.

  • Article 3 - opposing power. Delivery of the building plus filing for business registration gives effect against third parties from the following day.
  • Article 10 paragraphs 1 and 2 and the main text of paragraph 3 - the renewal request right, including the combined ten years.
  • Articles 10-2 through 10-9 - the special rule on renewal above the threshold, the definition of goodwill and its recovery protection, the exclusions, and termination for rent arrears.
  • Article 11-2 - the right to terminate on business closure.
  • Article 19 - preparation of the standard contract form.

Conversely, anything not listed does not apply. Article 11 on rent adjustment is absent, so the five percent ceiling in Article 4 of the Enforcement Decree does not operate above the threshold. Article 10-2 instead allows adjustment in light of taxes, public charges, surrounding market levels and changes in economic circumstances, with no numeric cap. Priority reimbursement, the lease registration order and minimum-deposit priority are also off the list.

Article 10 paragraph 4 is likewise absent, and that is easy to miss. Paragraph 4 is the implied renewal rule: if the landlord gives no notice of refusal or of changed terms before expiry, the lease is deemed renewed on the same terms with a one-year duration. Above the threshold, that year is not guaranteed.

2. The shape of the renewal right - when to ask, how long you can stay

The structure of Article 10 is the order of practice. Paragraph 1 covers the request and refusal, paragraph 2 the total period, paragraph 3 the terms after renewal, paragraphs 4 and 5 implied renewal and its termination.

2.1 The window - six months to one month before expiry

The tenant must request renewal between six months and one month before the term expires, and outside that window the right cannot be exercised at all. The residential deadline is two months, not one, so people who know both regimes are the ones most likely to confuse them. Use a channel that preserves the date: text message, email or certified content mail.

2.2 Ten years combined - no cap on the number of requests

Article 10 paragraph 2 permits the right to be exercised only within a total lease term, including the initial term, that does not exceed ten years. Two consequences follow.

  • There is no limit on how many times you ask. On a one-year lease you could ask nine times. What is capped is the aggregate period, not the count.
  • The initial term counts. If you signed for two years first, eight years of renewal remain, not ten.

The ten years arrived with Act No. 15791, effective 16 October 2018, replacing five years. The supplementary provisions of that amendment apply the revised paragraph 2 to leases concluded or renewed for the first time after entry into force. A lease that had already used up five years before that date does not get the ten retroactively; one renewed even once afterwards does. The answer comes from the original signing date and the renewal history.

2.3 Terms after renewal, and implied renewal

A renewed lease is deemed concluded on the same terms as before (paragraph 3, main text). Only rent and deposit may move, within the range set by Article 11. Below the threshold that range is the five percent in Article 4 of the Enforcement Decree, and under Article 11 paragraph 2 no further increase may be sought within one year of the contract or of an agreed increase.

If the landlord lets expiry pass in silence, paragraph 4 extends the lease by one year. The tenant may then terminate at any time under paragraph 5, effective three months after the landlord receives notice. Implied renewal runs two years for housing and one year for commercial premises.

3. The eight grounds for refusing renewal

The main text of Article 10 paragraph 1 says the landlord may not refuse without justifiable ground; the subparagraphs are the exception. Because this is a rule-and-exception structure, the landlord bears the burden of proving a subparagraph applies. When a refusal arrives, the first move is to ask which subparagraph it rests on.

Sub.GroundWhat the tenant should check
1The tenant has been in arrears of rent reaching an amount equivalent to three periods of rentCumulative, not consecutive. Housing uses two periods, not three
2The tenant leased by false or otherwise improper meansDemand specifics on what was allegedly misrepresented
3The parties agreed and the landlord provided reasonable compensationWas there agreement, was compensation actually paid, was it reasonable. A one-sided notice is not agreement
4The tenant subleased all or part without the landlord's consentConsent and its scope. Joint operation or temporary delegation is arguable

6All or part of the building has been destroyed so the purpose of the lease cannot be achievedIs trading genuinely impossible, or is repair feasible
7Recovery of possession is needed for demolition or reconstruction (a. the plan, including timing and duration, was specifically disclosed when the contract was made and is being followed; b. safety concerns from age, damage or partial destruction; c. demolition or reconstruction under other statutes)Check the contract and special terms for disclosure at signing. A plan assembled afterwards may fail item a
8The tenant has seriously breached tenant obligations or there is another grave reason making continuation difficultAbstract wording, easily overused. Demand concrete facts and evidence

The landlord's own occupancy is not on the list. Korean housing law has a ground for the landlord or lineal ascendants and descendants moving in; commercial law has none. A statement that the landlord intends to run a business there is not, by itself, an enumerated ground.

The connection matters more. Where a ground under Article 10 paragraph 1 exists, it does not merely permit refusal. Under the proviso to Article 10-4 paragraph 1, the goodwill protection duty never arises at all. A single episode of three-period arrears collapses both the renewal right and the goodwill claim.

The two Korean statutes share a shape but almost none of the numbers. Commercial means the Commercial Building Lease Protection Act; residential means the Housing Lease Protection Act.

The second row from the bottom is the important one. Goodwill protection exists only on the commercial side. A home is settled once the deposit comes back; a business is settled only when the intangible value accumulated at that address is handed to the next operator. That difference produced three new provisions in 2015.

5. Gwonrigeum - how goodwill entered the statute, and what protection means

5.1 13 May 2015, custom becomes text

Gwonrigeum, the premium paid for a going concern at a given address, had been part of Korean commercial dealings for decades without appearing in any statute. Because it moved privately between tenants, a landlord who said the money was none of his concern and refused the incoming tenant ended the matter. That gap was filled by Articles 10-3 through 10-5, inserted by Act No. 13284 on 13 May 2015.

Article 10-3 defines goodwill as consideration paid in addition to deposit and rent for the transfer or use of tangible and intangible property value, including business facilities and equipment, customer base, credit, business know-how and locational advantage. It also defines the goodwill contract between the tenant and a prospective new tenant. Having a definition means a courtroom no longer starts from arguing what goodwill even is. The amendment effective 16 October 2018 then widened the protected window from three months to six months before termination and removed traditional markets from the large-store exclusion, bringing market traders inside the protection.

5.2 The substance is a duty not to obstruct

The common misreading is worth naming. The statute does not order the landlord to pay goodwill. Goodwill still moves between the outgoing and incoming tenants. What the statute places on the landlord is a duty not to obstruct that transaction. Article 10-4 paragraph 1 prohibits four acts, from six months before the term ends until termination.

  • Sub. 1 - demanding or receiving goodwill from the prospective new tenant. The landlord taking the premium for himself.
  • Sub. 2 - causing the prospective new tenant not to pay goodwill to the tenant. Not taking it, just breaking the deal.
  • Sub. 3 - demanding rent and deposit that are conspicuously excessive in light of taxes, public charges, and the rent and deposit of nearby commercial buildings. Formally an offer of terms, functionally a wall.
  • Sub. 4 - refusing without justifiable ground to conclude a lease with the candidate the tenant introduced.

A proviso applies. Where any of the eight refusal grounds exists, this duty does not.

5.3 When you never got to introduce anyone

Read literally, the text assumes the tenant introduced a candidate. But a landlord who declares up front that no candidate will be accepted makes introduction pointless. The Supreme Court of Korea, in its decision of 4 July 2019 in case 2018Da284226, held that where a landlord has definitively expressed an intention not to conclude a lease with any candidate the tenant would introduce, without justifiable ground, damages may be awarded even though the tenant made no actual introduction. It also held that a goodwill contract need not have been concluded in advance.

That holding depends on the expression being definitive, and the same decision directs that this be judged from the words and conduct of both sides around termination and the surrounding circumstances. The practical lesson is short: capture the refusal in a form that survives, and where possible attempt an introduction anyway so a record exists.

5.4 The tenant carries a duty too

Article 10-4 paragraph 5 imposes a disclosure duty on the tenant: information the tenant knows about the candidate's ability to pay deposit and rent, and about the candidate's intention and capacity to perform tenant obligations, must be provided to the landlord. You cannot produce an unvetted candidate and then claim damages because the landlord declined. A written summary of the candidate's business plan, funding and trade discharges the duty and doubles as evidence.

6. When goodwill recovery is not protected

The goodwill provisions are strong but perforated. A claim collapses if it catches on any of three layers.

6.1 First layer - buildings the provisions never reach (Article 10-5)

  • Premises forming part of a large retail store or quasi-large retail store. These are concepts from the Distribution Industry Development Act, covering units inside hypermarkets and large malls. Traditional markets are excluded from the exclusion, so market units are protected.
  • State property or public property. Leases of buildings owned by the state or a local government fall outside the goodwill rules.

6.2 Second layer - a refusal ground exists against the tenant

This is the proviso to Article 10-4 paragraph 1. Three-period arrears, unauthorised subletting, intentional or grossly negligent damage - any ground under Article 10 paragraph 1 means the landlord's duty never arises. The dangerous detail is that arrears already repaid can still count, because subparagraph 1 speaks of arrears having reached that level.

6.3 Third layer - justifiable ground for refusing the new lease (Article 10-4 paragraph 2)

Paragraph 2 lists the justifiable grounds exhaustively, in four subparagraphs.

  • Sub. 1 - the candidate lacks the means to pay the deposit or rent.
  • Sub. 2 - there is a risk that the candidate will breach tenant obligations, or another substantial reason making the lease hard to maintain.
  • Sub. 3 - the building has not been used for profit-making purposes for one year and six months or more.
  • Sub. 4 - a new tenant chosen by the landlord concluded a goodwill contract with the tenant and paid that goodwill.

Subparagraph 3 is the one that appears most in practice, covering the landlord who says he will use the space himself or leave it empty for a while. The text is in the past tense, so merely announcing a plan does not automatically create the ground. That is why what actually happens to the space after termination is worth tracking, and why the three years in the next section matter.

6.4 What happens to a tenant past ten years

Does an exhausted renewal right take the goodwill with it? The regimes rest on different provisions, and Article 10-4 carries no ten-year limit. The Supreme Court of Korea decision of 26 November 2020 in case 2019Da249831 held that goodwill recovery remains protected even where the total lease term exceeds ten years so renewal can no longer be requested. That is not a guarantee of victory; it means the passage of ten years alone does not bar the claim.

7. Damages - how much, and by when

7.1 The cap is the lower figure

Article 10-4 paragraph 3 makes the landlord liable for loss caused by an obstructing act, and caps the award at the lower of the goodwill the new tenant agreed to pay the tenant and the goodwill as at termination of the lease.

If the goodwill contract says 120 million won but appraisal at termination gives 80 million, the ceiling is 80 million. If the contract says 60 million and appraisal gives 90 million, the ceiling is 60 million. The lower of the two is the roof, and the amount actually awarded sits somewhere beneath it.

Goodwill at termination is usually established by appraisal, and the appraisal criteria and standard goodwill contract published by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport serve as the working benchmarks. Litigation therefore adds appraisal cost and time, and appraised figures often land below expectations.

7.2 Three years

Article 10-4 paragraph 4 provides that the damages claim is extinguished by prescription if not exercised within three years from the date the lease terminated. The clock starts at termination, not at the date the tenant learned of the obstruction. Discovering later what happened to the premises does not help if three years have passed.

7.3 The record to build before you leave

  • The renewal request and the landlord's reply, in a dated form such as text, email or certified content mail.
  • Introduction and disclosure - the candidate's identity, means, business plan, and when it was sent to the landlord.
  • Any new terms the landlord proposed, with amounts and dates. This is the core evidence for the third obstruction type.
  • Comparable market data for nearby units, which sets the baseline for what counts as conspicuously excessive.
  • The goodwill contract, with the agreed figure and how it was calculated.
  • Trading records and post-termination use of the space - sales, supplier lists, fit-out spend, new signage or vacancy, all stored so the dates are visible.

8. Dispute routes - from conciliation to court

Korean commercial lease disputes rarely jump straight to litigation. Each stage differs in cost and speed, and the record built at one stage becomes the material for the next.

8.1 Certified content mail

Put the facts and the demand in writing first. Sending the renewal request itself by certified content mail removes any argument about the six-to-one-month window. Format and drafting are covered in Complete Guide to Naeyong-Jeungmyeong (Certified Mail) in Korea.

8.2 The Commercial Building Lease Dispute Conciliation Committee (Article 20)

Article 20 is the basis for the Commercial Building Lease Dispute Conciliation Committee. Committees are established at branches of the Korea Legal Aid Corporation, the Korea Land and Housing Corporation and the Korea Real Estate Board, and metropolitan and provincial governments may set up their own. They deliberate and conciliate disputes over rent and deposit adjustment, lease term, renewal and goodwill.

  • Cost and speed - cheaper and faster than court, and especially attractive where no appraisal is needed.
  • Limitation - the process struggles if the other side does not participate. It cannot compel attendance.
  • Residual value - even a failed conciliation puts the other side's position on paper, which is usable later.

8.3 Civil litigation

A goodwill damages claim ends up in civil court. Appraisal is often required, which stretches time and cost, and a common pattern is the landlord filing for possession first with the tenant counterclaiming for goodwill damages. Where cost is a barrier, the Korea Legal Aid Corporation offers free consultation on 132.

9. The same provisions read from the landlord's side

This is territory where tenant protection collides head-on with an owner's property rights. Read from the other direction, the provisions look like this.

  • Ten years is a constraint on disposal. For the owner, the use of the building is effectively fixed for a decade. Sale timing and any pivot to personal use become tied to the tenant's renewal cycle.
  • The goodwill rules constrain choice of counterparty. An owner used to picking tenants freely cannot, under Article 10-4, refuse an introduced candidate without justifiable ground.
  • Landlords have defences. The subparagraphs of Article 10 paragraph 1, the justifiable grounds in Article 10-4 paragraph 2, termination for three-period arrears under Article 10-8 and the exclusions in Article 10-5. These are not traps in the tenant's path; they were designed in from the start as balance.
  • Reconstruction plans are better disclosed at signing. That is why item a of subparagraph 7 requires specific disclosure when the contract is made. The owner reduces later litigation and the tenant can calculate a payback period.

Both sides gain at exactly one point: making the terms explicit in writing at signing. Reconstruction plans, permitted subletting, the scope of restoration and a procedure for cooperating on goodwill recovery, set out as special terms, visibly reduce disputes at expiry.

10. What is not settled here

  • Later amendments to the thresholds. The converted deposit figures above come from the Enforcement Decree amendment effective 2 April 2019 and were confirmed as of August 2026. Any change since must be checked against the effective date on the national statute portal.
  • Whether VAT enters the rent figure. How value added tax is treated in the converted deposit calculation can depend on contract wording, so no single answer is asserted here.
  • How the one year and six months rule is applied. The facts that establish subparagraph 3 of Article 10-4 paragraph 2 vary case by case. Relevant Supreme Court decisions exist, but their holdings are not restated here.
  • Committee locations and processing times. Which bodies host committees, in which regions, and how long an application takes all shift over time. Confirm with the relevant body before filing.
  • Regimes outside Korea. Everything above is Korean law. Commercial tenancy elsewhere rests on quite different foundations - England and Wales work through the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954 security-of-tenure regime with contracting out, France through the statutory bail commercial with its indemnity for eviction, and most United States jurisdictions leave renewal and any premium purely to the negotiated lease. Those systems are named only to mark the contrast; none of their detail is covered here.

Conclusion - putting both regimes on one calendar

A Korean commercial tenant has four dates to manage.

  1. Expiry. Everything else counts backwards from here.
  2. Six months before expiry. The renewal window opens and the goodwill protection window starts on the same day. Both clocks begin together.
  3. One month before expiry. The renewal window closes. A single day late and the right is gone.
  4. Three years from termination. The last day for a goodwill damages claim.

Two facts about your own contract complete the preparation. First, whether the converted deposit exceeds the regional threshold - above it, renewal and goodwill protection survive but the five percent cap and the one-year implied renewal do not. Second, the original signing date and renewal history, which decide whether the combined ten years applies at all.

The statute protects time and opportunity, not outcomes. The distance between a tenant who serves ten years and hands the goodwill on cleanly and a tenant who gets nothing under identical provisions usually comes down to whether the dates were kept and whether the record was made. The provisions are already in place.
