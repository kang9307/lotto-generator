1. Grok 4.7 Announcement and Core Positioning

Artificial intelligence research laboratory xAI officially introduced its latest large language model, Grok 4.7, through a technical release announcement on September 21, 2026. The new model succeeds the prior flagship release, Grok 4.6, delivering targeted architectural upgrades designed to expand autonomous capabilities across demanding software engineering and rigorous professional knowledge workflows.

In its official technical publication, xAI explicitly positioned Grok 4.7 as its 「most capable model for coding and knowledge work」. Rather than optimizing exclusively for ephemeral conversational queries or broad synthetic text generation, the research team focused development on multi-hour analytical operations. The model is specifically trained to sustain execution context across lengthy debugging procedures, broad architectural refactoring tasks, and complex domain evaluations that require autonomous intermediate verification.

Generally, generational advances in large language models coincide with expanded parameter footprints and corresponding price increases across hosted API endpoints. In this release, however, xAI maintained the exact baseline pricing schedule and standard token throughput rates established for Grok 4.6. This economic positioning provides commercial engineering teams with a direct upgrade route without introducing incremental operational costs per transaction.

The primary positioning points and architectural orientations confirmed in the release announcement are summarized as follows:

  • Official publication took place on September 21, 2026, establishing intensive coding and domain knowledge tasks as principal target use cases.
  • Development prioritized continuous reasoning across long-duration operational sessions alongside autonomous intermediate result checking.
  • Baseline inference pricing and generation speeds remain strictly equivalent to Grok 4.6, preserving stable economic parameters for enterprise production environments.

2. Coding and Software Engineering Benchmark Results

The practical engineering improvements incorporated into Grok 4.7 were substantiated through a comprehensive collection of software development benchmarks published by xAI. Prominently featured among these metrics is CursorBench 4.0, an industry benchmark designed to stress test code generation models on lengthy, multi-step implementation tasks executed within interactive developer development environments.

According to the official benchmark data, Grok 4.7 operating under its xHigh configuration attained a problem resolution score of 46.3% on CursorBench 4.0. This represents a 5.9 percentage point increase over the 40.4% score achieved by Grok 4.6 in its High configuration. In a cost-performance scatter plot presented by xAI, the model demonstrated an advantageous efficiency profile, achieving its 46.3% mark at an average task execution cost of approximately $6.01, establishing a competitive price-performance frontier.

Substantial performance gains were also documented on DeepSWE v1.1, a rigorous benchmark evaluating autonomous software issue resolution using real-world public repositories. Under high-effort reasoning conditions, Grok 4.7 achieved a resolution rate of 71.0%, improving upon the 65.2% mark recorded for Grok 4.6. Furthermore, on Terminal-Bench 4.0, which examines multi-hour command-line operations and procedural execution within terminal sessions, Grok 4.7 recorded 38.0%, nearly doubling the 20.3% score posted by Grok 4.6.

The primary software engineering benchmark figures confirmed in the primary publication include:

  • CursorBench 4.0: Grok 4.7 reached 46.3%, reflecting a 5.9 percentage point gain compared to 40.4% for Grok 4.6.
  • DeepSWE v1.1: Achieved a 71.0% resolution rate under high-effort parameters, compared to 65.2% recorded for the preceding model.
  • Terminal-Bench 4.0: Scored 38.0% in multi-step terminal execution, significantly improving over the 20.3% baseline established by Grok 4.6.

3. Professional Knowledge and Domain Benchmark Scores

Beyond specialized programming environments, the official evaluation suite for Grok 4.7 encompassed a wide range of specialized professional disciplines, including hardware design, legal analysis, clinical medical reasoning, and multi-hour business administration. These evaluations measure whether a frontier model can accurately parse domain-specific documentation, statutory texts, and technical schematics.

In electrical engineering evaluation via EEBench, which evaluates circuit design analysis and hardware problem-solving, Grok 4.7 registered a score of 64.0%. This reflects an 11.0 percentage point increase over the 53.0% recorded by Grok 4.6. This performance placed Grok 4.7 at the top of the comparison cohort detailed in xAI's publication, indicating refined mathematical precision and enhanced physical logic comprehension.

In legal document analysis, Grok 4.7 registered 19.6% on the Harvey Legal Agent Benchmark, surpassing the 15.8% mark of Grok 4.6. For prolonged corporate office tasks, the model achieved a score of 1,657 on AA Briefcase v1.1, improving upon the 1,546 points recorded for Grok 4.6. Additionally, the model demonstrated advancements on the GDPval metric across financial and legal simulated duties. In clinical healthcare evaluation, Grok 4.7 reached 56.7% on HealthBench Professional, exceeding the 48.5% result logged by the prior model version.

Confirmed benchmark results across specialized professional domains include the following verified metrics:

  • EEBench (Electrical Engineering): Grok 4.7 secured 64.0%, advancing by 11.0 percentage points from the 53.0% scored by Grok 4.6.
  • Harvey Legal Agent Benchmark: Achieved 19.6% in autonomous legal evaluation tasks, exceeding the 15.8% mark of its predecessor.
  • Office and Clinical Indicators: Recorded 1,657 points on AA Briefcase v1.1 and reached 56.7% on HealthBench Professional.

5. Architectural Enhancements and Grok Bot Harness Integration

In explaining the mechanisms behind these measurable score increases, xAI identified foundational model scaling and refined reinforcement learning methodologies as primary technical drivers. While specific parameter totals were withheld, the release text confirmed the integration of a 「new, larger base model compared to Grok 4.6」.

Beyond expanding the underlying parameter scale, post-training procedures were modified. xAI executed extended reinforcement learning training runs over challenging, heterogeneous task sets weighted heavily toward problems requiring multiple hours of sustained compute to resolve. This targeted reinforcement learning was implemented to ensure the model actively verifies its intermediate outputs (verifying its own work) and effectively retains context over prolonged token windows (managing longer context).

Furthermore, Grok 4.7 received specialized pre-training to natively interface with the proprietary Grok Bot harness. This native harness integration enables the model to operate smoothly within conversational agent frameworks and programmatic tool-calling workflows, mitigating interface misalignment and improving operational stability across general knowledge tasks.

The primary architectural modifications highlighted in the official announcement include:

  • Base Model Architecture: Upgraded to a substantially expanded foundational base model relative to Grok 4.6.
  • Extended Reinforcement Learning: Focused post-training runs on multi-hour analytical problems to improve autonomous self-checking.
  • Harness Integration: Built-in native compatibility with the Grok Bot harness to enhance conversational tool orchestration.

6. Safety Safeguards Stack and Cybersecurity Assessment

Model safety engineering and defensive cybersecurity mechanisms formed a prominent section of the Grok 4.7 release documentation. xAI detailed the implementation of an entirely redesigned safeguard stack aimed at preventing malicious exploitation while avoiding excessive false refusals during legitimate security research activities.

Regarding overarching alignment and adversarial resistance, xAI stated that Grok 4.7 represents the 「strongest model we've tested on refusals and jailbreak resistance」. Readers should note that this specific claim reflects internal testing conducted by xAI engineers rather than an audit executed by an independent third-party certification organization.

In addressing dual-use cyber risks, xAI highlighted results from HackerBench v0.3, an internal evaluation suite focused on hazardous cyber scenarios. In this benchmark, Grok 4.7 permitted only 3.3% of dangerous dual-use prompts to bypass safeguards, while maintaining access for legitimate security inspection tasks. Additionally, the model reached 62.4% on LatchBio's biosafety benchmark. To support defensive research, xAI has initiated invite-only access to Grok 4.7's red-teaming capabilities for vetted cybersecurity partner organizations.

Verified factual elements regarding safety and cybersecurity include:

  • Internal Testing Assessment: xAI designated the model as its most robust internally tested system regarding refusal handling and jailbreak resistance.
  • Defensive Benchmarking: HackerBench v0.3 testing demonstrated a 3.3% allowance rate for high-risk cyber prompts.
  • Defensive Partnerships: Select institutional security partners received private, invite-only access to red-teaming tools.

7. Pricing Structure, Variants, and Deployment Options

A notable commercial aspect of the Grok 4.7 release is xAI's decision to freeze baseline inference pricing. Despite utilizing an expanded base architecture and extended post-training runs, the standard token tariff matches that of the preceding Grok 4.6 model.

The standard baseline tariff is set at $2.00 per million input tokens and $6.00 per million output tokens. For latency-critical enterprise workloads requiring accelerated throughput, xAI introduced an optional fast variant. This high-speed variant delivers twice the output token generation rate at exactly twice the baseline price, charging $4.00 per million input tokens and $12.00 per million output tokens.

8. What Could Not Be Verified

This technical report is strictly constructed upon verified statements and factual data published in primary documentation by xAI. To maintain rigorous technical accuracy and eliminate unverified assumptions, the following parameters remain unconfirmed due to an absence of public disclosure in the release documentation:

  • Total Parameter Count and Active Weights: While xAI described a larger base model compared to Grok 4.6, specific parameter counts, mixture-of-experts routing structures, and layer dimensions were not disclosed.
  • Reinforcement Learning Compute Budgets and Infrastructure: Quantitative metrics regarding total training FLOPs, GPU cluster allocation, training duration, and power consumption were omitted from the official announcement.
  • Independent External Safety Audits: Claims regarding jailbreak resistance and HackerBench v0.3 results reflect internal testing, with no independent third-party verification report released alongside the announcement.
