Complete Guide to Blocking Smartphone Location Tracking

Settings, App Permissions, Ad ID, Location History & Stalkerware — Protecting Your Own Privacy

2026-06-28 Tech & IT

Introduction: More Than Just GPS

Simply carrying a smartphone means your movements are being recorded — but not only through GPS. Location tracking happens across multiple channels simultaneously. This guide explains each tracking pathway and walks through step-by-step blocking on both iOS and Android. Settings reflect the state of both platforms as of June 2026, and the focus is exclusively on lawful self-privacy protection.

Note: This guide is intended solely for protecting your own device and personal information. Using these techniques to evade lawful oversight or to assist others in surveillance evasion may violate applicable laws.

1. How Your Phone Knows Where You Are

There are four main channels through which smartphones determine and share location.

1.1 GPS (Satellite Navigation)

GPS receives signals from satellites to determine your precise coordinates — accurate to within a few meters in open spaces, but weaker indoors. Turning GPS off eliminates this channel, but the others remain active.

1.2 Wi-Fi Signal Triangulation

Your phone compares nearby Wi-Fi router MAC addresses and signal strengths against a database to estimate your position — accuracy in the range of tens to hundreds of meters, and it works well indoors. Critically, Wi-Fi scanning can track you even when you are not connected to any network, as long as the scan feature is enabled.

1.3 Cell Tower (Cellular) Positioning

Mobile carriers know which cell tower your device is connected to, allowing them to estimate your location to within a city block or several kilometers. As long as a SIM card is inserted, the carrier retains this information. In early 2026, Apple introduced a Limit Precise Location feature that reduces the granularity of the location data available to carriers.

1.4 App and Ad Tracking

Apps with location permission use GPS, Wi-Fi, and cell tower data to collect your position. Advertising networks use advertising identifiers — IDFA on iOS, GAID on Android — to link behavior and movement patterns across multiple apps and websites and serve targeted ads. Even without explicit location permission, some ad networks infer mobility patterns from behavioral data combined with the ad ID.

2. Managing Location Permissions on iOS

The central control point on iPhone is Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services.

2.1 Per-App Location Permission

Tap any app in the Location Services list to set its access level. Three options are available.

  • Never: The app cannot access your location at all. Use this for apps that have no legitimate need for it.
  • While Using the App: Location is accessible only while the app is in the foreground. Appropriate for delivery, navigation, and weather apps.
  • Always: The app can collect location in the background. Reserve this for apps with a genuine background need, such as theft-tracking apps.
Practical tip: Audit your installed apps and change anything set to "Always" to "While Using" or "Never." Very few apps genuinely need background location access.

2.2 Disabling Precise Location Per App

Since iOS 14, you can toggle Precise Location on or off for each app individually. With precise location off, the app receives only your approximate neighborhood rather than your exact coordinates. Turn it off for weather, news, and food delivery apps that do not need a street-level address.

2.3 App Tracking Transparency (ATT)

Since iOS 14.5, apps must request permission before tracking your activity across other companies' apps and websites. Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking and turn off "Allow Apps to Request to Track." This automatically denies all tracking requests system-wide.

2.4 Limit Precise Location from Carriers (New in 2026)

Apple's new feature, introduced in early 2026, reduces the precision of location data available to cellular networks. Enable it at Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data Options → Limit Precise Location. With this on, carriers can determine only a rough neighborhood rather than a street address. Note that this setting is independent of app Location Services.

2.5 System Services Location Controls

At the bottom of Location Services, tap System Services to control Apple's internal location uses. It is worth disabling Significant Locations (which records frequently visited places), Location-Based Apple Ads, and Location-Based Suggestions to reduce the amount of location data Apple collects.

3. Managing Location Permissions on Android

Menu paths vary slightly by manufacturer, but the core structure is consistent. Find location controls at Settings → Location.

3.1 Per-App Permission Control

Go to Settings → Location → App permissions to adjust each app's access. Options mirror iOS.

  • Allow all the time: Background access permitted. Minimize this.
  • Allow only while using the app: Foreground access only. Right choice for most apps.
  • Ask every time: Prompts for permission each session.
  • Don't allow: Location access blocked entirely.

3.2 Precise vs. Approximate Location

Since Android 12, you can independently choose Precise or Approximate location when granting access to an app. Precise location delivers GPS-level accuracy (within meters); approximate location is accurate only to a few kilometers. Grant approximate location to apps that need a general area but not your exact address.

3.3 Android 17 Location Transparency (2026)

Android 17, released in 2026, brings location privacy up to the same standard as camera and microphone access. A persistent indicator in the status bar now appears whenever a non-system app accesses your location. The permission dialog was also redesigned to make the Precise vs. Approximate choice visually clearer.

3.4 Disabling Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Scanning

Go to Settings → Location → Location services (sometimes labeled "Improve Location Accuracy") and disable Wi-Fi scanning and Bluetooth scanning. These features scan for nearby signals to improve location accuracy even when Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are turned off. Disabling them reduces passive tracking at the cost of slightly lower indoor location accuracy.

3.5 Turning Off Google Location History

If your device is linked to a Google account, Google Maps Timeline may be recording your movements. To turn it off: open Google Maps → tap your profile photo → Your data in Maps → Location History → Turn off. You can also delete existing history from the same menu. Consider reviewing Web & App Activity in your Google Account settings as well, since it may retain some location data.

4. Resetting and Deleting Your Advertising ID

Advertising identifiers are unique numbers that let ad networks profile your behavior and movement across services. Resetting cuts the link to your existing profile.

4.1 iOS — Reset Advertising Identifier

Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Advertising to turn off personalized ads. To reset the identifier itself, navigate to Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Reset → Reset Advertising Identifier. With ATT tracking disabled system-wide, apps cannot read your IDFA regardless, making this step most impactful when combined with the ATT setting.

4.2 Android — Delete Advertising ID

Go to Settings → Google → All services → Ads to reset or delete your advertising ID. On Android 14 and later, the Delete advertising ID option causes apps to receive a string of zeros instead of a real identifier, making cross-app tracking essentially impossible.

5. Deleting Location History

Past location data stored by services should also be cleaned up.

5.1 Google Timeline

Visit myaccount.google.com → Data & privacy → Location History to turn off the feature and delete existing data. Set an auto-delete period of 3 or 18 months to ensure old data is removed automatically going forward.

5.2 Apple Significant Locations

iPhone stores frequently visited places locally as Significant Locations. Find them at Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → System Services → Significant Locations. You can review the stored data, clear it, or turn the feature off entirely. The data is encrypted and stored only on-device, but disabling the feature prevents future collection.

6. Checking for Stalkerware

Stalkerware refers to apps secretly installed to monitor location, calls, and messages — often in contexts of domestic abuse, workplace surveillance, or unauthorized parental monitoring. Recognizing it is the first step to removing it.

6.1 Detection on Android

  • Go to Settings → Apps → See all apps and look for unfamiliar or vaguely named apps.
  • Check Battery usage and Data usage for apps consuming unusually high resources.
  • Review Accessibility services and Device admin apps for unknown entries.
  • Run a Google Play Protect scan.
Reference: The Electronic Frontier Foundation's Mobile Phones: Location Tracking guide also provides advice for people who suspect they are being monitored.

6.2 Detection on iOS

The iOS architecture makes third-party stalkerware installation very difficult on non-jailbroken devices. However, location may be shared without your knowledge through Apple ID family sharing or Find My. Check Settings → [Your Name] → Find My and Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services for any unexpected sharing. Jailbroken devices are an exception and are much more vulnerable.

6.3 When in Doubt: Factory Reset

If you suspect stalkerware but cannot identify the specific app, a factory reset is the most reliable removal method. Back up photos and contacts separately beforehand, and be cautious about restoring from a cloud backup that may include the offending app.

7. Family Location Sharing — Both Sides

Family location sharing serves legitimate safety purposes but raises valid privacy concerns, especially as children grow older.

7.1 Use Official, Transparent Tools

Apple Family Sharing and Google Family Link are designed to be visible to all parties — the person being located can see that sharing is active. This transparency distinguishes consensual sharing from covert surveillance, which in many jurisdictions is illegal regardless of the relationship.

7.2 Adjust as Children Grow

It is good practice to scale back location access as children mature and to have open conversations about what is shared and why. Shifting from location monitoring to open communication builds longer-lasting trust.

8. Additional Privacy Measures

  • Randomized Wi-Fi MAC address: Modern iOS and Android randomize the MAC address used in Wi-Fi probe requests by default. iOS 18 and later rotate the device MAC address approximately every two weeks, reducing passive tracking by commercial Wi-Fi analytics systems.
  • Use a trusted VPN: A VPN makes IP-based location inference harder, but choose a provider with a verified no-logs policy — the VPN itself can see your traffic.
  • Delete unused apps: No app means no permissions. Uninstall apps you no longer use.
  • Airplane mode when offline: Airplane mode cuts GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and cellular simultaneously — the strongest option, but also cuts all connectivity.
  • Browser location permissions: Websites can also request your location through the browser. Set browser location to "Ask" or "Block" in your browser settings.

FAQ

Does turning off GPS completely stop location tracking?

No. Wi-Fi triangulation, Bluetooth beacons, and cell towers can still locate you with GPS off. Disable Location Services entirely and turn off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scanning for significantly stronger protection.

Is "While Using the App" permission safe enough?

Much safer than "Always" — it blocks background collection. For apps you do not trust at all, "Never" is the safest option.

What does resetting my advertising ID actually do?

It breaks the link between your device and the behavioral and location profile ad networks have built. On Android 14+, deleting the ID entirely is even more effective.

Conclusion: Priority Action List

If you can only do a few things, start here, in order of impact.

  1. Audit all app location permissions — minimize "Always Allow," increase "Never"
  2. Enable ATT (iOS) or delete advertising ID (Android)
  3. Disable Wi-Fi and Bluetooth scanning (Android)
  4. Turn off Google Timeline and Apple Significant Locations; delete existing history
  5. Review iOS System Services location items
  6. Audit installed apps — remove anything unfamiliar

Location privacy is not a one-time setup. Review app permissions after each new install, and check settings periodically after OS updates, as upgrades can reset certain choices.
