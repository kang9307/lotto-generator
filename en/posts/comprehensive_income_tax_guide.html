Introduction: Who Needs to File Korea's Comprehensive Income Tax?

Every May, millions of Koreans receive notices about Jonghap Sogeukse (종합소득세) — Korea's comprehensive income tax. Unlike salary earners whose taxes are handled through year-end settlements (yeomaljeongsang), freelancers, self-employed individuals, landlords, and those with multiple income sources must actively file their own tax returns.

This guide is based on the Korean National Tax Service (NTS) official materials and covers the essentials for the 2025 tax year (income earned January–December 2025), filed in 2026. Whether you are a foreign resident in Korea, an expat with Korean-source income, or simply want to understand Korea's tax system, this guide provides a clear overview.

Note: All information is based on publicly available NTS guidelines as of June 2026. Tax law is subject to change. Please consult official sources or a tax professional before filing.

1. What Is Comprehensive Income Tax? Types of Income

Korea's comprehensive income tax aggregates most categories of personal income for a single annual return. Under the Income Tax Act, the following income types are generally included in the comprehensive income calculation:

1.1 Income Included in Comprehensive Taxation

  • Interest income: Bank deposit interest, bond interest, etc. Subject to comprehensive taxation if combined with dividend income exceeds KRW 20 million/year
  • Dividend income: Dividends from stocks, funds, etc. Combined with interest income above KRW 20 million triggers comprehensive taxation
  • Business income: Self-employed individuals, freelancers (3.3% withheld), real estate rental income. Always subject to comprehensive taxation
  • Employment income: Wages and salaries. Generally settled through year-end settlement. Must file separately if: working for 2+ employers without combining settlement, or having other taxable income alongside salary
  • Pension income: Public pensions (National Pension, public sector pensions) — exempt if year-end settled. Private pensions exceeding KRW 12 million/year are subject to comprehensive or separated taxation (taxpayer's choice)
  • Other income: Lecture fees, royalties, prizes, consulting fees exceeding KRW 3 million/year are generally subject to comprehensive taxation (separated taxation option available)

1.2 Income Filed Separately (Not Included)

  • Retirement income: Taxed separately under retirement income tax rules
  • Capital gains: Taxed separately under capital gains tax rules
  • Financial income under KRW 20 million: Withholding tax at source is final — no further filing needed

2. Filing Deadlines and Payment

Timely filing is critical. Missing the deadline triggers penalty taxes that can significantly increase your total tax burden.

2.1 General Deadline

  • Filing period: May 1–31, 2026 (for 2025 tax year)
  • Sincere Report Confirmation (성실신고확인서) filers: Extended to June 30, 2026
  • Payment: Due by the filing deadline. Installment payment is available for amounts exceeding KRW 10 million

2.2 Installment Payment (Bunap)

If your total tax liability exceeds KRW 10 million, you may pay up to 50% of the amount within 2 months after the filing deadline. This reduces the immediate cash burden while avoiding a late payment penalty on the deferred portion.

2.3 Late (Post-Deadline) Filing

If you miss the May 31 deadline, file as soon as possible via Hometax's 'Late Filing' option. The sooner you file after the deadline, the lower the failure-to-file penalty reduction. Filing within 1 month of the deadline qualifies for a 50% penalty reduction.

3.1 Calculation Example

If your taxable income is KRW 30 million: 30,000,000 × 15% − 1,260,000 = KRW 3,240,000 in calculated tax. From this, subtract applicable tax credits and previously withheld taxes to arrive at your final tax due or refund.

4. Major Deductions

Deductions are split into income deductions (reducing taxable income) and tax credits (reducing the final tax amount directly).

4.1 Key Income Deductions

  • Basic personal deduction: KRW 1.5 million for the taxpayer. An additional KRW 1.5 million per qualifying dependent (spouse, children, parents, etc.)
  • National Pension contributions: Full amount paid is deductible
  • Health and employment insurance premiums: Deductible for employment income earners
  • Small business owner deduction (노란우산공제): Contributions up to KRW 2–5 million annually for sole proprietors enrolled in the KOSMES mutual aid scheme
  • Overall deduction ceiling: KRW 25 million combined limit on certain special deductions

4.2 Key Tax Credits

  • Standard tax credit: KRW 130,000 for salary earners; KRW 70,000 for business income earners
  • Child tax credit: KRW 150,000 for one child; KRW 350,000 for two children; additional amounts for three or more
  • Education expense credit: 15% of qualified education expenses
  • Medical expense credit: 15–30% of qualified medical costs exceeding 3% of total income
  • Donation credit: 15–30% of qualified donations
  • Electronic filing credit: KRW 20,000 for filing electronically via Hometax

5. Business Expense Deductions for Self-Employed and Freelancers

For business income, taxable income = gross revenue minus necessary expenses (필요경비). There are two methods:

5.1 Bookkeeping Method (Gijang)

Maintaining formal accounting records (double-entry or simplified bookkeeping) allows you to deduct all actual business expenses — rent, labor costs, materials, marketing, equipment, etc. This is generally more advantageous when actual expenses are high. Failing to keep required books incurs a 20% non-bookkeeping penalty tax.

5.2 Standard Expense Rate Method (Chujesin)

For smaller businesses without formal books, the NTS allows a predetermined expense ratio by industry type:

  • Simple expense rate (단순경비율): Applied to small operators below the threshold. A fixed percentage of revenue is treated as expenses
  • Standard expense rate (기준경비율): Key costs (purchases, rent, payroll) must be documented; remaining expenses use the NTS rate

5.3 Pre-Filled Filing (Modu Chaeum)

The NTS pre-populates income data for many freelancers with 3.3% withheld income. If you receive a pre-filled return notice, review the data and submit — adding any missing deductions manually. This is the simplest option for most solo freelancers.

6. How to File on Hometax (Step by Step)

Electronic filing via Hometax (www.hometax.go.kr) is the most common method. In-person filing at a local tax office is also available.

6.1 Hometax Filing Steps

  1. Log in to Hometax: Use a Public Certificate, Kakao/Naver/PASS simple authentication, or a Financial Certificate
  2. Navigate to filing: Click 'Tax Filing (세금신고) → Comprehensive Income Tax (종합소득세 신고)'
  3. Select filing type:
    • Pre-filled / Simple Expense Rate (모두채움/단순경비율): for small-scale filers with pre-populated data
    • Bookkeeping return (기장신고): for those with formal books
    • Financial income return: when financial income exceeds KRW 20 million
  4. Enter income and deductions: Fill in revenue, expenses, income deductions, and tax credits. The system auto-calculates
  5. Submit and pay: Review the tax amount, submit, and pay via virtual account, internet banking, or card
  6. File local income tax: After national tax filing, file local income tax (10% of income tax) on Wetax (wetax.go.kr), which links automatically

6.2 Mobile Filing (Sontax App)

Simple filings (pre-filled returns) can be completed on the Sontax (손택스) mobile app. Complex returns (bookkeeping, financial income) are best handled on a PC.

7.2 Voluntary Late Filing Reduction

Filing voluntarily after the deadline — but before receiving a notice from the NTS — qualifies for penalty reductions. Within 1 month: 50% reduction. Within 3 months: 30%. Within 6 months: 20%. Within 1 year: 10%.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Do freelancers with 3.3% withholding still need to file?

Yes. The 3.3% withholding is not the final tax — it is an advance payment. Your actual tax liability depends on your total income and deductions. Depending on your expenses and deductions, you may receive a refund of part of the 3.3% withheld. Filing is mandatory.

Q2. I have a side job in addition to my salaried position. Do I need to file?

If your side income is business income (any amount) or other income exceeding KRW 3 million, you must file a comprehensive income tax return combining your salary and side income. If the side income is 'other income' below KRW 3 million, you may opt for separate taxation and may not need to file.

Q3. How does local income tax (지방소득세) work?

Local income tax is 10% of your final national income tax amount. It is filed separately on Wetax (wetax.go.kr) but linked automatically after you complete your Hometax national return. Do not forget this step — it is a separate obligation.

Q4. Can I get tax help without hiring a tax accountant?

For pre-filled returns and simple cases, Hometax provides enough guidance for self-filing. The NTS also operates free tax consulting services (세무상담) during the filing season. For complex situations — multiple income sources, significant business expenses, rental properties — engaging a licensed tax accountant (세무사) is worth the cost.

Q5. What if I cannot pay the full tax amount by the deadline?

File on time regardless of your ability to pay. This avoids the failure-to-file penalty. You can then apply for installment payment (bunap) or a payment extension / collection deferral (징수유예) through the NTS. Penalties are significantly lower when you file on time but pay late, compared to not filing at all.

9. Summary: Filing Right the First Time

Korea's comprehensive income tax system is nuanced, but the core principles are straightforward:

  • Know if you must file: Business income, side income, and high financial income almost always trigger a filing obligation
  • File by May 31: Or June 30 if you are a Sincere Report filer. Late filing is costly
  • Maximize legitimate deductions: Personal exemptions, insurance premiums, education costs, medical expenses, and donations all reduce your tax burden
  • Use Hometax or consult a professional: Pre-filled returns are easy; complex situations benefit from expert guidance

Tax law evolves every year. The information in this guide reflects publicly available NTS guidance as of June 2026. Always confirm the latest rules before filing.
