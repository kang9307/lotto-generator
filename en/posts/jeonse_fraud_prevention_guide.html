What Is Jeonse and Why Does Fraud Occur?

Jeonse (전세) is a uniquely Korean housing lease system in which a tenant pays a large lump-sum deposit — typically 50 to 80 percent of the property's market value — to the landlord and lives in the property rent-free for the lease term (usually two years). At the end of the term, the full deposit is returned.

This system works as long as the landlord can return the deposit. When property values fall sharply, when landlords use tenant deposits to buy multiple properties with no real equity (a practice called gaptuja or gap investing), or when fraudsters deliberately set up fake contracts, tenants can lose their entire deposit with little immediate recourse. Korea saw a wave of jeonse fraud cases in the early-to-mid 2020s that resulted in the enactment of the Jeonse Fraud Special Act in 2023 and a series of expanded tenant protection measures now in effect as of 2026.

This guide explains how the jeonse fraud risk works, what to check before signing, and what legal tools are available if you become a victim. Foreign nationals renting in Korea through the jeonse system are also covered.

1. Major Types of Jeonse Fraud

1.1 Kkangtongjeonse (Underwater Jeonse)

This is the most common structural risk. Kkangtongjeonse (깡통전세) occurs when the property's market value falls to or below the jeonse deposit amount. The term literally means "tin-can jeonse" — an empty shell. If the property is sold, the proceeds are not enough to return the deposit. A jeonse-to-value ratio above 80 percent is widely treated as a danger signal.

1.2 Zero-Equity Gap Investing

Fraudsters or highly leveraged landlords buy multiple properties using the tenant's deposit as the primary funding source, with little or no personal equity. When property prices drop, they have no assets to return deposits. This type of fraud often involves dozens of properties and many victims simultaneously.

1.3 Double Contracts

The landlord — or someone impersonating the landlord — signs jeonse contracts with multiple tenants for the same property, collecting deposits from all of them. Since only one person can actually occupy the property, the others receive nothing. This type of fraud tends to create many victims at once.

1.4 Trust Property Fraud

Some properties are legally owned by a trust company (신탁회사) rather than the individual listed as landlord. Under Korean trust law, the trust company must consent to any lease agreement. Landlords who sign jeonse contracts without trust company consent may render the lease legally unenforceable against the trust company, leaving the tenant unprotected when the trust is exercised.

1.5 Tenant Rights Timing Gap Abuse

Under the Housing Lease Protection Act, a tenant's right of opposability (대항력) does not arise until the day after tenant registration (전입신고). A fraudulent landlord may accept the deposit on the contract date and immediately register a mortgage or transfer ownership. Because the mortgage or transfer can take effect on the same day while the tenant's rights begin the following day, the tenant's claim becomes subordinate to the newly registered right.

1.6 Impersonation and Power of Attorney Fraud

Fraudsters impersonate the registered owner using forged identification, forged powers of attorney, or stolen seal certificates. Even contracts brokered through a real estate agent are not automatically safe if the agent does not cross-check the actual owner's identity.

2. Prevention Checklist Before Signing

2.1 Check the Property Registry Three Times

The property registration certificate (등기사항전부증명서 / 등기부등본) is the single most important document to verify. Check it at least three times: before signing the contract, on the day of signing, and on the day of balance payment. It is available at the Korean Court Internet Registry Office (iros.go.kr) for approximately 700 KRW.

What to check:

  • Section A (갑구): Registered owner name matches the person you are contracting with; no attachment, seizure, or auction commencement notice
  • Section B (을구): Any mortgage (근저당권), right of lease (전세권), or trust registration; the total of the mortgage secured amount plus your jeonse deposit
  • Any trust registration: if present, demand a written consent letter from the trust company
If the sum of the mortgage secured amount and your jeonse deposit exceeds 70-80% of the current market value, the risk of not recovering your deposit in foreclosure is high. Do not sign without resolving this.

2.2 Verify the Landlord's Identity Directly

Compare the landlord's national ID card or alien registration card against the name and ID number on the property registry. If a proxy is signing on the landlord's behalf, demand a notarized power of attorney and an original seal certificate (인감증명서). Call the landlord directly to confirm the arrangement.

2.3 Check the Jeonse-to-Value Ratio

Use the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport actual transaction price system (rt.molit.go.kr) or the HUG Ansimjeonse App to compare your deposit against recent sale prices in the area. A ratio above 80 percent is a red-flag zone.

2.4 Check the Building Register and Tax Payment Status

Download the building register (건축물대장) from Government24 (gov.kr) to check for unauthorized construction. Ask the landlord to provide a tax clearance certificate (납세증명서) confirming no unpaid national or local taxes. Unpaid taxes create a government tax lien that typically has priority over tenant deposits.

3. Mandatory Steps After Signing: Tenant Registration and Confirmed Date

3.1 Tenant Registration (전입신고)

Complete tenant registration at a local community service center (주민센터) or online at Government24 on the same day you move in after paying the balance. Your right of opposability (대항력) — the ability to assert your tenancy rights against a new owner or creditor — arises at midnight (00:00) the day after tenant registration. Any mortgage or ownership transfer registered between the payment of your deposit and the day after registration takes priority over your deposit claim.

3.2 Confirmed Date Stamp (확정일자)

Have your lease contract stamped with a confirmed date (확정일자) at a community service center, registry office, or online at Government24. This establishes your priority repayment right (우선변제권), which determines your position in the repayment queue if the property goes to foreclosure. The confirmed date must be obtained as early as possible — ideally on the same day you complete tenant registration.

Complete tenant registration and confirmed date stamp on the same day you pay the balance and move in. Delaying either step creates a window during which a new lien or ownership transfer could outrank your deposit claim.

3.3 Maintain Actual Residence

Your right of opposability is maintained only while you are actually living at the registered address. If you move out or change your registered address, those rights are extinguished. Be especially careful if you travel for extended periods during the lease term.

4. Jeonse Deposit Guarantee Insurance: HUG, HF, and SGI

Jeonse deposit guarantee insurance is the most effective financial protection available. When the landlord fails to return the deposit, the guarantee institution pays the tenant and then seeks recovery from the landlord.

5. If Fraud Occurs: Tenancy Registration Order

5.1 What Is a Tenancy Registration Order (임차권등기명령)?

When a lease expires and the landlord refuses to return the deposit, the tenant can petition the court to record their tenancy right on the property registry through a Tenancy Registration Order (임차권등기명령). Once the court grants the order and the registration is completed, the tenant's right of opposability and priority repayment right are preserved even after the tenant moves out of the property.

5.2 Application Procedure

  1. Gather required documents: original lease contract, property registry, resident registration transcript confirming move-in date, and confirmed date certificate.
  2. File the application at the district court (지방법원) with jurisdiction over the property location. Online filing is available at the Korean Court e-Filing System (efiling.scourt.go.kr).
  3. Pay the required fees: approximately 1,800 KRW in stamp duty and 31,200 KRW in service fees.
  4. After the court issues the order, the registry office completes the registration. Verify completion by re-downloading the property registry.
Do not move out before the tenancy registration is complete. Moving out before registration causes your right of opposability to lapse immediately. If you must move before registration, consult the Korea Legal Aid Corporation (KLAC, phone 132) first.

6. Jeonse Fraud Special Act and Victim Support

6.1 The Special Act

The Act on Support for Jeonse Fraud Victims and Housing Stability (전세사기피해자 지원 및 주거안정에 관한 특별법) was enacted in June 2023. As of 2026, the deadline for victim recognition applications and support eligibility has been extended to May 31, 2027. The Act applies to tenants whose first lease contract was signed on or before May 31, 2025.

6.2 How to Apply for Victim Recognition

Apply through your local district or city office (시·군·구청) or online through the Jeonse Fraud Victim Support Management System (jeonse.kgeop.go.kr). Additional information is available at the HUG Jeonse Fraud Prevention Center (khug.or.kr/jeonse).

6.3 What Victim Recognition Provides

  • Priority purchase right at auction: Victims may purchase their property at the auction price before other bidders.
  • Low-interest loan conversion: Assistance converting existing loans into low-interest housing fund loans.
  • Public rental housing access: Priority placement in public rental housing.
  • Free legal support: The Korea Legal Aid Corporation (KLAC) provides free civil litigation, including deposit refund lawsuits. Judicial scriveners (법무사) are available at reduced fees for auction and registration procedures.
  • Psychological and welfare support: Counseling and welfare referral services for victims.

6.4 Note for Foreign Tenants

Foreign nationals who have completed alien registration and have their registered address at the leased property are generally protected under the Housing Lease Protection Act. Foreign victims may also apply for recognition under the Special Act and access KLAC legal aid. Language interpretation assistance may be available. Contact KLAC at 132 or visit their website at klac.or.kr for current guidance. This is general information and not legal advice.

7. Emergency Response Steps After Fraud

  1. Preserve all evidence: Keep the original lease contract, all bank transfer receipts, kakao or text message histories, and every version of the property registry you downloaded.
  2. Do not move out yet: Leaving before a tenancy registration order is complete extinguishes your right of opposability.
  3. File a Tenancy Registration Order: Apply immediately at the district court or via e-Filing.
  4. Contact KLAC for free legal aid: Korea Legal Aid Corporation, phone 132, klac.or.kr.
  5. Apply for Jeonse Fraud victim recognition: Through your local district office or jeonse.kgeop.go.kr.
  6. Report to police: If criminal fraud is suspected, file a complaint with the police (dial 112 or visit a local police station).
  7. Claim from HUG if insured: If you have jeonse deposit guarantee insurance, file your claim with HUG or the relevant insurer promptly.
