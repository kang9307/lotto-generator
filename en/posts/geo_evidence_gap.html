Introduction: standing in front of the checklist

A list arrives telling you what to do so that AI systems will cite your site. It runs to about a dozen lines, and every line comes with a plausible reason attached. The trouble is that those dozen lines are not the same kind of thing at all. Some of them describe behaviour the reading side has publicly committed to in documentation. Others have never been properly checked by anyone. The list does not mark the difference.

Marking that difference yourself is what this article is about. It is not a verdict on which prescriptions are right; it is a way of asking what has to be observed before the sentence it works can stand. Hold that criterion and you stop having to reason from scratch every time a new item appears.

First, what this article does not say. It does not say any proposal is worthless. Not being adopted and being useless are different things, and a file the reading side ignores today may be read next year. Nor does it say anything works. The whole argument here is that the evidence is thin, and manufacturing a confident claim in the opposite direction to make that argument would be the same mistake in a mirror.

The concepts and practical elements of AI search optimisation are already covered on this site. What to measure and where to be careful is in GEO in the generative search era, the concrete method of measuring citation rates against a control group is in measuring GEO with a control group, and the items you can act on today are in seven strategies for the AI Overviews era. This piece sits one floor above them. Where those articles handle what to do and how to measure it, this one handles what the measurement proves.

1. The trigger - what happened when a standard that did not exist was published

In early August 2026, a site operator uploaded a file at their domain root in a specification that does not exist, and published a spec document alongside it. The contents described office cats: their names, job titles, breeds, and an affection score. None of it has anything to do with search or with answer generation.

Four things were then observed.

  • Several AI crawlers and search crawlers fetched the file
  • Search indexed the URL
  • An AI answer returned facts that exist nowhere except inside that file
  • Asked directly, a conversational model said the file could help with ranking and visibility

What makes those four interesting is that they happened to be the same four items being offered in practice as proof that a different file proposal works. A specification someone invented passing all four means those four cannot tell an invented thing from a real one.

Only one conclusion follows directly, and it matters that it is only one: those four items cannot serve as evidence for either side of the argument. No conclusion about any proposal being void comes out of this. A ruler with no discriminating power has been set aside, and once it is set aside the verdict returns to undecided.

Attribution. The above is a record of what the person running the experiment reports having observed on their own site. One domain, one observer, a short window, and the raw data has not been opened in a form a third party could reproduce. This article treats it as a claim rather than a settled fact. The argument here survives that caveat: if the invented specification passed even two of the four, those two already lack discriminating power.

2. Adopted and effective are different claims

If you take one section from this article, take this one. Almost every claim made about a tactic bundles three stages together, and each stage demands a different observation, with the lower ones never carrying the higher ones along.

StageThe actual claimHow it is observedWhat the observation licensesWhat it still cannot say
1. PlacedWe deployed itThe URL returns the fileIt existsPossibly nobody has read it
2. RetrievedSomeone fetched itA request line in the access logIt was fetchedParsing, trust, weighting all unknown
3. Outcome changedVisibility rose because of itMovement in citation or exposure metricsSomething movedWhether that thing caused it

2.1 The gap between fetching and using

Getting from stage one to stage two is settled by the log. A request came in, a response went out. Nothing here is in dispute.

The problem lies between stages two and three. Across that gap there is not a single signal visible from the server side. Whether the bytes were parsed, whether the content was trusted more than other documents, whether it was consulted while composing an answer, all of it happens inside the other party's system. The whole span between the last line of your log and the first line of the answer is blank, and filling in that blank is nearly every argument in this field.

2.2 Indexing proves existence, not effect

A URL being indexed tells you that the URL exists and contains words. Indexing is a low threshold. Being indexed and having your content influence how other pages are evaluated are claims on entirely different levels, and the first holding does not carry the second.

2.3 A model's answer is not evidence

The most frequently used and weakest piece of evidence is this one. Ask a language model whether a tactic works and it does not run an experiment or consult a study. It averages what has already been written most often on the subject and returns it with complete confidence. Any widely discussed tactic therefore gets a favourable answer whether or not it was ever tested.

The invented specification drew the same favourable answer, and once posts explaining that the file was a joke spread across the web, the same question produced the opposite answer. The point of that observation is that the answer changed not because the model reasoned better but because the surrounding text changed. Asking a model is closer to an opinion poll than to verification.

3. What evidence an effectiveness claim requires

To say stage three, that an outcome changed because of the thing, you need at least the following. The list is not novel. It is what any field uses when it wants to talk about causes.

  • Something to compare against - a group identical in every respect except that item. Without it there is no ground for attaching the change to the item
  • Separated time points - not only before and after adoption, but how the group that did not adopt moved over the same window
  • Confounder control - did you change something else at the same time, did the reading side ship an update, does a seasonal pattern overlap
  • Repetition - one observation is a sample of one. Does the same procedure point the same way when you run it again
  • A success criterion fixed in advance - write down what counts as success before you start, and refuse to move it later

3.1 Why this is unusually hard in search and recommendation

Those five can be satisfied in principle. Three structural obstacles stand in the way here.

  • You cannot randomise the treatment group. You have one site. Splitting your pages and applying the change to half is the best available approach, and those halves differ in topic and traffic to begin with
  • The outcome is not a fixed value. Answers vary by asker, timing, region, and account state, so the act of measuring is itself a sampling procedure
  • The other party changes mid-experiment. If the model or the search system ships an update during your observation window, the premise of a before-and-after comparison collapses, and those updates arrive unannounced

What an actual control-group measurement of citation rates looks like is set out in the earlier article. One thing is worth adding here. Even with that design in place, the strength of the conclusion tops out at a difference in this direction was observed under these conditions, and there is still distance between that sentence and it works.

4. A structure that resists observation - experiments that do not reproduce

Compress the previous section into one line and it reads: experiments in this field do not reproduce. And an experiment that does not reproduce cannot be falsified either.

4.1 The same conditions cannot be built twice

Ask the same question twice on the same day and the answers differ, because randomness is built into generation and the path that pulls in search results branches per request. Personalisation compounds it: account history, region, language, and device change what you see, so the screen you looked at is not the screen someone else gets. Timing is itself a condition. The index keeps updating, and the document cited yesterday has slipped today.

4.2 The method is not published

Which signals carry which weight is internal business, and what gets published tops out at principle-level guidance. Even those documents are often more specific about what is not used than about what is. Google's guidance on optimising for generative AI features states that you do not need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search, because Search itself does not use them (as of the 10 July 2026 update). Sentences like that are rare, which is exactly why they are valuable. What the reading side says in its own words is one of the few materials available for judging stage three.

4.3 The observation window belongs to the other party

Most of the tools showing exposure or citation are provided by the reading side or estimated by a third party. When a metric's definition changes, the number moves while your site sits still. Reading that kind of drift as the effect of a tactic is a common error. Where the layer that holds a file and the layer that lets you see logs diverge is set out separately in the article on robots.txt and AI crawlers.

5. What circulates as evidence instead

When rigorous proof is hard the space does not stay empty; it fills with something else. Roughly eight forms of evidence actually change hands in practice. Sorting them by which stage each one reaches settles most disputes.

Form of evidenceWhat it saysWhat it cannot sayDoes an invented spec pass it
Anecdote - we added it and numbers roseSomething changed on that siteAttribution. Other changes in the windowPasses
Crawl logsThe file was fetched (stage 2)Whether the content was usedPasses
Index checkThe URL is indexedWeighting or evaluationPasses

Correlation - top sites all do itCo-occurrenceDirection. Good sites may just do everythingMostly passes

5.1 What large-scale observation actually shows

Why the bottom two rows differ becomes clear with real numbers. One SEO tooling company published an aggregation of fetch logs for a particular AI-facing guidance file across 137,210 of its own web-analytics customer domains that received traffic in May 2026.

  • 28 percent were publishing the file, roughly 38,360 domains
  • Among files that responded normally, 97 percent received not a single request that month
  • Within the 3 percent that did get requests, AI-related traffic came to 19.5 percent in total, and AI retrieval bots to 1.1 percent
  • The largest single share was SEO audit tooling at 21.7 percent, which is to say people checking that their own file is still there

Two things have to be read together here. First, the limitations the researchers state themselves: the sample skews towards technically and SEO-aware customers, so 28 percent is an upper-bound estimate for adoption, and what was measured is whether the file was fetched, not what was done with it after fetching. Second, and consequently, these figures do not refute stage three. They are an observation that stage two is barely happening. Since stage three has no path to existence without stage two, that is still plenty to decide with.

A single-site observation points the same way. One site aggregated 19,489 requests to its zone across 23 hours from 8 to 9 August 2026 and found four requests for that file, three of which came from their own verification tooling. Over the same window, robots.txt drew 95 requests from actual crawlers. This is a sample of one through a 23-hour window and generalises to nothing on its own. Its value lies only in pointing the same direction as the larger aggregation.

6. The value and the limits of planting something fake

Putting something that does not exist in place and watching what happens has a name. It is a negative control. You run something that could not possibly work through the same procedure, and if it produces the same signals, you rule that the signal sorts nothing. It is an old instrument and it fits this field well, because filtering out fake signals is far cheaper than measuring real effects.

6.1 What this method proves

Exactly one thing is proved: that a given observation cannot distinguish a real thing from an invented one. Two consequences follow immediately. That observation can no longer serve as evidence for or against, and any claim resting on it alone has an empty foundation and returns to the undecided pile.

6.2 What this method does not prove

  • That the proposal is void. A ruler with no discriminating power was set aside; the possibility that other evidence confirms an effect remains untouched
  • That the situation will hold. If the reading side starts supporting it, the same file carries a different value. Adoption is time-dependent
  • That it fails at what it was designed for. Some proposals were aimed at a purpose other than search visibility in the first place. A marketing claim collapsing does not take the original purpose down with it

6.3 The limits of the method itself

  • The sample is one. One domain, seen once
  • Observer and experimenter are the same party. Without open raw data, reproduction cannot be checked
  • Observation changes the subject. As the experiment gets discussed, that discussion accumulates on the web, and model answers shift because of it. A second observation is not run under the first one's conditions
  • A negative control judges the discriminating power of a signal; it proposes no replacement. What to look at instead is a question outside this method

7. What is still worth doing, and what can wait

Doing nothing because the evidence is thin is not the answer either. The decision does not turn on certainty of effect alone but on three axes: is the cost low, would the item remain justified for other reasons if the tactic failed, and is it reversible.

ItemCostJustified for other reasonsVerdict
Title and opening paragraph carrying the page's answerLowHumans read that way firstDo it now
Cleaning up structured dataLowAn area search features document as usedDo it now
Keeping summary and update date true to the bodyLowTies directly to trust and return visitsDo it now
Not hiding text behind a login or a render stepMediumSame direction as accessibility and performanceDo it now
Keeping one auto-generated guidance fileNear zeroNo cost means no lossFine to keep. Attach no expectations
Maintaining a file by hand on an ongoing basisHighNoWait until the evidence settles
Reshaping sentences to machine taste for answer inclusionHighNo. It reads worse to peopleWait
Subscribing to visibility metrics with undisclosed methodsHighNoWait until the definitions are clear

One criterion runs through the whole table. If the cost is near zero and the move is reversible, doing it on weak evidence is rational; once the cost is real, the strength of the evidence starts to matter. Weak evidence is not the problem by itself. The combination of weak evidence and high cost is.

7.1 The ability to judge is itself an asset

One item rarely makes these lists and is worth more than most that do: making your own logs legible to you. Once you can see which names hit which paths and how often, the space that other people's anecdotes were filling shrinks. At minimum you can adjudicate stage two yourself, and that alone filters out half of what circulates.

8. The lifespan of a fashionable prescription

New prescriptions appearing, entering the checklists, and quietly disappearing is a cycle search optimisation has run several times. Explaining it as foolishness gets you nothing. Where the structure is the same, the same thing happens regardless of who is standing there.

8.1 Why it repeats

  • The reading side does not publish its method. A blank is created
  • Blanks always get filled. Whoever fills one may be diligent or may have something to sell, and from the outside those look identical
  • Verification is too expensive at individual scale. So other people's anecdotes become the substitute for verification
  • Correlation is easy to notice and causation is not. Observing that the good sites all do it tells you nothing about direction
  • Nothing can be falsified, so nothing dies. Instead of being ruled wrong, a prescription simply goes out of fashion

8.2 The lifecycle curve

  1. A proposal appears, usually with a clear and narrow original purpose
  2. Early anecdotes attach, in the form of we added it and something rose
  3. It enters the checklists, and from here it starts separating from its original purpose
  4. It gets tooled, with generators and scorecards
  5. Large-scale observation arrives, and stage two gets measured for the first time
  6. Positions adjust, usually quietly

What is worth noticing is that step five comes late. Aggregating fetch logs at scale is only possible for parties holding logs in bulk, and by the time such a study lands the prescription is already sitting in several checklists. That lag is structural and recurs every time, independent of whether any individual prescription had merit.

8.3 What the survivors have in common

Plenty of items survived the same history: writing clear titles, cleaning up duplicate URLs, using standard markup to declare structure. They share two properties. The reading side stated in documentation that it uses them, which gives them one of the few materials available for judging stage three. And they remain good for humans even when the reading side changes, so a reversed verdict costs nothing.

Checking for those two is the fastest screen when a new item shows up. If neither is present, weigh the cost: near zero, keep it; substantial, let it wait.

9. What could not be confirmed

  • Internal processing at the operators. Which file carries which weight sits outside public documentation and cannot be checked. The official document cited here is a declaration of non-use, not an account of how anything is used
  • Raw data from the invented-specification experiment. The access logs and query records were not published in a form a third party could reproduce, so this article treated the observation as a claim
  • Raw data from the large-scale aggregation. It is internal company data and cannot be reproduced externally. The sampling bias and the measurement boundary are carried over exactly as the researchers stated them
  • Representativeness of the single-site observation, and how other operators behave. A sample of one through a 23-hour window supports nothing beyond pointing the same direction, and one company's documentation cannot be transferred to another
  • Figures and document citations reflect public material as of 31 August 2026. This area updates often and six months could change it

9.1 This article sits inside the same limits

Stated plainly: this article read published material and reproduced no experiment. Its own capacity to judge stage three is borrowed from the bottom two rows of the table above and nothing more.

What it does claim is of a different kind. The claim that those observations cannot distinguish real from invented is a logical one, and it holds if the cited observations are broadly accurate. Conversely, a verdict that anything does or does not work is not offered here either. That is not a sentence the evidence assembled here can support.

10. Frequently asked questions

If my logs show a crawler fetched the file, does that mean it works?

That log line proves a request arrived and a response went out, and nothing more. Whether it was parsed, weighted as trustworthy, or consulted while an answer was composed is invisible from your server. Even the large-scale study states this as its own limit: what it measured is whether the file was fetched, not what was done with it.

Should I create the new AI-facing file format everyone is talking about?

Decide on cost and reversibility, not on effectiveness. Generated automatically in your build with no ongoing human attention, there is nothing to lose by having it. Requiring continuous hand-maintenance, or restructuring your content to fit it, is a combination of certain cost and unverified return.

Can I just ask an AI whether a tactic works?

That answer cannot be used as evidence. Instead of experimenting or consulting a study, the model averages what has been written most about the topic. The invented specification drew a favourable answer too, and once posts explaining the joke spread, the answer reversed. It reversed because the surrounding text changed, not because the reasoning improved.

Is there any way to verify effectiveness on a small site?

Rigorous verification is out of reach at individual scale. One site means no randomisation, the same question returns different answers, and the other party changes while you watch. Approximations exist: split your pages, apply the change to one group, compare the same window, and fix the success criterion in writing before you start. What you should come away with is less a conclusion than an accurate sense of which stage you observed.

So what should I do first?

Start with what is cheap and stays justified if the tactic fails. Does the title and opening paragraph carry the question the page answers, does the summary match the body, is the update date accurate, is the text hidden behind a login or a render step. These survive any change on the reading side.

11. Conclusion

What one invented specification showed is not a report card on any proposal. It is that the items we had been calling evidence cannot tell a real thing from an invented one. Once those items drop out, the verdict returns to undecided, and leaving an undecided question undecided beats holding on to a wrong answer.

Three things to take away today.

  1. Split the claim into three stages. Placed, retrieved, outcome changed. Asking which stage the other party has actually proven settles half the conversation
  2. Look at the form of the evidence first. Anecdote, crawl log, large-scale aggregation, and official documentation are not worth the same, and none of them proves stage three alone
  3. Decide on cost. Near zero, keep it on weak evidence; substantial, demand evidence. The problem is never weak evidence by itself but weak evidence paired with high cost

One more thing: the next prescription is coming. As long as the reading side keeps its method private, blanks will keep opening and keep getting filled. The question to ask then is not whether the prescription is right but whether the evidence on offer is the kind an invented specification would also pass. That single line filters out most of it.
