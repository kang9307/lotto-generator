How Korea's simplified VAT taxpayer differs from the general one

Not a different rate but a different calculation — value added ratios, input tax, tax invoices, and the moment your type changes (as of August 2026)

Aug 26, 2026 Finance & Economy

This is a general explanation of a Korean tax rule, not tax advice. How it applies in practice rests with a licensed tax professional and the district tax office with jurisdiction.

1. One line on the registration certificate sets the formula

Somewhere between the trade name and the business category, a Korean business registration certificate prints four characters: general taxpayer or simplified taxpayer. For someone opening a first business, that line usually slides past unnoticed. It gets looked up around the time the first tax bill lands, and the explanation that surfaces most often is that simplified means less tax.

That is half right. The two types do not differ in rate; they differ in the order in which the tax is worked out. The VAT rate is 10 percent either way. What splits is what the 10 percent gets applied to, and that single line decides how input tax is handled, whether a tax invoice can be issued, how many times a year a return is filed, and whether money can come back.

What follows traces where that structure diverges and how far the divergence reaches. Articles and amounts are current as of August 2026, with effective dates noted where they matter. This covers the rules of the Republic of Korea; readers outside Korea should treat it as a description of a foreign system rather than local guidance. If tax vocabulary itself is unfamiliar, the basics of how taxes are classified comes first, and for the filing screens themselves the Hometax walkthrough is quicker.

2. The difference is structural, not a rate

VAT is, as the name says, a tax on value added. Buy for 100 and sell for 150, and the value the business created is 50, which is what the tax should attach to. The system reaches that 50 in two different ways.

General taxpayer — subtracting real purchases

A general taxpayer uses what is called the invoice credit method. Tax on what was sold, minus tax on what was bought.

Amount payable = output tax (supply value × 10%) − input tax
Value-Added Tax Act, Article 37(2)

Input tax here is the full amount written on the tax invoices actually received. The 10 borne on a purchase of 100 comes straight out. In a month heavy with purchases the bill shrinks, and when purchases exceed sales there is more to subtract than to pay.

Simplified taxpayer — multiplying by an industry average

A simplified taxpayer does not bring actual purchases into the calculation. Instead, a value added ratio fixed in advance for each industry is applied to revenue to estimate the value added, and 10 percent is applied to that.

Amount payable = tax base (supply price) × industry value added ratio × 10% − deductible amounts
Value-Added Tax Act Article 63(2), Enforcement Decree Article 111(2)

The value entering the tax base is the supply price, not the supply value, and that distinction matters. Supply price includes VAT. A simplified taxpayer's calculation starts from the gross amount received; a general taxpayer's starts from the supply value with VAT stripped out.

Set the two formulas side by side and the rest of the differences explain themselves. One pulls the actual numbers from your own books into the calculation; the other pulls in the average for the whole industry. It trades individual accuracy for lighter bookkeeping, and where individual circumstances diverge sharply from the average, that divergence stays in the result.

Industry value added ratios — half of the simplified calculation

The ratio is not something a business picks. Article 111(2) of the Enforcement Decree sets it by industry, and the brackets below apply to supplies made on or after July 1, 2021.

IndustryValue added ratioAgainst supply price (×10%)
Retail, collection and sale of recyclable materials, food and beverage services15%1.5%
Manufacturing, agriculture, forestry and fishing, small parcel delivery20%2%
Accommodation25%2.5%
Construction, transport and warehousing, information and communications, other services30%3%
Financial and insurance related services, professional, scientific and technical services, business facility management and support and rental services, real estate related services40%4%

The figure often quoted as a simplified taxpayer rate of 1.5 to 4 percent is the right-hand column. The rate did not fall; 15 to 40 percent was applied once before the 10 percent. That is why the same revenue produces a simplified figure more than twice as far apart for a restaurant and a professional services firm.

Where a business spans several industries, each is calculated separately and the results are added. How revenue is allocated between them changes the outcome, so that allocation is worth settling with the district tax office or a tax professional first.

3. Heavy purchases turn the structure against you

This is where the folk wisdom that simplified is always better falls apart. A simplified taxpayer is not left without any purchase-side deduction, but the mechanism is different. Article 63(3)1 of the Value-Added Tax Act allows a deduction of 0.5 percent of the supply price of goods and services for which tax invoices were received in the taxable period. Set against subtracting the full input tax, the order of magnitude is not the same.

  • General taxpayer — the 1 million won of VAT borne on a 10 million won purchase comes out in full
  • Simplified taxpayer — 0.5 percent of an 11 million won supply price, or 55,000 won, is deducted
  • The gap widens in proportion to purchase volume, and the premise of the structure is that an average level of purchasing is already baked into the industry ratio

Put numbers on it. For a food and beverage business (ratio 15 percent) with an annual supply price of 50 million won, the simplified calculation is 50 million × 15% × 10% = 750,000 won. Run the same revenue as a general taxpayer and the supply value is roughly 45.45 million won with output tax of about 4.54 million won. Input tax comes off that, so the two calculations meet when input tax is around 3.8 million won — in other words, when invoiced purchases run in the eighty percent range of revenue.

That arithmetic illustrates the structure; it is not an actual tax figure. Various credits, penalty taxes and multi-industry operation all shift the result. The direction, though, is clear. Where the purchase share runs above what the industry ratio assumes, the calculation that subtracts real purchases comes out smaller; where it runs below, the reverse holds.

Only one side has a refund

When purchases exceed sales, the two types behave in completely different ways.

Under Article 37(3) of the Value-Added Tax Act, input tax exceeding output tax becomes a refundable amount for a general taxpayer. More can come back than went out.

For a simplified taxpayer, that slot is empty. Article 63(6) provides that where the total of deductible amounts exceeds the amount payable, the excess portion is deemed not to exist. It does not carry forward and it does not come back. It simply disappears.

The clause bites hardest in the opening year, when equipment and initial inventory absorb purchases before revenue has climbed. One side has a route to recover that outlay and the other does not. Why that timing gap is awkward on the bookkeeping side is taken up from another angle in the piece on payments, settlement and accounting.

4. Tax invoices — issuing rights shape who you can sell to

After the calculation itself, the next thing that reshapes daily practice is documentation. A general taxpayer issues tax invoices. For a simplified taxpayer, it depends on scale. National Tax Service guidance sets it out as follows.

  • Newly registered, or prior year supply price below 48 million won — cannot issue a tax invoice, issues a receipt instead
  • Prior year supply price of 48 million won or more — issuing is an obligation, and 10 percent is applied to the supply value when issuing
  • That obligation runs from July 1 of the year following the year the threshold was met, through June 30 of the year after that

Why does this change what a counterparty chooses? Because a business claiming an input tax credit needs the tax invoice as documentation. Where two suppliers quote the same price and only one can issue, the real cost differs for the buyer claiming the credit. So the first axis to check is not revenue scale but who the revenue comes from.

  • Industries selling mostly to end consumers — the counterparty is not claiming a credit, so issuing rights matter little
  • Industries selling mostly to other businesses — issuing capability can become a condition of closing the deal
  • Within one industry, a business weighted toward supply contracts and services carries this axis differently

5. The type is assigned, not chosen, and it changes on its own

Many treat the type as an option. The principle runs the other way. It is determined by a statutory test, and once the threshold is crossed the change is automatic.

Thresholds

  • Article 61(1) of the Value-Added Tax Act with Article 109(1) of the Enforcement Decree — a sole proprietor whose prior year supply price totals less than 104 million won is a simplified taxpayer
  • Article 61(1)3 — a real estate rental business or taxable entertainment venue is excluded from simplified treatment once that industry's prior year supply price reaches 48 million won
  • With two or more places of business, the supply prices are aggregated for the test, and holding another place of business that is not eligible for simplified treatment disqualifies the taxpayer
  • The article says sole proprietor, so corporations are outside the regime entirely

Industries excluded outright

Some businesses fall outside simplified treatment regardless of size. Article 109(2) of the Enforcement Decree holds that list.

  • Mining, real estate dealing, and electricity, gas, steam and water supply
  • Wholesale and commodity brokerage — collection and sale of recyclable materials excepted
  • Professional services such as legal practice, accountancy and medical practice
  • Taxable entertainment venues, and real estate rental businesses as specified by Ministry of Economy and Finance ordinance
  • Manufacturing, construction, and professional, scientific and technical services — except those supplying goods or services directly to end consumers as specified by ministerial ordinance
  • Businesses acquired from a general taxpayer, and businesses run by a sole proprietor subject to double-entry bookkeeping
  • Anything meeting the criteria set by the Commissioner of the National Tax Service — the exclusion standards by region, scale and line of business

When the change lands

Cross the threshold and Article 62(2) makes the business a general taxpayer from July 1 of the following year. July rather than the following January is the detail that most often catches people out. Per National Tax Service guidance, the move down from general to simplified also applies without a separate application, with real estate rental businesses treated as an exception.

A newly registered business has no prior year record, so the supply price from the start of business to the end of that taxable period is annualised over twelve months for the test. That is where a business opening in the second half of the year, reassured by half a year of revenue, finds itself above the line once annualised.

6. Filing cycles and payment — different calendars

Different formulas mean different calendars. On National Tax Service guidance the picture is this.

  • Individual general taxpayer — two six-month taxable periods. The first (Jan 1 to Jun 30) is filed and paid July 1 to 25; the second (Jul 1 to Dec 31) the following January 1 to 25
  • Corporate taxpayer — preliminary returns are added on top, so four filings a year at three-month intervals
  • Simplified taxpayer — a one-year taxable period (Jan 1 to Dec 31) with a single final return the following January 1 to 25 (Article 67(1))
  • A preliminary assessment period (Jan 1 to Jun 30) also exists for simplified taxpayers, and in certain cases such as a type conversion a separate return is due by July 25

Filing once a year lightens the bookkeeping and the closing work. It also means a larger amount lands in one go. A whole year arrives in January, which gives cash flow management a different character.

Exemption from payment

Simplified taxpayers have one more provision. Article 69(1) exempts the obligation to pay where the supply price for the taxable period totals less than 48 million won. The National Tax Service describes this threshold as applying from January 1, 2021, and where a taxable period runs short of a full year the figure is annualised over twelve months.

The heading of the article — exemption from the obligation to pay — is worth holding on to. What is exempted is payment, not filing. How it applies in a given case is best confirmed with the district tax office.

7. Which side suits you turns on three axes

By this point it is clear why the question of which type is better has no one-line answer. The two are not ranked; they are structures that favour different conditions. Three axes come up first in practice, with industry and initial investment layered on top.

IndustryA 15% ratio and a 40% ratio pull the simplified result far apartThe ratio sits in the slot before the 10% is applied

There is a shortcut in the order too. Start with the counterparty. If revenue comes from other businesses, issuing rights govern whether deals happen at all, and no amount of careful tax arithmetic overturns that. Where end-consumer revenue dominates, purchase share and the industry ratio come next.

The table sorts what to look at first; it is not a conclusion. Within the same industry, lease structure and whether suppliers issue tax invoices move the answer. The actual choice belongs with a tax professional working from your own numbers.

8. Waiving simplified status costs three years

Sometimes a business below the threshold wants general taxpayer treatment anyway — business customers demand tax invoices, or heavy opening purchases make the refund route necessary. That is what waiver of simplified taxation is for, and Article 70 of the Value-Added Tax Act sets out the procedure and its price together.

  • Filing deadline — the waiver report goes to the head of the competent tax office by the last day of the month before the month in which general taxpayer rules are to apply
  • Lock-in — simplified rules cannot be applied through the taxable period containing the day three years from the first day of that month, or from the first day of the month containing the start of business
  • Reapplication — after three years, an application to be treated as simplified again must be filed at least ten days before the taxable period begins
  • Exception — Article 70(4) opens an earlier route for a sole proprietor whose prior year supply price is 48 million won or more but below the amount set by Presidential Decree. The National Tax Service describes this as effective from July 1, 2024

Those three years are the real weight of the decision. Cross once and a substantial slice of the early years is spent as a general taxpayer. For an industry where opening purchases are heavy and then fall away, the later stretch has to enter the calculation too. Timing and the exception conditions are worth confirming with the district tax office before filing.

9. What to check when registering

At registration, the simplified application is filed together with the business registration itself. Applying does not settle it, though — the exclusions above have to clear first. The order runs like this.

  1. Confirm the form is a sole proprietorship — a corporation has no simplified option at all
  2. Check whether the industry sits on the exclusion list in Article 109(2) of the Enforcement Decree
  3. Ask the district tax office whether the exclusion standards published by the Commissioner (region, scale, line of business) apply
  4. For real estate rental or a taxable entertainment venue, work from the 48 million won line
  5. If two or more places of business are planned, assume aggregate testing
  6. See whether projected revenue sits near 104 million won — close to the line, the type will not last
  7. Estimate the split between business and end-consumer revenue
  8. Estimate first-year purchase volume and locate the industry's value added ratio in the table above
  9. For a second-half opening, redo the figures after twelve-month annualisation, and if waiver is on the table, price in the three-year lock-in

Some obligations survive whichever type applies. Comprehensive income tax on business income runs on its own calendar every May, and recording revenue and keeping documentation is required either way.

11. Frequently asked questions

Does simplified status mean no VAT at all?

No. The method differs; the liability remains. Where the supply price for the taxable period falls below 48 million won, Article 69(1) exempts the obligation to pay. What is exempted is payment, not the filing procedure.

Does a simplified taxpayer collect VAT from customers?

The tax base for a simplified taxpayer is the supply price, meaning the amount with VAT included. The calculation starts from the gross, so the tax is treated as sitting inside what was received. Where a simplified taxpayer is entitled to issue a tax invoice, 10 percent is applied to the supply value on that invoice.

Can you ask to switch types mid-stream?

Moving from simplified to general is open through the waiver report under Article 70. The reverse direction is the outcome of the threshold test, not an application. Per National Tax Service guidance it applies without separate notice, with real estate rental businesses as an exception.

What happens to the ratio if several industries are registered?

Each is calculated separately and the results are added. Because how revenue is allocated changes the outcome, the allocation basis is worth settling with the district tax office or a tax professional in advance.

Is the threshold measured with VAT included?

The measure is the supply price, which includes VAT. Article 61 and Article 109 of the Enforcement Decree both write the test against total supply price. With two or more places of business, the aggregate applies.

12. In short

What separates the two types is not the rate. General taxation subtracts real purchases; simplified taxation multiplies by an industry average. Everything else descends from that line — the 0.5 percent cap on the purchase-side deduction, the refund that exists on only one side, the invoice rights that turn on scale.

So the sentence that simplified is better does not stand without conditions. What held for a small business with light purchases selling to end consumers inverts where purchases are heavy or business customers dominate. The type is not a benefit but a method of calculation, and whether it fits is decided by industry, purchases and counterparty.

At registration, the most useful preparation is not a precise revenue forecast but checking whether your buyers will ask for tax invoices. Once that is settled, the rest follows.
