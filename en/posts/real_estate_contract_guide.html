Complete Guide to Real Estate Purchase & Lease Contracts in Korea 2026

Purchase, jeonse, and monthly rent — step-by-step procedures, registry checks, deposit stages, key clauses, fraud prevention, and agent fees explained

2026-06-28 Legal

Introduction — Why Understanding the Contract Process Matters

A real estate transaction is typically the largest financial commitment most people make. In Korea, a purchase can involve hundreds of millions of won, while a jeonse (전세) lease often locks up tens to hundreds of millions in deposit money with a single landlord. Yet many people sign contracts without fully understanding the procedures or the risks — and fraud, double contracts, and unverified mortgages regularly destroy savings that took years to build.

This guide covers the key steps and safeguards for Korean real estate contracts — purchase (매매), jeonse (전세), and monthly rent (월세) — as of June 2026. It is intended as general information only. Rules vary by location and change over time. Always consult a licensed real estate agent, judicial scrivener (법무사), or attorney for decisions specific to your situation.

1. Pre-Contract Checks — Registry, Building Register, Owner Verification

1.1 The Real Estate Registry (등기부등본)

The Certificate of Registered Matters (등기사항전부증명서, formerly 등기부등본) is the official legal record of a property's ownership and encumbrances. Anyone can download it for a small fee from the Supreme Court Internet Registry (www.iros.go.kr). Pull a fresh copy both the day before signing and on the day of final payment. The document has three sections:

  • Title Section (표제부): Address, floor area, structure. Verify these match the physical property.
  • Section A — Gap-gu (갑구): Ownership history, and any seizures (압류), provisional attachments (가압류), or injunctions (가처분). The current owner must match the person you are dealing with — verify using their national ID. If seizures or injunctions are present, reconsider the deal.
  • Section B — Eul-gu (을구): Mortgages (근저당권), leaseholds, and other encumbrances. The key figure is the total "maximum claim amount" (채권최고액) of all mortgages. If the sum of all mortgage claims plus your deposit exceeds 70–80% of the property's market value, your deposit is at risk in a foreclosure.

1.2 Building Register (건축물대장)

The Building Register (available free from Government24 / gov.kr) records the legal use, structure, and floor area of a building. If it shows "illegal structure" (위반 건축물), the building may have unauthorized additions or use changes. This can result in demolition orders, heavy fines, and — critically — may prevent you from completing residential registration (전입신고), which is essential for protecting a jeonse deposit.

1.3 Confirming the Owner and Any Representative

Always contract directly with the registered owner, or with a formally authorized representative. If a proxy appears, demand a power of attorney bearing the owner's registered seal (인감도장) and a seal certificate (인감증명서) issued within the last three months. Call the owner directly to confirm the authorization. Impersonation fraud is common — never skip ID verification.

1.4 Tax Arrears and Priority Rights

Unpaid national or local taxes create a lien that ranks above tenant deposits in foreclosure. With the landlord's consent, you can request a tax arrears confirmation from the National Tax Service (홈택스) or local government. In 2026, the government is expanding the Ansim Jeonse App (안심전세 앱, launch expected September 2026) to show the registry, confirmed-date status, number of registered households, and tax arrears for multi-unit buildings — all on one screen.

2. Purchase Contract Procedure — Step by Step

2.1 Market Research

Check actual transaction prices (not asking prices) on the Ministry of Land's Real Transaction Price Disclosure System (rt.molit.go.kr). Review transactions from the past three to six months in the same building or area. Asking prices can be significantly higher than what transactions actually close at.

2.2 Signing the Contract

Standard real estate contracts are prepared by the licensed agent and signed by both parties. The contract must specify: property description (address, area, parcel number), total price and payment schedule (earnest money / interim payment / balance dates), and any special clauses. Both parties keep one signed original each.

2.3 Earnest Money, Interim Payment, and Balance

The typical Korean purchase structure is: earnest money (계약금) 10% on signing, interim payment (중도금) 10–20% at a midpoint, and balance (잔금) 70–80% at transfer. Once the interim payment is made, neither party can unilaterally cancel without significant financial penalties — this is a critical legal inflection point. The balance is paid simultaneously with the ownership transfer registration.

2.4 Ownership Transfer Registration

The ownership transfer must be filed at the registry office within 60 days of the balance payment. In practice, most buyers hire a judicial scrivener (법무사) to handle the filing jointly on behalf of both parties. Acquisition tax (취득세) is also due at this stage — rates range from roughly 1% to 3% depending on property value, size, and buyer circumstances. On the morning of balance payment, pull one final registry printout to confirm no new encumbrances have been registered since the contract was signed.

3. Lease Contract Procedure — Jeonse and Monthly Rent

3.1 Signing the Lease

Standard lease contracts identify the property, state the deposit and (if applicable) monthly rent, set the lease term, and include special clauses. Under Korea's Housing Lease Protection Act, residential tenants are entitled to a minimum two-year term regardless of what the contract says. As with purchases, always verify the landlord's ID against the registered owner before signing.

3.2 Earnest Money and Balance for Leases

Jeonse and monthly-rent leases typically follow a two-stage payment: earnest money (roughly 10% of the total deposit) on signing day, and the balance on moving day. An interim payment mid-period is rare for leases. Before paying the balance on moving day, pull a fresh registry to check that no new mortgages or seizures have appeared since signing.

3.3 Residential Registration and Confirmed Date — Critical Tenant Protections

On moving day, complete two steps at your local community center (주민센터) or via Government24:

  • Residential Registration (전입신고): Establishes your legal right to occupy the property even if ownership changes (대항력). Under current law, this right takes effect at midnight of the day after registration. As of June 2026, the government is pursuing an amendment to make it effective immediately upon filing — check the current law before your transaction.
  • Confirmed Date (확정일자): A date stamp on your lease contract, obtained at the community center, registry office, or online via the Internet Registry. This grants priority repayment rights (우선변제권) in foreclosure proceedings — meaning your deposit is repaid before later creditors.

4. Key Contractual Clauses — What to Include

4.1 Rights Preservation Clause

Require the landlord to maintain the registered rights status shown at signing (no new mortgages, attachments, or disposals) through the balance payment date. If violated, the contract is void and the deposit must be returned immediately in full.

4.2 Tax Arrears Discovery Clause

If unpaid national or local taxes are discovered after signing, the tenant may cancel the contract and receive a full deposit refund. Without this clause, a tax lien discovered post-signing may not entitle you to an easy exit.

4.3 Immediate Deposit Return Clause

The landlord must return the deposit in full on the lease expiry date, regardless of whether a new tenant has been found. Landlords who delay return by citing failure to find a successor tenant are a common source of disputes.

4.4 Facility Repair Responsibilities

Specify which party bears repair costs for major fixtures (boiler, plumbing, electrical systems, air conditioning). The common default is: normal wear and aging is the landlord's responsibility; damage caused by the tenant is the tenant's responsibility. Pre-move-in renovations (wallpaper, flooring) should also be agreed upon and written down.

4.5 Loan Condition Clause (for Purchases)

If the buyer is funding the purchase via a mortgage loan, include a clause allowing cancellation and earnest money refund if the loan falls through. Interest rate changes and tightened lending regulations can derail financing plans, and this clause provides an exit without financial penalty.

5. Jeonse Fraud Prevention — 2026 Checklist

5.1 Calculate Total Prior Claims

The basic safety formula: (total maximum mortgage claims from registry) + (your deposit) should not exceed 70–80% of the property's market value. If it does, foreclosure may not fully recover your deposit. For multi-unit buildings, the deposits of all other registered households must also be factored in.

5.2 Jeonse Deposit Insurance

HUG (Korea Housing Finance Corporation / 주택도시보증공사) and SGI Seoul Guarantee (SGI서울보증) both offer jeonse deposit insurance. If the landlord cannot return the deposit on expiry, the insurer pays the tenant directly and pursues the landlord for recovery. Premium rates are roughly 0.1–0.2% of the deposit per year. Verify eligibility requirements (LTV ratios, prior lien limits) before signing the lease — not all properties or deposit sizes qualify.

5.3 Multi-Unit Buildings — Extra Care Required

For multi-household (다가구) buildings or villas, the total mortgage on the entire building plus all existing tenant deposits must be considered together, not just your floor. Ask the landlord for tenancy information for other units, or use the Ansim Jeonse App once it launches. If the landlord refuses to disclose, reconsider the contract.

5.4 Jeonse-to-Price Ratio (깡통전세 Risk)

When the jeonse deposit is close to or exceeds the property's purchase price, it is colloquially called a "kkangton jeonse" (빈 깡통, empty can). In foreclosure, there may be nothing left to return. Use the Ministry of Land's Real Transaction Price Disclosure System to verify the market price and keep your deposit well below 80% of that figure.

6. Real Estate Agent Commissions

6.1 Legal Caps for 2026

For residential purchases, the legal maximum commission rate ranges from 0.4% to 0.7% of the transaction price, tiered by price bracket. For residential jeonse and monthly rent leases, the cap is generally 0.4%. Exact caps are set by each city or provincial ordinance and may vary slightly by region.

6.2 Monthly Rent Conversion

For monthly rent leases, the commission base is calculated as: deposit + (monthly rent × 100). For example, a lease with a 10 million won deposit and 500,000 won monthly rent produces a base of 60 million won (10M + 500K × 100), to which the rate is then applied.

6.3 Negotiation and Verification

The legal rate is a cap, not a mandatory fee — you may negotiate a lower rate with the agent. Both the buyer (or tenant) and the seller (or landlord) each pay their own agent separately. Before paying, confirm the agent holds a valid registration and professional liability insurance (공제증서).

7. Dispute Resolution

7.1 Lease Dispute Mediation Committee

The Korea Real Estate Board's Lease Dispute Mediation Committee handles disputes over jeonse and monthly rent contracts, including deposit returns, repair obligations, and contract terminations. Mediation is faster and cheaper than litigation, and a successful mediation carries the same legal force as a court judgment.

7.2 Certified Mail Notice (내용증명)

When a landlord fails to perform or return a deposit, send a certified mail notice (내용증명) through the post office immediately. This officially documents what was demanded and when — critical evidence in any subsequent legal proceeding.

7.3 Court Action and Provisional Attachment

If the landlord still does not comply, file a payment order or deposit return lawsuit. Before litigation concludes, seek a provisional attachment (가압류) on the property to prevent the landlord from selling or encumbering it in the meantime. If jeonse deposit insurance was purchased, the insurer will pay the deposit directly and handle recovery from the landlord.

7.4 Jeonse Fraud Victim Support

Since 2023, Korea's Act on Support for Jeonse Fraud Victims provides recognized victims with access to emergency foreclosure deferrals, priority public housing placement, and low-interest refinancing loans. Applications are made through the Ministry of Land's Jeonse Fraud Victim Support Committee.
