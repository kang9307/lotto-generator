Introduction: Same Gold, Different Taxes

Whenever gold prices climb, interest in owning gold climbs with them. What is less widely understood is that where and how you buy the same gold can change the tax and fees dramatically. A physical bar carries VAT from the moment you buy it, and bank gold accounts or gold-related ETFs tax the gains you make. Among the options, Korea's KRX Gold Market - the physical gold market run by the Korea Exchange - is frequently cited as a particularly tax-advantaged route. Open a gold account at a brokerage and you can trade gold in 1g units much like stocks, and as long as you trade within the account, the trading gains are described as untaxed.

This article walks through what the KRX Gold Market is, how to start trading, the core tax-benefit structure, the VAT that applies when you withdraw physical gold, and how it compares with bars, gold ETFs, and gold banking, along with the risks. The goal is not to recommend a specific product or entry point, but to help you understand the decision criteria behind each method. 1. What Is the KRX Gold Market?

The KRX Gold Market is a physical gold trading market operated by the Korea Exchange (KRX). It was established as a public market to bring gold trading into the regulated system and make it transparent, and it is described as forming real-time quotes during market hours and letting standardized gold be bought and sold, much like the stock market. The gold traded is described as quality-verified standard gold of 99.99% purity (1kg and 100g formats) supplied by domestic refiners, which investors can trade in units as small as 1g.

A key point is that you do not need to store the physical metal yourself. Gold you buy is described as being kept safely through a public custody institution, so you can hold gold as an account balance without worrying about theft or loss. You withdraw physical gold through a separate procedure only when you actually need it.

2. How to Start Trading

2.1 Opening a Dedicated Gold Account

To trade on the KRX Gold Market, you first open a dedicated KRX gold account at a brokerage that is a member of the Korea Exchange gold market. A gold account is often separate from a regular equity account, so it helps to check first whether your usual brokerage supports KRX gold trading and whether the account can be opened remotely. Major brokerages are reported to support gold trading, but availability and terms can change over time, so confirm with each brokerage's latest guidance.

2.2 How Trading Actually Works

After depositing funds, you search the gold symbol in the HTS (PC) or MTS (mobile) and place buy and sell orders in much the same way as stock trading. The minimum trading unit is 1g, and quotes move in real time during market hours. Because you can start small, a dollar-cost-averaging approach - buying a set amount of gold each month - is often mentioned as feasible. That said, it remains a fluctuating investment asset whose price can fall.

3. The Core: KRX Gold Market Tax Benefits

The main reason the KRX Gold Market draws attention is tax. The benefits commonly cited across sources are:

  • Capital-gains tax exemption: Gains from buying and selling gold through the account are generally reported to carry no capital-gains tax.
  • No dividend income tax: The dividend income tax applied to gold banking and gold ETF gains is reported not to apply to in-account KRX physical gold trading.
  • VAT waiver: VAT is waived at the in-account buy-and-sell stage. (Physical withdrawal, covered below, is the exception.)
  • Excluded from comprehensive financial-income taxation: These gains are reported to be excluded from aggregation, which some describe as easing the burden especially for investors with high financial income.

In short, as long as you trade only within the account, the tax burden on trading gains is described as essentially minimal. Because this rests on tax law, however, it can change with future amendments and may apply differently by individual circumstance, so do not treat the content here as absolute and always verify with current tax law and a professional.

4. Withdraw Physically, and 10% VAT Applies

Here is a trap you must know. In-account trading is VAT-exempt, but the moment you withdraw your holdings as physical gold such as a bar, a VAT of generally 10% is reported to apply to the withdrawn amount. Physical withdrawal can usually be requested only in 1kg or 100g units, and bar-fabrication and withdrawal fees (cited at tens of thousands of KRW per bar) plus shipping and insurance costs are added on top.

Accordingly, the common advice is that if your goal is trading gains, keeping the gold inside the account without physical withdrawal is more tax-efficient. Unless you actually need the physical metal itself - for a gift, an heirloom, or the like - there is little reason to pay VAT to withdraw it. Confirm the exact VAT rate, withdrawal costs, and available units with your brokerage and at the time of the transaction.

Even for the same gold, tax and characteristics differ substantially by method. The table below organizes commonly cited points around decision criteria; specific figures and conditions vary by product and timing, so treat it as reference only.

As the table shows, on tax alone, KRX physical gold is frequently cited as the most advantageous method for in-account trading. Gold banking and gold-related ETFs tax trading gains, and once annual financial income exceeds a threshold (often cited at 20 million KRW), they can be aggregated into comprehensive financial-income taxation, pushing the rate higher. A physical bar carries 10% VAT from purchase, and you bear the storage and theft risk yourself.

6. Fees and Custody Costs

A tax advantage does not mean zero cost. KRX gold trading still involves a brokerage trading fee, generally cited at around 0.2-0.5% of the trade value. Online trading fees differ by brokerage and are sometimes lowered through promotions, so compare the actual fee rate at the brokerage you plan to use. In-account custody is described as handled through a public institution with little separate custody fee, but physical withdrawal adds the VAT and fabrication/shipping costs noted above.

7. Risks: Do Not Enter on Tax Alone

The tax benefit is a clear advantage, but KRX physical gold remains an investment asset where principal loss is possible. The main risks are:

  • Price risk: Gold prices rise and fall with international quotes, supply and demand, and rate and inflation expectations. Even with no tax, you lose money if the price falls below your purchase price.
  • Exchange-rate risk: The domestic gold price is influenced by both the international gold quote and the KRW-USD exchange rate. Even if the international price is unchanged, currency moves can shift the domestic price.
  • Withdrawal-related costs: Choosing physical withdrawal lowers your effective return via VAT and fees. Do not conflate a physical-holding purpose with an investment purpose.
  • Opportunity cost: Gold generates no interest or dividends. Holding a portion for diversification is the commonly discussed approach; concentrating your entire net worth in one asset is not advisable.

8. How to Decide

The KRX Gold Market can be summarized as a tax-efficient route for gold investment, but the optimal method depends on your purpose. First, if your goal is trading gains, KRX physical gold - traded within the account without physical withdrawal - is cited as tax-advantaged. Second, if you actually need the physical metal (for a gift or heirloom), weigh the VAT and costs and consider physical withdrawal or buying a bar. Third, if small amounts and automation matter and opening a brokerage account is cumbersome, a gold ETF or gold banking may be an alternative, but weigh the tax difference too. Fourth, whatever the method, approach it within spare-capital limits and diversified, on the premise of price and exchange-rate risk.

9. Conclusion

The KRX Gold Market lets you trade gold in 1g units just by opening a brokerage account, and in-account trading is reported to be exempt from capital-gains, dividend, and value-added tax and excluded from comprehensive financial-income taxation - which is why it is frequently presented as an attractive route on tax grounds. Keep in mind, though, that 10% VAT applies the moment you withdraw physically, and that the absence of tax does not erase price and exchange-rate risk. The right order is to first fix your purpose (trading gains vs. physical holding), your funding situation, and the volatility you can tolerate, then choose a method.
