KakaoTalk Chat Backup and Restore - What to Do Before You Switch or Lose Your Phone

What the free backup actually covers, the 14-day clock, and where TalkCloud is different

Aug 10, 2026 Life & Practical

Introduction: backup is something you can only do beforehand

Activating a new phone at a carrier shop takes about twenty minutes. In those twenty minutes the staff will move your photos and contacts, but they will not move your KakaoTalk conversations. You get home, open the app, and the chat rooms are all there, yet scrolling up shows nothing from last month. That is the moment most people start searching for answers. By then it is already too late.

KakaoTalk chat backup works exactly that way. It is not a service that quietly accumulates in the cloud so you can pull it down later; it is something you have to trigger yourself before you change devices, and even then it comes with an expiry date. So this article runs in that order. First, what stays with your account versus what lives only on your handset. Then what the free backup really carries and what it drops, what happens when the password slips your mind, and what actually works in each device-change scenario.

KakaoTalk is South Korea's dominant messenger, and everything here follows Kakao's own support documentation. Menu names and screen paths reflect August 2026; the app updates often, so item positions shift slightly between versions and devices. If a path does not match, search by what the feature does rather than by its label. This article covers official features only. Unofficial extraction tools and rooting-based methods violate the terms of service and tend to end in a broken handset, so they are left out entirely.

1. What your Kakao account keeps and what your phone keeps

The reason backup feels confusing is a single assumption: that signing in will bring everything back. It brings some things back and leaves others behind, and the line between them is sharp.

What lives with your Kakao account on the server side is essentially information about who you are. Your friend list, your profile, the emoticons you purchased, the existence of the chat rooms you belong to. Sign in on a new device and this generally follows you.

The messages themselves, however, sit on your device. KakaoTalk does not warehouse your conversation history on a server to redistribute later. Drop your phone in water and the conversations inside go with it. That is why the account can be perfectly intact while the chats come back empty.

  • Usually returns on sign-in: friend list, profile photo and status message, purchased emoticons, the skeleton of your chat room list
  • Lives on the device and must be handled separately: the full conversation history, and the photos, videos and files exchanged inside chat rooms
  • Not safely held in either place: older media that has expired. On the free tier, re-downloading photos and files after a certain period is not guaranteed

So when you hear the word backup here, read it as temporarily lifting your conversations off the device. That is a completely different axis from protecting the account itself. Hacking attempts and lost-phone response are covered separately in our KakaoTalk account security guide, so this article stays on the data side.

2. What the free chat backup carries and what it drops

This is the most misunderstood point of all. The free backup built into KakaoTalk is, as its name says, a chat backup and not a full data backup. Going by Kakao's support documentation, what it carries is text and nothing else.

Item Free chat backup Note
Conversation text Included Effectively the whole of the backup
Photos and videos Excluded Multimedia is not a backup target
Attached files (documents) Excluded Same as above
Messages over 4,000 characters Excluded Long text pasted as one message falls here
Secret Chat Excluded Bound to the device, so it does not travel
Open Chat Excluded Handled separately from ordinary chats
Friend list and emoticons Not applicable These come back with the account instead

The row that genuinely shocks people is photos and videos. If you restore years of history and find the text intact but a blank space where every image used to be, the backup did not fail — that is how it works. Which means there is one step to take before you ever open the backup menu: save the photos you care about to your phone album or another cloud service first. Skipping that single step is the most common way people lose things.

This is also where the contrast with other messengers matters if you are reading from outside Korea. WhatsApp and LINE hang their backups on the phone platform's own cloud account, and WeChat leans heavily on direct device-to-device transfer. KakaoTalk does neither: it produces its own encrypted bundle, holds it briefly on Kakao's side, and hands it back when you sign in on the new phone. Because of that design, the questions you would ask about a Google Drive or iCloud backup — where is it stored, how much space does it take — mostly do not apply here.

The practical consequence is that habits carried over from another messenger will mislead you. If your instinct is that switching phones automatically brings the attachments along, KakaoTalk will break that expectation. Treat the free backup as a text transcript and plan your media separately. For how to structure that second layer, see our complete cloud backup guide; from here on this article stays with the conversations.

3. Running the backup, and why the path moves between versions

The backup itself takes about three minutes. The sequence goes like this.

  1. Open KakaoTalk and go to the More (…) tab.
  2. Tap Settings (the gear icon).
  3. Find the Backup entry inside Settings. Depending on the version it may sit under Chats as a chat backup item.
  4. Run the temporary chat backup. The word temporary in the Korean label tells you the character of this feature better than any explanation.
  5. Set the password that will encrypt the backup data.
  6. Wait until progress reaches 100 percent. Do not close the app or let the screen sleep partway through.

Why the order matters — the backup has to finish on the old device, before you sign in on the new one. Sign in on the new phone first and KakaoTalk on the old handset is signed out, and from that moment you can no longer run a backup. Doing this at home before you head to the shop is the safest approach.

If the menu name is not where you expect, use the search field at the top of Settings rather than hunting through submenus. Kakao reorganizes the app frequently, and entries move one level in or out fairly often. The wording of the feature tends to survive even when its location does not.

4. The backup password - forget it and nothing brings it back

This is the most important section in the article. The password you set during backup is separate from your Kakao account password and functions as the encryption key for the backup data itself. Which means that if you forget it, not even Kakao can open it.

An account password can be reset after identity verification. A backup password has no reissue concept at all. Customer support will tell you the same thing. The data sits on the server in a state you cannot open — and in terms of outcome, that is no different from never having backed up.

  • Decide where you will record it. A password manager or a locked note, somewhere you can still reach if the phone is gone.
  • Avoid values you have nowhere to copy. Writing it only in your phone's notes app means the password disappears along with the phone.
  • Simple values like a birthday are a poor choice. This key opens a backup file, so its strength genuinely matters.
  • Confirm it right before restoring. Entry attempts are limited, so check your memory in advance if you are unsure.
What ruins people in backups is rarely the technology. It is memory. A backup where you never decided where to write the password down is a backup you only half finished.

5. Fourteen days - the condition people miss most

The free backup runs on a clock. The restore window is 14 days, and once it passes the backup data is deleted automatically and can no longer be restored. That one condition is why the strategy of backing up early as a habit simply does not function here.

In other words this is a moving tool, not a storage tool. It is designed around a flow where you trigger it just before receiving a new device or deleting the app, and restore on the new phone immediately afterwards. Any plan that relies on a backup you made last month is already void.

  • Run the backup on the day of the switch, or the evening before.
  • Complete the restore on the new device as soon as you can. Delay and the 14 days pass quietly.
  • The same rule applies before sending a phone for repair or performing a factory reset.
  • If long-term retention is your goal, look at a paid subscription or separate storage instead (sections 7 and 8).

6. What actually happens in each device-change scenario

Outcomes differ by situation, so here it is as a table. One common misconception deserves clearing up first — moving between Android and iPhone is not categorically blocked. Kakao's guidance describes the free chat backup as restorable across different operating systems, which is precisely why the restore screen shows the backup time alongside the operating system the backup came from.

The reason a cross-platform move still feels lossier has a different cause: only text was ever going to travel. Between two devices of the same platform, the manufacturer's transfer tool moves your album wholesale and masks the photo loss. Cross the platform boundary and that safety net disappears, leaving the strong impression that all the photos were destroyed.

Situation Chat text Photos and videos Do this first
Android to Android Transfers via backup and restore Not carried by chat backup Move the album with the maker's transfer tool, then back up chats
iPhone to iPhone Transfers via backup and restore Not carried by chat backup Use whole-device migration, then back up chats
Android to or from iPhone Transfers via backup and restore Not carried by chat backup Save photos and files separately, then back up chats
Reinstalling the app on the same phone Restorable if you backed up Not carried Back up before deleting. Afterwards there is no route
Phone lost or broken, no backup Not recoverable Not recoverable Protect the account first — the data cannot be reversed
Number change only, same device Stays on the device Stays on the device Just update the number registered to the account

The last two rows are a different species. Losing a phone with no backup is not a recovery procedure at all but a damage-control one, and changing only your number never involved moving conversations in the first place. For what to tap first in the minutes after a phone goes missing, the account security guide is the accurate reference.

7. TalkCloud - what the paid tier changes

Kakao runs a subscription service that keeps conversations and media in the cloud. It was known for years as TalkDrawer Plus, and the name currently surfaced in the app menu is TalkCloud. Older guides in search results still use the previous name, which causes no end of confusion; as things stand you reach it through More (…) → TalkCloud.

Its character is fundamentally different from the free backup. If the free backup is a moving box, this is a cupboard you leave switched on.

Criterion Free chat backup TalkCloud (paid subscription)
What it carries Conversation text only Conversations plus photos, videos and files
How it runs You have to trigger it Held automatically while subscribed
Retention 14-day restore window For as long as the subscription lasts
Password Set separately, unrecoverable if lost Account based
Cost None Monthly fee (check the official page for price and capacity)
Chats from before you subscribed Not applicable Must be uploaded via the past-data backup function

That final row is where paid subscribers most often come unstuck. Only conversations from the moment you subscribed accumulate automatically. Everything older has to be pushed up through the past-data backup function, and if you reinstall the app without doing that, the older data cannot be pulled back.

If you plan to use TalkCloud for a device change, the sequence is strict. Subscription and backup must reach completion on the old device before you sign in on the new one. Signing in on the new phone while the backup is still running stops it where it stands. And if you withdraw from KakaoTalk entirely, conversations are deleted and can be neither viewed nor recovered.

8. Desktop KakaoTalk and chat export - a copy, not a backup

The third option is pulling conversations out as a file. The menu inside a chat room includes an item that exports the conversation as text, and desktop KakaoTalk can save an open conversation as well.

This serves a different purpose from backup, though. An exported text file cannot be pushed back into KakaoTalk. There is no feature that reads such a file on a new device and rebuilds the chat room. So the division of labour looks like this.

  • Export is right when: you need a specific conversation preserved as evidence, a record, or reference material
  • Export is wrong when: you want the chat rooms themselves back on a new phone — that is the job of backup and restore, or of TalkCloud
  • A practical combination: export the one or two conversations that truly matter as text, and handle the general move with backup and restore
  • Where the desktop client helps: the wider screen makes it far easier to scan a long history and pull out only the stretch you need

The exact position and label of the export menu varies by version and platform. Opening the menu at the top right of a chat room and scanning the conversation-related entries will usually find it. 9. What the backup will not contain

Translating the table in section 2 into lived experience gives you this list. Think of it as the catalogue of things people notice missing after a restore.

  • Every photo, video and file. Not a target of the free backup. By far the largest source of loss.
  • Messages longer than 4,000 characters. Meeting notes or long documents pasted as a single message land here.
  • Secret Chat. Its structure is bound to the device, so it does not move.
  • Open Chat. Handled differently from ordinary chat rooms. Re-entry conditions vary per room, so check the ones that matter in advance.
  • Expired media. Older photos and files are not guaranteed re-downloadable on the free tier. That problem sits upstream of backup.
  • The detail of voice and video call records. A different category from conversation text.
  • Files received only on another device. Something downloaded solely on desktop falls outside the phone backup's scope.

10. When the app is gone or the account is lost - the order of recovery

If it has already happened, sequence is what saves you time. Check what is possible before chasing what is not.

  1. Establish whether a backup exists. Reinstall, sign in, and if there is backup history the chat restore screen appears after you enter your profile details. No screen means there is nothing to bring back.
  2. Sign in with the original Kakao account. Creating a new account at this step resets your existing information and makes restoration impossible. This is the most common and most fatal mistake.
  3. Check the backup time and operating system. Both are displayed on the restore screen, so confirm with your own eyes that this is the backup you made before proceeding.
  4. Enter the backup password. The value from section 4.
  5. Count the days. If more than 14 have passed, the data has most likely already been deleted.
  6. With no backup, let the data go and move to account protection. If the phone is missing, blocking account misuse is far more urgent than the conversations.

Where to stop — searching for recovery software after losing chats surfaces a field of unofficial tools. They violate the terms of service, and installing them can destabilise the device or hand over your account credentials. When the official route says no, treating that as a genuine no is the cheaper outcome.

11. Five habits that cause the loss

The routes by which people actually lose conversations look remarkably similar.

  • Signing in on the new phone first. The old handset signs out and the backup opportunity evaporates. The order is always back up the old device, then sign in on the new one.
  • Turning off the screen before progress hits 100 percent. An interrupted backup is not a backup.
  • Recording the password only in the phone's notes app. Lose the phone and the key goes with it.
  • Assuming photos come along for the ride. The free backup contains no photos. The album is a separate errand.
  • Putting off the restore. After 14 days the data deletes itself.

One more worth adding: the factory reset before a repair is a surprisingly large hole. When a service centre says a reset is required it is easy to agree on the spot, but if you never ran a backup, that is the end of your conversations. Being able to say you will step outside and back up first is the whole skill.

12. What could not be confirmed

These are the items left without figures. They sit in territory that changes readily, so nothing has been filled in by estimate.

  • TalkCloud's monthly price and storage capacity. The subscription product page renders dynamically in the app and on the web, so the exact figure and capacity for any given moment have to be read from Kakao's official subscription pages.
  • The precise date TalkDrawer Plus was renamed and repriced as TalkCloud. What can be established is only that the current in-app label is TalkCloud.
  • How far TalkCloud supports Android to iPhone migration. The official device-change guidance does not state cross-platform compatibility. Treat it as a separate matter from the free chat backup's cross-OS restore.
  • The current path and label of the chat export menu. Variation across versions and platforms is wide enough that no single path is specified here.
  • Re-entry conditions for individual Open Chat rooms. These depend on each room's settings and cannot be generalised.

Conclusion: three things worth ten minutes today

To put it plainly, KakaoTalk chat backup is packing for a move, not buying insurance. It is not something you arrange once and forget; it is luggage you have to pack yourself on the day. Free backup carries text only, the window is 14 days, the password cannot be reissued — those three lines are the spine of this article.

Which leaves a short list. First, decide where the backup password gets written down. It has to live outside the phone. Second, save the photos from the conversations you care about to an album or a cloud service. Knowing that chat backup does not carry images blocks most of the loss on its own. Third, when a device change gets scheduled, run the backup that morning. A backup made in advance vanishes after 14 days.

If you have a lot of history and the photos are what you would miss, the paid subscription is worth weighing. Just remember that switching it on does not retroactively upload your older conversations; the past-data backup step has to be completed before anything old is genuinely protected. Whichever route you take, the principle holds — backup is something you can only do beforehand.
