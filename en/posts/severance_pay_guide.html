1. What Is Severance Pay? — Concept and Legal Basis

Severance pay (statutory retirement benefit) is a legally mandated payment that employers in Korea must make to eligible departing employees. It is governed by the Act on the Guarantee of Employees' Retirement Benefits and is designed to function as deferred compensation supporting post-employment financial security.

There are two main structures: the traditional severance pay system, where a lump sum is paid at retirement, and the retirement pension system, where funds are accumulated at a financial institution and paid as a lump sum or annuity upon retirement. Both systems share the same minimum total benefit standard, but differ in administration and tax treatment.

Important note: This article provides general educational information based on Korea's system as of June 2026. Rules vary by country, employment contract, and applicable collective agreement. Please consult the Ministry of Employment and Labor, the National Tax Service, or a qualified professional before making any claims or tax filings.

2. Eligibility Requirements — Continuous Service and Working Hours

The right to claim severance pay in Korea arises when both of the following conditions are met simultaneously:

  • Continuous service of at least 1 year: Uninterrupted employment under the same employer exceeding one year. Probationary and trial periods count toward continuous service.
  • At least 15 contracted hours per week: Averaged over a 4-week period. Employees working fewer than 15 hours per week (ultra-short-time workers) are excluded.

Employment type (permanent, contract, or part-time) does not matter. Even fixed-term contract workers are entitled to severance pay if both conditions are satisfied. Severance pay must be paid within 14 days of the retirement date as a general rule.

3. How to Calculate Severance Pay — Average Wage and Service Period

The core formula is:

Severance Pay = Average Daily Wage x 30 days x (Continuous Working Days / 365)

3.1 Calculating the Average Wage

The average daily wage equals the total wages paid in the 3 months before retirement divided by the total calendar days in that period. The total wages include base salary, regular bonuses (pro-rated to the 3-month period), and allowances. Whether welfare benefits such as meal and transportation allowances are included depends on the employer's policy and applicable law.

If the calculated average wage is lower than the ordinary wage (base salary plus fixed allowances), the ordinary wage is used instead. This protective mechanism ensures employees are not disadvantaged by unusually low wages in the final 3 months.

4. Retirement Pension Plans — DB, DC, and IRP Compared

Korea's retirement pension system pre-funds retirement benefits through financial institutions. There are three main types.

4.1 DB Plan (Defined Benefit)

The retirement benefit amount is fixed in advance based on the employee's average wage in the 3 months before retirement multiplied by years of service — the same structure as traditional severance pay. The employer bears investment risk and is responsible for maintaining sufficient reserves. DB plans suit employees who expect strong wage growth over their career or who prefer stable, predictable benefits.

4.2 DC Plan (Defined Contribution)

The employer's annual contribution is fixed (at least 1/12 of annual wages), and the employee directly manages the invested funds. Final benefits depend on investment returns. DC plans may be more advantageous when expected investment returns exceed wage growth, or when employees have strong investment skills and interest. Employees choose from funds, deposits, bonds, and other products.

4.3 IRP (Individual Retirement Pension)

An IRP is a personal retirement pension account that employees open themselves. Retirement benefits received from DB or DC plans can be transferred into it, and additional personal contributions are allowed. All income earners (employees, self-employed, public servants, etc.) can open an IRP. It offers tax deduction benefits on contributions and tax deferral on earnings.

5. Mid-Term Settlement and Early Withdrawal Restrictions

Since July 2012, mid-term settlement (interim payment) of severance pay has been restricted in principle to protect employees' retirement funds. Early withdrawal from DC plans and IRPs follows the same permitted-reason framework. Legally allowed reasons include:

  • A non-homeowner employee purchasing a home in their own name
  • A non-homeowner employee paying a lease or rental deposit for residential purposes
  • The employee, spouse, or dependents requiring more than 6 months of medical care for illness or injury
  • The employee having received a bankruptcy ruling or personal rehabilitation decision within the past 5 years
  • Major property loss due to a natural disaster or equivalent event

Supporting documentation is required to prove qualifying reasons. Arbitrary early withdrawal outside these circumstances is not permitted.

6. Retirement Income Tax — General Structure

Retirement pay in Korea is subject to retirement income tax, which is assessed separately from employment income tax. This separate taxation generally results in a lower effective tax rate compared to regular income tax on the same amount.

6.1 Key Features

  • Service period deduction: The longer the service period, the larger the deduction applied, lowering the effective tax rate.
  • Annualized income deduction: The tax calculation converts the lump sum as if it were earned annually over the service period, further reducing the effective rate for long-serving employees.
  • Approximate effective rates: As a general reference only — under 5 years of service: roughly 1–2%; around 10 years: 3–5%; over 20 years: 7–10%. Actual tax varies significantly by individual circumstances.

Retirement income tax calculation is complex. Use the National Tax Service's Hometax system (hometax.go.kr) or consult a tax accountant for accurate figures.

7. Using an IRP — Tax Deferral, Annuity vs Lump-Sum

Rolling retirement pay into an IRP account defers retirement income tax until actual withdrawal. The deferred tax amount continues to be invested, generating additional compound returns during the deferral period.

7.1 Tax Benefits of Annuity Withdrawal

Receiving IRP funds as an annuity after age 55 provides the following reduced tax rates (as of June 2026; subject to legislative changes):

  • Annuity payments over 10 years or fewer: 70% of standard retirement income tax rate (30% saving)
  • Annuity payments over 11 years or more: 60% of standard retirement income tax rate (40% saving)
  • Lifetime annuity: flat 3% tax rate regardless of age

7.2 Lump-Sum Withdrawal

Withdrawing as a lump sum triggers full retirement income tax withholding. Investment gains in the account are subject to miscellaneous income tax (16.5% including local tax). While annuity receipt is more tax-efficient, individual circumstances — liquidity needs, health, life expectancy — should inform the decision.

7.3 Personal Contribution Tax Deduction

Personal contributions to an IRP during employment qualify for a year-end tax deduction. Combined with pension savings accounts, a deduction applies to up to KRW 9 million per year, with pension savings alone capped at KRW 6 million (16.5% credit for total annual salary up to KRW 55M; 13.2% above that). Limits and rates are subject to tax law revisions.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Can short-term contract workers receive severance pay?

Yes, if they meet both the 1-year continuous service requirement and the 15-hour-per-week threshold. Whether repeated short-term contracts constitute "continuous service" depends on the Ministry of Employment and Labor guidelines and court precedent.

Q. Can employees switch from DB to DC plans?

A DC conversion cannot be made without employee consent. The employer must obtain agreement from a majority of employees or the labor union. The treatment of previously accrued benefits must also be confirmed during conversion.

Q. Must I transfer my retirement pay to an IRP account?

Retirement benefits from pension-enrolled workplaces are generally transferred to an IRP account by default. Exceptions exist for employees aged 55 or older or for small amounts (KRW 3 million or less). Confirm eligibility with your financial institution or the Ministry of Employment and Labor.

Q. What can I do if my employer doesn't pay severance pay?

If severance pay is not paid within 14 days after retirement, delayed interest may apply. You can file a wage claim (including severance pay) through the Ministry of Employment and Labor's civil complaint portal (minwon.moel.go.kr) or the nearest regional labor office. The wage guarantee system may also allow the government to pay on the employer's behalf in certain cases.
