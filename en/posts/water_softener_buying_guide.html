How to Choose a Water Softener - What the Machine Does and What It Does Not

Lowering hardness and filtering water for drinking were never the same job

Aug 25, 2026 Life & Practical

If you swapped the shower head and the white haze on the glass screen stayed, or the crust in the kettle never went away, or soap still refuses to lather, the usual explanation is not a bad product. It is that the product was doing a different job all along. The things sold under the word softener contain completely different chemistry inside: some actually lower hardness, some only delay precipitation, and some have nothing to do with hardness at all.

This piece inverts the usual buying order. First settle what hardness is and which side your own water falls on, then separate what each method does from what it cannot do, and only then price the consumables and the install. Run it in that order and a large share of homes reach a do not buy conclusion early. That is one of the outcomes this article is aiming for.

This article explains water hardness and treatment principles in general terms and is not medical advice. Product performance depends on the incoming water quality, the volume you use and the installation conditions, so nothing here guarantees how any particular unit will behave.

1. A softener is not a purifier, and that is where to start

What separates the two machines is purpose, not quality. Under Korean law, Article 3(7) of the Drinking Water Management Act defines a purifier as a device manufactured to deliver drinking water through a dispensing tap in a state that meets the drinking water quality standards, by physical, chemical or biological processes or a combination of them. The destination is written into the sentence: the drinking water quality standards.

A softener is not headed there. What it works on is the calcium and magnesium dissolved in the water, which is to say hardness. Lower the hardness and the basin keeps fewer white marks and soap lathers properly. That is not the same as the water having become safe to drink. Different destinations mean different questions.

  • The question you put to a purifier - what does it remove and how much, and does the output meet drinking water standards.
  • The question you put to a softener - how far does it lower hardness, for how many tonnes does that hold, and what does holding it cost.
  • The question for both - is my actual complaint about the water I drink, or the water I wash and clean with.

If drinking water is the worry, the right places are the purifier buying guide and the piece on rental total cost rather than this one. Everything below is about washing and cleaning water, and specifically about hardness.

2. What hardness actually is

Hardness is the amount of calcium, magnesium and related metals dissolved in water. The U.S. Geological Survey describes it as being caused by compounds of calcium and magnesium and by a variety of other metals. By convention that amount is not itemised by element but reported as a single figure in mg/L expressed as calcium carbonate. It is an agreement that lets different substances be measured on one ruler.

The bands are drawn at much the same places by different bodies. The World Health Organization and the U.S. Geological Survey break at identical points.

Three things are worth fixing in place before going further.

  • Different units make the same water look like a different number. Alongside mg/L as calcium carbonate you will meet grains per gallon and German degrees, so match the unit before comparing any two figures.
  • The World Health Organization proposes no health-based guideline value for hardness. High hardness is not in itself a health finding, and by the same token lowering it is not a health intervention.
  • Taste is genuinely affected. The organization puts the taste threshold for the calcium ion in the range of 100 to 300 mg/L depending on the anion it comes with, and notes the threshold for magnesium is probably lower. People who say the water tastes different are not imagining it.

3. Limescale and soap - one cause, two symptoms

The trouble hardness causes shows up along two paths, but the cause is single: calcium and magnesium ions meet other things and form compounds that do not dissolve well.

The first is scale, the white crust. Heat the water and the balance that kept the minerals dissolved breaks, so calcium carbonate settles out as a solid. The U.S. Geological Survey notes that solid deposits of calcium carbonate can form in water heaters, which reduce the life of equipment, raise the cost of heating water, lower the efficiency of electric water heaters and clog pipes. Scale therefore appears wherever the water gets hot first: the base of a kettle, the inside of a heater, the holes in a shower head, the area around a washing machine element.

The second is soap residue. Soap reacting with calcium and magnesium ions produces salts that dissolve poorly, so instead of lather you get a cloudy, slippery film. The U.S. Geological Survey calls this soap scum, formed when soap reacts with the calcium that is relatively high in hard water. The World Health Organization records that water with hardness above roughly 200 mg/L may cause scale deposition in treatment works, distribution systems and pipework and tanks within buildings, and will also result in high soap consumption and subsequent scum formation.

  • Within one home it looks different in different places. If the cold tap is fine while the heater and kettle are badly affected, the phenomenon you are dealing with is temperature-driven precipitation.
  • Soap and synthetic detergent behave differently. Traditional soap is the sensitive one and leaves the residue, while body wash and dish detergent based on synthetic surfactants show it far less. That is why the person in the household who uses bar soap notices the problem first.
  • Not every white mark is hardness. Spots left where water dried contain other dissolved solids too, and rusty water or black particles from the plumbing are a different problem entirely.

4. What hardness looks like in Korean tap water

What the rules fix is an upper limit. Under the Korean Rules on Drinking Water Quality Standards and Testing, Annex 1, the aesthetic parameters set hardness at 1,000 mg/L or less in general, with 300 mg/L for tap water and 1,200 mg/L for bottled saline groundwater and deep sea water. The item is not applied to spring water and saline groundwater.

This is where a misreading starts. The figure 300 does not say that tap water is around that level; it says nothing may go above that line. The classification itself, aesthetic parameters, means items that have not been shown to harm health but may cause displeasure. Actual values move with whether the source is river water, a reservoir or groundwater, with which treatment plant serves you, and with the season.

So this article does not print regional hardness figures. Looking up a recent number for where you live is the only method that works, and the public channels already exist under the Korean system.

  • Water quality results from your local waterworks authority - published per treatment plant and per month, often split into raw water, treated water and tap samples.
  • The national waterworks information system - statutory water quality information searchable by area.
  • The nationwide tap water quality dataset on the public data portal - provided by K-water, carrying the monthly statutory plant-level records that local and regional water suppliers manage.
  • Seoul publishes an annual tap water quality report - issued by the end of June of the following year under Article 31 of the Water Supply and Waterworks Installation Act, covering quality from the intake through to the tap.

Once you have the number, drop it into the table above. If it sits near the soft band, the rest of this article is not for you. Few purchases waste money as reliably as a hardness-lowering appliance fitted to water that was not hard.

5. What each method does and does not do

Products sold under the word softener contain several different principles. The core of this section is that only the top two rows below actually lower hardness.

Ion exchange resin, cartridgeSame principle. With no regeneration, spent capacity is the end of itSame list, and the small capacity makes the effective window shortCartridge capacity divided by hardness. Harder water ends it sooner

None of this makes phosphate or vitamin cartridges useless. They simply do a different job. Where the only goal is less scale, phosphate chemistry may be enough, and for someone bothered by chlorine smell a vitamin cartridge may be exactly the answer. The problem is the habit of calling all three by one word, which leaves people who bought a unit to lower hardness owning something unrelated to hardness. If the specification does not say ion exchange resin, the safe assumption is that the product does not lower hardness.

One note on product standards. In the United States, NSF/ANSI 44 covers residential cation exchange water softeners and tests material extraction, structural durability and softening performance. It is a US standard, not a mandatory requirement in Korea, so where a certification mark appears it is worth checking what that certification was actually issued against.

6. Replacement intervals and running cost are the real price

What sets the cost of a softener is not the unit but how fast the consumables disappear. And that speed is not decided by the product; it is decided by your hardness and your water use. Two households buying the same model on the same day can end up a factor of two apart on annual cost.

The arithmetic finishes once these lines are filled in. If any is blank, the product is not yet in a comparable state.

  • Consumable price times replacements per year - this single line usually overtakes the purchase price within the first year, cartridge-type shower units above all.
  • For regenerating units, salt, regeneration water and power - every regeneration sends water to drain and takes electricity to move valves, and the salt has to be topped up indefinitely.
  • The basis behind the capacity claim - a figure quoted only as so many tonnes, with no statement of at what hardness, cannot be compared. Double the hardness and the throughput roughly halves.
  • Installation and removal - anything that touches plumbing carries a fitting cost, then a second cost for removal and making good when you move.

Approach it through rental and one more axis appears. Not the monthly fee but the total across the whole contract term, plus the cost of stopping partway, is what makes two contracts comparable. That method is set out in the piece on rental total cost and early termination. Only the appliance differs; the structure is identical.

7. Installation points - how far are you treating

Even with the same method, where you fit it changes the coverage and the conditions completely. And most disappointment comes from a mismatch between where the problem is and where the unit went. Spotting on the kitchen sink treated by fitting something to the shower head, for example.

FormCoverageWhat it needsCheck before buying

Pressure is an axis to calculate separately. Water passing through a media bed loses pressure, and the loss grows as the media clogs. A home with generous pressure will never notice, but upper floors and ageing pipework where pressure was already marginal generate complaints from the day of installation. In those homes the honest question to ask first is whether the shower is satisfying right now.

8. Which homes this is worth it for, and which it is not

The judgement rests on three conditions holding at the same time. Miss one and the money mostly evaporates.

  • Condition one - hardness where you live is genuinely on the high side, judged by the number you looked up through the channels above.
  • Condition two - scale or soap residue is causing repeated, concrete inconvenience. Descaling the kettle constantly, or the shower screen going cloudy within days.
  • Condition three - the place the inconvenience occurs and the place you would fit the unit line up. Treating a scaled water heater by changing the shower head does nothing, because of the order the water travels in.
  • The cases where it is not needed - soft-water areas where the name on the box did the persuading, goals that are really about drinking water, problems like rusty water or odour that are not hardness at all, and scale that exists but wipes off without bothering anyone.

One caveat belongs here. The World Health Organization notes that soft water below 100 mg/L may have a low buffering capacity and so be more corrosive for water pipes, while explicitly adding but not necessarily cation exchange softened water. The two are not the same kind of soft. Even so, driving already-low hardness lower is a direction with very little to gain.

9. What not to expect

This is where softener marketing most often crosses the line. Handling hardness and every item below are separate problems.

  • It is not disinfection. Ion exchange softening does not remove bacteria. If anything the resin bed is a place where water sits, which makes it a part that needs maintenance.
  • It is not heavy metal, nitrate or pesticide removal. Extension guidance states flatly that softening does not remove bacteria, hydrogen sulfide, silt or sand, lead, nitrate, pesticides and many other organic and inorganic compounds.
  • It is not a verdict on drinkability. Having passed through a softener does not mean the water meets drinking water quality standards. The device that takes those standards as its destination is the purifier as legally defined.
  • Sodium goes up. Regenerating ion exchange adds sodium in proportion to the hardness removed. The Water Quality Association puts it at roughly 8 mg/L of sodium per grain per gallon of hardness removed. Potassium chloride regeneration exists as an alternative. If you are managing sodium or potassium in your diet, that belongs with a clinician rather than a manual.

Skin and hair claims need particular care. A twelve week randomised controlled trial in the United Kingdom enrolled 336 children aged six months to sixteen years with moderate to severe atopic eczema and found no meaningful difference in symptom scores between the arm that had an ion exchange softener installed immediately alongside usual care and the arm that received usual care alone. The investigators concluded that ion exchange softeners should not be recommended for treating moderate to severe eczema in children. Improvements reported by parents came from unblinded measures and were read as response bias.

That result does not prove nothing changes for anyone. What it does establish is that skin improvement cannot serve as the reason for the purchase. Better lathering and less residue after rinsing are chemistry and are real, but the condition of skin or hair sits on a different level. Where there is a symptom, the route is a clinician, not an appliance.

What could not be confirmed

  • Measured hardness by region and treatment plant in Korea - the public channels are confirmed to exist, but no current nationwide comparison table was opened while writing this. So no figures are printed here, only the places to look.
  • Korean labelling duties for softeners - purifiers have a statutory definition under the Drinking Water Management Act, but whether any Korean rule compels a softener to declare its hardness reduction performance was not confirmed. NSF/ANSI 44 is a US standard.
  • Service life of phosphate and vitamin cartridges - the principles are clear, but no independent test data on the real effective window or reduction rate of units sold in the Korean market was found.
  • How much softening cuts detergent use - the World Health Organization records that hard water raises soap consumption, without quantifying the saving in the other direction.

10. Checklist before buying

  • You have a recent hardness figure for your own supply, and you have confirmed it is in mg/L as calcium carbonate.
  • You have picked one complaint: drinking water, washing water, or scale in heating appliances.
  • You have read the specification and know whether the unit contains ion exchange resin, phosphate chemistry or something else.
  • The capacity claim states how many tonnes at what hardness.
  • You have multiplied consumable price by replacements per year to get a first-year total.
  • For a regenerating unit, salt refilling, a drain and power are all available at the intended location.
  • The installation point sits upstream in the flow of the place the problem appears.
  • If renting you have checked the making-good terms, and in a shared block you have checked whether building management approval is needed.
