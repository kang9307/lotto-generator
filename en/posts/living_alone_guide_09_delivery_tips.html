Living Alone Guide Part 9: Delivery Tips

What to Do When You're Not Home

Working all day, packages coming all day...

If you work during the day, receiving packages in person is hard. A "delivered" text arrives while you are out, or your building has no security desk to hold parcels.

This guide collects ways to receive packages and food deliveries safely when you live alone. It reflects the Korean delivery setting, and the compensation rules and reporting steps below come only from official guidance.

1. Solutions for Missed Deliveries

Option 1: Concierge/Front Desk

  • If your building has one, set it as default
  • Add delivery note: "Please leave with concierge"
  • Pick up after work
  • Rules on holding parcels differ by building, so check the management rules first

Option 2: Parcel Lockers

  • Use the locker if your apartment or officetel has one
  • The location and passcode (or verification method) are usually sent by text or app
  • Many allow pickup at any hour, but operation differs by building

Option 3: Leave at Door

  • Add note: "Safe to leave at door"
  • Avoid it for expensive items - loss risk
  • Request a delivery photo as proof
  • According to the Korean standard terms revision, leaving a parcel at a place agreed with the customer counts as completed handover. If you agree to the doorstep and it disappears, responsibility is harder to sort out, so check the photo

Option 4: Pickup Points

  • Some carriers and sellers offer pickup at convenience stores
  • Select pickup option when ordering
  • Collect during store hours

It is better to pick a place that stays safe when nobody is home, such as a concierge desk or a parcel locker. The doorstep carries a loss risk.

2. Using Delivery Notes Effectively

Good Delivery Notes

  • "Please leave with building concierge, Apt #XXX"
  • "If not home, leave at door. Photo please"
  • "Use parcel locker if available"
  • "Please call before delivery"

Safety Tips

  • Don't write "lives alone" or "single person" on the address
  • Consider using surname plus placeholder instead of full name
  • A waybill prints name, address and phone number together, so don't add more personal details to the note

3. Food Delivery Tips

Single Portion Ordering

  • Minimum orders and fees: They vary by app, restaurant and time of day; a single portion can make the delivery fee a large share of the bill
  • Portion: Order extra only when you will really eat it as the next meal (leftovers thrown away are a waste)
  • Discounts: Check the conditions of first-order deals and coupons

Storing Leftovers

  • Refrigerate if not eating immediately
  • Noodles get soggy - eat those fast
  • Reheat thoroughly the next day

Using Delivery Apps

  • Compare prices across apps, counting the delivery fee and minimum order
  • Consider pickup if there is a pickup discount
  • Read photo reviews, keeping in mind that some reviews may be promotional

4. Extended Absence

When Traveling

  • Reschedule expected deliveries
  • Pause subscription boxes
  • Ask a trusted person to collect important packages

5. Safe Package Receiving

Don't Reveal You Live Alone

  • Use a title after the name or a gender-neutral name
  • When asked, imply someone else will receive
  • Avoid late-night deliveries

Contactless Delivery

  • Prefer "leave at door" option
  • Use intercom only, don't open door
  • Wait for the driver to leave before collecting

Safety comes first. Especially at night, verify it is actually a delivery person.

6. When a Package Is Lost or Damaged

The compensation rules below follow the Korean Fair Trade Commission standard terms for parcel delivery and the government guidance that explains them (Easy-to-Find Korean Law). Actual payouts depend on the carrier's terms and the case.

SituationRuleNote
Declared value left blank on the waybillCompensation cap of KRW 500,000For valuables, there is an option to declare the value and pay a surcharge
Value declared with surcharge paidUp to the top value of the declared-value bracketThe 2020 revision was described as raising the cap to as much as KRW 3 million (the bracket table itself was not checked)
Whole parcel lost in transitFreight refund plus loss based on the declared valueNo cap if the carrier or staff acted intentionally or with gross negligence
Partial loss or damageNotify the carrier within 14 days of receiptLiability can lapse if notice is late; claims lapse one year after receipt
Timing of paymentPriority payment within 30 days of submitting proof of lossMeant to stop delays caused by carrier, agency and driver blaming each other

What to do when you think it is lost

  1. Screenshot the delivery text, photo and any note about where it was left
  2. Tell the carrier right away - a late report makes cause and responsibility hard to establish and the carrier may refuse to pay
  3. Do not rely on a phone call alone; keep a case number and reply in writing (text, app, or certified mail)
  4. If the carrier admitted the loss but does not pay, call the 1372 Consumer Helpline (no area code)
  5. Then use the Korea Consumer Agency remedy process, and consider a payment order (demand procedure) if that fails

7. Delivery-Themed Scam Texts and Waybill Data

Smishing that impersonates couriers

  • Do not tap the link or phone number in texts like "address mismatch, please correct your address"; delete them
  • Track parcels in the courier app or the shop you ordered from
  • Never enter ID, identity verification or financial details through a text link
  • If you already tapped or an app may be installed, call the Korea Internet & Security Agency at 118 (no area code, free). Urgent hacking and virus help is available 24/7; general consultation is weekdays 09:00-18:00

Removing personal data from waybills

  • Peel off sticker-type waybills before discarding
  • If you cannot, black out name, address and phone number with a permanent marker or similar
  • Barcodes also hold data, so remove them too
  • Parcels left at the door for a long time raise exposure and theft risk, so a safe parcel locker is recommended

8. Smart Shopping

Online Shopping Tips

  • Free shipping: Don't buy unnecessary items just to hit free shipping
  • Subscriptions: Unit prices vary by terms, so compare them yourself
  • Reviews: Check verified purchase reviews

Early Morning Delivery

  • Great for fresh groceries
  • Collect before work
  • Be careful with frozen items in summer

9. Checklist

When Ordering

  • ☐ Write detailed delivery instructions
  • ☐ Set a backup delivery location (concierge, locker, door)
  • ☐ Confirm contact number
  • ☐ For valuables, check whether the value is declared on the waybill

Food Delivery

  • ☐ Compare prices across apps, delivery fee included
  • ☐ Check coupon conditions
  • ☐ Read reviews

After Receiving

  • ☐ Remove personal data from the waybill before discarding
  • ☐ Do not tap links in courier-themed texts

Wrap-up

When you live alone, deliveries are a big part of daily life. Handled well they are convenient; left unmanaged they lead to lost parcels and exposed personal data. The keys are good delivery notes, and keeping records and reporting quickly when something goes missing.

# Checklist: Receiving Packages and Deliveries Safely

As of 2026-09-29. Based on Korean rules; compensation depends on the carrier's terms and the case.

## 1. Pick a way to receive when you are out

| Situation | Method | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Building has a security desk | Hold at the desk | Availability differs by building |
| Building has a parcel locker | Parcel locker | Location and code arrive by text or app |
| Neither | Doorstep + delivery photo | Avoid for valuables. Leaving it at an agreed place counts as delivered |
| Long absence | Reschedule, pause subscriptions, ask someone to collect | |

## 2. When ordering
- [ ] The delivery note names the exact drop-off place
- [ ] The address does not say "lives alone" and the name is surname plus placeholder
- [ ] For valuables, I checked whether the value is declared on the waybill (blank means a KRW 500,000 cap)
- [ ] I avoided late-night delivery

## 3. Steps for a lost or damaged parcel
1. Screenshot the delivery text, photo and drop-off note
2. Tell the carrier right away (partial loss or damage: within 14 days of receipt)
3. Keep the case number and reply in a text, app message or certified mail
4. If the carrier admits it but does not pay, call the 1372 Consumer Helpline
5. Use the Korea Consumer Agency remedy process, and consider a payment order if it stays unresolved

Rules: priority payment within 30 days of submitting proof; claims lapse one year after receipt.

## 4. After receiving
- [ ] I removed or blacked out the waybill (name, address, phone, barcode) before discarding
- [ ] I did not tap links in "address mismatch" style texts
- [ ] If I did tap or an app may be installed, I call 118 (Korea Internet & Security Agency)
