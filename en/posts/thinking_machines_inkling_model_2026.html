Inkling: Thinking Machines Lab's 975B Open-Weight MoE Model, Explained

A frontier model trained from scratch with fully released weights - what its MoE design and open-weight status actually mean

2026-07-17 Tech & IT

Introduction: Mira Murati's First Frontier Model Arrives Open-Weight

On July 15, 2026, Thinking Machines Lab — the startup former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati founded in February 2025 — released its first production language model, Inkling. Two things stand out. First, it is a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) transformer with 975B total parameters but only 41B active per token. Second, Thinking Machines Lab trained the model from scratch itself and released the full weights to the public.

Until now, Thinking Machines Lab was known mainly for Tinker, its fine-tuning platform — essentially an infrastructure company that helped teams customize other labs' models, without a frontier model of its own. Inkling is the company's first foundation model, which makes this more than just another model launch: it marks a shift in what kind of company Thinking Machines Lab actually is. This post, prompted by the story surfacing on hada.io, is my own read on what Inkling is and why it arrived in this particular shape. It is grounded in material and benchmarks public as of July 2026, and it sticks to what can be verified rather than repeating marketing language.

1. Thinking Machines Lab and Inkling - What Actually Shipped

1.1 Murati's Second Bet

Thinking Machines Lab was founded in February 2025, just months after Mira Murati left OpenAI, alongside former OpenAI colleagues including John Schulman, Barrett Zoph, Lilian Weng, Andrew Tulloch, and Luke Metz. It raised $2 billion at a $10 billion valuation in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz in June 2025, and reports later surfaced of talks for a new round at roughly a $50 billion valuation — a rapid rise for a company barely a year old. That backdrop gives Inkling's release real weight: it's the first tangible answer to the market's question of whether Murati's team can actually ship a frontier-class model, not just raise money on the promise of one.

1.2 Core Specs

Inkling is a 66-layer decoder-based transformer. Each MoE layer holds 256 routed experts and 2 shared experts, with 6 routed experts activated per token. It was pretrained from scratch on 45 trillion tokens spanning text, image, audio, and video, then post-trained with large-scale asynchronous reinforcement learning across more than 30 million rollouts. Context length reaches up to 1M tokens on the Hugging Face weights, while the Tinker API offers 64K and 256K options. Reasoning effort is adjustable between 0.2 and 0.99, letting you trade cost against depth of reasoning depending on task difficulty. A lighter preview, Inkling-Small (276B total / 12B active), was released alongside it.

2. What Is Mixture of Experts? What 975B Total, 41B Active Actually Means

2.1 Separating "Total Capacity" From "What's Actually Used"

A standard dense transformer engages every parameter it has for every token it processes. MoE works differently: the model contains multiple "expert" sub-networks, and a router picks only a subset of them for each token. For Inkling, the total stored knowledge and capability lives across 975B parameters, but computing any single token only calls on 41B of them. The rest sit idle until the router decides they're needed.

2.2 Why This Architecture - Efficiency and Serving Cost

The upside is straightforward: you can scale up a model's representational capacity (975B total) while keeping the actual inference compute (41B active) at the level of a much smaller model. In other words, you get a "large knowledge warehouse" and "fast response speed" at the same time. From a serving-cost angle, this means Inkling can offer capabilities in the ballpark of a 975B-class model while spending compute closer to what a 41B model would need — considerably cheaper to run than serving a 975B dense model outright. There's a tradeoff, though: all 975B parameters still have to be loaded into memory, so total GPU memory requirements remain far larger than a genuinely 41B dense model's, even though per-token compute is light. "Light on compute, heavy on memory" is the accurate way to describe it. Inkling's router is sigmoid-based and reportedly uses an auxiliary-loss-free load-balancing bias to keep traffic from concentrating on a handful of experts — a modern answer to MoE's chronic expert-imbalance problem.

3. The Practical Value of a 1M Context and Multimodality

3.1 What a Million Tokens Changes

The Hugging Face weights support up to a 1M-token context window — enough to hold an entire mid-sized codebase or several thick books in a single pass. That matters for workloads where you want the model to answer with the full picture in view instead of a document chopped into fragments: repository-wide analysis, or reviewing sprawling logs and contracts in one shot. Note, though, that the Tinker API only exposes 64K and 256K context options; to actually use the full 1M window, you need to self-host the Hugging Face weights.

3.2 Native Text, Image, and Audio Processing

Inkling is designed to understand and reason over images and audio natively, without a separate conversion module. That loses less information than pipelines that caption images into text or transcribe audio before feeding it to a text-only model. In practice, it means a single model can handle mixed real-world input — meeting recordings, documents interleaved with screenshots, reports containing images — without stitching together separate tools.

4. What "Open-Weight" Means - Why It Isn't Open Source

4.1 Releasing Weights ≠ Full Open Source

Inkling's weights were released on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 license, which is itself a generous license allowing broad commercial use, modification, and redistribution. But under the Open Source AI Definition (OSAID) finalized by the Open Source Initiative (OSI) in October 2024, being genuinely open source requires releasing not just weights but training code and sufficient documentation of the training data to make the model reproducible. Thinking Machines Lab has not disclosed the composition of its 45-trillion-token training corpus or its training code. So, strictly speaking, Inkling is not an open-source model — it's an open-weight model. This distinction applies just as much to Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, and Gemma, most of which are casually called "open source" in 2026 despite falling into the same category.

4.2 Why It Matters in Practice

This isn't just a philosophical distinction. License terms determine the scope of commercial use, the conditions for redistributing derivative models, and restrictions on specific use cases. Llama, for instance, is often described as open but actually restricts certain commercial uses through its acceptable use policy, while Apache 2.0 licenses adopted by Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen, and now Inkling place far fewer constraints on commercial use. On reproducibility, without published training data, it's much harder for outsiders to verify why a model produces a given answer or where its biases originate. The first thing to get right in practice is not mistaking "open-weight" for "fully transparent AI."

5. The 2026 Open-Weight Landscape - Inkling vs Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM

5.1 The Landscape: Chinese Labs Have Been Leading

Through the first half of 2026, the open-weight race has effectively been led by Chinese labs. DeepSeek drove prices down, Qwen pushed the broadest lineup with the most permissive licensing, Kimi (Moonshot) specialized in agentic coding, and GLM (Zhipu) matched Western closed models on long-horizon coding performance — each carving out its own edge. US open-weight players, by comparison, have mostly been Meta's Llama, Nvidia's Nemotron, and Google's Gemma, and their presence relative to Chinese models has been comparatively muted. Mistral out of Europe forms another axis with permissive licensing, but hasn't been competing at the very top on raw performance.

5.2 Where Inkling Sits - "Leading US Open-Weight," With Caveats

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Inkling scores 41, ahead of the previous top US open-weight model, Nemotron 3 Ultra (38), and higher than Gemma 4 31B (29) and gpt-oss-120b (24). On the GDPval-AA v2 agentic benchmark, Inkling's 1238 Elo beats Kimi K2.6 (1190) and DeepSeek V4 Flash (1189), and it posts the highest score among compared models on FORTRESS Adversarial (78.0%), a benchmark for resisting adversarial prompts. That supports calling it the most competitive non-Chinese open-weight model since Llama 3. But it doesn't win across the board: on Terminal Bench 2.1, a long-horizon coding benchmark, Inkling scores 63.8% against GLM 5.2's 82.7%, and it also trails GLM 5.2 and Kimi K2.6 on HLE, AIME 2026, and SWE-Bench Verified. Inkling is the best US open-weight model — not the best open-weight model, period.

6. Why Developers and Enterprises Choose Open-Weight Models

6.1 Self-Hosting and Privacy

Holding the weights yourself means prompts and data never have to pass through a third-party API server. For regulated industries and teams working with proprietary code or documents, that alone is often decisive. Inkling is also available through third-party API providers such as TogetherAI, Fireworks, Modal, Databricks, and Baseten — but because the Apache 2.0 weights exist, the option to run it entirely on your own infrastructure, bypassing all of them, remains open.

6.2 Fine-Tuning and Cost Structure

Thinking Machines Lab paired Inkling's release with its Tinker platform, putting fine-tuning front and center. Teams can fine-tune on their own data to build domain-specific variants, shifting the cost basis from per-token API pricing to whatever hardware they own. Tinker's limited-time 50% launch discount signals a clear intent to lower the barrier to fine-tuning. That said, self-hosting a 975B-scale model demands substantial GPU memory, so smaller teams are likely better served going through an API or using Inkling-Small (276B total / 12B active) instead.

7. Limitations and What Still Needs Verification

7.1 The 63% Hallucination Number

The most notable warning sign is a reported hallucination rate of 63%, with accuracy around 40%, on AA-Omniscience-style benchmarks. That means Inkling's answers shouldn't be trusted at face value for fact-sensitive work — law, medicine, finance, or anywhere a wrong answer is costly. Thinking Machines Lab itself flags roleplay and indirect prompt injection as residual safety risks on its project page, and says it explicitly trained toward faithfulness as a target — an effort to reduce risk, not a guarantee that the risk is gone.

7.2 Benchmarks Are Still Mixed

Inkling leads among US open-weight models, but compared with the top Chinese open-weight models — GLM 5.2, Kimi K2.6 — it falls behind on several coding and long-horizon reasoning benchmarks. Don't get anchored on the "best US model" framing; verify it against your own golden set of real tasks — coding, document summarization, agentic workflows — before deciding how much to lean on it.

Conclusion: What to Check Today

Inkling is symbolically significant as the first frontier-class open-weight model from Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab. Its 975B-total, 41B-active MoE design is a sensible way to pursue large knowledge capacity alongside relatively light inference cost, and the 1M context plus native text, image, and audio multimodality are strengths you can feel immediately in real workloads. Releasing Apache 2.0 weights genuinely broadens the options for teams that care about self-hosting, fine-tuning, and privacy.

At the same time, this post doesn't overstate the case. Inkling leads the US open-weight camp, but it still trails top Chinese open-weight models like GLM 5.2 and Kimi K2.6 on several benchmarks, and a 63% hallucination rate is something you must verify against your own data before adopting it for real work. It's also worth remembering that "open-weight" doesn't mean "fully transparent, flawless model." The 2026 open-weight race is now accelerating on both the US and Chinese sides simultaneously, and Inkling reads most accurately as the signal that a serious US contender has properly entered that race.
