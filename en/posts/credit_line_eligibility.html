What Actually Decides Your Korean Overdraft Credit Line

Where the approved limit comes from, why the unused portion counts in full under DSR, and what changes the moment the account exists - as of August 2026

Aug 11, 2026 Finance & Economy

First - what this covers and where it stops

This describes the Korean system as of August 2026 and is not financial advice. No bank-specific product names, rates, or limit figures appear here, because they differ by product and change monthly. DSR caps and their scope are policy values that have moved over time, and the Financial Services Commission has already flagged a planned expansion of DSR coverage in its 2026 household debt management plan. Your approval, limit, and rate are not settled by anything written here; the lender decides them through underwriting. Before applying, check FINE (fine.fss.or.kr), run by Korea’s Financial Supervisory Service, along with the product manual and terms from the lender itself.

A credit line is not borrowed money. It is the right to borrow

If your account shows minus 3 million won against a 50 million won limit, what the bank handed you was never the 3 million. It was the right to pull 50 million at any moment. Almost everything else in this article follows from that one distinction. Interest attaches to the 3 million you drew. Regulation and every other lender’s underwriting look at the 50 million.

Compare a credit line to a lump-sum personal loan on the same terms and the comparison breaks. A lump-sum loan fixes its principal the instant it is disbursed, and that principal is the debt. A credit line lets the debt move every day, sometimes down to zero. The contract, however, does not disappear when the balance does.

AxisLimit-type loan (overdraft line)Lump-sum personal loan
How money leaves Draw and repay at will inside the committed limit The full amount lands once, at disbursement

At a zero balance Interest is zero. The contract is still alive Full repayment ends the contract

That last row is the reason the rates differ. The bank has to keep 50 million available for a moment it cannot predict, and the cost of standing ready gets recovered somewhere. Same borrower, same bank, and a credit line still struggles to price below a lump-sum loan. For the actual rate levels, the comparative disclosure at the Korea Federation of Banks consumer portal is the accurate source.

This article stays on how the limit gets set and what changes after the account exists. The mechanics of daily interest accrual, whether a line works as an emergency fund, and repayment sequencing are handled separately in Overdraft Credit Line Complete Guide - Structure, Interest, Uses, and Cautions.

What splits the limit - the axes underwriting actually reads

No single number produces a limit. Values from different layers filter the result in sequence, and the lowest one survives as your final limit. High income stops mattering the moment the regulatory ceiling binds first, and regulatory headroom is worthless if the lender’s internal grade pulls the number down on its own.

FactorWhat it does in underwritingCan you move it
Income The baseline for repayment capacity and the denominator of DSR. The recognized amount itself shifts with the income type (wage, business, pension, other) and how it is documented Not quickly. But assembling the documentation is available today
Employment type and tenure Permanent staff, contract worker, freelancer, and sole proprietor each get different income recognition and employment verification. This is where stability is read Tenure changes it. The months right after a job move read poorly
Credit score and internal grade Splits approval and the rate band. Lenders do not use the bureau score raw; they run internal models alongside it Yes, as a clean repayment record accumulates
Existing debt The DSR numerator. A credit line enters at its full limit, not its balance The one axis you can cut immediately, by repaying, reducing the limit, or closing
Regulatory cap (DSR) The ceiling those values build together. A willing lender still cannot exceed it Only indirectly, by reducing debt
Relationship and salary transfers Used as a preferential item under some lenders’ internal rules Yes, though the size of the effect is not disclosed

Only the fourth row is genuinely within reach. Income does not change overnight, credit scores move on a scale of months, and regulation is not something a borrower negotiates. Cleaning up a credit line you never use, by contrast, takes a few minutes in a banking app and shows up in the very next underwriting run. How the score itself is built is broken down factor by factor in A Complete Guide to Improving Your Credit Score in Korea.

Under DSR, a credit line counts at its full limit, not its usage

This is the single most important sentence here. Explaining the total borrowings figure that decides whether borrower-level DSR applies, the Financial Services Commission policy Q&A states that a limit-type loan is measured by the limit amount rather than the amount actually used. An account resting at zero with a 50 million won limit is counted as a 50 million won loan.

The effect lands in two layers.

  • Whether you fall under the rule at all changes. Borrower-level DSR applies once total borrowings pass a threshold, and the limit amount is inside that total. An untouched line can be what pushes you into the regulated band.
  • Your remaining headroom shrinks. Inside the band, annual principal and interest against annual income must stay under the cap, and the full limit converts into a repayment burden that pushes that ratio up.

Borrower-level DSR has long run at 40 percent for banks and 50 percent for non-bank institutions. Older Q&A pages on the Commission’s own site still show 60 percent for the non-bank side, written before the figure was lowered. When you check a regulatory ratio, look at when the page was written before you trust the number.

Stress DSR sits on top of that. The three-phase plan announced by the Commission on 20 May 2025 and effective 1 July 2025 covers what it describes as virtually every household loan subject to DSR across all sectors, with a stress rate of 1.50 percent. Personal loans fall in scope when the balance exceeds 100 million won. The stress rate is not interest you pay; it is a hypothetical rate added only when the limit is computed, so it works by shrinking what you can borrow rather than by raising what you owe. Phase-by-phase scope and the arithmetic are covered in Complete Guide to Korea’s 3-Phase Stress DSR.

With a large loan such as a mortgage ahead, reducing or closing an unused credit line is the most direct way to lower the figure that enters the DSR calculation. That is not encouragement to borrow more; it is a description of how the arithmetic runs on identical facts. The mortgage side is laid out in Korean Mortgage Loans Complete Guide.

What changes the moment the account exists

People treat an untouched overdraft line as if it were effectively nothing. It is unmistakably present in at least two places.

  • Every other lender’s underwriting. The limit amount is inside total borrowings, so nobody calculates your next application as though the line were absent.
  • The new credit event and the inquiry trail. The pull for underwriting and the new agreement both persist in credit records.

One common confusion deserves clearing up. DSR calculation and credit scoring are separate systems. Counting the full limit is a statement about a regulatory computation set by the authorities. How a credit bureau reflects an unused limit inside its score is a different question, and the scoring models are not published, which means any claim that opening a line costs a specific number of points has no source behind it. The point-drop figures circulating online do not earn belief.

Restricting this to what is certain: the regulatory computation counts the entire limit as debt. The direction of any scoring effect can be guessed, but its magnitude is undisclosed. Your own score and the items driving it are far quicker to read directly through the free access services the credit bureaus provide.

Is leaving it unused the answer - two faces of an idle limit

Measured purely in interest, an unused limit is free, because interest attaches only to the drawn balance. Interest is the only part that is free.

  • It is not free in regulatory terms. As above, the entire limit lands in DSR and in total borrowings.
  • Fees may exist. Some products carry clauses tied to the committed limit. You cannot know without reading the fee section of the product manual.
  • Long dormancy can hurt at renewal. An unused limit ties up funding from the lender’s side, which makes it a factor when the limit is revisited at maturity.
  • Habitual use creates the opposite problem. With no fixed repayment date, balances routinely sit unchanged for years.

So keeping one open as an emergency reserve carries a price. The price is not interest; it is headroom for your next loan. A mortgage or lease deposit loan planned within a year or two changes the arithmetic entirely, and no such plan makes the burden far smaller. The decision compresses into one question: when is your next large loan.

The re-underwriting that comes around every term

A Korean credit line is contracted for a defined period, and the lender underwrites it again at maturity. This is the second place it diverges from a lump-sum loan. Once disbursed, a lump-sum loan holds its terms until contractual maturity, whereas a credit line is structured to ask again, periodically, whether the relationship continues.

Re-underwriting produces one of three outcomes.

  1. Renewal on the same terms - when income and credit have not moved much
  2. A reduced limit or repriced rate - after lower income, higher debt, a fallen score, or a payment delay
  3. Refusal to renew - when the read on repayment capacity has changed substantially

The second and third only become painful when a balance is outstanding at that moment. A new limit below your current balance creates a gap you have to close. Without cash on hand, the pressure pushes toward borrowing elsewhere to cover it, and costs climb steeply from that point.

Preparation is simple, but timing decides whether it works.

  • Two or three months before maturity, check the balance and bring it down to a level you could absorb even after a cut.
  • Refresh the documents such as employment certificates and income statements. Underwriting without current papers resolves against you.
  • If a job change, resignation, or leave is coming, confirm the maturity date before it happens.
  • If renewal terms worsen, comparing other lenders is legitimate. Just remember a new application is a fresh underwriting event.

If several obligations already overlap beyond what you can carry, using the formal programs beats grinding through renewals. The options are laid out step by step in Credit Recovery and Debt Restructuring Complete Guide.

The rate cut request right - statute and what actually persuades

A rate is not a value fixed once at signing. Article 30-2 of the Banking Act lets anyone who has entered a credit agreement with a bank request a rate reduction on the ground of improved credit standing, with the procedure in Article 18-4 of the Enforcement Decree and Article 25-4 of the Regulation on Supervision of Banking Business. This is a statutory right, not a favour.

ApplicantRecognized groundsDocuments to prepare
Individual Employment, promotion, higher income, a higher credit score, and similar improvements in credit standing Employment certificate, withholding tax receipt for wage income, pay statements, credit bureau score confirmation
Sole proprietor or company Improved financial condition, higher credit rating Financial statements, credit rating report, revenue evidence

Three points about the procedure are worth carrying.

  • The bank has a duty to tell you. It must disclose that a rate reduction may be requested when a credit agreement is concluded, and failure to do so carries a fine of up to 20 million won (Banking Act Article 69(4)).
  • There is a deadline for the answer. The bank must communicate its decision and the reasons within 10 business days of the request, excluding time spent waiting for documents.
  • The bank applies criteria. It looks at whether credit standing influenced the pricing of that loan in the first place, and whether the improvement is material enough to justify recalculation. If you already sit in the lowest band, or the product prices independently of credit, improvement may not translate into a cut.

When a request is refused, the real waste is letting the reasons go unread. There is a purpose to the law requiring not just a decision but the reasons behind it. Written reasons tell you exactly what to close before the next attempt. Applying in a year when a promotion and a raise actually landed together, or after other debt was cleared, is the whole technique: apply when the change has genuinely occurred.

Early repayment fees and closing the account

Early repayment fees are prohibited in principle under the Financial Consumer Protection Act and may be charged only when repayment occurs within three years of the loan date. On top of that, the amendment to the Regulation on Supervision of Financial Consumer Protection resolved in July 2024 took effect in mid-January 2025 and narrowed the chargeable scope itself.

  • Only actual costs may be charged. That means funding-disruption costs such as interest lost while a new borrower is found or the rate gap on re-lending, plus administrative and origination costs such as stamp duty, appraisal fees, judicial scrivener fees, and origination commissions.
  • Adding items beyond that scope is prohibited as an unfair business practice under the Act.

Limit-type loans differ in character because drawing and repaying at will is built into the product; paying down a balance is normal use rather than an exit. Even so, whether a fee applies and how it is calculated varies by product, so read the fee clauses in the terms and product manual directly. To close the agreement outright, the sequence is to bring the balance to zero and then file the closure request. Reducing the limit is a lighter procedure than closing, and the reduction alone already lowers the amount entering DSR.

What a payment delay actually sets in motion

This section exists to lay out the sequence, not to frighten. Delinquency on a credit line starts differently from a lump-sum loan. With no fixed repayment date, the first signal is usually an over-limit position or unpaid interest.

  1. Default interest begins. A penalty margin is added on top of the contract rate.
  2. Further draws stop. New withdrawals can be suspended even with limit remaining.
  3. The benefit of time is lost. Past a certain period of continued delay, the entire balance becomes immediately due.
  4. It lands in credit records. Delinquency information feeds credit evaluation and is visible in other lenders’ underwriting.
  5. It spreads to other loans. One delinquency can trigger limit cuts or renewal refusals elsewhere.

The stretch between steps 2 and 3 is what matters. Room to negotiate still exists there. Paying interest first while re-planning principal, or converting into an installment loan, are live options at that stage, and choices vanish fast as the delay lengthens. Once high-rate borrowing starts covering earlier borrowing, recovery gets far harder, exactly as covered in Card Loans and Cash Advances: A Complete Caution Guide. Calling the lender before a delay begins rather than after is the only cheap option in the entire sequence.

Where this diverges from comparable products elsewhere

Limit-based lending is hardly unique to Korea. The divergence is in how an unused limit enters regulatory measurement.

  • United States - lines of credit, HELOCs, and credit cards give revolving credit wide use. Utilization against the limit is a familiar concept in consumer scoring, but that belongs to scoring models and starts from a different place than Korea’s approach of putting the entire unused limit into a regulatory computation.
  • Japan - bank card loans and overdraft facilities take a comparable form, and lending regulation there is designed differently from Korea’s borrower-level DSR.
  • China - concepts such as unsecured personal lending and granted credit limits are in use, with different approval and supervisory structures.

No rate or limit figures for those markets appear above, for a plain reason: they were not verified. For anyone dealing with the Korean system, one practical difference carries the weight. In Korea an unused limit genuinely eats into the headroom for your next loan. Advice written for other markets, applied without that knowledge, will misfire.

What could not be confirmed

  • The maturity assumption used for limit-type loans in DSR - the Commission’s policy Q&A describes computing them on the limit while assuming amortization over a period that accounts for rollovers, but the assumed maturity for personal loans has been adjusted by policy over time. No specific number of years is asserted here. Confirm with the lender’s formula at the time you apply.
  • How much credit bureaus weigh an unused limit - the scoring models are not published, which is why no point-change figure appears above.
  • Whether fees attach to unused limits and at what rate - a matter of each lender’s product terms.
  • The detail and timing of the 2026 DSR scope expansion - flagged as a plan in the household debt management plan released on 1 April 2026, with specifics to be announced separately.
  • Renewal cycles and reduction criteria for credit lines - internal lender rules. Your own agreement states the term, which makes your contract the most accurate source.

Summary - three checks before, three after

Reduced to the questions that actually help a decision:

If you are considering opening one

  1. Is a large loan planned within two years? If so, the limit you open now reduces your headroom then.
  2. How much do you actually need? Committing to what you need rather than the maximum you can get keeps the DSR figure equally small.
  3. Have you read the fee and maturity clauses? Term and fees live in the contract, and that is usually where later trouble originates.

If you already hold one

  1. Is an unused limit just sitting there? Reducing is simpler than closing and pushes in the same direction.
  2. When does it mature? Review balance and documents two or three months ahead.
  3. Has your income or credit improved since last year? Then this is the moment for a rate cut request.

The mistake people make most often with credit lines is treating the balance and the limit as the same thing. The number on your statement is the balance; the number underwriting reads is the limit. Separate those two and most of the rest resolves on its own.

This is not financial advice, and nothing here settles your own approval or terms. DSR caps and coverage shift with policy, and rates, limits, and fees are set by each lender per product.
