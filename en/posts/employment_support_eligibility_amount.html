Korea's Employment Support Program - Who Receives It and How Much

Where Type I and Type II split, the 600,000 won monthly allowance for 2026, and why it cannot run alongside unemployment benefits

Aug 11, 2026 Law & Legal

Read this first - the reference date and the limits of this article

This article reflects the position as of August 2026 and is neither legal advice nor an eligibility determination. The requirements and figures set out here are general guidance. Whether a given person falls into Type I or Type II, whether allowance entitlement is recognised, and how much is actually paid depend on household composition, income, assets and work history as matters of fact. Those determinations are made by the competent employment center, not by this page. The income thresholds track the median income standard published each year, so the lines move when the year changes. For an individual case, contact the Ministry of Employment and Labor counselling centre (1350, reachable within Korea) or your local employment center, and read the current statutory text at the Korean Law Information Center (law.go.kr). Everything described here is a Korean scheme and does not apply in other countries.

It sits exactly where unemployment insurance stops

After a job ends, unemployment benefits are the first thing most people think of. But in Korea those benefits demand an entrance ticket: a record of employment insurance contributions. If you only ever worked part-time jobs, or freelanced, or have never held a job since graduating, or already exhausted your entitlement, that door is shut.

The National Employment Support Program occupies precisely that space. Its statutory basis is the Act on the Employment Promotion and Support for Livelihood Stability of Job Seekers, and instead of leaning on insurance contributions it looks at income and assets. That is why it is commonly described as Korea's unemployment assistance scheme.

What the program hands over comes in two strands. One is the employment service package of counselling, vocational training and work experience. The other is cash to carry living costs through that period. Whether the second strand attaches is the single axis that separates the types. Hold on to that sentence and the eligibility tables become much simpler.

The age range runs from 15 to 69. Few support schemes open that wide at both ends. Many people skip past it believing it is a youth-only measure, when in practice people aged 35 and over enter through a different type.

Type I and Type II - the split is whether cash appears

Official notation uses roman numerals, Type I and Type II, and Korean search traffic tends to use the plain numerals. Either way the structure is the same.

  • Type I - employment services plus the job-search allowance. Because cash covers living costs, income and asset thresholds apply.
  • Type II - employment services plus employment activity costs. The threshold is far lower, but the cash element is small.

Type I then divides into a screening route and a selection route. The screening route recognises applicants who meet every requirement; the selection route exists for people missing one of them and selects within budget. The name is doing real work here: applying to the selection route does not guarantee a place. This is where disappointment most often occurs.

The Type I screening route - four gates

The screening route requires clearing all four gates. One failure and you are looking at a different route.

  1. Age - 15 or over and 69 or under.
  2. Income - average monthly gross income for the household at or below 60 percent of median income.
  3. Assets - household assets of 400 million won or less, eased to 500 million won for ages 15 to 34.
  4. Work history - 100 days or 800 hours or more within the previous two years.

The point that trips people most often is that income is measured at household level. Your own income can be zero and the household total still disqualifies you. That is usually why a young applicant living with parents fails the screening route. That said, how far the household extends is decided by residence registration and dependency relationships, so sharing an address does not automatically merge two people, and living apart does not automatically separate them. The employment center makes that call.

The work-history gate is misread just as often. 100 days or 800 hours does not mean full-time career history alone. Part-time and short-term work with an insurance record is described as counting. The reverse is the real problem: if all your work left no record, the evidence stalls. That case moves to the selection route below.

The selection route - short on work history, or young

The selection route has two branches.

The first is the economically inactive branch. Income and assets match the screening route, but work history falls short of 100 days or 800 hours. The typical profile is someone who has never held a job since graduating, or whose career has been interrupted for a long stretch.

The second is the youth special case. A person aged 15 to 34 with household income at or below 120 percent of median income and assets of 500 million won or less is described as eligible. That work history is not assessed here matters a great deal.

Do not misread what the selection route is. It does not decide automatically once the criteria are met; it selects within an available budget. Identical circumstances can therefore produce different outcomes depending on when and where you apply. If the screening route is available to you, it is the more predictable path.

Type II - a low threshold and a small cheque

Type II takes everyone who does not fall into Type I, and the structure is simple.

  • Youth (15-34) - neither income nor assets are assessed.
  • Mid-career (35-69) - household income at or below 100 percent of median income.
  • Designated groups - described as including homeless people, North Korean defectors, single parents and marriage migrants, with no income, asset or work-history test.

The absence of an income test for young applicants matters in practice. A young person blocked from Type I by parental income can still receive the service side, meaning training and counselling. But calibrate the expectation precisely: what Type II pays is an activity cost, not a living allowance.

The 2026 job-search allowance - 600,000 won plus up to 400,000

This is the number people arrive looking for. The Ministry of Employment and Labor states that the allowance rose from 500,000 won a month in 2025 to 600,000 won a month from 1 January 2026.

  • Base amount - 600,000 won a month for six months, so up to 3.6 million won.
  • Dependant add-on - 100,000 won a month each, capped at 400,000 won.
  • The monthly ceiling is therefore 1 million won, and up to 6 million won over six months.
  • Who counts as a dependant - minors aged 18 or under, people aged 70 or over, and people with severe disabilities.

The add-on is routinely overlooked. With young children at home, or a parent aged 70 or over in the household, the extra sum comes to more than half the base amount. That is why family registration documents belong in the application bundle from the start.

One qualification matters. The allowance does not go to every Type I participant. It goes to Type I participants whose allowance entitlement has been recognised. And if you work or earn during the participation period, that must be reported; depending on the level, payment is reduced or restricted.

Some Work24 screens and older question-and-answer pages still display 500,000 won. The increase took effect on 1 January 2026, so where the figure differs from screen to screen, take the current guidance and the employment center as the reference.

Type II activity costs - a different kind of money

What Type II pays even carries a different name: employment activity costs, meant to defray what job preparation costs rather than to replace income. Work24 guidance lists these items.

  • Participation allowance - paid for taking part in drawing up the action plan, with a base figure of 150,000 won.
  • Training participation allowance - paid during vocational training, described as up to 200,000 won a month for up to six months.

Set beside the Type I allowance, the scale is plainly smaller. Anyone entering Type II expecting a scheme that pays money ends up disappointed. The value of Type II lies not in cash but in access to training and counselling: a training plan tied to the national training card, and referral into work-experience programs. That is the real return.

These items are also the part most exposed to year-by-year reform, which is why they appear again in the caveats section below.

The action plan - the document the money hangs from

Recognition does not put money in the account straight away. First comes counselling and an individual action plan, which records what you will do over the coming months: training courses, a CV, a number of applications, interviews, work-experience placements.

Treating that document as a formality is a mistake. Work24 states that activity counts as job-search activity only when it is the activity set out in the action plan. Turn the sentence around and it reads like this.

  • Activity outside the plan may not be recognised, however much of it there is.
  • Activity inside the plan done in form only is reassessed.
  • When circumstances change, the plan itself has to be revised with the counsellor.

So it genuinely matters not to promise more than you can deliver at the meeting where the plan is written. If childcare, caring duties or health limit how much you can do, say so there. Signing a plan you cannot keep and then failing it later is the worst of the available paths.

What happens when the activity obligation is not met

The allowance does not arrive on a calendar. Each instalment follows a check that the activity was carried out. Where that check fails, the instalment does not land.

  • Not carried out - the instalment may be reduced or withheld.
  • Repeated failures - payment can be halted, and the entitlement itself becomes unstable.
  • Income earned but not reported - this stops being non-compliance and becomes a fraud question.

Non-compliance and fraud carry entirely different weight. The first costs you that month's payment. The second means returning money already received and paying more on top. If something has changed, reporting it always ends more cheaply than hiding it.

Two allowances that attach after you find work

The purpose of the program is employment, not payment, so separate mechanisms attach afterwards. Their names are similar enough to confuse.

Early employment success allowance

This exists so that finding work quickly does not leave you worse off. Where a Type I participant takes a job or starts a business before the third scheduled instalment, in other words early in the plan period, 50 percent of the remaining allowance has been described as payable. Dependant add-ons are excluded from that calculation. For employees, the job must also involve 30 hours a week or more with employment insurance enrolment.

Employment success allowance

This one attaches to staying. Where a person at or below 60 percent of median income, or in a designated group, takes a stable job and remains in it, the described payments are as follows.

  • Six months of continuous service - 500,000 won.
  • A further six months, twelve in total - 1 million won.
  • A combined maximum of 1.5 million won.

Here too, a stable job is described as 30 hours a week or more with employment insurance enrolment. A very-short-hours job therefore sits outside the reach of this allowance. That gives you one more reason to check the contracted weekly hours before accepting an offer.

The relationship with unemployment benefits - they cannot overlap

This is the most asked and most misunderstood point. The answer up front: they cannot be drawn together. Work24 states explicitly that a person cannot participate in the program while receiving unemployment benefits.

The reason is that the two schemes share a skeleton. Both support living costs on condition of job-search activity. A structure in which the same activity draws money from two sources in the same period does not hold together.

The bottom row is what catches people in practice. You cannot apply the day after benefits end; a six-month gap sits in between. Public job programs and other central or local employment subsidies are described as receiving the same treatment.

Order therefore matters. If you hold entitlement to unemployment benefits, checking that route first is usually the better move. Being linked to your previous wage, it often exceeds a flat 600,000 won, and the prescribed benefit period can run past six months. Eligibility conditions, how the reason for leaving is assessed and how the amount is calculated are set out separately in the complete guide to Korean unemployment benefits. If you left under a recommended resignation and the entitlement itself is unclear, read what a recommended resignation legally is and what to check before signing first; that is the right order.

Conversely, for people for whom unemployment benefits were never possible, this program is effectively the only route. New entrants with no insurance record, people whose careers were interrupted for years, and people who have already exhausted their entitlement all sit here.

Where the application starts

There are two counters, and the paperwork is broadly the same at both.

  1. Online - apply through Work24 (work24.go.kr). Watching an introductory video and completing job-seeker registration are described as prior steps.
  2. In person - the employment center for your area of residence. Easier if documentation is complex or the household is not straightforward.

Alongside identity documents, the application asks for material evidencing income and assets and evidence of work history. If you intend to claim the dependant add-on, bring documents establishing the family relationship from the outset. Supplying them later simply delays the decision.

The outcome is described as notified generally within one month of submission. Applications are accepted year-round, though the selection route remains subject to budget conditions as noted above.

Questions during participation go to the Ministry of Employment and Labor counselling centre (1350, reachable within Korea). Keeping a note of what you were told helps later, when you have to explain something.

What benefit fraud costs - repayment is not the end

This section is not here to alarm anyone. It is here to show how the sanctions stack, because seeing the stack also explains why reporting is the cheaper option.

The Korea Ministry of Government Legislation explains that where the allowance was obtained by false or otherwise improper means, the following follow.

  • Cancellation of the decision recognising entitlement.
  • Repayment order for all or part of the sums received, under Article 28 of the Act.
  • Additional levy of up to the amount improperly received. Returning what you took is not where it ends.
  • Five-year bar on applying for employment support from the date of cancellation.
  • Joint liability for anyone who conspired in it.
  • Criminal penalties under separate provisions of the same Act.

To be explicit about what falls inside that category: not disclosing a job you have started, recording job-search activity that never happened, and using another person's identity. Rather than looking for room there, look at it the other way round. Report the job promptly and a separate mechanism, the early employment success allowance, is waiting. The scheme has already been designed so that finding work quickly does not cost you.

Reporting income is what allows the reduction rules to work as intended. Delay it and every payment made in the meantime becomes an improperly received amount, enlarging the base on which the additional levy is calculated. Even late, disclosing it yourself always produces the better outcome.

Common reasons applications fail

Gather the points that recur in employment center consultations and they converge on this list.

  • Only counting your own income - Type I income is assessed at household level. Zero personal income is no help if the household total is over.
  • Forgetting the published price of property - a substantial group clears the income gate and fails on assets.
  • Work history that cannot be evidenced - if all your work left no record, 100 days or 800 hours is hard to prove. That case looks to the economically inactive selection route.
  • Less than six months since unemployment benefits ended - Type I participation is restricted. Work out the timing first.
  • Currently receiving livelihood benefit - Type I participation is described as restricted.
  • Skipping job-seeker registration and the introductory video - the application does not proceed at all.
  • Mistaking the selection route for the screening route - the classic source of the complaint that the criteria were met but nothing happened.
  • Getting the upper age limit wrong - it is 69. Plenty of mid-career applicants pass it by as a youth scheme.

How it compares with schemes elsewhere

Readers who have worked outside Korea tend to file this alongside unemployment insurance, but the character differs. Where Korean unemployment benefit is insurance, this program is assistance. Entitlement comes not from a contribution history but from income and assets today.

In the United States, the closest reading is to hold two things apart. Unemployment insurance is contribution-based, administered state by state, and turns on the reason for separation, which puts it on the same side of the line as Korean unemployment benefit rather than this program. The nearer analogue is the workforce development side, the American Job Center network and the training and placement services delivered under federal workforce legislation, which reach people regardless of insurance history. The difference is that the American structure leans heavily on services and reimbursement, while Korea attaches a flat monthly cash payment to the service package under Type I. Because eligibility rules, benefit levels and state discretion vary so widely, setting dollar figures beside won figures says very little.

If two features distinguish the Korean design, they are these. First, the 15 to 69 age band is unusually wide. Second, there is a route that removes the income test altogether for young applicants, namely Type II youth. Together they cover a large part of the gap that insurance leaves behind.

What could not be confirmed

These are the items official guidance did not settle. Each is something to confirm with the employment center before applying.

  • The 2026 composition of Type II activity costs. Work24 lists a base participation allowance of 150,000 won and a training allowance of up to 200,000 won a month for six months, but this is the part most often reworked from year to year. Check the item list and the figures as at your application date.
  • How the early employment success allowance is calculated in 2026. The structure of 50 percent of the remaining allowance is documented, but a worked instalment example following the increase to 600,000 won could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • The article number for the criminal penalty. Repayment and the additional levy sit in Article 28, which is confirmed; the numbering of the criminal provision is not stated here because the full statutory text could not be opened directly. Check the current text at law.go.kr if you need the number.
  • The detail of asset valuation. The 400 and 500 million won lines are confirmed, but how vehicles, financial assets and debts enter the calculation follows the operational manual. If you sit near the line, take advice.
  • How the household boundary is drawn. How far family members living elsewhere are aggregated is a case-by-case judgement.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply while still employed

The scheme is built for people who are actively looking for work. With income coming in, either the income gate blocks the application or the reduction and restriction rules for the allowance engage. If you are preparing to move jobs, settle the question of timing in the consultation first.

Is one participation the end of it

Re-entry is not structurally barred, but a waiting period is described as applying. If you have participated before, confirm with the employment center when you become eligible to apply again.

Do I apply for vocational training separately

Where training forms part of the action plan, it proceeds as part of that plan. It frequently connects to the national training card, so it is efficient to discuss card issuance in the same consultation.

When does the money actually arrive

Each instalment is paid after the activity for that period has been verified. Late verification therefore means late payment. Uploading evidence promptly is the most reliable way to hold the payment schedule.

What if household income sits right on the line

Do not disqualify yourself by arithmetic at home. The median income standard changes annually, the threshold shifts with household size, and income is not recognised by simple addition. The determination belongs to the employment center.

Conclusion - five things to settle before applying

The eligibility tables look complicated, but what you actually have to check reduces to five items.

  1. Do unemployment benefits come first. Where entitlement exists, that route is usually better, and six months must pass after it ends before Type I opens.
  2. Where does household income fall. Which of the three lines you stand on, 60, 100 or 120 percent, determines the type.
  3. Does work history over the past two years exceed 100 days or 800 hours. Above it, the screening route; below it, the selection route.
  4. Are there dependants. A child aged 18 or under, a person aged 70 or over, or a person with a severe disability adds up to 400,000 won a month.
  5. Can you keep the plan. The allowance hangs from compliance with the action plan, so agree only to a volume you can actually deliver.

If you remember one set of numbers, make it 600,000 won a month in 2026, up to 1 million won a month with dependants, for six months. And remember that this is not money that arrives while you wait; it is money that settles on top of a confirmed record of doing what you agreed to do.

Cases that look identical can end differently depending on household composition, how income is recognised, how assets are valued and how work history is evidenced, and those determinations are made by the competent employment center. Figures and requirements move with the annual median income standard and yearly reforms, so always confirm them as at your application date.
