Complete Guide to Age Standards for Youth and Children in Korea: Cheongsonyon Bohobeop vs. Adong Bokjibeop

A Thorough Comparison of Age Criteria Under the Youth Protection Act and the Child Welfare Act

2025-11-17 Lifestyle

1. Introduction: Why Age Standards Matter

In Korea, the age definitions for youth and children go far beyond simply categorizing people by how old they are -- they determine the scope of various legal protections and rights. In particular, the Cheongsonyon Bohobeop (Youth Protection Act) and the Adong Bokjibeop (Child Welfare Act) each apply different age thresholds, which can be genuinely confusing.

This guide provides a clear side-by-side comparison of the two laws and explains in detail how they play out in real life.

Key Point: The Youth Protection Act covers everyone under 19 (international age), while the Child Welfare Act covers everyone under 18. But there is more to the story.

2. Age Criteria Under the Youth Protection Act (Cheongsonyon Bohobeop)

2.1 Legal Definition of "Cheongsonyon" (Youth)

Under Article 2, Paragraph 1 of the Cheongsonyon Bohobeop, a "cheongsonyon" (youth) is anyone under the age of 19 (international age). However, a person who reaches 19 in a given year is excluded starting January 1 of that year.

Category Age Criterion Notes
Youth (Cheongsonyon) Under 19 (int'l age) Excluded from Jan. 1 of the year they turn 19
Adult 19 or older (int'l age) Same as the age of majority under the Civil Act

2.2 Key Areas Covered by the Youth Protection Act

The Youth Protection Act protects young people in the following areas:

  • Harmful media regulation: Restricts access to alcohol, tobacco, adult content, and similar materials
  • Access restrictions: Limits entry to and hours at bars, karaoke rooms (noraebang), PC cafes (PC bang), and other venues
  • Employment protections: Restricts working hours and conditions when youth are employed
  • Harmful substance regulation: Protects youth from hallucinogens, narcotics, and other substances

2.3 Real-Life Applications

Example 1: Movie Ratings

"Cheongsonyon gwallam bulga" (restricted) movies cannot be viewed by anyone under 19 (international age). If your birthday is January 2, you are treated as an adult starting January 1 of the year you turn 19, so you can watch these films.

Example 2: Purchasing Alcohol and Tobacco

To buy alcohol or tobacco at a convenience store or supermarket, you must show ID. Under the Youth Protection Act, anyone under 19 (international age) is prohibited from purchasing these products.

3. Age Criteria Under the Child Welfare Act (Adong Bokjibeop)

3.1 Legal Definition of "Adong" (Child)

Under Article 3, Paragraph 1 of the Adong Bokjibeop, an "adong" (child) is anyone under the age of 18 (international age) -- one year lower than the Youth Protection Act threshold.

Category Age Criterion Legal Basis
Child (Adong) Under 18 (int'l age) Child Welfare Act, Art. 3, Para. 1
Youth (Cheongsonyon) Under 19 (int'l age) Youth Protection Act, Art. 2, Para. 1
Adult 19 or older (int'l age) Civil Act, Art. 4

3.2 Key Areas Covered by the Child Welfare Act

The Child Welfare Act guarantees the fundamental rights and welfare of children:

  • Prevention and protection from child abuse: Protection from physical, emotional, and sexual abuse
  • Child welfare facilities: Management and support for daycare centers and child-rearing facilities
  • Child protection agencies: Discovery, protection, and treatment of abused children
  • Foster care and adoption: Support for alternative care of children who need protection
  • Child allowance and welfare benefits: Child allowance payments for children under 8 (international age)

3.3 Real-Life Applications

Example 1: Mandatory Reporting of Child Abuse

Teachers, healthcare professionals, and childcare workers are legally required to report child abuse immediately when they become aware of it involving anyone under 18 (international age). Reports can be made by calling 112 (police) or the local child protection agency.

Example 2: Child Welfare Facility Access

Children under 18 who need protection can receive care at child welfare facilities. Once they turn 18, they transition to self-sufficiency support programs.

4. Youth Protection Act vs. Child Welfare Act: Key Differences

4.1 Age Criteria Comparison

Law Target Age Primary Purpose Areas of Application
Youth Protection Act (Cheongsonyon Bohobeop) Under 19 (int'l age) Protection from harmful environments Harmful media, harmful businesses, harmful substances
Child Welfare Act (Adong Bokjibeop) Under 18 (int'l age) Ensuring fundamental rights Abuse prevention, welfare facilities, welfare benefits

4.2 Overlap Zone (Under 18)

Anyone under 18 (international age) is protected by both laws:

  • Shielded from harmful environments under the Youth Protection Act
  • Eligible for welfare services and abuse prevention under the Child Welfare Act

4.3 The Gap (Age 18 to Under 19)

A person who is 18 or older but not yet 19 (international age):

  • Is still protected under the Youth Protection Act (restricted from harmful media, etc.)
  • Is no longer protected under the Child Welfare Act
  • This means, for example, they cannot watch restricted movies at a theater, but they also cannot access child welfare facilities

5. Age Standards in Other Related Laws

5.1 Age of Majority Under the Civil Act (Minbeop)

Article 4 of the Minbeop (Civil Act) sets the age of majority at 19 (international age). Once you reach legal adulthood:

  • You can independently perform legal acts such as signing contracts and buying real estate
  • You can marry without parental consent
  • You can vote (the voting age was lowered to 18)

5.2 Framework Act on Youth (Cheongsonyon Gibon-beop)

Article 3, Paragraph 1 of the Cheongsonyon Gibon-beop defines youth as anyone between the ages of 9 and 24 (international age). This is a different standard from the Youth Protection Act and is used for youth policy and support programs.

5.3 Labor Standards Act (Geullogijun-beop)

The Geullogijun-beop (Labor Standards Act) classifies anyone under 18 (international age) as a minor worker, subject to:

  • A maximum of 7 hours per day and 35 hours per week
  • A general prohibition on night work (10:00 PM to 6:00 AM)
  • A requirement for consent from a parent or guardian

6. Common Confusion and How to Resolve It

6.1 Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: I am 18 (international age). Can I drink alcohol?

A: No. Under the Youth Protection Act, anyone under 19 (international age) is prohibited from purchasing or consuming alcohol.

Q2: Am I immediately an adult once January 1 of the year I turn 19 has passed?

A: Yes. Under the Youth Protection Act, you are considered an adult starting January 1 of the year you turn 19.

Q3: How do I access protections under the Child Welfare Act?

A: If you are under 18 (international age) and at risk of abuse, neglect, or other harm, you can report it by calling 112 (police) or your local child protection agency.

6.2 Practical Tips to Reduce Confusion

  • Understand the purpose of each law: The Youth Protection Act focuses on "blocking harmful environments," while the Child Welfare Act focuses on "guaranteeing fundamental rights."
  • Calculate using international age: When determining your age, use international age (man nai). If your birthday has not yet passed this year, subtract one from your current calendar year minus your birth year.
  • Always carry ID: If you are near a borderline age, always carry identification to avoid unnecessary misunderstandings.

8. The Importance of Legal Protection

The age standards for youth and children are not just numbers. They represent how a society chooses to protect its minors and determine what rights to grant them.

"Children are not future adults; they are citizens of today." -- Educator Maria Montessori

8.1 The Role of Parents and Guardians

  • Understand your child's rights and the scope of their legal protections
  • Take an active interest in shielding your child from harmful environments
  • Stay alert to prevent child abuse and catch warning signs early

8.2 Society's Responsibility

  • Thoroughly manage and inspect businesses that are off-limits to youth
  • Operate child welfare facilities transparently and raise their quality
  • Strengthen training for mandatory child-abuse reporters

9. Conclusion: Better Protection Through Clear Understanding

Clearly understanding the age criteria in the Youth Protection Act and the Child Welfare Act is the first step toward protecting future generations. Although the two laws have different purposes and scopes, both ultimately exist to support the healthy growth of youth and children.

Key Takeaways:

  • Youth Protection Act (Cheongsonyon Bohobeop): Under 19 (international age) -- focused on blocking harmful environments
  • Child Welfare Act (Adong Bokjibeop): Under 18 (international age) -- focused on guaranteeing fundamental rights
  • Everyone under 18 is protected by both laws
  • Understanding the purpose behind each law helps reduce confusion
