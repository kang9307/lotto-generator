Complete Guide to Blocking Spam Calls and Texts - Types, Blocking, Reporting, Prevention

From iPhone and Android built-in features to carrier services and official reporting channels by country, all in one guide

Jul 6, 2026 Tech & IT

Intro: Why Spam Calls and Texts Still Won't Go Away in 2026

Spam calls and texts are an old nuisance, but as of July 2026 they show no sign of slowing down. Automated dialing systems can blast thousands of messages at once, so what used to be simple advertising has blended with texts impersonating government agencies or delivery companies, and calls that ask for money directly.

This guide is not about explaining how these scams work in detail. It is a practical, defense-first walkthrough: how to tell what's landing in your inbox, how to block it using your phone's built-in features and your carrier's tools, and where to report it if you've already been targeted. We'll go in order, from iPhone and Android blocking features to the official reporting channels in Korea, the US, Japan, and China.

1. Types of Spam Calls and Texts - From Ads to Smishing and Voice Phishing

1.1 Four Broad Categories

Spam calls and texts generally fall into four categories. The first is plain advertising spam - loans, insurance, and shopping promotions sent for marketing purposes. The second is smishing: texts that impersonate a delivery notice, a fine, or a government notice, with a link designed to get you to tap it. The third is voice phishing (vishing): calls that impersonate a prosecutor's office, police, or a bank and ask directly for personal data or a wire transfer. The fourth is caller ID spoofing, where the displayed number is faked to look like a family member's number or an official hotline. This guide focuses not on how each tactic is carried out, but on how to recognize, block, and report it once it reaches you.

1.2 What's Changing Lately

Recent coverage points to a rise in calls placed from international numbers and links hidden behind shortened URLs, making it harder to judge risk from the number or link format alone. That means a familiar-looking number is no longer proof of legitimacy - if the content feels off, verify it through a separate channel rather than trusting the caller ID.

2. Built-in Blocking on iPhone

2.1 Blocking Calls

On iPhone, go to Settings, then Phone, and turn on Silence Unknown Callers to send calls from numbers not in your contacts straight to voicemail without ringing. In the same menu, turning on call screening for unknown callers routes missed calls and voicemails from unfamiliar numbers into a separate list you can check later, and enabling your carrier's spam-identification feature lets calls flagged as spam or fraud get filtered automatically.

2.2 Filtering Texts

For texts, go to Settings, then Messages, and turn on Filter Unknown Senders so texts from numbers outside your contacts land in a separate tab, cutting down on the chance you tap a link without thinking. To block a specific number outright, open it from Messages or Recents and choose Block this Caller.

3. Built-in Blocking on Android

3.1 Blocking Calls

On Android, open the Phone app's call log, select a number, and choose Block/Report spam to automatically block future calls and texts from it. In the Phone app's settings, under Spam and Call Screen, turn on Caller ID and spam protection and enable the option to block spam and scam calls, which filters calls flagged by your carrier's and Google's shared databases.

3.2 Filtering Texts

The Google Messages app automatically flags texts it suspects are spam and displays them separately; reporting one as spam helps it catch similar patterns more accurately going forward. If you use a manufacturer's default messaging app instead, it usually offers a comparable spam-report and block option worth checking.

4. Carrier Services and Spam-Blocking Apps

4.1 Free Carrier Blocking Services

Beyond your phone's own settings, carriers offer network-level spam filtering that catches unwanted calls before they even reach your device. Most major carriers provide a free spam-blocking service, and once enrolled, calls flagged as spam at the network level can be blocked without leaving any trace on your handset. Names, enrollment steps, and coverage vary by carrier, so check with your carrier's support center or official app for the details that apply to you.

4.2 Using Spam-Blocking Apps Carefully

Third-party apps that show a number's reputation ahead of time, based on shared spam databases, are also available. When using one, check its reputation on the official app store first, and avoid apps that request excessive permissions - such as full access to your contacts or call history - beyond what the stated purpose requires.

5. Reducing Your Exposure - Minimizing Personal Information

5.1 Managing Your Number Online

Prevention matters as much as blocking. Avoid entering your phone number out of habit on every online survey, giveaway, or sign-up form. On marketplace listings or public boards, don't post your number in plain text; use a masked or in-app contact option whenever one is available and only share the number directly when it's truly necessary.

5.2 Reviewing App Permissions

Check whether an app requests access to your contacts or call history when you install it, and get in the habit of declining that access unless it's essential to the service. A number already exposed in multiple places can't be fully walked back, but limiting future exposure from new sign-ups and giveaways is a realistic, forward-looking approach.

6. If You've Already Received Spam or a Phishing Attempt

6.1 If You Tapped a Smishing Link

The rule with a smishing text is simple: never tap the link. If you already have, switch to airplane mode immediately to cut the connection, then run a security scan to check for malware.

6.2 If You Took a Voice Phishing Call

If a caller asks for personal information, an account number, or a verification code, never provide it on the spot. To confirm whether the caller's claimed organization is real, don't call the number that appeared on your screen or in the text - look up the organization's official number independently and call that instead.

6.3 If You've Already Lost Money

If you've already transferred money or shared account details, the first move is to contact your bank's call center or your national emergency/fraud line immediately to request a payment freeze. After that, file a report with the appropriate reporting body or police, and change any passwords or verification credentials that may have been exposed.

7. Official Reporting Channels by Country

7.1 Korea

Forward a spam text as-is to #118, or file a report through KISA's 118 center (dial 118, no area code). Smishing and voice-phishing consultations also go through 118, and if you've already lost money, report it to 112 immediately.

7.2 United States

Forward spam texts to 7726 (SPAM) to report them to your carrier, and register your number with the National Do Not Call Registry at donotcall.gov (note: this won't stop illegal scam calls). Report fraudulent calls and texts to the FTC (consumer.ftc.gov) and the FCC.

7.3 Japan

Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications runs a consultation center for unregistered calls and fraudulent billing, and general scam and consumer-trouble consultations go through the nationwide Consumer Hotline 188 (iyaya). Non-emergency police consultations, including suspicious calls, can go through #9110. Your carrier's support center also takes reports of unregistered numbers.

7.4 China

Spam texts, harassing calls, and suspected fraud can be reported to the 12321 Network Undesirable and Spam Information Reporting Center (12321.cn or the 12321 app). 96110 is the dedicated hotline for fraud warnings, consultation, and reporting - if you receive a call from this number, answer it and follow the guidance. Reports can also be filed through the National Anti-Fraud Center app.

8. Conclusion - Three Things You Can Do Today

Spam, smishing, and voice phishing can't be eliminated entirely, but getting the basics right cuts your exposure and risk substantially. Here are three moves for today.

1) Turn on Silence Unknown Callers if you're on iPhone, or Caller ID and spam protection if you're on Android.
2) Sign up for your carrier's free spam-blocking service.
3) Save your country's reporting numbers (Korea: 118/112, US: 7726/FTC, Japan: Consumer Hotline 188, China: 12321/96110) in your contacts.
