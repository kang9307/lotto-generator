Introduction: Two Taxes on Owning Property in Korea

Owning real property in Korea means paying at least one, and sometimes two, annual holding taxes. The first is jaesan-se (재산세), a local tax levied by the municipality on virtually all types of property. The second is jongbuse (종합부동산세, often abbreviated as 종부세), a national tax imposed on top of jaesan-se when the combined official price of property held nationwide exceeds a deduction amount. Together these two make up Korea's boyu-se, or property-holding tax system. This article describes the Korean system only.

Tax law changes often, and 2026 has two reform proposals on the table. The 2026 Tax Reform Proposal, announced by the Ministry of Finance and Economy on 3 August and finalised as the government bill on 1 September, would raise the jongbuse basic deduction for a one-household one-home owner to 1.4 billion KRW. The 2026 Local Tax Reform Proposal, announced by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety on 26 August, would extend the one-home jaesan-se special rate to 2029. Neither has passed the National Assembly, so neither is a rule in force. The rates, deductions and dates below are the rules in force as of 29 September 2026, with the proposals kept separate. This is not tax advice; confirm your own liability with your local government, the National Tax Service, Wetax or Hometax.

In short, jaesan-se is the basic holding tax that every owner pays, while jongbuse is an additional holding tax paid on top of it when the combined official price of housing or land exceeds the deduction amount. Both use 1 June each year as the assessment date.

One-sentence summary: Jaesan-se (local, July/September) is paid by every property owner; jongbuse (national, December) is an additional tax on holdings above a deduction amount.

Jaesan-se — How It Works

Assessment Date and Taxpayer

The person who owns a property on 1 June each year is liable for that year's jaesan-se. If you acquire a property before 1 June, you pay for that year; if you acquire it after 1 June, liability starts the following year. In a sale, the date of final payment (transfer of ownership) decides who holds the property on 1 June, so the sale timetable around that date decides who is assessed.

Tax Base: Standard Market Value × Fair Market Value Ratio

The jaesan-se tax base for housing is the standard market value (for housing, the official price) multiplied by the fair market value ratio. For housing recognised as a one-household one-home, the 2026 ratios are set by Article 109 of the Enforcement Decree of the Local Tax Act (amended 29 May 2026): 43% up to 300 million KRW, 44% above 300 million up to 600 million KRW, and 45% above 600 million KRW. This special ratio has been extended one year at a time since 2023, so the 2027 ratio depends on next year's decree amendment. Multi-home owners and corporations use 60% (Korea.kr policy briefing, April 2025; not re-confirmed for 2026). Land uses a separate ratio.

Tax base = Standard market value × Fair market value ratio (one-household one-home 43/44/45% for 2026; other housing 60%)

Standard Rates for Housing

Housing jaesan-se is calculated with progressive rates by tax-base bracket (standard rates 0.1–0.4%). Expressed with a progressive deduction, the table is as follows.

Tax base (KRW, standard rate)RateProgressive deduction
Up to 60 million0.1%–
60 million – 150 million0.15%30,000 KRW
150 million – 300 million0.25%180,000 KRW
Over 300 million0.4%630,000 KRW

For a one-household one-home with a standard market value of 900 million KRW or less, Article 111-2 of the Local Tax Act applies a special rate 0.05 percentage points below the standard rate (0.05–0.35%). The current article carries a note that it is valid only for tax liabilities arising in 2026, so unless it is extended, the 2026 jaesan-se is the last year it applies. The Ministry of the Interior and Safety's 2026 Local Tax Reform Proposal would extend it to 2029, but that proposal was only at the stage of public notice (27 August – 23 September) with submission to the National Assembly planned for late October.

Payment Schedule

Article 115 of the Local Tax Act sets the jaesan-se payment periods as follows.

  • 16–31 July: Half of housing tax + non-residential buildings + vessels and aircraft
  • 16–30 September: Remaining half of housing tax + land

If the annual housing tax is 200,000 KRW or less, it may be levied and collected in full in July, as provided by local ordinance. Payment channels include Wetax (wetax.go.kr), E-tax, the ARS line (1588-5500; as of June 2026, not re-confirmed) and banks.

Jongbuse — How It Works

Who Is Subject: Combined Official Prices

Jongbuse is imposed when the official prices of all housing and land held nationwide, summed per person as of 1 June, exceed the deduction amount. The NTS lists the deduction amounts as follows.

  • Housing: Combined official price of housing − 900 million KRW (1.2 billion KRW for a one-household one-home owner)
  • Separately aggregated land: Combined official price of business land − 8 billion KRW
  • General aggregate land: Combined official price of bare or miscellaneous land − 500 million KRW

The 2026 Tax Reform Proposal (government bill) would change the housing deduction amounts. They are summarised separately under "2026 Tax Reform Proposal" below.

Tax Base: (Combined Official Price − Deduction) × Fair Market Value Ratio

The jongbuse tax base also passes through a fair market value ratio. The NTS tax calculation flowchart gives 60% for housing (as of 29 September 2026). The law lets the Presidential Decree set this ratio between 60% and 100%.

Jongbuse tax base = (Combined official price of housing nationwide − 900 million KRW / 1.2 billion KRW for one-home) × Fair market value ratio (60%)

Tax Credits for One-Household One-Home Owners

Beyond the larger basic deduction (1.2 billion KRW), a one-household one-home owner can claim tax credits. According to the NTS calculation flowchart, the two components are:

  • Age credit: 20% (age 60+), 30% (age 65+), 40% (age 70+)
  • Long-term holding credit: 20% (5+ years), 40% (10+ years), 50% (15+ years)
  • Combined cap: 80%
Check the reference dates for age and holding period and the detailed requirements with the NTS (126, Hometax). The reform proposal would change this credit structure, but it is not yet in force.

Payment and Filing

The NTS computes the tax and sends an assessment notice, so taxpayers simply pay the assessed amount. Self-assessment and payment within the same period is also allowed.

  • Payment period: 1–15 December each year
  • Instalments: If the tax exceeds 2.5 million KRW, it can be paid in instalments within six months
  • Channels: Hometax (hometax.go.kr), banks and similar

2026 Tax Reform Proposal (Government Bill) — Not Yet in Force

The "2026 Tax Reform Proposal", announced by the Ministry of Finance and Economy on 3 August 2026 and finalised as the government bill on 1 September, includes jongbuse items. Korea.kr presented it under the headline "Owner-occupied single home: up to 2 billion KRW market value excluded from jongbuse". These are statutory amendments that take effect only after the National Assembly passes them. The main items in the government material are:

  • Raise the one-household one-home basic deduction from 1.2 billion to 1.4 billion KRW (official price)
  • Cut the deduction for a non-resident single home from 1.2 billion to 900 million KRW, and for multiple homes from 900 million KRW to "400 million KRW + (the owner-occupied share of 500 million KRW)"
  • Unify rates by housing value instead of number of homes, and raise the fair market value ratio for owners of three or more homes from 60% to 80%
  • Switch the credit from holding period to residence period, and introduce a cap (6 million KRW) on one-household one-home tax credits
  • Phase the jongbuse reform in over two years from 2027

Figures and timing may change during parliamentary review, so do not read this list as the rules for the jongbuse paid in December 2026. The December 2026 jongbuse will be assessed under the current rules above (1.2 billion / 900 million KRW, 60%, rates by number of homes).

Here is the payment flow laid out as a calendar year. It is the general flow under the current rules; whether and how much you are billed will differ by person.

Key point: ownership on 1 June decides liability for both taxes. When signing a sale contract, it helps avoid disputes to write down who bears that year's holding taxes, since it depends on whether final payment and registration fall before or after 1 June.

The Role of the Official Price

Both taxes are calculated from the official price (gongsi gagyeok), not the transaction or market price. This government-assessed value is published by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport as of 1 January each year. It has generally been set below market value, and in the early 2020s there was a policy push to raise its ratio to market value. You can look up the official price for any property at the Real Estate Public Price Information portal (realtyprice.kr).

Because official prices and fair market value ratios are adjusted each year with housing policy, the same property can carry a different tax burden from year to year. The 2026 status of the official-price policy was not confirmed from a primary source in this update, so look up the year's official price on the portal directly.

Jaesan-se One-Home Special Rules and Reliefs

Jaesan-se has several special rules and reliefs. The best known is the one-household one-home special treatment.

  • A special rate 0.05 percentage points below the standard rate (0.05–0.35%) for a one-household one-home with a standard market value of 900 million KRW or less — under the current article, through the 2026 tax year
  • The one-home fair market value ratio (43/44/45%) applies to every one-household one-home with no price cap, and is set through the 2026 tax year
  • The extension of the special rate to 2029 is in the 2026 Local Tax Reform Proposal (government bill) and has not yet passed the National Assembly

There are other reliefs — rental housing, public-use land and so on — each with its own requirements and time limits. Consult your local government or a tax professional before relying on any of them.

Special rates and reliefs can change or end with amendments to the law. No tax saving is guaranteed.

Exclusion from Jongbuse Aggregation (Hapsan Baeje)

Jongbuse has a scheme called hapsan baeje (합산배제). Qualifying rental homes, unsold new homes, company housing and similar properties can be excluded from the jongbuse aggregation. According to the NTS, the filing period for aggregation exclusion and special treatment is 16–30 September each year; missing it can mean losing that year's benefit. Requirements are detailed, so rely on NTS guidance or a tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. When is jaesan-se paid?

Jaesan-se is paid in two instalments: 16–31 July (half of housing + buildings) and 16–30 September (half of housing + land). If the annual housing tax is 200,000 KRW or less, it may be billed in full in July under local ordinance. The assessment date is 1 June.

Q. How do I know whether I owe jongbuse?

For 2026, you are liable if the combined official price of housing you hold nationwide exceeds 900 million KRW (1.2 billion KRW for a one-household one-home owner). The 1.4 billion KRW figure in the reform proposal needs National Assembly approval and, under the government plan, would phase in from 2027. Check the notice on Hometax in December or use the NTS mock calculation service.

Q. Isn't paying both taxes double taxation?

They are separate taxes under different legal frameworks — jaesan-se is a local tax and jongbuse is a national tax. However, when jongbuse is calculated, the jaesan-se attributable to the jongbuse tax base is deducted from the computed tax, so it is not pure double taxation. Check the detailed calculation with the NTS.

Q. How is jointly owned property taxed?

For jaesan-se, each co-owner is assessed on their share. For jongbuse, each share is counted as a home owned and aggregated, but joint ownership can change how the one-household one-home test and the calculation work. Joint-ownership jongbuse is complex, so consult a tax professional.

Conclusion: 1 June Is the Key Date

The most important date for understanding jaesan-se and jongbuse is 1 June. Whoever owns the property that day pays that year's holding taxes: jaesan-se as a local tax in July and September, jongbuse as a national tax in December. One-home owners should check the jaesan-se special rate and the jongbuse deductions carefully, and remember that in 2026 reform proposals for both taxes are before the National Assembly, so the rules may differ from the 2027 tax year.
