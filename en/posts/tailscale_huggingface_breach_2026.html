What Network Access Control Cannot Stop - Connection and Permission Are Different Layers

Access arriving on a valid credential is not filtered by the network, so what is left to do the filtering

2026-08-01 Tech & IT

Introduction: The Wrong Question Produces the Wrong Answer

When a security incident becomes public, the first question is almost always the same one: what got breached? It is an easy question to answer. You name a product, and every organisation that does not use that product closes the tab feeling safe. The trouble is that this question almost always aims at the wrong layer.

Two first-party post-mortems published in July 2026 make the mismatch unusually visible when read side by side. One is a technical timeline written by the organisation that was intruded upon. The other was written by the company behind a network access tool that was named in connection with the event, and it functions as both an explanation and a self-assessment. Its conclusion can be summarised in one sentence: no vulnerability in the product was found or exploited. And yet, in the very same piece, that company holds itself accountable, because the entire purpose of a zero trust network is to prevent lateral movement, and lateral movement happened.

The starting point of this article is that both statements can be true at once. The tool did what it was designed to do, and the incident still grew. So what was missing? The answer sits outside the tool.

What this article covers and what it does not. This article deals only with defensive structure. It contains no attack procedure and no description of how any vulnerability was abused — not which input triggered which behaviour, not how isolation was left behind. That material exists in the primary sources and is deliberately not reproduced here. It also names no product or company as the party at fault. As shown below, in this particular case that attribution would not even be accurate, and more importantly it hides the lesson.

2. Connection and Permission Are Different Layers

Designs collapse the moment these two words get used interchangeably. Separated, each answers a completely different question.

AspectConnection layerPermission layer
Question answeredMay this subject reach this farHaving arrived, what may it do
When it decidesOnce, before the session opensOn every single request
What it decides onDevice identity, enrollment approval, tags, postureSubject role, target resource, action type, data scope
When it worksPublic ports disappear and the reachable surface shrinksOne compromise stays one compromise
When it does notThe number of attempts becomes unlimitedOne compromise becomes total
Shape of the workA project. It has a start and an endOperations. It has no end
MeasurabilityVisible immediately as node counts and open portsInvisible. It only surfaces during an incident

The last two rows explain what actually happens inside organisations. The connection layer is a project and the permission layer is operations. Projects get budgets and completion reports; open-ended operational work gets neither. So many organisations end up with an excellent connection layer while the permission layer sits untouched for years in whatever shape it had on day one.

2.1 What an overlay network genuinely solves

  • Reachable surface reduction. Management ports vanish from the public internet. That alone removes most opportunistic scanning and brute-force pressure
  • Moving the boundary. The decision basis shifts from which IP did this come from to which identity is this. Being inside the office network stops conferring trust
  • Transit protection. The content stays protected even when the intermediate path cannot be trusted
  • Explicit joining conditions. What may enter the network becomes a policy file. Code remembers instead of people
  • A record of connections. Who connected, when, and to where is retained. Section 6 returns to this

2.2 What an overlay network does not solve

  • What a valid identity is allowed to do. The network layer does not know which queries an application permits, and it cannot know. That is the application vocabulary
  • How much data can be taken. Reading one record and reading a million records are the same connection at the network layer
  • Where credentials sit and how many there are. That is a secret management problem, not a network problem
  • Whether the joined subject is a person or an automation. A tag can label it, but a tag does not constrain behaviour
  • Whether a historic grant was ever justified. No network tool can tell you who granted this three years ago and why

Every remedy the vendor proposed in its post-mortem — replace reusable static keys with workload identity federation, turn on network flow logs, apply strict admission control to node joins, keep node state in hardware-backed secure storage, attach device posture conditions — narrows the conditions under which joining is permitted. These are good recommendations with real effect, but none of them decides what a joined subject may then do. That was never that layer's job.

3. When the Credential Is Valid, Control Stays Silent

Follow the definition of access control and the conclusion writes itself. Control examines whether the presented credential is valid. If it is, the subject passes. That is the job. Therefore access that arrives holding a valid credential is, from the control's point of view, indistinguishable from legitimate access.

This case shows the structure plainly. According to the primary sources, 136 keys were obtained from the production secret store, and one of them was a reusable auth key issued for continuous integration nodes. With that key, 181 nodes joined the network. The joining procedure itself was normal, because the key was valid.

The usual objection. If that is so, what is control even for? It is for a great deal — but its usefulness extends to adversaries who do not hold a credential. Against one who does, the next layer has to take over. That is why defence is built in layers rather than by thickening a single one. Which layer detection and blocking each occupy is covered separately in intrusion detection and intrusion prevention, and the input boundary at the application layer in the fundamentals of web security.

3.1 Concentrating credentials concentrates the blast radius

A secret store is a good thing. It pulls credentials out of code and configuration files and puts them under one managed roof. But the act of gathering them creates a new property.

  • Scattered, one exposed credential was one problem
  • Gathered, a subject that reaches the store reaches everything inside it
  • So the access right to the store itself becomes the union of the permissions of every credential it holds
  • The fix is not to abolish the store but to split it, divide read rights per subject, and record every retrieval
  • More fundamentally, there is a direction where there is nothing to retrieve. Section 5 covers it

4. Least Privilege - Why It Is Deferred Even Though Everyone Agrees

Least privilege is the least disputed principle in security, and one of the least practised. Not because the principle is wrong, but because cost and reward are asymmetric.

4.1 The shape of the asymmetry

  • Widening takes five minutes. Something blocked starts working, the effect is immediate, and nobody objects
  • Narrowing takes days. You have to enumerate what calls what, and one mistake produces an outage on the spot
  • Successful narrowing produces nothing. Work whose reward is the absence of incidents has no sentence to put in a status report
  • Permissions widened during an outage stay. A broad grant made at three in the morning becomes permanent unless a rollback step exists
  • Old subjects have no owner. When the creator leaves the team, nobody is empowered to reduce that scope. No decision maker means neglect
  • The just make it work default. Most tools ship with generous default permissions, so narrowing is always extra work

4.2 Observation before redesign

The realistic starting point is not rewriting policy. It is recording what is actually used right now.

  • Step 1. Produce the list of granted permissions. If the list cannot be produced, that inability is the first finding
  • Step 2. Record permissions actually exercised over ninety days. Record only, block nothing. There is no outage risk
  • Step 3. Compare granted against used. The gap is what can be reclaimed today with nothing breaking
  • Step 4. Make new subjects narrow first. Changing what you create today beats stalling forever on fixing everything that exists
  • Step 5. Split read from write before anything finer. If granular separation is hard, this single line already removes a large share of the exposure
  • Step 6. Attach an owner to reclamation. Temporary grants get an expiry date and a name written down together

The remediation list in the primary source includes an item that reads as exactly this work: the credential scope on internal service connectors was narrowed. Work done after an incident is work that could have been done before one. It simply never reaches the top of the queue beforehand.

5. The Lifetime of Tokens and Keys

If breadth of permission is what may be done, lifetime is until when it may be done. In practice the second is neglected far more often.

The problem with long-lived credentials is simple. The interval between the moment of leakage and the moment of discovery becomes unbounded. A key issued three years ago with no expiry still works today, whenever in those three years it may have leaked. And usually nobody can say precisely what it is used for now.

Credential formTypical lifetimeBehaviour once leakedDirection of reduction
Static passwordEffectively indefiniteShared, leaves no traceRemove from machine access
Reusable enrollment keyMonths to indefiniteOne key can create many subjectsMake single-use or abolish
Long-lived API tokenMonths to yearsBroad scope, hard to revokeNarrow scope and add expiry
Short-lived session tokenMinutes to hoursStealing it buys a short windowMake it the default
Workload identity federationPer requestThere is no secret at rest to stealThe target state
Signing keyYearsExposure enables forgeryIsolate storage, rehearse rotation

The fifth row states the direction. The safest secret is the one that does not exist. One of the vendor recommendations said precisely this: replacing reusable static keys with workload identity federation means there is nothing left to leak in the first place. This is not a property of one product but a general direction in credential design.

5.1 If rotation hurts, rotation stops happening

One more detail in the primary source deserves attention. The affected organisation rotated everything, including credentials on clusters that were never reached. That is only possible if rotation is a cheap operation in normal times.

  • If rotation is manual, it cannot be done during an incident. So only the necessary ones get rotated, and whatever that judgement misses survives
  • If rotation causes downtime, it gets postponed. Postponed means never
  • If the list of rotation targets does not exist, you first have to discover what to rotate. That discovery time is exposure time
  • So the peacetime metric is a single one: how many hours does a full rotation take, and when did we last actually do it?
  • This rehearsal is independent of any incident. And only organisations that have rehearsed can do it under pressure

6. Where Detection Belongs - The Time It Takes to Notice

There will always be cases you cannot block. So half of defence is not blocking but noticing. On this point the primary source is remarkably candid.

According to the report, the first signals came from several security layers at once — both the layer watching runtime behaviour and the layer aggregating logs. And then the same report adds this: the criticality of the alert was not raised correctly, the on-call team was not triggered, and precious response time was lost.

That sentence has to be read precisely. Detection happened. It simply did not arrive. These are two entirely different failures with opposite remedies.

  • If the failure is an absent signal, adding sensors is the answer
  • If the failure is an undelivered signal, adding sensors only raises the noise floor. What needs fixing is the severity model and the paging path
  • Failing to separate these leads to organisations already drowning in signals buying more sensors
  • The check is one question. How many alerts actually woke a human last quarter, and how many of those were real?

6.1 What must survive for reconstruction to be possible

The affected organisation could reconstruct roughly 17,600 actions across 4.5 days for one simple reason: the records existed. The report states that reconstruction combined its own platform logs with logs recovered from an external execution environment. Without records, this incident would have ended at something probably happened.

The vendor post-mortem contains one important principle about record design. Network flow logs are written at both ends of every connection, so if one side erases its own logs, the other side still holds them. Generalised, that becomes the following.

First principle of record design. Separate the party that produces records from the party that keeps them. Kept in the same place, whoever is compromised can erase their own traces. This applies not only to network logs but to application audit logs and control-plane change history alike.

[MINIMUM RECORD SET] to reconstruct four days, these seven must survive.

1. Authentication events   who authenticated, when, from where   keep 90 days min
2. Authorization decisions why a request was allowed or denied   keep 90 days min
3. Data access             subject, target, row count, bytes     keep 180 days min
4. Credential events       issue, use, rotation, revocation      keep 365 days min
5. Control-plane changes   policy, role, approval-setting edits  keep 365 days min
6. Node joins              what entered the network and when     keep 365 days min
7. Connection flows        recorded at both ends separately      keep 30 days min

HOW TO VERIFY
  - pick an arbitrary moment 45 days ago
  - can you identify the subject that touched a given resource using only these
  - if you cannot, that item is the blank in your current setup

The separate problem of sensitive values leaking into the logs themselves is out of scope here. Where masking has to be applied so that nothing escapes is covered at implementation level in removing sensitive data at the point it is produced.

7. Supply Chain and Third Parties - Outward Grants Lead Inward

This section names no service. Naming would be pointless, because structurally the same shape exists in every organisation.

When granting access to an external service, organisations usually evaluate whether to trust that service. Contracts, certifications and security questionnaires are the instruments of that review. What actually gets decided, however, is not trust but what opens up on our side if that service is compromised. And that question is usually absent from the checklist.

  • What you grant outward becomes inward permission. The broad scope granted for convenience at integration time simply stays
  • There is no list of integrations. Nobody knows organisation-wide which team connected what and when. Without a list there is no reclamation
  • Revocation is never designed. There is a screen for connecting and none for disconnecting, or there is one that nobody has ever pressed
  • Non-production shares credentials with production. This is the most common form. When experimentation, evaluation and continuous integration environments use the same keys as production, the boundary of the experiment becomes the boundary of production
  • Automated subjects move at a different speed from people. Roughly 17,600 actions in 4.5 days will not trip a threshold calibrated to human behaviour. Rules built around a person's daily volume are powerless in front of an automated subject
  • Non-production is also less watched. Attaching detection only to production is the norm, so where the boundary is continuous, entry happens on the unwatched side

The practical conclusion is not a tool choice but a boundary declaration. Write down explicitly which environments may reach production credentials, and make sure environments not on that list cannot. The broader question of which layer should contain an automated subject, and how, is treated separately in containing automated agents at the kernel layer.

9. Three Easy Misreadings

9.1 We adopted zero trust, so we are covered

Zero trust is not a product category but a decision principle. Its content is do not treat network location as a basis for trust, and that principle has to apply across both the connection layer and the permission layer. Applied only to the connection layer, the result is an organisation that trusts an overlay network instead of an office network. The object of trust changed; the structure did not. It is telling that the vendor itself raised this exact point in its own post-mortem.

9.2 We do not use that tool, so this does not concern us

What was confirmed here was access with no vulnerability, on a valid credential, following the normal procedure. That structure is not a property of a tool. It is a property of access control in general. It holds whether you run your own private network, buy a commercial product, or use a cloud provider feature. When a sentence still stands after you delete the product name from it, the lesson belongs to everyone.

9.3 Detection failed

As section 6 showed, what failed here was delivery, not detection. Blurring the distinction misdirects the remedy. And this failure mode is common. In an organisation receiving hundreds of alerts a day, failing to pick out the real one is not individual carelessness but a severity design problem. The thing to fix is the criteria, not the person.

10. Summary - Change the Question and the Work Becomes Visible

What to take from this is not a verdict on a particular tool. The tool did its layer's job. What remains is a question about how the layers are arranged, and that question has a different answer in every organisation.

  • What got breached is almost always the wrong question. If the answer is a product name, there is nothing to learn
  • What could it do once inside is the right question. The answer is a permission list, and that list can be shortened today
  • The connection layer shrinks the reachable surface. That is worth a great deal on its own. It is also where it stops
  • The permission layer shrinks the blast radius. This work has no end, which is why it must be run as operations
  • Lifetime shrinks the exposure window. The safest secret is the one that does not exist
  • Detection shrinks time. And the final stretch of detection is not technology but the path to a human
  • Records make the aftermath possible. Producing and keeping must be separated

One closing note. Both of these post-mortems wrote down what was unflattering to themselves. One recorded that an alert was not escalated properly; the other recorded that a key existed which never needed to exist. That is where the value of an incident report comes from. A published failure becomes another organisation's checklist. This article is nothing more than that checklist, tidied up.

## 6. What not to do

- Summarising an incident as a single product name. The lesson disappears
- Sharing unverified damage estimates
- Treating a detection failure and a delivery failure as the same thing
- Believing a connection-layer rollout substitutes for permission cleanup
- Granting temporary permissions with no expiry date and no named owner
- Rehearsing rotation for the first time during an incident
- Attaching detection to production only

Frequently Asked Questions

Was the network access control tool breached?

Based on the first-party post-mortems, that cannot be said. The vendor stated that no vulnerability in its product was found or exploited, and that instead a valid enrollment key obtained elsewhere was used through the normal registration path. If a product defect were the cause, patching would end it. If a valid credential travelled a normal path, patching changes nothing. What has to change is the scope and lifetime of what that credential unlocked.

How exactly do connection and permission differ?

The connection layer answers may this subject reach this far; the permission layer answers having arrived, what may it do. However narrow the connection, broad permissions turn one compromise into a total one; however narrow the permissions, unlimited reachability makes attempts unlimited. Neither substitutes for the other.

Why is least privilege always deferred?

Because widening takes five minutes with a visible payoff, while narrowing takes days and produces nothing observable when it works. Add the habit of leaving outage-time grants in place, plus the fact that old subjects have no owner. The realistic starting point is not a redesign but recording ninety days of actual usage and reclaiming the gap against what was granted.

What does detection failed actually mean?

In this case there was no absence of signal. The primary report states the first signals arrived from several layers at once, but that the criticality was not raised correctly and the on-call team was never paged. Detection happened and delivery did not. An absent signal is fixed by adding sensors; an undelivered signal only gets noisier if you do.

What can we check this week?

Four numbers: the count of access subjects, the share of credentials with no expiry, the gap between granted permissions and ninety-day actual usage, and log retention. If any of them cannot be produced, that fact is the first finding, and tool evaluation comes after.
