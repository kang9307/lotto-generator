Introduction: A New Paradigm for Dieting

In modern society, dieting has moved beyond simple weight loss to become a cornerstone of a healthy lifestyle. In today's flood of information, however, telling reliable advice from misinformation has become genuinely difficult.

According to the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, the adult obesity rate in Korea (BMI of 25 or higher) was reported as 48.8% for men and 26.2% for women in the 2024 survey. In 2023 it was 45.6% for men and 27.8% for women. Regaining lost weight, the so-called yo-yo effect, is also a common problem. The widely quoted figure that 95% of dieters regain the weight, however, traces back to a small 1959 study and is not supported by modern evidence, so it is more useful to focus on maintenance strategies than to fix a failure rate.

This guide presents science-based methods for sustainable weight management and healthy nutrition. Rather than short-term extreme dieting, it focuses on building healthy eating habits you can maintain for a lifetime.

The Science of Weight Management

Calorie Balance and Metabolism

Understanding Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)

BMR calculation (Harris-Benedict formula):
Men: 88.362 + (13.397 × weight kg) + (4.799 × height cm) - (5.677 × age)
Women: 447.593 + (9.247 × weight kg) + (3.098 × height cm) - (4.330 × age)

Factors that affect BMR:
- Muscle mass: 15-25 kcal per kg per day
- Age: decreases 2-3% every 10 years
- Sex: men have 10-15% higher BMR than women
- Genetics: ±20% individual variation

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

TDEE = BMR × activity factor

Activity factors:
- Sedentary (no exercise): 1.2
- Light (1-3 sessions/week): 1.375
- Moderate (3-5 sessions/week): 1.55
- High (6-7 sessions/week): 1.725
- Very high (twice daily): 1.9

The Physiology of Weight Loss

The Fat-Burning Mechanism

Lipolysis process:
1. Calorie deficit → hormonal changes
2. Release of adrenaline and noradrenaline
3. Fatty acids released from fat cells
4. Transport through the bloodstream to muscles
5. ATP production in mitochondria

Optimal fat-burning conditions:
- Moderate-intensity exercise (60-70% of max heart rate)
- Fasted state or 3-4 hours after a meal
- Adequate hydration
- Sufficient sleep (7-9 hours)

Nutrient Function and Optimal Intake

The Role of the Three Macronutrients

Carbohydrates: The Main Fuel

Types and characteristics of carbohydrates:

Simple sugars:
- Glucose, fructose, sucrose
- Rapid absorption, quick blood-sugar spikes
- Best consumed right after exercise

Complex carbs:
- Starch, dietary fiber
- Slow absorption, stable blood sugar
- Suitable as an everyday energy source

Recommended intake:
- 50-65% of total calories
- Dietary fiber: WHO says at least 25 g/day; the Korean 2025 standard uses 12.5 g per 1,000 kcal (at least 30 g for men and 25 g for women aged 50 and over)
- Refined sugar: under 10% of total calories

Protein: The Building Blocks of the Body

Functions of protein:
- Builds muscles and organs
- Synthesizes enzymes and hormones
- Supports immune function
- Increases satiety

Complete vs. incomplete proteins:
Complete: animal proteins (meat, fish, eggs, dairy)
Incomplete: plant proteins (legumes, nuts, grains)

Recommended intake:
- Average adult: 0.8-1.2 g per kg of body weight
- Active exerciser: 1.6-2.2 g per kg
- Older adults: 1.2-1.6 g per kg

Fat: An Essential Nutrient

Classifications of fat:

Saturated fats:
- Animal fats, coconut oil
- Keep under 10% of total calories

Unsaturated fats:
- Monounsaturated: olive oil, nuts
- Polyunsaturated: omega-3, omega-6

Trans fats:
- Found in processed foods
- Minimize intake

Recommended intake:
- 15-30% of total calories
- Omega-3: 1-2 g/day

Vitamins and Minerals

Water-Soluble Vitamins

Vitamin C:
- Function: antioxidant, collagen synthesis
- Recommended: 100 mg/day
- Sources: citrus fruits, strawberries, broccoli

B Vitamins:
- B1 (thiamine): carbohydrate metabolism
- B6 (pyridoxine): protein metabolism
- B12 (cobalamin): blood cell production
- Folate: DNA synthesis

Fat-Soluble Vitamins

Vitamin D:
- Function: calcium absorption, immune regulation
- Recommended: 15-20 μg/day
- Sources: sunlight, fatty fish

Vitamin A:
- Function: vision, immune function
- Recommended: 700-900 μg/day
- Sources: carrots, spinach, liver

Vitamin E:
- Function: antioxidant
- Recommended: 12 mg/day
- Sources: nuts, vegetable oils

Dietary Strategies for Weight Loss

Calorie Control Methods

Gradual Calorie Reduction

Safe rate of weight loss:
- 0.5-1 kg per week (2-4 kg per month)
- Daily calorie deficit: 500-750 kcal
- Avoid extreme restriction (very-low-calorie diets such as below 1,200 kcal only after talking with a clinician or dietitian)

Example:
For someone with a TDEE of 2,000 kcal
- Target intake: 1,500 kcal
- Expected loss: 0.5 kg per week
- A sustainable pace

Meal Timing and Frequency

Intermittent fasting (16:8):
- 16 hours fasting, 8 hours eating
- Skip breakfast or skip dinner
- Improves insulin sensitivity

Small, frequent meals:
- 5-6 small meals a day
- Stabilizes blood sugar
- Boosts metabolism

Cautions:
- Tailor to your lifestyle
- Avoid extreme methods
- Maintain nutrient balance

Foods That Increase Satiety

High-Protein Foods

How protein boosts satiety:
- Thermic effect of food (TEF): 20-30% of calories
- Hormone regulation: GLP-1 and PYY release
- Muscle preservation

Recommended foods:
- Chicken breast: 165 kcal, 31 g protein per 100 g
- Salmon: 208 kcal, 20 g protein per 100 g
- Greek yogurt: 59 kcal, 10 g protein per 100 g
- Legumes: 127 kcal, 9 g protein per 100 g

High-Fiber Foods

Benefits of dietary fiber:
- Increases satiety
- Stabilizes blood sugar
- Improves gut health
- Reduces calorie absorption

Soluble fiber:
- Oatmeal, apples, legumes
- Helps lower cholesterol

Insoluble fiber:
- Whole grains, broccoli, nuts
- Promotes bowel movement

Principles of a Healthy Diet

Models for a Balanced Diet

Harvard Healthy Eating Plate

Plate composition:
- 1/2: vegetables and fruits (a variety of colors)
- 1/4: whole grains (brown rice, oats, quinoa)
- 1/4: healthy protein (fish, chicken, legumes)

Additional considerations:
- Use healthy oils
- Drink enough water
- Moderate dairy intake
- Limit refined sugar

K-Food Diet

Korean-style healthy meals:
- Rice (bap) + soup (guk) + side dishes (banchan) structure
- Use of fermented foods (kimchi, doenjang fermented soybean paste)
- Include seaweeds (miyeok seaweed, dasima kelp)
- Centered on seasonal vegetables

Benefits:
- Rich in dietary fiber
- Watch sodium intake
- Balanced nutrients
- Culturally adaptable

A Food-Group Selection Guide

Grains and Starches

Prioritize:
- Brown rice, barley, oats
- Sweet potato, potato (with skin)
- Quinoa, amaranth

Limit:
- White rice, white bread
- Instant noodles
- Processed cereals
- Snacks and cookies

Protein Sources

Animal:
- Fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines)
- Chicken breast, lean meat
- Eggs, low-fat dairy

Plant:
- Legumes (soybeans, lentils, chickpeas)
- Nuts (almonds, walnuts)
- Seeds (chia seeds, flaxseed)

Fat Sources

Healthy fats:
- Olive oil, avocado oil
- Nuts and seeds
- Avocado
- Fatty fish

Fats to avoid:
- Trans fats (margarine, shortening)
- Excess saturated fat
- Reused frying oil

Nutrition Strategies for Healthy Weight Gain

Principles of Healthy Weight Gain

Calorie Surplus

Goal setting:
- 0.25-0.5 kg gain per week
- Additional 300-500 kcal per day
- Focus on muscle gain

Nutrient ratios:
- Carbs: 50-65%
- Protein: 10-20%
- Fat: 15-30%

High-Calorie Nutrient-Dense Foods

Healthy high-calorie foods:
- Nuts: 30 g almonds = 174 kcal
- Avocado: 1 whole = 320 kcal
- Olive oil: 1 tbsp = 119 kcal
- Granola: 1/2 cup = 230 kcal

Foods to avoid:
- Sugary drinks
- Processed foods
- Fast food
- Excess refined sugar

Nutrition Guide by Age

The figures in this section and in the pregnancy and lactation section were likewise not checked against the source tables in the 2026-09-29 update (update not confirmed).

Adolescence (Ages 13-19)

Special Needs During Growth

Energy needs:
- Boys: 2,200-3,200 kcal/day
- Girls: 1,800-2,400 kcal/day

Key nutrients:
- Calcium: 1,300 mg/day (bone growth)
- Iron: boys 11 mg, girls 15 mg/day
- Protein: 1.2 g per kg body weight
- Zinc: supports sexual development

Adulthood (Ages 20-64)

Focused on Preventing Lifestyle Diseases

Key management points:
- Maintain a healthy weight
- Manage blood pressure and blood sugar
- Control cholesterol
- Manage stress

Dietary characteristics:
- Limit sodium (under 2,300 mg/day for adults is the 2020 Korean value; the 2025 revision adjusted some age groups and the adult value was not checked against the source table. WHO: under 2,000 mg sodium, or 5 g salt)
- Limit saturated fat
- Increase dietary fiber
- Emphasize antioxidant-rich foods

Older Adults (65+)

Preventing Sarcopenia Through Nutrition

Special considerations:
- Prevent loss of muscle mass
- Maintain bone density
- Adapt to reduced digestive function
- Watch for drug-nutrient interactions

Nutritional strategies:
- Increase protein: 1.2-1.6 g per kg body weight
- Boost vitamin D: 20 μg/day
- Vitamin B12: consider reduced absorption
- Drink enough water

Nutrition for Special Situations

Exercise and Nutrition

Pre- and Post-Workout Nutrition

Before exercise (1-2 hours):
- Carb-focused (banana, oatmeal)
- Moderate protein
- Minimal fat
- Plenty of water

After exercise (within 30 minutes):
- Carbs:protein = 3-4:1
- Replenish muscle glycogen
- Promote muscle synthesis
- Examples: chocolate milk, protein shake

Strength Training and Protein

Optimal protein intake:
- 20-25 g of high-quality protein post-workout
- At least 2.5 g of leucine
- Split intake every 3-4 hours
- Casein protein before bed

Boosting protein absorption:
- Pair with vitamin C
- Include appropriate carbs
- Drink enough water

Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Nutritional Needs During Pregnancy

Additional calories:
- 2nd trimester: +340 kcal/day
- 3rd trimester: +450 kcal/day

Key nutrients:
- Folate: 600 μg/day (prevents neural tube defects)
- Iron: 27 mg/day (prevents anemia)
- Calcium: 1,000 mg/day (skeletal development)
- DHA: 200 mg/day (brain development)

Cautions:
- Limit high-mercury fish
- Avoid raw foods
- Limit alcohol and caffeine

Practical Meal-Management Tips

Meal Planning and Prep

Weekly Meal Planning

Planning steps:
1. Check your weekly schedule
2. Consider nutrient balance
3. Use seasonal ingredients
4. Set a budget
5. Write a shopping list

Time-saving tips:
- Weekend batch cooking
- Use frozen vegetables
- Cook multiple dishes at once
- Prep banchan (side dishes) ahead

Healthy Snack Management

Recommended snacks:
- A handful of nuts (30 g)
- Greek yogurt + berries
- Apple + almond butter
- Boiled eggs
- Carrots + hummus

Snack timing:
- 2-3 hours after a meal
- Before/after exercise
- Mid-afternoon (3-4 PM)
- Last snack 2 hours before bed

Managing Dining Out and Delivery

Making Smart Restaurant Choices

Menu selection criteria:
- Prioritize grilled, steamed, or boiled items
- Dishes with lots of vegetable sides
- Ask for sauce on the side
- Watch portion sizes of rice

Fast-food alternatives:
- Lead with salads
- Go light on dressing
- Choose water or unsweetened drinks
- Be careful with sides

Supplement Guide

Basic Supplements

Multivitamins

When they're helpful:
- Irregular eating
- Restrictive diets
- Older adults
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Selection criteria:
- Products tailored by sex and age
- Avoid megadosing
- Prefer natural sources
- Third-party certified

Omega-3

EPA/DHA guidelines:
- General: 1,000 mg/day
- Cardiovascular-risk groups: 2,000 mg/day
- Pregnancy: 200-300 mg DHA

Quality checks:
- Oxidation prevention
- Heavy-metal testing
- rTG form preferred
- Refrigerated storage

Special-Purpose Supplements

Exercise Supplements

Creatine:
- Boosts strength and power
- 3-5 g/day
- No loading phase required

BCAA:
- Prevents muscle breakdown during exercise
- Leucine:isoleucine:valine = 2:1:1
- Useful for fasted workouts

Protein powder:
- Meal replacement or supplement
- Whey, casein, or plant-based
- Check for artificial additives

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1: Do I have to cut carbs entirely to lose weight?

A: No. Carbohydrates are the primary energy source for the brain and muscles. Cutting them out entirely may lead to short-term weight loss, but most of it is water weight, and it often causes rebound weight gain and health problems over time. The key is choosing complex carbs over refined ones.

Q2: How many meals a day is ideal?

A: It depends on your lifestyle and metabolic profile. Whether you stick with traditional three meals or go with smaller, more frequent meals, total calories and nutrient balance matter more. A regular, consistent meal pattern is the key.

Q3: Can I still snack while dieting?

A: Yes. Choosing healthy snacks within your daily calorie budget can actually help prevent overeating and stabilize blood sugar. Pick nutritious options like nuts, fruit, or yogurt.

Q4: Can I get enough nutrients without supplements?

A: A balanced diet can cover most nutritional needs. That said, nutrients like vitamin D, omega-3s, and some minerals can be hard to get from food alone, and supplements may be worth considering as needed.

Q5: What should I do when my weight hits a plateau?

A: Plateaus are normal and reflect your body adapting to a new weight. It can help to step up exercise intensity, recalculate calories, or take a temporary maintenance break.

Q6: Why does losing weight get harder with age?

A: BMR drops, muscle mass declines, and hormones shift with age. To overcome this, maintaining muscle through strength training and increasing protein intake matter most.

Conclusion

A healthy diet and good nutrition are not a short-term goal but a lifelong journey. A science-based approach is the key to success.

Core principles of sustainable weight management:

  1. Gradual approach: steady improvement over abrupt change
  2. Balanced nutrition: harmonious intake of all nutrients
  3. Personalization: choose methods that fit your situation
  4. Lifestyle improvement: integrate diet and exercise
  5. Long-term perspective: a lifestyle change, not a temporary diet
  6. Science-based choices: rely on trustworthy information

There is no such thing as a perfect diet, but there is certainly an optimal one for each of us. Start with small changes today. Begin with one healthy meal, consistent meal times, and proper hydration, then gradually build healthier eating habits over time.

True health isn't measured by the number on the scale but by the condition of body and mind that lets you live an energetic daily life.

## 2. When you check your eating pattern

- [ ] I compared my carbohydrate, protein and fat shares with the ranges above.
- [ ] I checked that free sugars from drinks and snacks stay under 10% of energy.
- [ ] I read the WHO 5 g salt figure and the Korean adult figure (2,300 mg is the 2020 value) as separate things.
- [ ] I get at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables on most days.
- [ ] My rate of loss is around 0.5-1 kg a week and not faster.
- [ ] I did not start a very-low-calorie diet, such as below 1,200 kcal, without talking to a clinician or dietitian.
