Automating Slide Illustrations With AI - The Irasutoya Claude Skill Guide

Single-Call Transparent PNG Placement and the 20-Image Commercial Licence Rule

2026-09-14 Tech & IT

When drafting pitch decks, technical reports, architecture reviews, or corporate blog posts, presentations filled purely with dense text quickly fatigue the audience and dilute the core message. Placing a well-chosen visual illustration at the right inflection point instantly establishes context, guides reader comprehension, and elevates overall visual presentation. However, in practical office environments, the manual labor of hunting down appropriate graphical assets is notoriously tedious, creating an unwelcome friction that continually fractures the writer's intellectual focus.

Knowledge workers routinely find themselves opening multiple browser tabs across various stock asset portals, cycling through trial-and-error search queries, verifying convoluted copyright licensing terms, and discovering that downloaded raster files have opaque white backgrounds that necessitate tedious background-removal masking in separate graphic editors. Furthermore, assembling mismatched clips from disparate websites produces an inconsistent visual patchwork where line weights, color palettes, and artistic styles clash, undermining the professional cohesion of the final slide deck.

For more than a decade, the Japanese website いらすとや (Irasutoya), created and maintained by illustrator Takashi Mifune (みふねたかし), has stood out as a widely trusted haven for pragmatic communicators. Housing an enormous archive of approximately 25,000 distinct illustrations provided as clean, transparent PNG files, Irasutoya offers a warm, whimsical, yet remarkably clear aesthetic. It is universally recognized across Japan in government pamphlets, public transport notices, corporate presentations, television broadcasts, and YouTube channels, and has gained widespread international affection among engineers and creators. Nevertheless, because the website operates on top of Google's legacy Blogger platform, its built-in search functionality has long been notoriously fragile and unforgiving, often demanding precise Japanese phrasing that leaves non-native speakers and busy professionals frustrated.

In September 2026, open-source developer hsol released the irasutoya Claude Skill, an automation layer designed to eliminate this exact productivity bottleneck. Embedded within the conversational and agentic workflows of Anthropic's Claude, this skill empowers users to simply articulate what they need in plain language. Claude deduces the conceptual term, dispatches a background script that navigates Blogger's search quirks, fetches the authentic transparent PNG, and directly embeds it into slides, Markdown documents, or design canvases. This article provides an extensive technical investigation into why simple web fetches fail on Blogger, how the skill's 12-way parallel orthographic query engine resolves linguistic variance, how to utilize the command-line interface, and above all, the critical 20-image commercial licence rule that every professional must navigate.

This technical review is based strictly upon primary documentation, including the open-source GitHub repository (hsol/irasutoya) and the official Irasutoya terms of service. BrainDetox maintains strict editorial independence, neither promoting proprietary AI services nor embellishing capabilities, and focuses entirely on reproducible software engineering and copyright compliance.

1. Introduction: The Time Spent Hunting for Visual Assets

A surprising fraction of the working hours dedicated to building modern documentation is consumed not by synthesising domain knowledge or refining logical narratives, but by hunting down auxiliary visual assets. When an engineer drafts an enterprise migration roadmap or a product manager prepares an executive pitch, abstract concepts like server downtime, database replication, or incident triage call out for simple, relatable graphical motifs. Yet searching through generic commercial photo libraries often yields overly stiff, hyper-realistic imagery that feels jarringly misplaced in lightweight technical documentation.

On the other hand, attempting to generate quick spot illustrations using generative text-to-image AI platforms like Midjourney or DALL-E introduces severe workflow drawbacks of its own. Generative models struggle to produce uniform artistic consistency across dozens of presentation slides, as each generation varies unpredictably in rendering style, perspective, and color depth. Furthermore, generated outputs typically arrive as flattened rectangular raster files rather than pre-masked transparent PNGs, demanding secondary background-removal processing before they can sit cleanly atop custom slide themes.

This explains why professionals repeatedly return to curated archives like Irasutoya. Every single illustration in its 25,000-piece catalog shares an identical visual DNA, allowing multiple disparate characters and props to be composed together seamlessly without visual discord. More importantly, every file is pre-rendered with crisp alpha-channel transparency. However, the manual friction of visiting the website, wrestling with rigid search mechanics, downloading files, and moving them across directories remains substantial. By wrapping this entire lifecycle into an autonomous agent command, the irasutoya Claude Skill transforms an unruly manual chore into a low-latency background utility.

The significance of this tool reaches far beyond mere convenience. It demonstrates how modern LLM agents can bridge the gap between contemporary natural language reasoning and rigid legacy web platforms, effectively serving as an intelligent semantic middleware layer that unlocks vast, established content archives for modern automated authoring pipelines.

2. What Is the Irasutoya Claude Skill: Concepts and Operations

The irasutoya Claude Skill is a lightweight, zero-overhead extension engineered to integrate with Anthropic's agentic tooling environments, including Claude Code CLI, Claude Desktop, and interactive Claude Design canvas workspaces. When a user writing a technical document or slide deck instructs the assistant to place an illustrative character depicting a specific event, the AI translates the conversational intent into canonical Japanese semantic concepts, executes the search script, and automatically downloads and embeds the resulting transparent PNG.

2.1 A Single Call from Search to Transparent PNG Insertion

In a standard manual workflow, an author must interrupt their writing train of thought, switch application windows to a browser, navigate to the Irasutoya homepage, experiment with Japanese search terms, evaluate pagination results, open individual asset pages, save the original image to a downloads folder, and finally drag the file into their slide deck or code repository. This multi-step process demands sustained cognitive attention and typically drains several minutes per graphic.

By contrast, the Claude skill consolidates search, candidate ranking, and binary asset retrieval into a single deterministic tool invocation. According to official project benchmarks, this complete round-trip pipeline executes in approximately 1.5 seconds. Because the script resolves everything end-to-end, authors remain completely immersed in their primary task while high-resolution graphical assets appear dynamically in their workspace.

Because the fetched assets are original transparent PNGs with full alpha channel support, they blend instantaneously into any document background, dark-mode presentation theme, or web layout without requiring graphic editing software or post-processing filters. This affords non-designers and software engineers the ability to produce polished, expressive visual deliverables with negligible overhead.

2.2 CLI and Claude Design Environment Support

The architecture of the tool is consciously decoupled from proprietary chat interfaces. It provides a dual operational surface: a robust command-line interface (CLI) driven by an independent Python script that developers can invoke directly from terminal workflows, as well as native hooks for Anthropic's conversational environments.

A critical engineering consideration addressed by the skill involves Claude Design canvas artifacts. Modern browser sandboxes that render dynamic artifacts enforce strict Content Security Policies (CSP) that forbid loading remote media directly from third-party hosts like Google Blogger CDNs. If an agent simply injects an external image URL, the browser blocks the asset, leaving behind broken placeholders. To ensure seamless rendering, the skill supports local relative asset path referencing as well as instantaneous base64 data URI conversion, allowing illustrations to render flawlessly inside locked-down sandboxed environments.

3. Why a Naive Fetch Fails: Orthography and Blogger Search Limits

When software engineers first conceptualize building a search tool for an image blog, the instinctive assumption is to construct a trivial HTTP client that queries the site's search query parameter and scrapes the first resulting image tag. However, anyone attempting this approach against Irasutoya will immediately encounter catastrophic recall failure due to fundamental incompatibilities between Blogger's legacy search architecture and the orthographic complexity of the Japanese language.

3.1 Japanese Orthographic Inconsistency: Kanji and Katakana Mixing

Irasutoya is famously hosted on Google Blogger. The built-in search engine of Blogger is an antiquated system that relies on strict, literal string matching. It lacks modern inverted index tokenizers, morphological analyzers, or fuzzy semantic matching capabilities. If a search string does not match the exact sequence of characters stored in a post's title or body text, the engine simply returns an empty result set.

This limitation collides disastrously with Japanese orthography. In Japanese, identical semantic concepts can be validly written using kanji (Chinese characters), hiragana, or katakana, often interchangeably. Over its decade-long history of thousands of posts, Irasutoya has never enforced rigid editorial naming conventions. A title created in 2013 might write the word for monkey using the kanji 猿, whereas a companion illustration created in 2018 might write it using katakana サル.

This orthographic divergence causes naive queries to fail completely. In documented tests, executing a search for サル バナナ (monkey and banana in katakana) yields exactly zero hits on Blogger. Conversely, substituting the kanji form to query 猿 バナナ immediately surfaces two authentic, perfectly matching illustrations. A similar phenomenon governs everyday items: querying eyeglasses using kanji 眼鏡 versus katakana メガネ yields completely disparate subsets of the catalog. An agent issuing single-variant searches will inevitably miss the vast majority of relevant assets.

3.2 Why Particle Attachment and Verb Conjugation Break Search

The failure mode becomes even more pronounced when processing natural language prompts. When a human user asks an assistant to find an illustration of someone eating an apple, an LLM naturally translates the concept into the standard Japanese phrase リンゴを食べる (to eat an apple).

Submitting リンゴを食べる directly to Blogger results in zero hits. Because Blogger does not tokenize text into morphemes, it treats the noun concatenated with the accusative grammatical particle を as an indivisible string (リンゴを), which does not appear verbatim in titles. Furthermore, verbs in post titles frequently appear in progressive, descriptive, or nominalized grammatical forms rather than the dictionary form (食べる).

Only when the grammatical particle is severed and replaced by whitespace—querying リンゴ 食べる—does the search engine successfully match the stored illustration titles. Without systematic grammatical normalization, morphological decomposition, and particle stripping, automated queries against Irasutoya are doomed to near-total failure.

4. Technical Solution: 12-Way Parallel Fanout and Client-Side Ranking

Rather than attempting to re-index the entire multi-gigabyte catalog or lamenting Blogger's search limitations, the irasutoya Claude Skill resolves this architectural challenge on the client side through an intelligent query fanout engine coupled with parallel network dispatch and heuristic post-filtering.

4.1 Generating 12 Queries and the Parallel Fanout Mechanism

When supplied with a target conceptual term, the script's transformation engine decomposes the phrase and algorithmically generates up to 12 distinct orthographic permutations. The deterministic transformation pipeline applies the following discrete rules:

  • Grammatical particle stripping: Function particles including を, に, で, and が are pruned from clause boundaries to isolate independent lexical stems.
  • Exception vocabulary preservation: Naive regular expressions that blindly remove particles risk corrupting compound nouns. The script maintains an internal exception dictionary to protect words like のり (seaweed or glue), ensuring its initial character の is never mistakenly stripped as a possessive particle.
  • Bidirectional kana conversion: Phrases rendered in hiragana are converted to katakana and vice versa, accommodating variations in authorial spelling habits.
  • Kanji and katakana synonym swapping: High-frequency semantic equivalents such as 猿 and サル, or 眼鏡 and メガネ, are dynamically exchanged across queries.
  • Verb tail truncation: Inflectional verb suffixes and auxiliary endings are trimmed back to root stems to ensure compatibility with diverse conjugational appearances in post titles.

Crucially, the resulting batch of up to a dozen permutations is not processed sequentially over high-latency serial HTTP connections. Utilizing Python's asynchronous IO and low-level curl primitives, all permutations are dispatched in parallel against Blogger's feed endpoints simultaneously. This concurrent fanout ensures that searching twelve orthographic variants takes virtually the same elapsed time as a single query, preserving a snappy 1.5-second total response window.

4.2 Partial-Match Demotion and Summary Endpoint Optimization

Dispatching a dozen wide-net search queries inevitably floods the client with dozens of raw candidate items, introducing false positives and irrelevant noise. The skill mitigates this by passing all retrieved candidates through a strict client-side re-ranking filter.

A prominent heuristic is the deliberate demotion of substring matches. In an early bug documented during development, querying for サル (monkey) caused the raw search engine to place アンドリューサルクス (Andrewsarchus, an extinct prehistoric carnivorous mammal) at the very top of the results, because the letters サル appeared verbatim inside its taxonomic name. The skill's ranking module penalizes items where the query term exists only as a fragment embedded within a longer, unrelated compound word, guaranteeing that mainstream, prototypical monkey illustrations take priority.

In addition, the author implemented a crucial network optimization: querying Blogger's /feeds/posts/summary feed endpoint rather than the conventional /feeds/posts/default route. Both endpoints return the requisite image metadata, entry IDs, and titles, but the summary endpoint sheds bloated HTML bodies, cutting response payloads by approximately 40%. Across twelve concurrent requests, this bandwidth reduction substantially lowers client memory overhead, accelerates parsing speed, and minimizes the risk of network throttling.

5. Installation and Usage: CLI Architecture and Configuration

The structural philosophy underpinning the irasutoya skill emphasizes radical simplicity and zero friction. In an era where modern developer utilities frequently burden user systems with massive container images and fragile dependency trees, this project relies exclusively on core operating system utilities.

5.1 Environment Requirements and Installation

The runtime requirements are constrained strictly to Python 3 and standard curl. There are no third-party package dependencies, virtual environments, or compiled extensions required. From a networking standpoint, the host environment merely requires outbound HTTPS connectivity to www.irasutoya.com and Google's static asset domain blogger.googleusercontent.com.

Installation into Claude's skill registry requires a single Git clone invocation pointing to the designated user skills directory:

git clone https://github.com/hsol/irasutoya ~/.claude/skills/irasutoya

By default, downloaded image files are stored in ~/Downloads/irasutoya. Users can customize this destination directory by exporting the IRASUTOYA_OUT environment variable in their shell configuration.

6. The Critical Commercial Licence Boundary: The 20-Image Rule

While the technical engineering of this skill is impressive, the single most critical factor for professionals and corporate teams adopting this workflow is strict adherence to copyright terms. Because Irasutoya is widely celebrated online as a free image resource, many users fall into the perilous misconception that its assets exist in the public domain and can be utilized without quantitative limitation. In reality, the official terms of service establish a rigid quantitative ceiling for commercial utilization.

6.1 Free-Use Scope and the Billing Threshold at 21+ Images

Irasutoya provides exceptionally generous foundational terms. For personal, non-commercial endeavors, usage is virtually unrestricted. Even for commercial business entities, incorporating illustrations into marketing collateral, customer-facing presentations, YouTube video productions, or corporate websites is entirely free and does not require attribution or copyright credits. However, this commercial exemption carries a strict quantitative threshold.

Under the official licensing terms, a maximum of 20 distinct illustrations may be used free of charge within a single commercial design or project. The moment a single commercial project incorporates 21 or more distinct illustrations, a paid commercial licence agreement must be executed with the author, accompanied by appropriate licensing fees. Repeating the exact same illustration across multiple pages within the same document counts as a single illustration, but utilizing 21 different artworks triggers mandatory licensing.

The official guidelines define the boundary of a single commercial design across several distinct operational contexts:

  • A single presentation slide deck: The entire slide deck constitutes one commercial design unit, regardless of total slide count.
  • A single printed promotional item: One catalog, brochure, marketing leaflet, or poster project represents a discrete design unit.
  • A single website or mobile application: The collective UI graphics, landing pages, and permanent assets of one domain represent one unit.
  • A single video production: An independent YouTube video, promotional clip, or webinar recording constitutes one design unit.
  • A single corporate training manual: An internal instructional handbook or printed curriculum represents a single operational project.

In enterprise slide production, it is remarkably easy for an author to insert two or three spot graphics per slide across a 30-slide pitch deck, quickly exceeding 50 images. Relying on an automated AI tool without tracking aggregate image counts creates genuine legal exposure. Maintaining strict awareness of this 20-image threshold is essential for any professional team.

6.2 How the Skill Notifies Users and the Duty to Verify Licensing

The developer of the irasutoya skill recognized this legal reality and embedded explicit accounting logic directly into the software. Notably, the skill treats this boundary as an informational advisory rather than an impassable programmatic gate.

The script tracks cumulative image retrieval when commercial context signals are detected. Upon crossing the 20-image threshold on a single project, it emits a clear, one-time terminal advisory warning the user that they have exceeded the free commercial allowance. If the user acknowledges the notification and instructs the tool to proceed, the skill honors the request and continues execution. The tool provides operational reminders, not legal counsel; the ultimate legal responsibility for verifying and complying with Irasutoya's published terms rests squarely upon the end user.

7. Implications and Practical Tips for Creators and Professionals

The emergence of the irasutoya Claude Skill offers profound practical utility for international knowledge workers, communicators, and independent creators who have historically struggled with the friction of foreign-language asset discovery.

7.1 Dramatically Cutting Search Fatigue and Unifying Visual Tone

For international professionals lacking fluency in Japanese, querying niche domestic websites is fraught with hesitation. Figuring out whether a concept is conventionally cataloged in kanji or katakana, or wrestling with machine translators to derive colloquial Japanese search terms, wastes valuable creative momentum. With this Claude skill, a user can simply instruct the model in English or Korean to illustrate an engineering sprint retrospective, and the assistant handles semantic translation, orthographic expansion, and asset delivery within seconds.

Moreover, presentation quality hinges on visual unity. Assembling heterogeneous vectors from disparate free repositories results in a visually fragmented deliverable. By anchoring an entire document to Irasutoya's singular aesthetic and using the --label filter to pin down specific contexts (such as business, medical, or education), authors can produce clean, cohesive, and approachable materials with unprecedented speed.
