Measuring a Model's Size From the Outside

What becomes a signal and what does not - and how far an estimate should be trusted

Aug 29, 2026 Dev & Infra

Introduction: The cell in the proposal that stays empty

Somewhere in an adoption review there is a field nobody can fill. How big is this model. Deciding whether self-hosting is even possible requires knowing how many gigabytes the weights occupy. Setting a performance expectation requires knowing roughly what weight class it belongs to. What the provider publishes is a price sheet and a context length. So someone on the team repeats a number they saw somewhere, the number lands in the meeting notes, and two months later it is being used as a premise with no source attached.

Not publishing is not misconduct. Weight count exposes training expenditure and infrastructure shape fairly directly, so keeping it private is an ordinary business decision. But that decision leaves everyone outside guessing at the interior from observable signals, and having to judge for themselves how solid the guess is.

One approach that tackles this head-on was published recently. IKP (Incompressible Knowledge Probes), proposed on the Trillion Labs blog, measures how many rare facts a model has retained and works backward to a weight class. The premise is that reasoning ability can be improved through training method, but facts that must be memorized do not compress.

Rather than restate that work, this piece steps back one pace. What kind of task is inferring the interior from the exterior, in principle, and where does it fall apart? The short version: every signal available from outside is a composite of several causes, which is why the answer is never a figure but always a range.

1. Why anyone wants to know the size

The question is often treated as idle curiosity, but in practice it usually gets asked right before a decision.

  • To establish a cost floor — the whole weight set has to live in memory, so size determines how many accelerators a self-hosted deployment needs and what the minimum configuration looks like. This is the one place where size answers almost directly
  • To calibrate performance expectations — knowing the weight class gives a feel for what difficulty it will clear unaided and where prompting has to make up the difference
  • To make results reproducible — re-checking a paper or an internal experiment requires the conditions of the comparison. Without a size, the comparison does not hold together at all
  • To read the market — judging how far open-weight models have closed the gap requires looking not only at scores but at the scale used to produce them
  • To decide whether to move down — whether a cheaper tier is enough depends on knowing what you are running now, and the same number is needed at the negotiating table when arguing about whether a rate is reasonable

Only the first of these maps one-to-one onto size. The rest do not resolve automatically even if the number were handed to you. That observation returns near the end of this piece, because a good share of the effort spent estimating size is really a different question mistranslated into size.

2. Four things the word size points at

Before estimating anything, one thing has to be settled: what is being measured. Without that agreement, two people argue using one word for different numbers.

QuantityWhat it determinesHow it appears from outside
Total parametersMemory residency, storage, deployment difficultyAlmost invisible. Only an observed correlation with stored knowledge
Active parametersCompute spent processing a single tokenTracks per-token generation time and unit price relatively closely
Non-embedding parametersCapacity of the body, independent of vocabulary sizeInvisible. Only vocabulary size is visible, through the tokenizer
Training tokensSeparates models that share a parameter countInvisible. Only indirectly inferable from knowledge coverage

In practice the first two rows are where conversations derail. When someone calls a model big, whether that is a statement about memory or about compute leads to entirely different conclusions. Since mixture-of-experts designs became common, those two can differ by more than a factor of ten.

The fourth row gets forgotten just as often. The same parameter count trained on a different volume of tokens produces a different model. Half the reason attempts to predict capability from size keep missing lives right there.

3. The signal this proposal chose: knowledge that will not compress

The idea behind IKP is simple. Ask about facts that cannot be derived by reasoning, meaning information that cannot be answered unless it is stored somewhere, and work backward from how many are answered correctly to storage capacity. What year a particular event began is not something intelligence alone produces.

Two assumptions carry it. One is that a strong relationship exists between the amount of stored fact and model scale. The other is that reasoning improves over time at a given size while memorized volume does not improve nearly as fast. Methods of solving problems can be made efficient; the things that must be remembered still take up room.

The stated measurement conditions are below. Numbers carried out of a source have to travel with what was measured and under what conditions.

  • The baseline was built from 93 open-weight models. The relationship between recalled fact volume and scale is fitted first on models whose size is public, then closed models are placed on that line
  • Questions were split into seven difficulty bands. Easy facts are answered by small models too, so without banding the discrimination disappears at the top end
  • Validating by removing one model at a time and predicting it from the rest gave a typical error of about 1.48x as reported
  • Trends over time were checked separately across 100 models, and the handling of mixture-of-experts designs was checked against a comparison of 41 MoE models
  • MoE models were converted on a total-parameter basis, following the observation that recalled volume ties more strongly to total parameters than to active ones

The stated limits are equally clear. An exact parameter count cannot be recovered; above the trillion mark only two public models exist as reference points, so uncertainty there is large; and figures for models that refuse often should be read as a lower bound rather than as actual capacity.

This approach has one advantage over the alternatives. It is less disturbed by how the model is served. Price and latency are rewritten by serving infrastructure, but what a model remembers sits inside the weights. In exchange it brings in a new variable, training data composition. A small model trained heavily on facts and a large one weighted toward code and reasoning do not sit on the same line.

4. The other signals visible from outside

Beyond recalled knowledge there are several observable values. The trouble is that each one is a composite of several causes. Separating what each indicates from what it cannot makes it possible to judge which claims are usable as evidence.

SignalWhat it indicatesWhat it cannot indicate
Time to first tokenQueue state, prefill headroom, routing structureParameter count. Input length and server load dominate it
Time per output tokenWidth of the decode path, memory bandwidth headroomTotal parameters. In an MoE model it lights up only the active side
Concurrent throughputBatching headroom and infrastructure scaleModel size. A large cluster runs a large model fast too
Token priceThe provider's market strategy and target customerCost. There is a layer of policy between price and cost
Context lengthDecisions about attention design and cache budgetParameter count. It is a design choice, not a consequence of scale
Tokenizer vocabulary sizeScale of the embedding matrix, model lineageSize of the body. A large vocabulary can sit on shallow layers
Knowledge coverageStorage capacity combined with training dataSeparation of the two. Different data mixes make equal sizes look unequal

The right-hand column is closest to the point of this piece. No single signal points at size on its own. Working backward requires holding the remaining variables fixed, and an outside observer has no handle to hold them with.

5. Price is strategy, not cost

The signal that looks easiest is the one that misses by the most. Unit price is public and lands on a clean number, which makes it look comparable, but cost is only one of several inputs to the process that produces it.

  • Introductory pricing — a new entrant may price below cost to buy share, and a free developer tier severs the link to cost entirely. Reading size out of prices in this range comes out too small
  • Bundling — usage included in a higher subscription tier does not convert into a per-unit price. The same model wears a different face per channel
  • Tiered rates — batch processing, off-peak windows and cache hits carry separate rates. One set of weights ends up with three or four prices
  • Utilization — the same hardware costs more in practice when it sits idle. A provider who fills demand well can sell cheaper and still keep margin
  • Hardware generation and power — cost itself varies with contract terms and regional electricity rates. From outside, even the generation the model runs on is unknown

Put together, the arrow from price to size crosses an unknown twice: once from price to cost, once from cost to size. Treating the output of two such leaps as evidence means reading a company's sales policy and calling it model scale. Why per-token rates falling does not lower the bill is covered separately in Token Prices Keep Falling but Your Bill Does Not.

6. Latency and output distributions: two windows, two views

Time and probability sit closer to physics than price does. Each still comes with its own kind of blur.

6.1 Time to first token and time per token measure different things

Treating latency as one number reveals nothing. It has to be split at least in two. Time to first token is queue waiting plus the prefill pass over the whole input. The interval between subsequent tokens is the cost of one generation step. The former grows with input length; the latter is roughly constant.

Scale relates to the latter. Generation steps are usually bound by memory bandwidth, so the more weights that must be read to produce one token, the slower it goes. Pulling that relationship out of a real measurement, however, means walking through several layers of fog.

  • Dynamic batching — requests are processed alongside other users' traffic, so the same request shows different speeds at different times of day
  • Prefix caching — when the leading portion is already computed, prefill is skipped wholesale. That is why sending the same prompt twice makes the second one fast
  • Speculative decoding — when a small model proposes several tokens and a large one verifies them in a single pass, per-token time no longer lines up with the size of the main model
  • Routing and placement — a query may go to a different backend, and the value also shifts with which accelerator in which data center it lands on. The thing being measured changes request by request

All four change latency for reasons unrelated to size. Latency-based estimation only becomes comparable under the same clock time, the same input length and enough repetitions, and even then it lights up only the active-parameter side. What actually becomes the bottleneck on the hardware axis is laid out in What Actually Decides Inference Hardware.

6.2 Output distributions, a narrow window

Seeing even part of the probability a model assigns to the next token yields far more than reading the text alone: vocabulary size, the structure of the output layer, similarity among models descended from a shared lineage. That is why academic work has moved in this direction. Structural properties such as an upper bound on the output layer's dimensionality can, in principle, surface once enough varied output is collected.

In practice this window is mostly closed. Commercial APIs either withhold probabilities or cap them at a handful of candidates, and even those come out after safety filtering and sampling settings. Structural estimation of this kind also needs a large volume of queries, which raises a billing problem alongside a terms-of-service problem. Commercial usage terms commonly restrict attempts to determine a model's internal structure. So no method appears here. That research uses this approach is as far as this section goes.

7. What mixture-of-experts broke

Before mixture-of-experts designs became common, size, cost and capability mostly moved together. Bigger meant smarter, slower and dearer. That no longer holds.

  • Memory has to hold the total. Self-hosting cost is therefore set by total parameters
  • Computing one token uses only the active portion. Response speed and unit price therefore track active parameters
  • The amount of retained fact has been observed to attach more to the total side. That is the basis on which this proposal converted MoE models to a total-parameter footing
  • The result is that fast, cheap and well-informed can now coexist, which the old intuition cannot explain. And from outside, dense and sparse cannot even be told apart; the proposal states plainly that its method makes no such distinction

Because of this, quoting a single number of billions already misstates one side. Quote the total and you set wrong expectations about speed and price; quote the active count and you set wrong expectations about deployment cost and breadth of knowledge. Two numbers together are the minimum for a sentence that holds. Why architectures moved this way is covered in LLM Architecture Trends 2026.

8. Four ways estimates go wrong

However refined the method, a target that will not hold still throws the result off. What actually breaks estimates is usually not methodology but these four.

  • Distillation — training a small model on a large one's outputs gives the small model capability out of proportion to its size. A regression from capability to size bends here
  • Quantization and serving optimizations — the same weights at lower precision change memory footprint and speed. Reading size out of latency picks up a systematic error at this point
  • Routing — one name can front several models. When short easy queries go to a light backend and hard ones to a heavy one, what has been measured depends on the mix of queries
  • Version replacement — an endpoint name can stay while the thing behind it is refreshed. Last month's measurement belongs to last month's model, and nothing guarantees it still holds today

One more sits on top. What reaches us through an API is usually not the model alone but a product with a system prompt, tool calls and retrieval augmentation layered on. A correct answer does not prove the fact was in the weights. For knowledge-based estimation that distinction is fatal.

So any measurement taken from outside has to carry a date, an endpoint and a settings record. Without those three there is no way to re-check the number, and a number that cannot be re-checked is not evidence.

9. How to hold an estimate

So how far should one trust it? The rule collapses to a single line. A number without a range and stated assumptions does not get used.

Having a feel for what an error multiplier means makes the judgment easier. If a method's typical error is around 1.5x, an estimate of three hundred billion means somewhere between roughly two hundred and four hundred fifty billion. That width is ample for sorting weight classes and useless for comparing vendors. Two estimates from the same method differing by 1.3x are not different; that is noise.

What to ask when handed an estimateIf there is no answer
Total parameters or active parameters?Do not compare it against other estimates
When was it measured?Treat it as having no expiry date, which means do not cite it
What model set formed the baseline?Anything outside that set is extrapolation; drop confidence sharply
What error was reported?Do not read it as a point estimate. Use it for order of magnitude only
Were refusals and safety filters corrected for?Read it as a lower bound only
Was retrieval or tool use involved?Do not interpret it as a property of the model alone

Even an estimate that clears this table supports limited claims. This model is roughly in that weight class is the ceiling; this model has N billion parameters overreaches regardless of where the sentence came from. The moment a citation strips the conditions the original author attached and carries only the number, a carefully built estimate turns into an unsupported fact. Most contamination happens not in the method but at the citation step.

10. What to measure instead of size

Back to the point deferred earlier. A large share of size questions were really other questions, and those other questions are usually directly measurable, often more easily.

  • Accuracy on your own work — an in-house evaluation set of a few dozen items is enough to start. It answers the adoption decision more directly than any public leaderboard
  • Failure type and frequency — how it fails matters more than how often. Silent wrongness and outright refusal call for entirely different handling, and refusal rates vary enormously by domain while having nothing to do with size
  • Cost per task — look at the total to finish one unit of work, not the price per token. It only means something once retries and long reasoning traces are inside the number
  • Latency distribution — split p50 from p95 instead of averaging. What users feel is the tail
  • Effective context — check whether quality holds at the advertised length rather than trusting the number. The two often differ
  • Tolerance to replacement — does it survive a silent model refresh? Keeping a regression set makes it possible to detect the swap at least after the fact

These values share a property. They can be re-measured by hand, and they move when the target moves. A parameter count estimated from outside satisfies neither. How to start building an evaluation set is written up separately in An Eval Without a Definition of Failure Only Produces Numbers.

Does that make size estimation useless? Not at all. Mapping the shape of the market, locating how far the open-weight camp has come, judging whether a given capability came from scale or from data and training method — for those, estimates of this kind are the only material available. But that is the seat where an industry is understood, not the seat where a system is designed. Design decisions run on numbers measured directly.

What could not be confirmed

  • The concrete composition and item count of the question set IKP used could not be confirmed from the public post. Only the seven difficulty bands are stated
  • What distributional assumption produced the reported figure of about 1.48x, and whether it varies by size band, could not be confirmed
  • Figures obtained by applying this method to commercial models are not reproduced here. There is no way to verify them, and carrying them over would circulate a conditioned estimate as fact
  • Reproduction by other research groups, and how service terms treat structural estimation, could not be confirmed. The former is recent enough that follow-up work does not yet exist; the latter varies by provider and no clause-by-clause comparison was made

Conclusion: three things that remain

Measuring the interior from outside is, in principle, the task of isolating one cause from a composite signal. As long as the rest cannot be held fixed, the answer arrives as an interval rather than a point. Accepting that settles how to treat estimates.

  • Settle which size is meant first. Since total and active diverged, no comparison holds without that agreement
  • Do not cite a number that lacks a range and assumptions. Citations that strip conditions cause most of the contamination
  • Run decisions on numbers you measured. Accuracy, cost per task and tail latency are measurable without knowing the size, and knowing the size does not answer them

Reading a weight class from recalled knowledge is interesting not because it is accurate but because it picked a signal that wobbles less where price and latency wobble most. A good estimate comes not from hitting the right answer but from knowing which ground is steadier. And someone who knows that is not embarrassed to attach a range to their own number.
