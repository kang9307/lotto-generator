How to Clean a Washing Machine - A Step-by-Step Guide to Killing Odor and Mold

From the Tub Clean Cycle to the Gasket, Dispenser and Drain Filter

Jul 26, 2026 Lifestyle

Introduction: The Signs That Mean Clean It Now

Fresh towels come out smelling musty. Black flakes cling to dark clothing. Opening the door releases a whiff of drain. Peel back the rubber gasket and the underside is speckled black. If any of that sounds familiar, the problem is not your detergent brand. The machine itself is contaminated. A washer supplies water, detergent residue, lint and skin oils on a continuous schedule, which is close to ideal for microbial films. Microbial colonization, biofilm formation and the resulting malodour on washing machine surfaces are well-documented subjects in the research literature.

The good news is that most cases are fixable at home. What you need is one approved cleaner, a couple of old towels, and about thirty minutes spent on the four places the water never reaches. This guide walks through the supplies, the step-by-step procedure, the mistakes that keep the smell alive, the safety lines you must not cross, and the point at which you should stop and call someone. If you are still shopping for the machine itself, see our Complete Washing Machine Buying Guide first. One rule outranks everything below: your own manual beats this article, because brands disagree about which cleaners are allowed.

1. Why the Smell Happens - Three Causes

1.1 Trapped Water and a Sealed Door

When a cycle ends, water still sits in the outer tub, the drain hose and the folds of the door gasket. Close the door on top of that and you have created a warm, dark, humid box. LG advises opening the door and the dispenser drawer after washing so the interior can dry, and Samsung gives the same guidance for the lid and detergent drawer. Half the odor problem is decided by this one habit.

1.2 Too Much Detergent and Fabric Softener

More detergent feels like more cleaning power. In practice, surfactant and softener that the rinse cannot carry away stays on the tub wall as food and glue for microbes. Fabric softener is the worst offender because its oily base leaves a tacky film in the dispenser and its channels. Dose to the printed line, or slightly under it.

1.3 Washing Almost Everything Cold

Cold washing saves energy and protects fabrics, but there is a hygiene cost. Review work in the field notes that lowering wash temperatures can favour the survival of microorganisms both inside the machine and on the laundry itself. If you wash cold by default, shorten the interval between tub clean cycles rather than lengthening it.

3. Step 1 - Run the Tub Clean Cycle (1 to 4 hours, easy)

Empty the drum. Never run this cycle with laundry inside. Cycle names vary by brand: Tub Clean, Self Clean, Pure Cycle, Drum Clean.

3.1 Front Loader

  1. Empty the drum completely.
  2. Add the cleaner before water enters, either to the dispenser or into the drum.
  3. Select the tub clean or self clean cycle and start it.
  4. When it finishes, leave the door open so the interior dries.

Per LG guidance, the Tub Clean cycle supplies hot water and holds it at about 60 degrees Celsius, and should be run after every 30 washes or whenever the display shows tcL. Runtime varies by model, and several hours is common on front loaders.

3.2 Top Loader

  1. Empty the tub and fill to the highest water level.
  2. Once full, add the cleaner, agitate briefly to dissolve it, then soak for 20 to 30 minutes.
  3. Run the tub clean cycle, or a standard cycle, all the way through.
  4. If a lot of residue surfaces, run one extra rinse.

The order is the thing to remember: front loader, cleaner first; top loader, water first. A top loader submerges a large surface so soaking works, while a front loader tumbles in a small volume of water and needs the cleaner already dissolved.

3.3 No Cleaning Cycle on Your Model

On a top loader, substitute the highest water level plus hot water on a standard cycle. On a front loader, run the hottest available cycle empty. Confirm in the manual that your model permits running empty and what its maximum temperature is.

4. Step 2 - Clean the Door Gasket (15 minutes, easy, front loaders only)

This is the single biggest odor source on a front loader, and the cycle water does not reach into the folds. It has to be done by hand.

  1. Pull the lip of the gasket back with your fingers. Do not yank it.
  2. Remove standing water, hair, coins, buttons and lint from inside the fold.
  3. Wipe the folded surface all the way around with a cloth and cleaning solution. Work black spots with a soft brush.
  4. Dry it completely with a towel, then leave the door open.

Samsung advises cleaning the door seal on front load models weekly, pulling the gasket back carefully, wiping inside the folds and drying fully. LG similarly advises wiping the gasket surface and pulling the lip back to clean underneath. Black staining that has already set into the rubber may not come out entirely, which is cosmetic rather than a fault.

5. Step 3 - Wash the Detergent Drawer (10 minutes, easy)

Detergent and softener harden in the dispenser and block the channels, which reduces how much actually reaches the wash and leaves residue behind. This is odor source number two.

  1. Pull the drawer out fully, press the release point and remove it.
  2. Take out every removable insert and the softener cap.
  3. Soak in warm water and scrub with a soft brush.
  4. Also brush the roof of the cavity the drawer slides into. This is the part everyone forgets.
  5. Dry fully, reinstall, and leave the drawer slightly open between washes.

Samsung advises cleaning the detergent drawer about once a month, rinsing it in warm water and using a soft brush to remove buildup that degrades performance.

6. Step 4 - Clear the Drain Pump Filter (15 minutes, moderate)

Behind the small lower panel of a front loader sits the drain pump filter. Lint, hair and coins collect there, and a clogged filter means poor drainage and a strong smell.

  1. Unplug the machine and start with a completed cycle.
  2. Open the lower cover. Put a towel and a shallow pan underneath.
  3. If there is an emergency drain hose, uncap it and drain the standing water first. There will be more water than you expect.
  4. Twist the filter counterclockwise, remove it, clear the debris and rinse it.
  5. Shine a light into the filter cavity and clear anything left inside.
  6. Reinstall the filter fully tightened and close the cover. A loose filter leaks.

Samsung advises cleaning the debris filter on front load models at least monthly, or every 40 washes. Top loaders have no lower pump filter; instead, pull out the lint filter near the top of the tub and rinse it. That is the part top load owners skip most often.

7. Step 5 - Build the One Minute Drying Habit (1 minute, easy)

Do all of the above and keep the old habits, and the smell returns within weeks. Four rules cover it.

  • Leave the door and dispenser open after each wash. If small children or pets could climb into the drum, use a door stopper that leaves only a gap, and keep the laundry area closed off.
  • Wipe the water pooled under the gasket with a dry towel.
  • Take the laundry out immediately. Wet fabric left in the drum picks up odor the same day.
  • Do not use the machine as a hamper for dirty clothes between washes.

If the machine lives in a damp utility area, ventilation and humidity control matter too. We covered that separately in the Complete Dehumidifier Buying Guide.

9. Five Mistakes That Keep the Smell Alive

9.1 Assuming One Cycle Finished the Job

An established biofilm does not release in a single pass. LG advises running Tub Clean once a week for three consecutive weeks when the musty smell is already present. Black flakes appearing after the first run mean material is coming loose, so add a rinse and continue with the next round.

9.2 Skipping the Places Water Never Reaches

Gasket folds, the cavity behind the detergent drawer, and the drain filter. No cycle cleans those three. Nearly every case of persistent odor traces back to one of them.

9.3 Being Generous with Detergent

Overdosing increases residue, which increases smell. Use the printed dose, and in soft water areas even slightly less usually washes just as well.

9.4 Shutting the Door Right After a Wash

The most common and most decisive mistake. Sealed plus wet plus warm is a growth chamber.

9.5 Attacking It with Metal Scourers or Harsh Chemicals

Scratches in the stainless drum and the rubber gasket hold soil better than smooth surfaces do. A soft cloth and brush are enough.

Safety warning - never mix chlorine bleach with an acidic cleaner.
Combining chlorine bleach with acids such as vinegar or citric acid can release toxic chlorine gas. Health authorities including the Washington State Department of Health repeatedly warn against mixing bleach with other cleaners. Exposure can cause coughing, burning of the eyes, nose and throat, difficulty breathing and chest tightness, and can be fatal at high concentrations. Using them one after the other is also dangerous, not just mixing them in the same load. If you switch products, rinse and drain thoroughly and leave at least a full cycle in between. Ventilate while you work, and leave the room immediately if you notice any symptoms.

10. Safety - The Lines Not to Cross

  • Never combine cleaners. As above. Bleach with vinegar, bleach with citric acid, bleach with acidic limescale removers: all off limits.
  • Use only what your manufacturer allows. LG recommends an oxygen-based cleaner and advises against chlorine-based and acidic cleaners because of discoloration and corrosion risk. Samsung, however, describes adding liquid chlorine bleach to the detergent compartment when odor persists. The brands genuinely differ, so your manual is the final word.
  • No high-foaming products. Excessive foam is cited as a cause of leaks from the washer. Dish soap and hand-wash detergent do not belong in a cleaning cycle.
  • Unplug before opening the filter panel. You are working next to standing water. Do not touch the outlet with wet hands.
  • Do not disassemble wiring, the motor or the pump. The user-serviceable range ends at the dispenser, the gasket and the drain filter cover.
  • Ventilate and limit skin contact. Wear gloves, open a window or run the extractor, and keep children and pets out of the room.

11. When to Book a Professional

These situations are past the point of DIY.

  • Three weeks of repeated tub clean cycles plus thorough hand cleaning, and black flakes still appear.
  • The machine is old and has never been deep cleaned.
  • Water does not drain, or the machine leaks onto the floor.
  • Grinding, knocking or severe vibration during the spin cycle.
  • No power, or repeating error codes.
  • Greasy marks keep transferring onto the laundry.

Drainage failure, leaks, abnormal noise and electrical faults are repair problems, not cleaning problems, so the manufacturer service line comes first. For a purely hygienic teardown clean, a specialist service is an option, but check your warranty status first, since unauthorized disassembly can affect coverage. When booking, ask whether they actually remove the tub or only run a chemical wash, and whether a post-service test run and leak check are included. The same seasonal-appliance logic applies to cleaning an air conditioner yourself.

12. Conclusion - A Thirty Minute Routine

To summarize. First, run a tub clean cycle once a month. Front loader: cleaner in before the water. Top loader: fill to the highest level, dissolve, soak, then run. Second, hand clean the three places the cycle cannot reach: the gasket folds, the detergent drawer and its cavity, and the drain filter. Those three decide roughly ninety percent of the outcome. Third, invest one minute a day. Open the door and dispenser, wipe the water under the gasket, take the laundry out at once. Fourth, dose detergent to the line. More product means more residue, which means more smell.

Then the safety piece. Chlorine bleach and acidic cleaners never meet, under any circumstances. Use only the cleaners your manual permits, and remember that manufacturers genuinely disagree on this point. If the odor is already established, do not expect one cycle to fix it; repeat weekly for about three weeks and watch the trend. If black flakes never stop, or if drainage, leak or noise symptoms appear, that is the moment to stop cleaning and start calling. None of this is difficult work. Doing it often, briefly, and without skipping a spot is the entire method.

Recommended cleaners, cycle names, service intervals and the range of user-serviceable parts vary by model and manufacturer, so always check your own instruction manual first.
