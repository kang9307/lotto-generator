Korea's Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction: what enrollees should check now

Claiming the maturity payout, refunds split by cause, tax on the employer share, and the alternatives still open

Aug 11, 2026 Finance & Economy

Financial and tax disclaimer

This article is general information, not financial or tax advice. Every requirement, amount and ratio here reflects Korean government guidance as published in August 2026 by the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency and the Ministry of Employment and Labor, and the rules that apply differ by enrollment year and plan type. Individual maturity payouts, refund amounts and termination decisions are determined by the operating agency. Confirm your own contract at the Naeil Chaeum Gongje subscription portal or with the service center at 1588-6259. This is a Korea-only program; readers outside Korea should treat it as a policy case study.

Where this program stands in August 2026

Search for the Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction and you still get a wall of pages titled 「how to apply in 2026」. Most of them are copies of notices written before the program closed. The counter is shut. The subscription portal operated by the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency carries the line 「enrollment is currently unavailable because the Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction program sunset in 2024」 on its program page, and the Work24 guide states plainly that new support stopped from 2024 and only those who already filed a subscription may claim government contributions.

So this article does not explain how to apply. Only two groups still have a stake here: workers whose contracts were formed before 2024 and are still running, and people who missed the window and are looking for a replacement. The first group needs to know when and how the maturity payout is claimed, how much comes back if they leave or are let go, and what tax lands on the money. The second group needs to know what is still open and how those programs differ in character.

The Youth Leap Account, which shares the word 「youth」 and gets searched alongside this one constantly, is a completely separate scheme. Different ministry, different funder, different term. There is a dedicated section on that below, because reading this article while conflating the two produces the wrong answer every time.

1. Why enrollment closed - what a sunset means

In Korean government notices, 「sunset」 does not mean the same thing as abolition. It means the scheme stopped accepting new participants as a budget line, not that its legal basis vanished. Rights already established survive. Government contributions keep accruing, maturity payouts keep being made, and early terminations keep being processed.

By its final years the program had already narrowed sharply. The eligibility text preserved in the Work24 guide describes young workers taking their first job at a construction or manufacturing SME with 5 to fewer than 50 employees. A scheme that once covered most industries had been cut to two, which is also why the surviving population of enrollees clusters in those sectors.

A sibling program was wound down alongside it. The Youth Incumbent Naeil Chaeum Gongje Plus, aimed at workers already in post, carries its own portal notice: 「enrollment is currently unavailable because the Youth Incumbent Naeil Chaeum Gongje Plus program sunset in 2023」. It closed a year earlier. Both now exist only as administration for existing members.

The picture as a list:

  • Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction — sunset in 2024, closed to new entrants. Only maturity and termination processing remain
  • Youth Incumbent Naeil Chaeum Gongje Plus — sunset in 2023, closed to new entrants
  • Naeil Chaeum Gongje (the five-year family) — no closure notice on the portal. Still running for SME and mid-sized company owners and their core personnel
  • SME Employee Preferential Savings Deduction — a later product for incumbent employees, built on top of a bank savings account

2. The 12 million won payout comes from three pockets

The fastest way for an enrollee to understand their own contract is to know which pocket each won came from. Termination refunds and tax treatment both follow that split. The two-year structure described in the Work24 guide looks like this.

PocketAmountHow it accruesCharacter
Worker contribution4,000,000 won160,000 won a month for 20 months, then 200,000 won for 4 monthsPaid out of your own salary
Employer contribution4,000,000 wonMonthly automatic transfer by the companyMoney the company put in
Government support4,000,000 wonDeposited across five installmentsMoney from the treasury
Maturity payout12,000,000 won plus interestAfter 24 months of continuous service-

2.1 The interest is not fixed

Nobody receives exactly 12 million won. Portal guidance states that the crediting rate is a compound variable rate that changes each quarter and is published on the portal, applied over the period from the contract effective date to the maturity date. Two people on the same plan therefore end up with slightly different totals depending on when their contracts took effect. A maturity notice that does not read 12,000,000 is not an error.

2.2 Learn your installment number

Every notice in this program is written in installments, not months. Government support arrives across five of them, and termination refund ratios are expressed in installment bands. Without knowing how many installments your contract has accrued, you cannot estimate a refund. That figure appears on the contract lookup screen of the subscription portal. Anyone weighing a resignation should open that screen first.

3. Claiming the maturity payout - when, where, how

The money does not land automatically. You have to claim it. Portal guidance sets two conditions that must both hold.

  • Service — continuous employment at the same company for the full 24-month subscription period from the contract effective date
  • Full accrual — the worker contribution, the employer contribution and the government support have all been deposited

The second condition is where people get stuck. A worker completes the full 24 months, but the employer never filed its government support claims on time, so the accrual is behind and the maturity claim screen will not open. Checking only your own payment history and waiting produces nothing but lost months. If you have met the service requirement and the claim is blocked, ask your HR contact about the company's support claim history first.

The claim itself is filed on the Naeil Chaeum Gongje portal. Unlike early termination there is no short countdown attached, but several items have to be verified through your employer, so processing it before you leave the company is far easier. If a job change is planned right after maturity, do not reverse that order.

4. Early termination - the cause decides the amount

The biggest misconception about early termination is 「at least I get back what I put in」. That is true for your own contribution. The problem is the other 8 million won. Who caused the separation completely changes where the employer contribution and the government support end up.

Termination causeWorker contributionEmployer contributionGovernment support
Employer-attributedPaid to the workerPaid to the worker50 to 100% of the accrued amount to the worker, depending on contract duration
Worker-attributedPaid to the workerReturned to the company0 to 50% of the accrued amount, depending on contract duration

Korea SMEs and Startups Agency guidance states that for employer-attributed termination the worker contribution and the employer contribution (described as the employer contribution for pre-2023 enrollments) are paid to the worker, with 50 to 100 percent of accrued government support also going to the worker. For worker-attributed termination it uses the phrase 「contributions are paid back to whoever accrued them」. The company's money goes to the company; the worker's money goes to the worker. Government support lands at 0 to 50 percent.

4.1 What counts as an employer cause

The Work24 guide sorts causes into employer, worker and other. Employer examples include business closure, unpaid wages and failure to file support claims. Worker examples include changing jobs and failing to pay the worker contribution. Mapped onto situations that actually come up:

  • The company shut down — employer cause. A large share of government support flows to the worker
  • Wages went unpaid — unpaid wages is listed as an employer cause, though the recognised scope and duration conditions are decided by the operating agency
  • Accrual stopped because the company never filed its support claims — treated as an employer cause under failure to submit claims
  • You left for a better offer — worker cause. The employer share returns to the company and government support is capped at half
  • You could not keep up your own payments — worker cause. A lapse in payment is itself a termination trigger

4.2 A ten-day deadline that is easy to miss

The Work24 guide requires early termination to be filed on the subscription portal within 10 days of the cause arising. People miss this constantly, because it collides with the week of clearing out a desk and filing for unemployment benefits. The moment a separation is confirmed, this belongs at the top of the list rather than the bottom.

4.3 The twelve-month line

Government support refund ratios are set in installment bands, and for worker-attributed terminations the guidance points to whether the contract passed 12 months as the dividing line. Leave before it and nothing of the government share remains; leave after it and up to half of the accrued amount is paid. The exact band boundaries and the ratio in each band, however, differ by enrollment year and plan type. Read them off the installment number on your own contract screen rather than transplanting the figures in this article.

5. Does the employer share really go back to the company

This question comes up more than any other because the employer's 4 million won is a third of the payout. The short answer for worker-attributed termination is yes, it goes back. Repayment to the accruing party is the governing principle.

One wording difference is worth noting. Portal guidance calls the employer side the employer contribution while adding in parentheses 「employer contribution for pre-2023 enrollments」. The naming and the funding arrangement for this money differed between the pre-2023 vintage and later ones. Which vintage your contract belongs to changes how you should read the notices, and it is exactly why online accounts contradict each other. Go by your own contract screen and the operating agency's notices, not by forum posts.

The practical decision usually takes this shape. The job has gone sour but maturity is a few months away. Leaving now sends the employer contribution back to the company and caps government support at half. The shorter the remaining term, the more sitting tight is worth. Conversely, where there is a genuine employer fault such as unpaid wages or workplace harassment, documenting the cause and getting an agency determination before the exit is recorded as voluntary is what moves the number. The same resignation, recorded under a different cause, is worth several million won.

6. Does it transfer when you change jobs - the narrow re-entry door

There is no portability. The contract does not follow you to a new employer. Because the Work24 guide classifies changing jobs as a worker-attributed termination, leaving voluntarily terminates it.

While the program was live there was an exception. Where the separation was caused by the employer — suspension of business, closure, insolvency, or a recommended resignation — one re-entry was permitted, conditional on finding new employment within six months of losing employment-insurance status. That condition is widely documented from the operating period, but how the route is handled now that new enrollment is closed could not be confirmed on official pages. It is recorded in the section below on what remains unconfirmed.

If you are being separated for company reasons, the safe sequence is this.

  • Pin down the cause first — the wording on the resignation letter and the employment-insurance separation code become the evidence for every later determination
  • Respect the 10-day deadline — the termination filing clock runs regardless of whether re-entry turns out to be possible
  • Record your re-employment date — if a six-month condition could apply, the date is the proof
  • Call the service center about your own case — 1588-6259. General guidance and an individual determination are not the same thing

7. Tax on the payout - Restriction of Special Taxation Act Article 29-6

Treating all 12 million won as tax-free leads to an unpleasant year-end settlement. The three pockets have different characters, so they are taxed differently.

  • Your own contribution — money you paid from salary that was already taxed
  • The employer share — money the company paid on your behalf, so it is counted as employment income and carries earned income tax
  • Interest — subject to interest income tax

That is why a separate relief exists. Article 29-6 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act (income tax reduction on performance compensation fund receipts) cuts the earned income tax on the employer-funded portion of Naeil Chaeum Gongje family payouts. The reduction rates published by the Korea SMEs and Startups Agency in its product notices read as follows.

  • Naeil Chaeum Gongje (five-year family) — a reduction equal to 50% of earned income tax on the employer's payments, or 30% for mid-sized companies
  • SME Employee Preferential Savings Deduction — 50% reduction on income tax on the employer support at maturity, and 90% for young workers

Note that the notation differs by product. Which rate attaches to a Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction payout may turn on plan type and the age condition at the time of receipt, and could not be confirmed on an official page for this article. The relief is also not automatic. Portal Q&A guidance describes submitting a reduction application to your company's payroll contact by the end of the month following receipt of the maturity payout. Miss that window and withholding closes out at the full rate.

For the wider set of year-end items that get overlooked, the tax saving guide for salaried workers in Korea follows on well, and for pairing this with retirement planning and tax credits there is the pension savings and IRP guide.

8. How it differs from the Youth Leap Account

Plenty of people treat these as one thing. Both start with 「youth」 and both build a lump sum. But one difference — who puts the money in — changes everything downstream.

AxisYouth Tomorrow Fill-up DeductionYouth Leap Account
Ministry and operatorMinistry of Employment and Labor · Korea SMEs and Startups AgencyFinancial Services Commission · Korea Inclusive Finance Agency · banks
CharacterA deduction contract tied to continued employmentA bank savings product

The difference that actually bites is what a resignation touches. The deduction sits on top of an employment relationship, so leaving shakes the contract. A savings account is decoupled from the employer, so a job change leaves it intact. The finer rules on the account side — ordinary termination, special termination, partial withdrawal — are set out in that article and are not repeated here.

9. What is still open

Anyone who missed the window needs to know not which product has a similar name but which door is actually open for their situation. As of August 2026 the landscape looks like this.

ProgramAdministered byEligibility axisWho tops it upTerm

9.1 The ones where your employer chips in

If what mattered about the Youth Tomorrow Fill-up Deduction was 「the company pays in too」, these two carry that trait forward. Naeil Chaeum Gongje has no closure notice on the portal and continues to operate. The weighting is heavier on the employer side: the ratio between the core employee and the owner must be at least one to two, and the portal's worked example pairs 100,000 won from the employee with 240,000 won from the owner. Contract terms run from three to ten years with one-year extensions.

The SME Employee Preferential Savings Deduction is built on a bank savings account. An employee pays freely within a range of 100,000 to 500,000 won a month, the company adds 20 percent of that payment (20,000 to 100,000 won a month), and the whole amount goes to the employee at maturity. IBK, Hana, NH Nonghyup and KB Kookmin add preferential rates, and the crediting rate is compound and variable. Three-year and five-year plans exist. Unlike the youth deduction, non-regular and part-time workers are also listed as eligible applicants.

Both share one constraint: they cannot start without the employer agreeing. This is not something a worker signs up for alone. If your company has an HR or general affairs contact, asking whether it participates is step one, and there is material to argue with — the employer side gets deductible expense recognition and a human resources development tax credit.

9.2 The one you can do on your own

The Youth Tomorrow Savings Account has a different character because it is a Ministry of Health and Welfare program. The government matches rather than an employer, and eligibility turns on household income rather than company size. Local government notices set the age range at 15 to 39, household income at or below 50 percent of the standard median income, and require the applicant to have at least 100,000 won a month in earned or business income. The term is three years, and completing financial education plus submitting a plan for using the funds are part of the maturity requirements.

The thing to watch here is timing. This account is not open on a rolling basis; it recruits during a fixed window each year. The 2026 intake ran from early to mid May and has already closed. Even if you clearly qualify, there is no door into it this year and the next announcement is the thing to wait for. Because it involves an income and asset assessment, eligibility is judged as of the application date. If it interests you, check Bokjiro and your local district office notices in advance.

Low-income households with earned income should also look at Korea's earned income tax credit. It serves a different purpose than asset building, but the income tests rhyme closely enough that many people qualify for both.

10. How comparable programs look elsewhere

The idea that an employer helps a worker build a lump sum is not uniquely Korean. What differs by country is what the scheme is attached to, and that attachment decides what happens when someone changes jobs.

  • United States — employers commonly match a percentage of a worker's retirement plan contributions, and the matched amount often becomes fully the worker's only after a vesting period. That service requirement rhymes closely with the Korean deduction's logic
  • Japan — payroll-deduction workplace savings schemes have long been established, split by purpose such as housing acquisition or retirement, and some employers add an incentive on top
  • United Kingdom — workplace pension auto-enrolment makes the employer contribution structural, while youth asset building runs separately through individual accounts for home purchase and retirement to which the government adds a bonus
  • China — enterprise annuities operate as an employer option, while tax-favoured individual pension accounts have been built out on the personal side

The comparison brings out what was distinctive about the Korean scheme. It targeted a lump sum over two years, not a retirement horizon, and its purpose leaned toward SME recruitment and retention more than asset accumulation. That is why eligibility was narrowed by sector and company size, and why the whole program could be closed once policy priorities shifted. Matching that sits inside a pension system does not disappear so easily.

11. What this article could not confirm

Items that could not be pinned down on official pages are collected here. None of them is asserted as a figure in the body, and applying any of them to your own contract requires confirmation from the operating agency.

  • The full per-installment refund table — the ranges of 50 to 100 percent for employer causes and 0 to 50 percent for worker causes are confirmed in agency guidance, but the individual ratio in each installment band is split across separate tables by enrollment year and plan type, so it is not consolidated here
  • How the re-entry route operates after the sunset — one re-entry on employer fault with re-employment within six months of losing insured status is well documented from the operating period, but its current handling could not be confirmed
  • The reduction rate applying to this program's payout — Article 29-6 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act as the legal basis, and the per-product rates (50%, 30% for mid-sized companies, 90% for young workers), are confirmed; which value attaches to this particular payout is not
  • Tax treatment of the government support portion — that the employer share counts as employment income is confirmed; how the treasury-funded portion is withheld is not
  • Any time limit on claiming the maturity payout — no explicit deadline after maturity was found in the guidance. Not delaying is the safe course

Conclusion: five things to check now

For anyone whose contract is still live, this order works best.

  1. Start with your installment number. The figure on the portal's contract lookup screen is where every calculation begins. Estimating in months gets it wrong
  2. Check whether the employer accrual is behind. Your own payments can be perfectly current and the maturity claim still will not open if the company skipped its support filings
  3. If you are weighing a resignation, put the remaining months next to the 8 million won. Worker-attributed termination sends the employer share back to the company and leaves at most half the government support
  4. If there is employer fault, build the record first. The same departure, recorded under a different cause, changes the refund. And the filing deadline is 10 days
  5. Once the payout lands, remember the end of the following month. The reduction application has to reach your company for the earned income tax on the employer share to be cut

For those who missed the window, the test is single. If you want a scheme where the employer contributes, you need the employer's agreement; if you want one you can decide alone, you are looking at income tests and recruitment calendars. The first path is Naeil Chaeum Gongje and the SME Employee Preferential Savings Deduction; the second is the Youth Tomorrow Savings Account. The second has already closed its intake for this year, so the only available move is to line up eligibility in advance.

Every figure here is current as of August 2026, and requirements and crediting rates change with amendments and quarterly publication. Reopen the operating agency's guidance immediately before acting.
