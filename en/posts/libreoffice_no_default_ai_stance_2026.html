This article rests on a statement The Document Foundation posted on its own blog in September 2026, and on the summaries of it by GamingOnLinux and GeekNews. The wording of the conditions follows the source, and no evaluation of other companies' products or other organizations is included. It follows a single axis: how a reader can use the fact that the conditions a default-bundled AI must meet were fixed in a document for their own tool selection.

What the statement says — a conditional hold

It began with a user's question. The source opens by quoting the question of whether no AI is now a feature. The skeleton of the answer is this: an office suite used by tens of millions of people, in schools, hospitals, public bodies, law firms and thousands of other organizations, will not add a feature to its default configuration until that feature can be delivered under the conditions the project defined according to its own principles. The key word in that sentence is default. It is not a rejection of AI itself but a position that puts conditions on what is installed switched on by default.

The weight of default is large in organizational deployment. A default reaches thousands of computers without any separate decision, so the moment something enters the default, the feature stops being a choice and becomes a premise.

The purpose here is not to argue whether the position is right. The six conditions the source presents are not specific to office products; they take a form that can be carried over as questions an organization can ask before adopting any AI feature that handles documents. The more concrete the conditions, the easier it is to compare tool candidates by the same yardstick.

  • Target. LibreOffice's default configuration. An AI installed as an extension is not the target of these conditions.
  • Attitude. A hold, not opposition. The source says that if an approach meeting the conditions emerges, it will be evaluated very carefully.
  • Status. The source states that among the integrations available now, none meets all six conditions.
  • Timing. The GamingOnLinux summary presents the statement as dated September 3, 2026, after the LibreOffice 26.8 release (August 26, 2026).

What deserves notice in the table is that the six conditions are built to be understood as a whole. Conditions 1 to 3 concern the movement of data, 4 and 5 concern dependence, and 6 concerns the very existence of choice. If even one is missing, the condition for default bundling as the source describes it is not filled, however well the others are met.

Another thing that can be read from the wording is that the conditions are paired as does not and can. Conditions 2 and 3 take the form of prohibition, and 1 and 6 take the form of choice. A prohibition can be confirmed by observation for violations, and a choice can be confirmed by a test that actually exercises the choice. Because the verification methods split this way, it is better to prepare observation tests and operation tests separately when testing tool candidates.

Conditions 1 to 3 — where it runs and what leaves

The first three conditions ask where the data goes. The reason the source explains this part at length is that office documents are not ordinary text. The source writes that in many deployments the documents are material the law protects: medical records, case files, tender documents and pupil data. On that footing comes the sentence that the content of a document belongs to the user and that transmission must be the result of the user's decision.

Condition 1 lets the user choose where execution happens. The options are their own computer, infrastructure they control, or an independent service they pick. This choice is set against a configuration whose default quietly goes to one provider. The source's wording is that such a default is not a choice. The view is that because most users do not change defaults, the decision has in effect been made in advance.

Condition 2 attaches the power to decide about transmission to that choice. Even if the location can be chosen, the meaning shrinks if documents go out automatically. The source sentence quoted in the GamingOnLinux summary is to the effect that the only assurance that survives an audit is that the document does not leave the machine. It can be read as saying that policy documents and contracts can be assurances, but a design that structurally keeps documents from leaving is the simplest to explain in an audit.

Condition 3 is telemetry. The source writes that LibreOffice collects no usage data now and will not start when AI features arrive. The reason this condition stands separately is that it deals with a different layer from 2. Condition 2 is the movement of document content, and condition 3 is the collection of records about how it is used, for instance which features were used and how much. Even if documents do not leave, if usage records do, the organization lets the outline of its work be known outside.

Laid side by side, the three conditions also have a logic of order: fix the location (1), control what leaves that location (2), and do not even keep a record of what leaves (3). If the earlier condition collapses, the later ones lose meaning.

  • The question for 1. Where does inference run when first switched on, and can the user change that location?
  • The question for 2. Which part of the document goes out, and when? Is there a user confirmation step before it goes?
  • The question for 3. Are there records collected about use of the AI feature? If so, can they be turned off?
  • What the three questions share. The answer can be checked from actual behavior, not from a feature description document.

Conditions 4 to 6 — dependence, format, optionality

The remaining three conditions deal with what one gets tied to. Condition 4 is vendor independence. The source sentence is that an integration that works only with one company's API is a lock-in mechanism, however the release notes present it. What deserves attention here is the manner of expression. The source does not point at a particular company but at a structure. The criterion for judging dependence is not which company it is but whether it still works once that one is removed. This criterion can be used as it stands in tool selection.

Condition 5 is ODF preservation. Generated content must be ODF with structure, styles and semantics intact, and the source writes that producing it in a proprietary format is another form of lock-in. In a document tool, when AI produces a result, structures such as tables, styles and tables of contents can be flattened into a lump of text. What the condition demands is that the result come in carrying the structure of ODF as it is.

Condition 6 is full optionality. It must be installable and removable, and absent from the interface for anyone who does not want it. The source includes here administrators whose policy forbids its use. In an organizational deployment, can be turned off and cannot be seen are different. If a visible button remains, questions from staff and accidental use follow.

  • The question for 4. If one company's service is removed from this feature, what remains?
  • The question for 5. When the AI-generated result is opened in another tool, is the structure kept?
  • The question for 6. Can organizational policy leave the feature out at the installation stage or remove it from the screen?
  • What the three questions share. They ask not whether the feature is good but whether it can be removed or replaced later.

A discussion of office tools from another angle is The arrival of Bento - a single-file software experiment where one file becomes an office app. That article treats document preservation and dependence differently, so read together with condition 5 of this statement it makes clearer how much weight format preservation carries in choosing a tool. The problem of document access that follows a move to subscription is covered in MS Office 2019 and 2021 Mac perpetual licenses going read-only.

Why it is not opposition to AI — the tone of the source

This statement is often summarized as a rejection of AI, but the tone of the source is different. The source does not accept the frame that no AI is a feature as it stands. Instead it spends its sentences defining the conditions needed to put something into the default configuration. And at the end it writes that this standard is not a permanent rejection: if an approach meeting the conditions appears, it will be evaluated very carefully.

The source also mentions the structure of the foundation. There is a sentence that for a company that sells subscriptions an AI assistant justifies a price increase. It goes on to explain that The Document Foundation, as a nonprofit, has no subscription tiers, no upsells and no incentive to monetize data. This comparison contains no company's name. It is a statement that an organization with a different structure can make a different decision, and this article adds no interpretation beyond that.

This tone is also useful for tool selection. Dividing candidates into use and do not use produces an argument, but dividing them into use if these conditions are met produces a list to demand from suppliers. When the conditions are concrete, a supplier may answer by moving to meet them or by saying it cannot, and either way the organization gains grounds for its judgment.

  • Handling of the frame. It takes the question that no AI is also a feature, but turns the answer away from a debate on features toward the definition of conditions.
  • Nature of the period. The hold is not a decision with a deadline but a state re-evaluated depending on whether the conditions are met.
  • Mention of the foundation structure. A general statement that a different sales model means different incentives, and it does not assert the intentions of others.
  • What the reader takes away. A way of changing the question from whether to do it to under what conditions to do it.

The path when users want it — local extensions

That AI is not put into the default configuration does not mean AI cannot be used. According to the source, users who want AI features can install third-party extensions, and those extensions connect to locally running models such as Ollama or LM Studio. The source explains that in this case the document never leaves the computer. This structure is one in which conditions 1 and 2 among the six above are met by the user's own choice.

The table below divides the ways of connecting AI into broad kinds and sets out the conditions to look at first for each. Its content is not the source's description but reader-oriented interpretation derived from the condition wording, and the actual result of each approach depends on the product and its settings.

The last row of the table is the type the source most explicitly took issue with. Here too the focus of the problem is not a particular company but the structure in which the default is fixed to one. Even a service from the same company could be evaluated differently from the viewpoint of conditions 1 and 4 if the user selects and connects it and can change it at any time.

The local model path also carries practical conditions this article does not cover: the hardware to run the model, the quality of the model and the maintenance of the extension. The source does not discuss the required specification or quality here, and an organization examining this path should test performance and a maintenance plan separately from the six conditions above.

Using it as an organization tool-selection checklist

The six conditions become a question sheet as they are when an organization examines a new tool. The core of the sheet is to demand evidence, not claims. A supplier answering that it supports something is different from something confirmed by a settings screen, documentation or test results. The table below sets out, per condition, the evidence to demand and the attitude to take when it is not met.

QuestionEvidence to demandConditionIf not met
Can the user choose where inference runs?Settings screen, administrator documentation, a demonstration per location1Limit the scope of adoption or look for an alternative where the location is not fixed
Does the document stay put without user approval?A list of communication destinations, a confirmation screen before sending, processing terms in the contract2Set first a rule against using it on sensitive documents
Does it collect usage data?Privacy documents, a setting to turn collection off, network observation results3If collection cannot be turned off, consider deferring adoption
Does it work with one company removed?A demonstration of swapping the backend, documentation of open interfaces4Reflect the migration cost and dependence risk in the contract
Does the result keep structure in an open format?Comparison results from opening the saved file in another tool5Set a procedure in which people re-check the structure of important documents
Can it be deployed with the feature removed?Installation options, administrative policy items, removal test results6Do not deploy to departments where policy forbids it
  • Ask the questions in the same order. Asking every candidate the same six questions produces a comparison table by itself.
  • Build the evidence by testing. What the documentation says can differ from behavior, so confirm directly with a small sample.
  • Write down the pass and fail criteria in advance. If criteria are set after seeing the results, they move to fit the conclusion.
  • Judge by scope. Judge ordinary documents and sensitive documents, and internal deployment and personal use, separately.
  • Set a time for re-evaluation. As the source itself says it will evaluate again if an approach meeting the conditions appears, tool selection does not end at one time either.

What is most often omitted from this checklist is the if not met column. If only the pass criteria are set and the response to failure is not, exceptions multiply after the results come out and the criteria lose force. Writing the response to failure at the start is the procedure that makes the conditions actually work.

What it means to fix conditions in a document

The value of the six conditions in practice comes not only from their content but from the fact that they are published in a document. Once the conditions are public, outside users and administrators can look at the product by the same standard, and when a feature arrives later they can check whether it met the conditions. As the source wording has it, however an integration is introduced, the judgment is made on structure.

There is also something to notice in the form of the conditions. All six deal not with a good AI but with whether the user can control it. There is no condition about performance, accuracy or the kind of model. This can be read as a judgment that, in software that handles documents, the way a feature is placed must be settled before its quality. Whether that is an explicit explanation in the source is not confirmed, and it is this article's interpretation.

The limits are also clear. The conditions are a declaration of principle, and the procedure for judging whether they are met is not given in the source. Who acknowledges satisfaction by what test, and how partial satisfaction is handled, remain tasks for the future. When readers bring these conditions to their own organization, they must therefore set the judging procedure themselves. The tables in this article are only a starting point and not a finished audit standard.

Finally, these six conditions came from a specific context, the default configuration of an office product. The size of the risk differs between an individual experimenting on their own computer and an organizational deployment in which thousands of people receive the same settings. It is natural to read the strictness of the conditions as built for the latter.

What could not be verified

  • A direct reading of the whole blog original. Only the parts on which the web tool summary and the GamingOnLinux summary agreed were quoted as condition wording. The exact English wording sentence by sentence needs further checking against the original.
  • The expression an open interface supporting multiple backends in condition 4. Because it may have been reconstructed in a summary, it was not quoted as a source sentence, and only the source sentence that working only with one company API is dependence was included.
  • The posting date of the statement. It follows the September 3, 2026 given in the GamingOnLinux summary, and the date shown on the blog page could not be checked directly.
  • Specific names and behavior of third-party extensions. The source mentions connecting to local models such as Ollama and LM Studio, but the communication structure of individual extensions could not be confirmed.
  • The basis for no integration currently meeting all conditions. It is the source statement, and which integrations were examined by what standard is not in the source.
  • Other reporting such as the download volume of LibreOffice 26.8. Only headlines were visible and the bodies were not opened, so they are not cited.
