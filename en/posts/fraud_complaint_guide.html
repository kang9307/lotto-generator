Complete Guide to Filing a Fraud Complaint in Korea — Elements, Evidence, Procedure

Based on Korean Criminal Act Article 347 — From Legal Elements to Evidence, Complaint Drafting, and Damage Recovery (As of June 2026)

2026-06-30 Law & Legal
Legal Disclaimer: This article provides general information based on Korean law as of June 2026 and does not constitute legal advice for any specific case. Laws vary by country and change over time. For any actual legal matter, please consult a qualified attorney or legal professional.

Introduction: Is Filing a Criminal Complaint the Right First Step?

When someone discovers they have been defrauded, the instinctive reaction is often "I'll file a police report." But a criminal complaint is not a mechanism for directly recovering lost money — its purpose is to have the perpetrator criminally punished. Recovering your money requires a separate civil process. Moreover, not every financial dispute constitutes criminal fraud. "Borrowing money and failing to repay" and "taking money with no intention of repaying from the start" are fundamentally different under Korean law.

This guide, based on Korean criminal law and case precedents as of June 2026, covers the legal elements of fraud, evidence collection, how to draft and file a criminal complaint, the investigation and prosecution process, damage recovery options, voice phishing special provisions, and the statute of limitations. It does not replace professional legal advice.

1. Legal Basis — Korean Criminal Act Article 347

Article 347(1) of the Korean Criminal Act states: "A person who obtains property or gains financial benefit by deceiving another shall be punished by imprisonment for not more than ten years or a fine not exceeding twenty million won." Following the December 2025 amendment to the Criminal Act, the maximum sentence was raised to 20 years imprisonment, and this enhanced penalty applies to offenses committed after the amendment.

For fraud resulting in financial gain of KRW 500 million or more, the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes applies, carrying a minimum of three years to life imprisonment — a substantially more severe range.

2. The Five Elements of Fraud

Fraud in Korea requires five elements, each causally connected to the next.

2.1 Deception

Deception is broadly defined by Korean courts as "any active or passive act that violates the duty of good faith and honesty in property transactions and causes the other party to form a false belief." This includes not only outright lies but also deliberate concealment of material facts. Whether an exaggerated sales pitch crosses into criminal deception depends on how far it departs from what is socially tolerated.

2.2 Mistaken Belief (착오)

The victim must have formed a false belief as a result of the deception. If the victim knew the truth at the time they handed over property, the element of mistaken belief is not satisfied and fraud may not be established. The false belief must be causally linked to the deception — meaning the victim would not have acted as they did had they known the truth.

2.3 Disposition Act

The victim, acting on the mistaken belief, must voluntarily transfer property or confer a financial benefit. The voluntariness distinguishes fraud from theft or robbery, where property is taken without the victim's will.

2.4 Acquisition of Property or Financial Gain

The offender must actually acquire property or financial gain. Korean courts have held that even if the victim suffers no net economic loss (for example, because the offender provides something of equivalent value), fraud is still established if deceptive means were used to obtain the property. The offender's gain is the key element, not the victim's loss.

2.5 Fraudulent Intent (편취 고의)

The offender must have had the intent to deceive at the time of the transaction. This is the dividing line between civil and criminal liability. Korean courts consistently hold that if the offender had both the will and capacity to perform at the time of the agreement, subsequent non-performance is a civil breach of contract, not criminal fraud. Fraudulent intent is inferred from circumstantial evidence: the offender's financial state at the time, simultaneous fraud schemes against multiple victims, immediate diversion of funds, and so on.

3. Fraud vs. Civil Breach of Contract

The most common area of confusion. The table below summarizes the key distinctions.

AspectCivil Breach of ContractCriminal Fraud
Core testHad will and ability to perform at time of agreementHad no will or ability to perform from the outset
Subsequent non-performanceCivil matter onlyCriminal if intent existed at outset
ResolutionCivil lawsuit (damages, payment order)Criminal complaint + civil track in parallel
OutcomeDamages judgmentPrison / fine + compensation order
Standard of proofPreponderance of evidenceBeyond reasonable doubt

Typical examples that are usually civil matters: lending money that is not repaid, investment losses due to business failure. Examples that may constitute criminal fraud: simultaneous pyramid schemes against multiple victims, receiving funds while already bankrupt and concealing this fact, selling the same asset to multiple buyers.

4. Evidence Collection — What to Gather and How

The first 48 hours after discovering fraud are critical. Act before the suspect can destroy evidence or move funds.

4.1 Banking Records

Obtain official bank transfer certificates (금융거래확인서) from your financial institution rather than relying solely on app screenshots. Document the suspect's account number, transfer dates, amounts, and any transaction notes.

4.2 Digital Communications

Back up KakaoTalk, text messages, and emails both as screenshots and in their native export format. Store copies in cloud storage and on a physical device. Highlight messages containing promises of returns, repayment commitments, or false representations about the business or investment.

4.3 Written Agreements

Preserve original loan agreements, contracts, and IOUs. Any false information in a written contract is itself evidence of deception.

4.4 Recordings

Recording a phone call you are personally party to is lawful in Korea. Recording conversations you are not part of without consent is illegal under the Protection of Communications Secrets Act. Keep recording files in their original format and note the date and context.

4.5 Evidence of the Suspect's Financial Condition

Evidence that the suspect was already insolvent at the time of the transaction, or was running simultaneous schemes against other victims, is crucial for establishing fraudulent intent. Reports from other victims, court records, and news articles can all be useful supporting material.

5. Drafting the Criminal Complaint (고소장)

A criminal complaint (고소장) is a formal written document submitted to investigating authorities requesting criminal prosecution. The Supreme Prosecutors' Office website provides a sample complaint form for fraud cases. Key elements include:

5.1 Identifying Information

Include the complainant's full name, ID number, address, and contact information. Include the suspect's identifying information to the extent known. Even if the suspect cannot be fully identified, file with whatever information is available.

5.2 Statement of Purpose

State clearly that you are filing a complaint against the suspect for violation of Criminal Act Article 347 (fraud).

5.3 Statement of Facts

Describe the facts in chronological order using the five Ws and one H (who, when, where, what, how, why). Describe specifically: the deceptive acts, the dates and amounts of property transferred, the mistaken belief caused, and the circumstantial evidence of fraudulent intent. Use objective, factual language rather than emotional descriptions.

5.4 Evidence List

Attach a list of all supporting evidence. Label each exhibit clearly and briefly describe its contents so investigators can orient themselves quickly.

5.5 Request for Prosecution

State that you request prosecution of the suspect. While fraud is not a crime requiring the victim's sustained complaint for prosecution (반의사불벌죄 아님), stating your intent clearly signals your commitment to the case.

6. Filing the Complaint — Police, Prosecutors, and Online

6.1 Local Police Station

The most common approach is submitting the complaint to the civil affairs section of the police station with jurisdiction over the place where the offense occurred or where the suspect resides. Bring originals and copies of all evidence.

6.2 Directly to the Prosecutor's Office

Complaints may also be filed directly at the relevant prosecutor's office. This may be more effective for large, complex cases.

6.3 Online

The National Police Agency Cyber Crime Reporting System (ECRM, ecrm.police.go.kr) accepts online reports, particularly useful for internet fraud, shopping mall scams, and SNS-based fraud.

7. Investigation, Prosecution, and Trial

7.1 Investigation

After the complaint is accepted, a detective is assigned, the complainant is interviewed, the suspect is called in for questioning, and evidence is analyzed. The process can take several months to over a year depending on the complexity of the case.

7.2 Referral and Prosecution Decision

Upon completing the investigation, police forward the case to the prosecutor's office (송치). The prosecutor then decides whether to indict (기소), decline prosecution (불기소 — no grounds, prosecutorial discretion), or suspend prosecution. If the complaint is declined, the complainant may appeal to the High Prosecutor's Office (항고) and subsequently petition the court to compel indictment (재정신청).

7.3 Trial and Sentencing

If indicted, a criminal trial proceeds. Appeals may be filed against first-instance judgments. Sentences for fraud range from fines to imprisonment, with the possibility of suspended sentences. The severity depends on the scale of the fraud, the number of victims, and the defendant's prior record.

8. Damage Recovery

8.1 Compensation Order (배상명령)

During the criminal trial, victims can apply for a compensation order under the Act on Special Cases Concerning the Promotion of Legal Proceedings. If granted, the order is included in the criminal judgment and carries the same enforceability as a civil judgment, without the need for a separate civil lawsuit.

8.2 Civil Lawsuit

The most reliable route to monetary recovery is a separate civil action for damages (손해배상) or unjust enrichment (부당이득 반환). A final criminal conviction greatly strengthens the civil case. Before filing, consider applying for a prejudgment attachment (가압류) to preserve the defendant's assets.

8.3 Small Claims and Payment Orders

For smaller amounts (under KRW 30 million), the small claims court procedure (소액사건심판) provides a simplified track. A payment order (지급명령) becomes as enforceable as a judgment if the debtor does not object within two weeks.

9. Voice Phishing — Special Provisions

Telecommunications-based financial fraud (voice phishing) is governed by the Special Act on Prevention of Telecommunications-Based Financial Fraud and Recovery of Damage, which provides faster remedies than the ordinary criminal process.

9.1 Immediate Reporting

Report to 112 (police) or the receiving bank's call center immediately to request a payment suspension on the destination account. Acting before funds are withdrawn is the single most important step.

9.2 Victim Refund Procedure

Once a payment suspension is in place, the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) initiates a creditor-extinction process. If the account holder does not object within two months, the creditor rights are extinguished and the FSS determines and orders a refund amount. The financial institution then refunds the victim. This procedure is of limited use if funds have already been withdrawn.

9.3 Penalties for Voice Phishing

Voice phishing carries heavier penalties than ordinary fraud. Providing a "burner" bank account (대포통장), even unknowingly, can result in criminal liability, so victims should be cautious.

10. Statute of Limitations

Following the December 2025 Criminal Act amendment, the statute of limitations for fraud is 10 years (Code of Criminal Procedure Article 249(1)(3)). Time begins to run from the date the offense was completed and stops when the suspect flees abroad or when formal investigation commences against an identified suspect. For large-scale fraud under the Specific Economic Crimes Act, different rules may apply.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: The person promised to pay me back but didn't. Is that automatically criminal fraud?

Not necessarily. If they had the will and ability to repay at the time of the agreement, failure to repay later is a civil breach of contract. Criminal fraud requires proving there was no intention or ability to perform from the very start.

Q: Should I accept a settlement offer if the suspect asks me to withdraw the complaint?

This is a personal decision, but note that fraud is not a crime that requires the victim's continued complaint for prosecution — the prosecutor can still indict independently. Before signing any settlement, have a lawyer review whether it extinguishes your civil claims as well.

Q: Can I file a complaint against a suspect who is overseas?

Yes, but practical difficulties arise. International law enforcement cooperation through mutual legal assistance treaties (MLATs) is possible but slow. Interpol notices are another option. The effectiveness depends heavily on which country the suspect is in.

Conclusion

Responding to fraud effectively requires running both the criminal and civil tracks in parallel. Criminal prosecution punishes the wrongdoer; civil proceedings recover your money. The two are not mutually exclusive and should proceed simultaneously. Securing evidence immediately upon discovering the fraud — before it can be deleted or funds moved — is the most important first action. Professional legal counsel at the earliest stage significantly increases the chance of a successful outcome.
