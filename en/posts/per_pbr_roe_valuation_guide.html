Reading PER, PBR and ROE - Where Valuation Ratios Lie

Ratios do not give answers. They generate questions - starting from the fact that PBR equals PER times ROE

2026-07-30 Finance & Economy

Introduction: the habit of hunting low PER stocks was badly taught

Sorting a stock screen by PER and reading from the bottom is a habit almost every retail investor passes through. Low is cheap and high is expensive is a simple enough idea to stick. Yet the habit contains a specific error: it uses a ratio as a conclusion. A low PER states only that the market is attaching a low price to that company earnings, and the ratio never tells you why.

The angle of this article is different. PER, PBR and ROE are not tools that supply answers. They are tools that generate questions. When a low PER security appears, the evidence that the ratio did its job is not a buy button but the question why is it low. Was the profit a one-off? Is this a sector that structurally receives low multiples? Or does the market expect earnings to fall shortly? The value of a ratio lies in narrowing down what must be verified.

There is also a problem with learning the three ratios separately. They are mathematically locked together. PBR is PER multiplied by ROE - an identity that falls out of the definitions alone. Without that relationship, it is impossible to see why the sentence PBR is below 1 so it is undervalued is dangerous. A low PBR at a company with low ROE may be a justified discount rather than an undervaluation. That identity is the spine of this article.

Scope note for readers outside Korea. This article deals only with reading the financial ratios of individual companies listed in the Korean market. The figures come from financial statements prepared under Korean IFRS (K-IFRS), and the verification routes are the Korean electronic disclosure system and Korea Exchange statistics. Trading rules such as session hours and settlement are covered separately in the Korean stock market trading guide, and market segmentation with listing and delisting requirements in the difference between KOSPI, KOSDAQ and KONEX. Readers opening a set of financial statements for the first time should start with reading financial statements.

Definitions and institutional descriptions here were verified in July 2026 against material from the Korea Council for Investor Education, the Financial Services Commission, the Financial Supervisory Service and Korea Exchange. One point in advance: this article contains no actual PER or PBR figure for any individual security. Every worked example uses a fictional company and fictional numbers, and figures that move constantly, such as market average multiples, are not reproduced - only where to look them up.

1. Definitions - the denominator decides the character of the ratio

The discussion only holds if the definitions are precise. Investor education material from the Korea Council for Investor Education describes PER as short for price earnings ratio, the share price divided by earnings per share. The same material offers a worked example in which a company earning 30,000 won per share with a price of 150,000 won has a PER of 5.0. PBR is the price per share divided by net asset value per share, where net assets are assets less liabilities. ROE indicates how much profit the invested shareholder capital produced; equity of 10 billion won generating 1 billion won of profit gives 10 percent.

One structural difference is easy to miss. The PER denominator is a flow accumulated over a period, while the PBR denominator is a stock standing at a point in time. Profit is a stream earned across a year; net assets are a balance remaining at the reporting date. Placing two ratios with denominators of different nature side by side and reading it as both are low so it is definitely cheap can mean counting the same information twice, or ignoring information that offsets.

RatioFormulaNumeratorNature of denominatorQuestion the ratio poses
PER (price earnings ratio)Price divided by earnings per share (EPS)Market price of one shareFlow - profit over a periodHow many times does the market pay for one unit of this company profit
PBR (price to book ratio)Price divided by book value per share (BPS)Market price of one shareStock - net asset balance at the reporting dateHow many times book net assets does the market pay
ROE (return on equity)Net income divided by shareholder equityProfit earned in the periodStock - capital left in by shareholdersHow well does shareholder capital manufacture profit
EPS (earnings per share)Net income divided by share countProfit earned in the periodShare countHow much profit is allocated to one share
BPS (book value per share)Net assets divided by share countAssets less liabilitiesShare countHow much book value is allocated to one share

The point to take from the table is that PER and PBR share the same numerator. Both use the price. So the entire difference between them comes from the denominator. When the price rises both rise, and when it falls both fall. If the two ratios moved in opposite directions, the cause was not the price but a change in a denominator, and finding the reason for that change is the analysis itself.

Further, the ROE denominator is the same net assets used in PBR, and the ROE numerator is the same profit used in the PER denominator. The three ratios are different combinations of the same two financial figures. Which is why the identity in the next section holds.

2. PBR = PER times ROE - the three cannot be read separately

The relationship is not an estimate or a rule of thumb but an identity derived straight from the definitions. Substituting shows it. ROE is net income over shareholder equity, and because dividing numerator and denominator by the share count leaves the ratio unchanged, it equals earnings per share divided by book value per share.

[Definitions]
PER = Price / Earnings per share (EPS)
PBR = Price / Book value per share (BPS)
ROE = Net income / Equity = EPS / BPS

[Multiplying two of them]
PER x ROE = (Price / EPS) x (EPS / BPS)
          = Price / BPS      (EPS cancels)
          = PBR

[Therefore]
PBR = PER x ROE

What this identity means in practice is decisive. There are only two routes to a low PBR: a low PER or a low ROE. And the two routes tell an investor opposite things. If PBR is low because the price is depressed relative to earnings, that may be a question about market pricing. But if PBR is low because shareholder capital fails to manufacture profit, that is a question about the company, and the low PBR is the appropriate result.

Official documents treat the three as one bundle. The press release for the corporate value enhancement plan guideline, published on 2 May 2024 by the Financial Services Commission together with Korea Exchange and the Korea Capital Market Institute, classifies the key indicators a company may select: market valuation covering PBR and PER, capital efficiency covering ROE, ROIC, COE and WACC, shareholder return covering dividends, treasury share cancellation and TSR, and growth covering revenue, profit and asset growth rates. The same material directs companies to analyse the selected indicators as a time series and compare them against the industry average or peer companies in order to establish the current position accurately. In other words, the direction of official guidance is not to treat any single ratio as an absolute standard.

  • Low PBR with low ROE - under the identity, PER need not be especially low. The first reading of this combination is a reflection of weak profitability.
  • Low PBR with high ROE - the identity requires a very low PER to hold. Because the combination is rare, the order of enquiry is the durability of that ROE and whether assets are overstated.
  • High PBR with high ROE - the identity holds naturally. What needs checking is what produced the high ROE and whether that level can be sustained.
  • High PBR with low ROE - the identity implies a very high PER. Most likely the denominator shrank because profit has nearly disappeared.
  • Knowing two of the three fixes the third automatically. Counting all three as independent evidence is therefore counting the same information several times.

To summarise: the sentence PBR is below 1 so it is undervalued reads only half of the identity. Without the other half, ROE, there is no basis for judging whether the discount the market applied is unwarranted or appropriate.

3. Trailing and forward PER - the same name, different numbers

Seeing different PER values for the same security on different screens is common. The calculation is not wrong; the period of earnings used in the denominator differs. Investor education material from the Korea Council for Investor Education distinguishes the branches clearly. One divides the current price by last year profit, with a variant using the sum of the most recent four quarters of net income rather than the prior fiscal year. The other divides the current price by the net income the company is expected to generate this year, and that estimate is calculated from the consensus produced by brokerage analysts. The same material states the view that an estimated PER reflecting expected future results matters more than one built on past results.

BasisEarnings in the denominatorSource of the numberUpdate frequencyTrap to watch

Trailing - last four quartersSum of the four most recent quartersQuarterly, half-year and annual reportsEvery quarterA single one-off item inside the window shifts the value
Forward - current year estimateExpected net income this yearAnalyst consensusWhenever estimates changeWith few contributors, one house view dominates
Forward - next year estimateExpected net income next yearAnalyst consensusWhenever estimates changeThe window most easily made to look optimistic
No basis labelUnknownUnknownUnknownA number that must not be used for comparison

A fictional case makes the stakes obvious. Take fictional Company A. It is not a real company and all numbers are invented. Suppose the price is 50,000 won per share, last year confirmed EPS was 5,000 won, and this year estimated EPS is 2,500 won. Trailing PER is 10 times and forward PER is 20 times. Same price, same company, yet one screen says cheap and another says expensive. Concluding from the trailing 10 times without asking why earnings are expected to halve means the ratio actively clouded the judgement.

Practical ways to establish the basis follow. If none can be confirmed, the PER on that screen has an unknown basis and should not be used as comparative evidence.

  • Look for the fiscal year label beside the number. A year or quarter attached to it means the trailing family.
  • Check for a marker indicating an estimate. Words such as estimate, outlook or consensus, or an abbreviation denoting a forecast.
  • Check the number of contributing institutions. Where the screen shows it, a small count means a wide confidence band.
  • Check the fiscal year end. Placing a non-December year end company beside December year end peers is not a same-date comparison.
  • Establish whether the basis is consolidated or separate. As covered later, this alone can split the value widely.
  • If it cannot be confirmed, calculate directly. Net income and share count from the periodic report are enough to produce EPS.

Doubting a ratio requires access to the underlying numbers. In the Korean market those originate in two places: the filings companies submit and the statistics the exchange compiles.

The electronic disclosure system (DART) is operated by the Financial Supervisory Service, and its guidance describes the system as a comprehensive corporate disclosure system through which listed companies and others submit filings over the internet and users such as investors can retrieve them over the internet immediately on submission. The menus are recent disclosures, filing search (integrated search, search by company, advanced search) and company overview, with usage enquiries directed to the Financial Supervisory Service line 1332.

5. When ratios lie - the moments a numerator or denominator shifts

This section is the core of the article. A ratio never miscalculates. The lie always begins in the denominator, and in the accounting treatment that produces it. Six routes follow.

5-1. One-off gains and losses shake EPS

Net income does not contain only operating profit. Gains on disposal of property or holdings, litigation or compensation receipts, and conversely the impairment loss recognised when an asset carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount, all enter net income. So PER can move sharply even when the business is identical to last year.

Take fictional Company B. It is not a real company and the numbers are invented. Suppose normal annual net income is 10 billion won, but this period a land sale produced a 40 billion won disposal gain, lifting net income to 50 billion won. With an unchanged price, PER falls to one fifth. The screen displays an ultra low PER stock. Next year the 40 billion won is gone, so PER returns to where it was. The ratio was accurate; the interpretation was wrong.

Impairment runs the other way. The supervisory guideline on accounting for research and development costs at pharmaceutical and bio companies, published on 19 September 2018 by the Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service, sets out that capitalising development expenditure as an intangible asset requires all six recognition conditions to be met, and that where development and research costs are intermingled and cannot be separated, the entire amount is recognised as an expense. It further directs that once development costs are recognised as an asset, the entity must assess future economic benefits and recognise the excess as an impairment. The same material notes that error correction under the guideline could increase operating losses in the financial statements. In other words, when an accounting judgement changes, the profit figures for an unchanged business change with it, and the PER calculated on top changes too.

5-2. PER does not exist for a loss-making company

If net income is negative, PER is negative, and a negative PER cannot be interpreted. Most screens therefore show a blank or not applicable. Investor education material from the Korea Council for Investor Education notes that where a company is in loss rather than profit, calculating a PER value may have little meaning. The error to guard against is reading the blank as no problem on the ratios. A blank is not an absence of information but strong information that there is no profit. In this case the axis of judgement must move from the income statement to the cash flow statement.

5-3. Treasury shares and capital reductions change EPS and BPS

There is a route by which a company moves both ratios without changing its business at all. A share buyback is the clearest. When a company spends cash to buy its own shares, equity falls by that amount and at the same time the share count over which profit is allocated falls. The results are these.

  • BPS falls, so PBR rises. Net assets shrank, so at the same price PBR goes up. On the ratio alone it looks as though the stock got more expensive.
  • EPS rises, so PER falls. The same profit is divided by fewer shares. On the ratio alone it looks as though the stock got cheaper.
  • One event moves the two ratios in opposite directions. This is the most tangible reason the three cannot be read separately.
  • ROE rises. The denominator, equity, shrank. Profitability of the business did not improve; the denominator got smaller.
  • Acquisition and cancellation are different. Treasury shares merely held can return to the market later; cancelled shares cannot. The Financial Services Commission guideline listing treasury share cancellation separately under shareholder return indicators reads as recognition of that difference.
  • Capital reductions require care over direction. Fewer shares lift EPS and BPS, but a reduction without consideration undertaken to clear accumulated deficits is itself a signal of deteriorating financial condition. An event that improves the ratios is not the same as an event that improves the company.

5-4. PBR loses meaning where earnings come from intangibles

The PBR denominator is net assets as carried on the balance sheet. Yet K-IFRS imposes strict conditions on recognising internally generated intangible value as an asset. The supervisory guideline above requires all six conditions for capitalising development expenditure and directs full expensing where research costs cannot be separated. Brand recognition, accumulated data, a user base, organisational capability - these generate the earnings yet leave almost nothing on the balance sheet.

The consequence follows. A company earning through software or brand carries small net assets, so its PBR comes out structurally high. A company holding large plant and property carries large net assets, so its PBR comes out structurally low. That gap does not mean one is undervalued; it is a gap created by accounting recognition rules. Comparing the PBR of those two companies side by side is close to measuring length with two different rulers.

5-5. Changes in estimates and standards rewrite past numbers

Financial statement figures embed estimates: depreciation method and useful life, bad debt estimates, provisions, assessments of recoverable amounts. When an estimate changes, profit changes, and when an error is found, prior statements are sometimes restated. Which means the net income used to compute last year PER can later become a different number. When comparing PER as a time series, it is safer to use the comparative figures presented in the most recent report.

5-6. Consolidated net income contains someone else share

Net income in consolidated statements includes both the share attributable to owners of the parent and the share attributable to non-controlling interests. Using total net income to compute EPS means counting profit that is not yours. Where subsidiary ownership percentages are low the gap becomes substantial. The two shares are presented separately at the foot of the income statement in the periodic report, so this can be verified directly.

6. How to separate out the cause of a high ROE - the three piece split

ROE is easy to learn as higher is better, but omitting the fact that the value also rises when the denominator shrinks is dangerous. A company with small equity produces a high ROE from modest profit. The standard treatment therefore reads ROE in three pieces. This too is an identity.

Net income divided by equity equals the product of net income over revenue, revenue over total assets, and total assets over equity, because revenue and total assets in the middle cancel and only net income over equity remains. These are called net margin, asset turnover and financial leverage.

ComponentFormulaWhat a high value meansWhen to read it as a warning
Net marginNet income divided by revenueEach unit of revenue retains more profit. Pricing power or a cost advantage existsWhen one-off gains thickened it for a single year
Asset turnoverRevenue divided by total assetsAssets are cycled quickly into revenue. Asset efficiency is goodWhen it rose because assets shrank, that is, from cutting investment
Financial leverageTotal assets divided by equityOther people money was used to scale the business against equityAmplified earnings volatility, exposure to rate rises, exhausted borrowing capacity
Product of the threeNet margin x asset turnover x financial leverageEquals ROEWhen it was never checked which piece lifted the value

The point of the decomposition is that the same level of ROE can describe entirely different businesses. Take fictional Company C and Company D. Both show ROE of 15 percent, but C has a thick net margin and low leverage while D has a thin margin and assets several times its equity. When conditions deteriorate the two ROEs respond completely differently. A small fall in revenue at D leaves interest costs unchanged and profit collapses. Leverage pushes ROE up in good times and drags it down harder in bad ones.

  • ROE built on leverage is weakly durable. Borrowing cannot expand indefinitely, and terms change at maturity.
  • Normal leverage levels differ by sector. Financials are built on high leverage by design, so the same ruler used for manufacturers does not apply.
  • Buybacks also raise leverage. Equity shrinks, so total assets over equity grows.
  • ROE only acquires meaning against the cost of capital. Sustaining an ROE below the cost of capital means capital is eroding value.
  • Official guidance does not judge on ROE alone either. The Financial Services Commission guideline press release lists ROIC, COE and WACC alongside ROE as capital efficiency indicators.
  • Read at least three years. A single year ROE can be manufactured by one non-recurring item.

7. Sector baselines - why PBR below 1 means undervalued is wrong

Sector differences are not a matter of taste but differences in the nature of the financial figures that produce the ratios. Investor education material from the Korea Council for Investor Education points to sector characteristics, writing that banking and pharmaceuticals have fundamentally different business characteristics, and advising that a reasonable decision requires checking the sector PER value and comparing within the same sector. Because sector levels themselves change over time, this article does not reproduce values and sets out only why the structures differ.

Sector characterNature of net assets (BPS)Structural reason multiples run low or highWhat to read instead in this sector
FinancialsMost assets are measured as financial instrumentsCarrying amounts sit close to market values, so there is a structural reason for PBR to hover near 1. High leverage is also normalSpread between ROE and cost of capital, capital adequacy, credit loss indicators
Utilities and infrastructureLarge tangible assets dominate net assetsTariffs and investment are set within a regulatory frame, so the ROE ceiling is effectively fixedDividend stability, investment plans and funding structure
Growth IT and platformsThe value source barely appears on the booksSmall net assets push PBR up, and growth expectations already priced in push PER up tooRevenue growth rate and its duration, timing of conversion into profit
Manufacturing and heavy industryPlant and inventory are a large share of net assetsCyclical swings in profit make PER look low at peaks and high at troughsPosition in the cycle, multi-year average profit, utilisation rates
Pharmaceuticals and bioVaries greatly with whether development spend is capitalisedHeavy expensing shrinks net assets, and losses make PER hard to computeCash on hand and burn rate, progress through development stages

It is now possible to state why the PBR of 1 threshold rests on weak ground. A PBR of 1 means the market priced the company at exactly its book net assets. Returning to the identity, PBR is PER times ROE, so if ROE is low enough, a PBR below 1 is the logical outcome. If shareholder capital barely manufactures profit, valuing that capital below its carrying amount is a rational market response. Conversely, for a company earning from intangible sources, net assets fail to capture the business substance, so the mark at 1 carries no meaning.

One addition. PBR is widely explained by analogy to the share a shareholder would receive if the business stopped today and assets were sold, but that reading has limits. Carrying amount is not liquidation value, many assets sell below carrying amount in a hurried disposal, and liabilities and costs rank ahead in a liquidation. PBR is more accurately read as a market multiple on accounting net assets, not an approximation of liquidation value.

8. Comparability - what must match for a comparison to hold

Ratios are not instruments for judging absolute levels but instruments for comparison. And a comparison holds only when the conditions match. The Financial Services Commission guideline press release directing companies to analyse key indicators as a time series and compare against the industry average or peers points the same way. If even one item below fails to match, that comparison is not evidence.

  • Same sector - different business structures give net assets and profit a different nature.
  • Same trailing or forward basis - last year confirmed against this year estimated is not a comparison.
  • Same date - prices change daily. Yesterday value and today value cannot be mixed.
  • Same statement scope - mixing consolidated and separate changes the scale itself.
  • Same ownership basis - standardise on net income attributable to owners of the parent.
  • Same fiscal year end - a different year end does not capture the same point in the cycle.
  • Same accounting framework - Korean listed companies report under K-IFRS, but comparisons with overseas companies must allow for framework differences.
  • Consistent share count basis - check whether it is shares in issue net of treasury shares or total shares issued.
  • Preference share treatment - where preference shares exist, the ordinary share portion must be separated for EPS to be right.
  • Whether one-off items were removed - adjusting only one side produces a tilted comparison.
  • Currency - for companies with heavy overseas revenue, exchange movements are mixed into profit.
  • Putting these conditions as columns of a table and filling them in is the fastest check. If blanks remain, stop comparing.

One misunderstanding to clear up. Compare against the sector average does not mean a rule to buy anything below the average. A gap against the average is an instruction to find the reason for the gap. A company receiving an unusually low multiple within its sector usually has a reason, and whether that reason is the kind that resolves is the object of judgement. Rule-based screening on ratios themselves is discussed separately in quant investing and factors.

9. Consolidated, separate and the accounting framework - two numbers for one company

That a single company has two net income figures is the point beginners trip over most often. Separate financial statements are the statements of the parent entity alone, while consolidated financial statements combine the parent and its subsidiaries into a single economic unit. A parent with consolidated subsidiaries prepares consolidated and separate statements together, while a company without subsidiaries prepares only one set.

How the gap appears in the ratios can be seen through fictional holding company E. It is not a real company and the numbers are invented. Suppose the operating businesses sit in subsidiaries while E itself holds only shareholdings and trademarks. Separate basis net income is filled mostly with dividend income received from subsidiaries and trademark income, while consolidated basis net income captures the whole operating performance of the subsidiaries. Because the two magnitudes differ, separate basis PER and consolidated basis PER become entirely different numbers at the same price. The question is not which is correct but which one the screen in front of you shows, and whether the comparison set uses the same one.

  • Consolidated is the default for business substance. Group-wide revenue and profit live there.
  • Read separate too when looking at dividend capacity. Dividends are paid out of the distributable profit of the parent. Dividend ratios themselves are covered in the dividend investing guide.
  • Strip non-controlling interests out of consolidated net income. EPS must contain only the share attributable to owners of the parent.
  • Equity method is not consolidation. Holdings without control are reflected by the equity method rather than consolidated, so they are not added into revenue.
  • Both statements appear as distinct items in the periodic report. They can be checked side by side in the financial section.
  • The text of the standards is the final authority. The preparation principles for consolidated and separate statements must be verified in the relevant K-IFRS standard, and accounting standards can be amended.

A note on institutional context. Part of why PBR became a policy topic in Korea recently is the debate on enhancing the value of listed companies. On 2 May 2024 the Financial Services Commission, together with Korea Exchange and the Korea Capital Market Institute, released the corporate value enhancement plan guideline, classifying the key indicators a company may select into market valuation, capital efficiency, shareholder return and growth, placing PBR and PER under market valuation and ROE under capital efficiency. From the perspective of this article, what matters is not whether the policy succeeded but the fact that even an official document declined to set any single ratio as a target and instead required time series and peer comparison together.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Does a low PER mean a cheap stock?

A low PER is a starting point for enquiry, not a conclusion. There are two routes to a lower PER - a fallen price or risen earnings per share - and in the latter case the meaning changes entirely depending on whether that profit repeats or was a one-off. Investor education material from the Korea Council for Investor Education also advises comparison within the same sector because sector characteristics differ fundamentally.

Q. Is a PBR below 1 automatically undervalued?

No. PBR is PER times ROE, so a low ROE makes a PBR below 1 the logical outcome. If shareholder capital barely manufactures profit, a valuation below carrying amount is the rational response. And because the nature of net assets varies by sector, the same 1 means different things.

Q. How do I tell whether a broker screen shows trailing or forward PER?

Look for the fiscal year label beside the number, a marker indicating an estimate, the word consensus, and the count of contributing institutions. If none appear, the basis is unknown and the figure should not be used for comparison. In that case it is safer to calculate directly from net income and share count in the periodic report.

Q. Is a high ROE always a sign of a good company?

Not until the cause is separated out. ROE decomposes into net margin times asset turnover times financial leverage, and the nature of the risk differs with which piece lifted the value. In particular, ROE manufactured by leverage amplifies earnings volatility.

Q. Should I use consolidated or separate financial statements?

Consolidated is the default for business substance, but the essential thing is using the same basis as the comparison set. In a holding structure where subsidiaries run the operations, net income differs greatly between the two bases and the PER value itself changes. Separating the share attributable to owners of the parent from consolidated net income must be checked as well.

Conclusion - a low PER is a place to start, not a conclusion

Three sentences. First, PER, PBR and ROE are not tools that supply answers but tools that narrow down what must be verified. When PER is low, the evidence that the ratio worked is not a buy decision but the question why. Second, the three are bound by the identity that PBR equals PER times ROE, so they cannot be counted separately. A low PBR at a company with low ROE may be a justified discount rather than an undervaluation. Third, the ratio never miscalculates - the lie starts in the denominator. One-off gains and losses, losses, buybacks and capital reductions, the accounting for intangibles, and mixing consolidated with separate all shake the denominator.

Four practical takeaways. One, before reading a ratio, establish what sits in its denominator. Trailing or forward, consolidated or separate, parent share or total. Two, read the three together. The identity holds, so knowing two fixes the third, and a combination that does not fit is itself the place to look. Three, always split ROE into three pieces. ROE from leverage and ROE from margin describe different companies. Four, if the comparison conditions cannot be matched, do not compare. If sector, basis, date or statement scope fails on even one count, that comparison is not evidence.

Finally, a word about the nature of this information. Ratio definitions rarely change, but the accounting rules and calculation conventions that produce them do. Data providers compute a ratio of the same name differently, and when a supervisory guideline on accounting treatment appears, past figures are sometimes adjusted. The sources below therefore record which institution material was used for each claim. Remembering the route to verification lasts longer than memorising a value.
